# Q1 through SB 53 and its lineage: the answer

*G1b thin pass, 2026-10-07, Claude (Opus 5.5). Repaired the same day after an independent verifier's report (`de-novo-feedback-1.md`; what changed is in `response-to-de-novo-feedback-1.md`). Disposable: nothing here is adopted into `def/` or the plan except by a later decision.*

*How to check it.*
- *Every `P-` id is a passage in `records/passages.yaml`, with page, line range and exact quote. `tools/check.py` confirms each one against the `pdftotext -layout` extraction.*
- *The tables at the end are generated by `tools/answer.py` from the records, not written by hand.*
- *Every "yes", "no" or "open" there is the output of **our** frame for the source, unreviewed. It is our reading of what the source says, not the source's own classification.*

**The question** (candidate Q1, `influx/competency-questions-draft.md`): *For a single incident with 60 deaths, which sources would call it catastrophic, severe or systemic, under what conditions, and through how many independent lineages?*

**The slice.**
- **California SB 53, at full depth.** Both of its definitions of "catastrophic risk" and of "critical safety incident" are covered: Business and Professions Code §22757.11 and Labor Code §1107.
- **The four documents that carry SB 53's ">50 people or $1B" bar**, at the passages that carry it:
  - New York's RAISE chapter amendment, S.8828, in its text as introduced;
  - xAI's Frontier AI Framework of 2025-12-30;
  - Anthropic's Frontier Compliance Framework v2;
  - OpenAI's Frontier Governance Framework.
- **Single passages from neighbouring documents** that the slice's documents point at:
  - xAI's 2025 Risk Management Framework;
  - OpenAI's Preparedness Framework v2;
  - and, added in the repair, Anthropic's RSP v3.4 (footnote 1) and the EU AI Act's Art. 3(65).

What the slice leaves out is listed under *Coverage* below. Out of coverage is not the same as "no".

## The short answer

1. **No document in the slice calls an incident "catastrophic", "severe" or "systemic" in those words.**
   - SB 53, RAISE and xAI use "catastrophic" of a *risk*.
   - The FCF, the FGF and the AI Act use "systemic" of a *risk*.
   - The FGF and the PF use "severe" of *harm*.

   Q1 therefore has to be put in two forms:
   - **ex ante:** is a risk of an outcome like this one catastrophic or systemic?
   - **ex post:** what label does the source give the incident itself?

   For the ex-post form, SB 53's label is "critical safety incident". The companies also have labels of their own:
   - the FCF has an "AI Event" step that feeds the statutory "Serious AI Incident" and "Critical Safety Incident";
   - the FGF has "AI safety incident", whose severity an unpublished response plan grades.

   These are recorded in the FCF's and FGF's §2.6 but not translated in this pass.
2. **Ex ante, 60 deaths in one incident clears every casualty bar in the lineage**, under every reading of the bar clause (P-sb53-bp-bar). Whether the risk *is* catastrophic still turns on facts Q1 doesn't state. Our frame leaves nine open for SB 53:
   - the incident involved a frontier model doing one of three listed things;
   - a frontier developer's conduct "materially contribute[d]";
   - the risk was "foreseeable" and "material";
   - none of three exclusions applies.

   RAISE adds that the developer is nongovernmental and the model is within New York's reach. The companies' own sentences add that the model is theirs.

   **So for Q1 the answer is a list of conditions, not a yes or no.** That follows from Q1 stipulating two facts. It says nothing about which conditions matter more.
3. **Ex post, SB 53's label is "critical safety incident", on conditions.** It has four limbs (P-sb53-bp-csi, P-sb53-bp-csi4):
   - **Limb (2)**, "Harm resulting from the materialization of a catastrophic risk", carries the catastrophic-risk conditions.
   - **Limbs (1) and (3)**, weight theft that "results in death" and "Loss of control … causing death", have no >50 bar.
   - **Limb (4)** needs no harm at all, only deceptive subversion "in a manner that demonstrates materially increased catastrophic risk".

   For Q1 the result is open: 60 deaths without a frontier model, or from a cause outside the limbs, is no critical safety incident. The label's consequences attach to the incident:
   - SB 53: a report to the Office of Emergency Services within 15 days "of discovering" it, or to an authority within 24 hours where there is "an imminent risk of death or serious physical injury" (P-sb53-bp-report, P-sb53-bp-report24);
   - RAISE: 72 hours "from a determination that a critical safety incident has occurred" (P-raise-report). A different trigger as well as a different window.
4. **"Systemic" in the FCF and FGF is a class that contains SB 53's catastrophic risk and the EU's systemic risk** (P-fcf-umbrella, P-fgf-umbrella).
   - **Their own definition sentences** reuse SB 53's numbers but change the bar. It becomes an example inside an open class ("includes … including but not limited to"), where SB 53's is a floor.
   - **They drop SB 53's exclusions,** and move its conduct list into a category table that may or may not restrict the definition (R-fcf-categories, R-fgf-categories).
   - **In the 40-death variant** SB 53's closed bar gives *no*, and both companies' own sentences stay *open* through their unbound genus. That is computed: read as closed, the same sentences give *no*. The umbrella rows stay open regardless, because the EU member's conditions are not stated by any scenario.
   - **The two sentences, and the umbrella sentence around them, share wording with each other that is in neither SB 53 nor the EU Code** (L-fcf-fgf-template). They are not two independent readings of SB 53.
5. **In Q1 itself, "severe" is the one place where a reading changes a result** (R-fgf-severe; computed at the row, in every scenario). In the variants, pooling, what "a single incident" attaches to, and closure also change rows (`weight-and-defects.md` §1).
   - The FGF's systemic risk is "risks of severe harm … including risks that a model will materially contribute to greater than 50 fatalities" (P-fgf-def). On that sentence, 60 fatalities from one incident is severe harm.
   - OpenAI's PF defines "severe harm" as "the death or grave injury of thousands of people" (P-pf-severe), so 60 deaths alone is not. Open-world, the PF stays open, since unstated grave injuries could add up (D-q1-world).
   - The FGF also reuses two PF sentences containing "severe harm", verbatim and unattributed, one of them two lines below its definition (L-fgf-pf-reuse). That is evidence for the PF sense.
   - Our lean stays with the FGF's own sentence, at low-moderate confidence. The record keeps both readings.
   - For casualties the two senses are about 20 to 200 times apart; for dollars, $1B against hundreds of billions.
6. **Independent lineages: one.**
   - Six definitions in five documents carry the bar. All trace to SB 53:
     - xAI by quotation: its footnote matches SB 53's (c)(1) and (A)–(C) exactly, checked by script, and stops before the exclusions;
     - RAISE by near-verbatim copy, never naming SB 53;
     - the FCF and FGF by restatement through a shared template.
   - SB 53's own upstream was not followed. The HSE's 2001 "50 people or more in a single event" is a lead, unchecked (L-sb53-upstream).
7. **The answer depends on a date Q1 doesn't give.**
   - RAISE takes effect 2027-01-01 (as introduced).
   - The FCF v2 starts 2026-07-24; FCF v1 was in force earlier and wasn't read.
   - xAI's 2025 framework carries the bar. Its 2026 framework (from 2026-06-30) doesn't, and takes its terminology from the EU Code. Whether the 2026 one replaces the 2025 one, or sits beside it as an EU-facing framework, is undetermined (L-xai-faif-revised-2026).

## Source by source

### SB 53, Business and Professions Code Chapter 25.1

**Catastrophic risk** (§22757.11(c), P-sb53-bp-cr, P-sb53-bp-cr-conducts, P-sb53-bp-cr-excl). The frame `F-sb53-bp-cr` splits the one sentence into slots. Each is listed with SB 53's words and what Q1 tells us about it.

| Slot | SB 53's words | For Q1's incident |
|---|---|---|
| knowledge standard | "foreseeable" | unknown. California law's to fill (R-foreseeable, not followed). Judged as of the conduct, on our reading |
| materiality | "material" | unknown. Its dimension is itself ambiguous (R-material) |
| conduct bearer | "a frontier developer's development, storage, use, or deployment of a frontier model" | unknown: whose model, how much compute (P-sb53-bp-fd, P-sb53-bp-fm) |
| causal contribution | "will materially contribute to" | unknown (R-materially-contribute) |
| magnitude bar, aggregation, counting unit | "the death of, or serious injury to, more than 50 people or more than one billion dollars ($1,000,000,000) in damage to, or loss of, property arising from a single incident" | **met** for the casualties. Pooling (R-pooling) is moot here. What "a single incident involving a frontier model doing …" attaches to (R-single-incident-attachment) decides only whether the conduct list applies to the casualty arm; the row stays open either way |
| model conduct | "involving a frontier model doing any of the following: (A) Providing expert-level assistance in the creation or release of a chemical, biological, radiological, or nuclear weapon. (B) Engaging in conduct with no meaningful human oversight, intervention, or supervision that is either a cyberattack or, if the conduct had been committed by a human, would constitute the crime of murder, assault, extortion, or theft, … (C) Evading the control of its frontier developer or user." | unknown. A closed list: 60 deaths caused any other way (say, through a model's medical advice) are outside, on the attaches-to-both reading |
| exclusions | "(A) Information that a frontier model outputs if the information is otherwise publicly accessible in a substantially similar form from a source other than a foundation model. (B) Lawful activity of the federal government. (C) Harm caused by a frontier model in combination with other software if the frontier model did not materially contribute to the harm." | unknown |
| property, for the dollar bar | "tangible or intangible" (P-sb53-bp-property), less "loss of value of equity" (P-sb53-bp-equity) | not reached |

The definition is closed ("means"). Under our frame, a 40-death outcome is *not* a catastrophic risk under SB 53, whatever else holds (`v-40-deaths`).

**Critical safety incident** (§22757.11(d), P-sb53-bp-csi, P-sb53-bp-csi4). Any one of four limbs:

| Limb | SB 53's words | Relation to catastrophic risk | Casualty bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Unauthorized access to, modification of, or exfiltration of, the model weights of a frontier model that results in death or bodily injury" | none | any death or bodily injury |
| 2 | "Harm resulting from the materialization of a catastrophic risk" | realizes one (`thin:materialization`) | >50, unless partial materialization counts (R-materialization-partial; it decides limb (2)'s clause at 40 deaths, but no row, since the other limbs stay open) |
| 3 | "Loss of control of a frontier model causing death or bodily injury" | none | any death or bodily injury |
| 4 | "A frontier model that uses deceptive techniques against the frontier developer to subvert the controls or monitoring of its frontier developer outside of the context of an evaluation designed to elicit this behavior and in a manner that demonstrates materially increased catastrophic risk" | evidences an increase in one (`thin:risk-increase-evidenced`) | none: no harm needed |

"Loss of control" in limb (3) and "Evading the control" in the catastrophic-risk definition are both undefined and worded differently. Both descendant frameworks file evasion under loss of control (P-fcf-loc, P-fgf-loc-cell), which is weak evidence for reading loss of control as the wider term (R-control).

"Severe" and "systemic" never occur in SB 53 (counted in the extraction). Its only "severity" grades a *violation*, for the civil penalty (P-sb53-bp-severity).

**"Catastrophic risk" also carries duties of its own**, apart from any incident:
- the frontier AI framework (§22757.12);
- the ban on materially false or misleading statements about it (P-sb53-bp-misleading);
- reports of internal-use assessments to OES.

### SB 53, Labor Code Chapter 5.1 (the whistleblower chapter)

The same legislature defines both terms again for this chapter (P-sb53-lab-cr, P-sb53-lab-csi, P-sb53-lab-csi4), with three differences:
- **Foundation model in place of frontier model,** throughout. A frontier developer's sub-frontier foundation model is therefore inside this chapter's catastrophic risk and outside the other's.
- **Limb (1) of the incident adds property:** "death, bodily injury, or damage to, or loss of, property".
- **Small rewordings.** In (B), "if committed by a human" replaces "if the conduct had been committed by a human"; in exclusion (C), "where" replaces "if".

Which definition "catastrophic risk" reaches inside limb (2) depends on the chapter it is read from (R-cr-origin).

### New York RAISE, S.8828 (as introduced 2026-01-08)

§1420(3)–(4) are SB 53's B&P definitions word for word, except:
- "fifty" for "50";
- no "($1,000,000,000)";
- lower-case list markers (P-raise-cr, P-raise-csi).

Two differences change what the same words reach:
- "Person" is defined as nongovernmental (P-raise-person). The frame diff reports **"bearer: SAME WORDS, different test"**.
- The act applies only to frontier models "developed, deployed, or operating in whole or in part in New York state" (P-raise-scope).

Its reporting window and trigger differ from SB 53's (short answer 3). It takes effect 2027-01-01 (P-raise-effective, as introduced). The catalog's report that it was signed on 2026-03-27 rests on a secondary source.

### xAI

- **FAIF of 2025-12-30.** "This risk includes, but is not limited to, Catastrophic Risk as defined in the TFAIA" (P-xai25-scope). Its footnote quotes §22757.11(c)(1) with (A)–(C), exactly (P-xai25-fn, P-xai25-fn-conducts), and stops before the exclusions; incorporation "as defined in the TFAIA" brings those in. Under this document a 60-death incident is classed as under SB 53.
- **RMF of 2025-08-20:** "more than one hundred deaths or over $1 billion in damages from weapons of mass destruction or cyberterrorist attacks on critical infrastructure" (P-xairmf-bar). 60 deaths falls below the casualty arm. Its dollar arm is open unless Q1 is read closed-world.
- **FAIF effective 2026-06-30.** It contains none of "TFAIA", "50 people", "billion" or "catastrophic" (searched). It uses "systemic risk" throughout, takes its terminology from the EU Code (its footnote 1), and speaks of "severe or systemic harm" (P-xai26-severe-systemic). It is a candidate source for Q1's word "systemic" that this pass did not translate.

### Anthropic, FCF v2 (effective 2026-07-24)

- **The umbrella:** "references to 'systemic' risks include both catastrophic risks under the TFAIA and systemic risks under the EU AI Act" (P-fcf-umbrella). Whether "include both" is exhaustive is R-fcf-umbrella, given the same test as the FGF's. Moot at the row.
- **Its own sentence:** "foreseeable and material risks of large-scale harm from the most advanced (i.e. state-of-the-art) models at any given point in time, including but not limited to >50 fatalities arising from a single incident, or 1 billion dollars of financial damages" (P-fcf-def). The section it opens is about "systemic risks stemming from our models" (P-fcf-our-models). Against SB 53 (computed drift, L-fcf-derived; partly shared with the FGF, L-fcf-fgf-template):

  | Change | SB 53 | FCF's sentence |
  |---|---|---|
  | closure | closed: a floor | open: an example |
  | harm counted | "death of, or serious injury to" | "fatalities" |
  | dollar harm | "damage to, or loss of, property" | "financial damages" |
  | dollar comparator | "more than one billion" | "1 billion" |
  | causal link | "a frontier developer's … materially contribute" | "from" |
  | added | — | "at any given point in time" (coverage that moves with time) |
  | moved | the conduct list | into the risk categories, widened from a closed list of four crimes to an open class, "serious crimes (such as assault, extortion, or theft)", whose examples omit murder (P-fcf-loc) |
  | dropped | — | all three exclusions |
- **Its footnote on the RSP** (P-fcf-rsp-sense) is consistent with the RSP v3.4's own footnote 1 (P-rsp34-fn1). The RSP uses "catastrophic risk" "in its plain meaning rather than adopting any specific statutory definition", with "existential threats or fundamental destabilization of global systems" as examples. So the RSP's term is deliberately unbound (R-fcf-rsp-catastrophic). That does not make a 60-death incident "not catastrophic" under the RSP; it means the RSP doesn't decide.

### OpenAI, FGF and PF

- **The umbrella** reads as closed ("we mean both", P-fgf-umbrella). The definition sentence then says "includes" (P-fgf-def). Which governs is R-fgf-umbrella-closure, moot at the row in every scenario.
- **Against SB 53, the sentence:**
  - makes the model the contributor ("a model will materially contribute");
  - counts "fatalities";
  - drops the exclusions;
  - names its genus "severe harm".
- **Its conduct list is in the category table** the FGF ties to "this FGF definition" (P-fgf-categories-intro). The loss-of-control cell carries SB 53's (B) and (C) nearly verbatim, murder included (P-fgf-loc-cell). The other cells' descriptions come from the EU Code (L-eu-code-to-fgf-categories).
- **"Severe harm"**: see short answer 5. The FGF's note that the PF "may use different definitions of catastrophic risk" (P-fgf-pf-sense) is about "catastrophic risk", not "severe harm".

## Coverage: what this slice does not answer

Q1 asks about "sources" generally. This pass answers for SB 53 and its lineage only. In particular, it does not answer for:
- **The EU AI Act's systemic risk beyond Art. 3(65).** That definition is now read (P-act-3-65). Its conditions concern capabilities, Union-market impact and propagation, with no casualty bar, and Q1 states none of them, so the umbrella rows stay open. The EU Code's systemic-risk tests were not read.
- **xAI's 2026 framework's "systemic risk"** (above).
- **The companies' own incident ladders** (short answer 1).
- **The UK NRR** (by OVERVIEW §2.6, >50 fatalities falls in its "Moderate" band; not checked here), **IASR 2026**, **the RSP beyond its footnote**, **the PF beyond three passages**, **Meta, GDM and the other frameworks**, and **Anthropic's FCF v1**, which was in force at the earliest date computed.
