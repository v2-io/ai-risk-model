**The question** (candidate Q1, `influx/competency-questions-draft.md`): *For a single incident with 60 deaths, which sources would call it catastrophic, severe or systemic, under what conditions, and through how many independent lineages?*

**The slice.**
- **California SB 53, at full depth.** Both of its definitions of "catastrophic risk" and of "critical safety incident" are covered: Business and Professions Code §22757.11 and Labor Code §1107.
- **The four documents that carry SB 53's ">50 people or $1B" bar**, at the passages that carry it:
  - New York's RAISE chapter amendment, S.8828, in its text as introduced;
  - xAI's Frontier AI Framework of 2025-12-30;
  - Anthropic's Frontier Compliance Framework v2;
  - OpenAI's Frontier Governance Framework.
- **Two neighbours, one passage each**, because the slice's own documents point at them:
  - xAI's 2025 Risk Management Framework;
  - OpenAI's Preparedness Framework v2.

What the slice leaves out is listed under *Coverage* below. Out of coverage is not the same as "no".

## The short answer

1. **No document in the slice calls an incident "catastrophic", "severe" or "systemic".**
   - SB 53, RAISE and xAI use "catastrophic" of a *risk*.
   - The FCF and FGF use "systemic" of a *risk*.
   - The FGF and the PF use "severe" of *harm*.

   An incident reaches these words only in reverse, as "Harm resulting from the materialization of a catastrophic risk" (P-sb53-bp-csi). So Q1 has to be put to these sources in two forms:
   - **ex ante:** is a risk of an outcome like this one a catastrophic or systemic risk?
   - **ex post:** what does the source call the incident itself?
2. **Ex ante, 60 deaths in one incident clears every casualty bar in the lineage.**
   - It meets SB 53's "more than 50 people … arising from a single incident" (P-sb53-bp-bar) under every reading of the clause.
   - Whether the risk *is* catastrophic still turns on conditions Q1 doesn't state (nine open facts in the computed table):
     - the incident involved a frontier model doing one of three listed things;
     - a frontier developer's conduct "materially contribute[d]";
     - the risk was "foreseeable" and "material";
     - none of three exclusions applies;
     - (and, for RAISE only) the developer is nongovernmental and the model is within New York's reach.

   For Q1 the answer is therefore a list of conditions, not a yes or no.
3. **Ex post, SB 53 has one label for the incident: "critical safety incident".**
   - Only one of its four limbs, (2), needs the catastrophic-risk conditions.
   - Limbs (1), weight theft "that results in death", and (3), "Loss of control … causing death", have no casualty threshold. They would cover one death as well as sixty.
   - This label is the one with consequences: a report to the Office of Emergency Services within 15 days, or to an authority within 24 hours where there is "an imminent risk of death or serious physical injury" (P-sb53-bp-report, P-sb53-bp-report24). RAISE's window is 72 hours (P-raise-report).
4. **"Systemic"** is the FCF's and FGF's word for a class that *contains* SB 53's catastrophic risk (P-fcf-umbrella, P-fgf-umbrella).
   - Their own definition sentences reuse the numbers but loosen the bar. It becomes an example inside an open class ("includes … including but not limited to"), where SB 53's is a floor.
   - Both sentences drop SB 53's closed list of conducts and its exclusions.
   - A 60-death single-incident risk falls inside their sentences on fewer conditions than SB 53 requires. For the FGF this holds only if its sentence adds to its umbrella (R-fgf-umbrella-closure). A 40-death risk is excluded by SB 53 and left undecided by both.
5. **"Severe" is the one place where a yes or no turns on a single reading.**
   - The FGF's systemic risk is "risks of severe harm … including risks that a model will materially contribute to greater than 50 fatalities" (P-fgf-def). So on its own terms, 60 fatalities from one incident is severe harm.
   - OpenAI's PF defines "severe harm" as "the death or grave injury of thousands of people" (P-pf-severe), so 60 deaths alone is not. (Open-world, the PF stays open, since unstated grave injuries could add up; see D-q1-world.)
   - The FGF says the PF "may use different definitions" (P-fgf-pf-sense). Which sense the FGF's words carry is R-fgf-severe. Our lean is the FGF's own, but the record keeps both.
6. **Independent lineages: one.**
   - Six definitions in five documents carry the bar.
   - All of them trace to SB 53, by quotation (xAI), by near-verbatim copy (RAISE, which never names SB 53), or by restatement in each company's own TFAIA compliance framework (FCF, FGF).
   - xAI's quotation matches SB 53's words exactly (checked by script).
   - Where SB 53's bar came from was not followed. The HSE's 2001 "50 people or more in a single event" is a lead, unchecked (L-sb53-upstream).
7. **The answer depends on a date Q1 doesn't give.**

   | Date | Documents in the slice that carry the bar |
   |---|---|
   | 2026-03-01 | SB 53 and xAI's 2025 framework.<br>Anthropic's FCF v1 was in force but wasn't read. |
   | 2026-10-07 | SB 53, the FCF v2 and the FGF.<br>xAI's 2026 framework has dropped the bar (L-xai-faif-revised-2026). |
   | 2027-02-01 | RAISE as well, if enacted as introduced (P-raise-effective). |

## Source by source

### SB 53, Business and Professions Code Chapter 25.1

**Catastrophic risk** (§22757.11(c), P-sb53-bp-cr, P-sb53-bp-cr-conducts, P-sb53-bp-cr-excl). The frame `F-sb53-bp-cr` splits the one sentence into eight slots. Each is listed with SB 53's words and what Q1 tells us about it.

| Slot | SB 53's words | For Q1's incident |
|---|---|---|
| knowledge standard | "foreseeable" | unknown. California law's to fill (R-foreseeable, not followed). Judged as of the conduct, on our reading |
| materiality | "material" | unknown. Its dimension is itself ambiguous: importance, a likelihood floor, or magnitude (R-material) |
| conduct bearer | "a frontier developer's development, storage, use, or deployment of a frontier model" | unknown: whose model, how much compute (P-sb53-bp-fd, P-sb53-bp-fm) |
| causal contribution | "will materially contribute to" | unknown (R-materially-contribute) |
| magnitude bar, aggregation, counting unit | "the death of, or serious injury to, more than 50 people or more than one billion dollars ($1,000,000,000) in damage to, or loss of, property arising from a single incident" | **met**: 60 > 50, one incident. Two ambiguities in the clause, pooling (R-pooling) and what "a single incident" attaches to (R-single-incident-attachment), are both moot at 60 deaths (computed) |
| model conduct | "involving a frontier model doing any of the following: (A) Providing expert-level assistance in the creation or release of a chemical, biological, radiological, or nuclear weapon. (B) Engaging in conduct with no meaningful human oversight, intervention, or supervision that is either a cyberattack or, if the conduct had been committed by a human, would constitute the crime of murder, assault, extortion, or theft, … (C) Evading the control of its frontier developer or user." | unknown. A closed list: 60 deaths caused any other way (say, through a model's medical advice) are not a catastrophic risk under SB 53 |
| exclusions | "(A) Information that a frontier model outputs if the information is otherwise publicly accessible in a substantially similar form from a source other than a foundation model. (B) Lawful activity of the federal government. (C) Harm caused by a frontier model in combination with other software if the frontier model did not materially contribute to the harm." | unknown |
| property, for the dollar bar | "tangible or intangible" (P-sb53-bp-property), less "loss of value of equity" (P-sb53-bp-equity) | not reached at 60 deaths |

The definition is closed ("means"). A 40-death outcome is therefore *not* a catastrophic risk under SB 53, whatever else holds. The `v-40-deaths` scenario computes this.

**Critical safety incident** (§22757.11(d), P-sb53-bp-csi, P-sb53-bp-csi4). Any one of four limbs:

| Limb | SB 53's words | Needs the catastrophic-risk conditions? | Casualty bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Unauthorized access to, modification of, or exfiltration of, the model weights of a frontier model that results in death or bodily injury" | no | any death |
| 2 | "Harm resulting from the materialization of a catastrophic risk" | yes, all of them | >50, unless partial materialization counts (R-materialization-partial: moot at 60, decisive at 40) |
| 3 | "Loss of control of a frontier model causing death or bodily injury" | no | any death |
| 4 | deceptive subversion of the developer's controls, "outside of the context of an evaluation …" | no | none needed |

"Loss of control" in limb 3 and "Evading the control" in the catastrophic-risk definition are both undefined and worded differently. Their relation is open (R-control).

"Severe" and "systemic" never occur in SB 53 (counted in the extraction). Its only "severity" grades a *violation*, for the civil penalty (P-sb53-bp-severity).

### SB 53, Labor Code Chapter 5.1 (the whistleblower chapter)

The same legislature defines both terms again for this chapter (P-sb53-lab-cr, P-sb53-lab-csi), with three differences:
- **Foundation model in place of frontier model,** in the conduct, the model-conduct list and the exclusions. A frontier developer's sub-frontier foundation model is therefore inside this chapter's catastrophic risk and outside the other's.
- **Limb (1) of the incident adds property:** "death, bodily injury, or damage to, or loss of, property".
- **Small rewordings.** In (B), "if committed by a human" replaces "if the conduct had been committed by a human"; in exclusion (C), "where" replaces "if".

Which definition "catastrophic risk" reaches inside limb (2) depends on the chapter it is read from (R-cr-origin). This is address theory's origin-relative resolution, in the statute's own structure.

### New York RAISE, S.8828 (as introduced 2026-01-08)

§1420(3)–(4) are SB 53's B&P definitions word for word, except:
- "fifty" for "50";
- no "($1,000,000,000)";
- lower-case list markers (P-raise-cr, P-raise-csi).

Two differences change what the same words reach:
- "Person" is defined as nongovernmental (P-raise-person; SB 53's chapter leaves it undefined). The frame diff reports **"bearer: SAME WORDS, different test"**.
- The act applies only to frontier models "developed, deployed, or operating in whole or in part in New York state" (P-raise-scope).

It takes effect 2027-01-01 (P-raise-effective, as introduced). The catalog's report that it was signed on 2026-03-27 rests on a secondary source, and whether the enacted text matches this one was not checked.

### xAI

- **FAIF of 2025-12-30.** "This risk includes, but is not limited to, Catastrophic Risk as defined in the TFAIA" (P-xai25-scope), with SB 53's definition quoted in full in a footnote (P-xai25-fn). The quotation is exact. Under this document a 60-death incident is classed exactly as under SB 53. xAI's scope is wider ("not limited to"), but its wider part is not defined.
- **RMF of 2025-08-20,** the earlier framework: "more than one hundred deaths or over $1 billion in damages from weapons of mass destruction or cyberterrorist attacks on critical infrastructure" (P-xairmf-bar). A 60-death incident falls below the casualty bar. Its dollar arm is open unless Q1 is read closed-world.
- **FAIF effective 2026-06-30.** It contains none of "TFAIA", "50 people", "billion" or "catastrophic" (searched), and speaks of "severe or systemic harm" (P-xai26-severe-systemic), undefined in the passage read.

Neither xAI document says it replaces the other. The succession is our inference.

### Anthropic, FCF v2 (effective 2026-07-24)

- **An open umbrella.** "references to 'systemic' risks include both catastrophic risks under the TFAIA and systemic risks under the EU AI Act" (P-fcf-umbrella). Any SB 53 catastrophic risk is an FCF systemic risk.
- **Its own sentence:** "foreseeable and material risks of large-scale harm from the most advanced (i.e. state-of-the-art) models at any given point in time, including but not limited to >50 fatalities arising from a single incident, or 1 billion dollars of financial damages" (P-fcf-def). Against SB 53's (computed drift, `L-fcf-derived`):

  | Change | SB 53 | FCF's sentence |
  |---|---|---|
  | closure | closed: a floor | open: an example |
  | harm counted | "death of, or serious injury to" | "fatalities" |
  | dollar harm | "damage to, or loss of, property" | "financial damages" |
  | dollar comparator | "more than one billion" | "1 billion" |
  | causal link | "a frontier developer's … materially contribute" | "from" |
  | dropped | — | the closed conduct list; all three exclusions |
  | added | — | a coverage condition that moves with time ("at any given point in time") |

  "A single incident" sits after "fatalities". On one reading it doesn't govern the dollars (R-fcf-dollar-attachment; moot here).
- **Its footnote on the RSP:** "The RSP uses 'catastrophic risk' in a different sense than this Framework, referring to risks at the most extreme end of the severity spectrum (such as existential threats …)" (P-fcf-rsp-sense). On Anthropic's report, the RSP would not call a 60-death incident catastrophic. The RSP itself was not read; this is the FCF's claim about it, recorded as nested (A-fcf-rsp-sense).

### OpenAI, FGF and PF

- **The FGF's umbrella reads as closed:** "when we refer to 'systemic' risks, we mean both catastrophic risks under the TFAIA and systemic risks under the EU AI Act" (P-fgf-umbrella).
- **Its own sentence then says "includes"** (P-fgf-def). Whether that sentence adds to the two statutory members or summarizes them is R-fgf-umbrella-closure, moot for every scenario computed.
- **Against SB 53,** the sentence:
  - makes the model, not the developer's conduct, the contributor ("a model will materially contribute");
  - counts "fatalities";
  - drops the conduct list and the exclusions;
  - names its genus "severe harm".
- **"Severe harm"** is where OpenAI's two documents part by two orders of magnitude: 60 deaths is severe harm in the FGF's sentence and not in the PF's footnote (P-pf-severe). See short answer 5.
