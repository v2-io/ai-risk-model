# On the brief, and what I re-examined because of it

*Written mid-spike, 2026-10-06, in answer to the coordinator's after-the-fact second pass on its brief, which listed its primes and asked whether I'd rather continue or hand over to a fresh agent with a cleaner brief.*

**Answer: continue, and re-examine three things.** By the time the list arrived I had read the repo's alignment material and swept the extracted corpus (about 210 documents) for definitions of alignment and for role words. Several findings had already come from outside the brief's frame: the corpus's own multi-referent definitions, ETSI's and NIST's stakeholder lists, CAISI's "CCP alignment" metric, and Davidson's "singular" and "secret loyalties". A fresh agent would have to redo that sweep, and the sweep is where most of the evidence is. Still, I can see where the primes had taken hold, so I re-examined each of these on purpose instead of letting it stand:

1. **The two-bases reading (lifecycle/market vs. writes-into).** I had picked it up as the organizing frame for roles. In the corpus I have found at least five other bases of division: task (NIST), responsibility for an asset (ETSI's "data custodians", "system operators"), a training act with a threshold (SB 53), authority to instruct (the companies' "principal" and "chain of command" vocabulary), and affectedness (NIST's "affected individuals/communities", ETSI's "affected entities", the AI Act's undefined "affected persons"). `04-actors.md` treats the bases as plural and asks which relations they are, instead of taking two as given.
2. **`alignment.md` as "our side" rather than a source.** I had accepted that. Model beta's own draft says the opposite ("His view, included on the same terms as any source's", `def-agent-surfaces.ud`). In `03-structure.md` I read the map as a model with its own commitments, including what its edges mean and which relations its two tables merge, before using its rows as our vocabulary.
3. **"Default referent or none" as the question.** I had been testing those two options against each other. `03-structure.md` asks first whether the question is well-formed, and the answer it reaches is that it isn't quite. Joseph's broader ask (implications of an ambiguous definition; established terms for existing actors; new actors) is the target, and the default question is one corner of it.

The "principal–agent theory" pointer (prime 4) did shape both literature briefs I sent out. I have marked the research reports' leads as the coordinator's and my guesses, and I treat their findings as evidence to weigh, not as confirmation of the frame.
