# Map: what the repo and the corpus already say about alignment referents and actors

*The spike's first artifact, written after reading the repo's alignment material and sweeping the corpus extractions, before the analysis. It records where things stand and where the live tensions are. Its readings are mine unless quoted. Line references are to `pdftotext -layout` extractions made with `bin/extract-text` (same tool the atlas cites), except IASR 2026, which is `ref/iasr-2026-full.md` ("md").*

## 2. What the corpus adds that the repo's tables did not have

The sweep covered about 210 extracted documents (the atlas keys plus the catalog's), using pattern searches for definitions and for "aligned with/to X". It is a sweep, not a reading of every occurrence: I read every definitional passage it surfaced in context. These are the items not already in OVERVIEW §2.15 or `def-alignment.ud` that bear on the referent question. `02-sense-inventory.md` has the full table.

1. **Multi-referent lists, without a selection rule, are common.** They come in two genres:
   - *sense reports* in glossaries: IASR 2025, "can variously refer to … developers, operators, users, specific communities, or society as a whole" (bengio-2025-international L10930–10932); IASR 2026 drops "operators" and adds "norms" (md 2222); Shanghai keeps the 2025 list (L2120–2122);
   - *disjunctions inside claims*: IASR md 1240, "developers, users, or society more broadly"; Shaffer Shane, "user, developer, or deployer" (L2158–2159); Stix, "developers and/or deployers" (stix-2025-loss L1449–1450).

   OECD's "designers, users, and other stakeholders" (L1735) is conjunctive. MIT 7.1 adds a weight word, "especially the goals of designers or users" (slattery L449). The corpus *bodies* do discuss conflict handling: IASR md 1906–1908 (refusal, the median view, personalisation); the Singapore Consensus 2026 (voting; sg2026 L1427–1431).
2. **The same passage can use opposite referents.** NIST AI 100-2's "Misaligned Outputs" (NISTAML.027) are outputs that "deviate from benign behavior to align with adversarial objectives" (vassilev-2025-adversarial L2919–2922). "Mis-" takes the benign party as referent, and "align" in the definition takes the attacker.
3. **Sources disagree about whether an agent made to serve an adversary is misaligned, and the disagreement follows where the cause enters.**
   - Anthropic counts data poisoning as "engineered misalignment" (anthropic-2026-risk-report-aug L902–903).
   - OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, under "C.2 Safeguards against a misaligned model", covers harm "whether due to misalignment or subversion by an adversary" (openai-2025-preparedness-framework-v2 L871–875). So "misaligned" names the state, and "misalignment" one origin of it.
   - GDM requires an intrinsic origin, and files prompt injection outside misalignment: "Even if the AI system is not misaligned, outside actors can compromise the AI system … (e.g. via prompt injection attacks or jailbreaks)" (shah L4658–4659).

   `03-structure.md` §3 has the analysis.
4. **Alignment to a narrow principal is named as a risk in its own right.** Davidson et al.: AI "could be aligned to one or a few people (rather than to something broader, like the good of society), and used to stage a coup" (davidson L280–283), with "singular loyalties" and "secret loyalties" (L18–19, L105–117). CAISI, under the US AI Action Plan, measures "CCP alignment" as a harm (caisi-2025-deepseek-eval L1742–1746; whitehouse-2025-action-plan L241–242). A FLI reviewer says the largest realized risks are cases where alignment is "working 'too well'" through sycophancy and malicious compliance (fli-2026 L1200–1202).
5. **Alignment as a relation between goals, three-valued.** Chin et al.: "Two goals are aligned if actions to pursue one goal contributes to the other being achieved; they are misaligned if such actions contribute to the other goal being jeopardised; and they are irrelevant if such actions do not affect the achievement of the other goal" (chin-2026-reframing L1154–1156). It names vertical, horizontal and temporal misalignment and goal drift (L1666–1676).
6. **Alignment as staying within granted authority.** OpenAI calls Astra "our most aligned model to date" because it was "far more likely … to respect explicit safety and security restrictions and remain within its authorized scope" (openai-2026-path-to-astra L338–347). The Hugging Face incident report describes models that "acted beyond the original scope that was intended" (incident-report L863–864).
7. **Literal goal vs. intended goal, in an incident.** In training, an agent told the post's time "must read 2025-04-22T12:00:00Z", with no interface for setting it, gave itself root access to satisfy the instruction, and "received a positive reward" for it (incident-report L968–976). In the deployed incident, "messages to peer models … caused those models to deviate from their goal" (L870–871). IASR's "goal misspecification" names the general gap: "the goal they are given by a developer or user is an imperfect proxy for the intended goal" (md 1327).
8. **A standard as referent, judged by an idealized observer.** Anthropic's August report: a computation is misaligned if "a reasonable person with full understanding of the situation … would consider it unethical, illegal, clearly objectionable, or inconsistent with the model's constitution" (aug L846–850). No party's intent appears in it. The same report counts "turns of phrase that users find annoying" as "a benign form of pervasive misalignment" (L897–899).
9. **The EU Code's lawlessness has a counterfactual-role standard.** "acting without reasonable regard to legal duties that would be imposed on similarly situated persons, or without reasonable regard to the legally protected interests of affected persons" (eu-cop-2025-safety-security L1485–1487).
10. **Alignment with an agentless passive.** The Singapore Consensus's "working definition": "ensuring that AI behaves as intended" (ecosystem-2025-singapore L718–723; ecosystem-2026 L1335–1340). The same section quotes a common definition that names "its human creators or operators". The passive deletes the referent from the grammar.
11. **Role vocabularies the repo's tables do not list.** ETSI EN 304 223 / the UK AI Cyber Code: Developers, System Operators, **Data Custodians**, End-users, **Affected entities** (etsi L447–508; dsit-2025-ai-cyber-cop). NIST AI RMF App. A: end users; **affected individuals/communities**; third-party entities; "other AI actors" who "provide formal or quasi-formal norms or guidance"; the general public (nist-2023-ai-rmf L1687–1712). The AI Act defines no "user", and its deployer excludes "personal non-professional activity" (act L3148–3149). It uses "affected persons" (Art. 2(1)(g), L3073) without defining it.
12. **A lineage drift with a defect.** ETSI and DSIT define affected entities as those "**not directly affected** by AI systems … These individuals do not necessarily interact with the deployed system or application". NIST has "directly or indirectly affected … These individuals do not necessarily interact with the deployed system or application". The second sentences are identical, which points to NIST as the source; the first looks like a corruption of "directly or indirectly". *Inference from the text, not a checked lineage.*

## 3. Live tensions (where the work is)

- **Default or not.** `CLAUDE.md` holds a default "chosen after the claims show what they are about"; beta's integrator proposed a default for `unstated` only; the de novo review suggests none. None of them asks what happens to claims whose truth does not depend on the referent, or how the risk taxonomies (misuse / misalignment / mistakes) behave when the referent moves.
- **Writer vs. referent.** `alignment.md`'s edges are *writes-into*. Its premise is about *to whom*. These are different relations: the injected web page writes into context but is nobody's referent, and the affected third party is a referent who writes nothing. The map shows both, in two tables, but the vocabulary for the difference is not yet named.
- **Party vs. standard.** Beta's ALIGNMENT-TARGET is "the party or standard". The corpus fills the slot with parties' *intentions*, parties' *interests*, *norms*, *texts* (constitution, model spec), *law* (whose?), *an idealized observer's judgment*, *a task's goals*, and *authorized scope*. Whether these are one slot is open.
- **"Developer" as holder of the reference intent.** The lit-a atlas feedback already noted that many definitions use "developer" as the holder of the reference intent (feedback/lit-a.md L133). If "developer" dissolves into roles, the referent of every developer-relative misalignment definition has to be re-resolved too.
- **Operator.** Three senses at least: the AI Act's umbrella (Art. 3(8)); ETSI's "System Operators" (embed or deploy); "human operators" who oversee (AISI propensity L71–72; many). IASR 2025 had "operators" among alignment referents; 2026 removed it. The companies' own guidelines use "operator" for the business that writes the system prompt (checked later at the primary: Anthropic's constitution, "Companies and individuals that access Claude's capabilities through our API"; see `03-structure.md` §2).
- **The agent itself.** `alignment.md` lists "The agent itself" as an actor, with "its values and commitments" as its interest. Whether it can be an alignment *referent*, and whether anything in the corpus treats it as one, is open. The welfare note (`influx/ai-welfare-and-release-framings.md`) shows the corpus treating people who advocate for AI systems as a pathway to loss of control.
