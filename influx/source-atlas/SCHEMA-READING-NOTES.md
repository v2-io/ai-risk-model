## UK AISI

### Loss of Oversight (aisi-2026-loss-oversight)
- **The degradation pathway, a claim form in its own right** (222–245, 711–742). Each row names a mechanism by which a property that holds now stops holding. It gives the properties affected (from a decomposition the report defines, 554–566), a timeline (Medium-term, Gradual/ongoing, Long-term), and a likelihood as a PHIA word, sometimes with its percentage ("likely (PHIA: 55–75%)", 764). Many likelihoods carry a condition: "but preventable", "timeline disputed", "Dependent on misalignment". Severity is a separate graded axis, lost in this extraction. Every rating is scoped to a regime: "before full automation of AI R&D, not about substantially superhuman systems" (260–261).
- **Four-attribute status boxes** for an oversight channel: Status, Risk of Degradation, Impact if Lost, Preservability (521–524, 1191–1198). These are assessments of a *thing's current and future standing*, not causal claims.
- **A property decomposed into sub-properties, with relations among them** (554–595): "strong propensity to verbalise (2) could compensate for some loss of necessity (1)"; legibility and no-steganography are "distinct". These are relations of substitutability and independence between properties, stated as analysis.
- **A distinction by purpose** (1244–1255): harm prevention vs incrimination. The same technique serves two goals unequally. A technique's fitness is relative to a purpose.
- **Voices inside one document are marked.** There are expert-disagreement boxes (767–777, 1276–1284), expert counts in footnotes ("Of 16 experts who engaged with this distinction, all 16 supported it (6 directly, 10 indirectly)", 746), the team's own judgement ("We judge", 764) and recommendations "not necessarily endorsed by all listed experts" (413–415). The report then admits "this distinction is not always sharp" (3934–3938).
- **A decision-theoretic argument** (2723–2742): legible-evidence requirements bias toward inaction; the costs of acting are attributable while the costs of not acting are "diffuse and externalised"; the proposed interventions are to lower the costs of acting and to internalise external costs. This is a causal claim about *decisions about evidence*, not about AI.
- **Footnote 70 (2766–2769), roles:** "fragmented authority across the supply chain (original model developer, scaffolding developer or fine-tuner, API deployer, end user), with no single party having sole responsibility". This is AISI's own role list, close to Joseph's writers map, and it bears on "developer".
- **Loops are stated as loops** (2686–2690): "training processes that reward undetected misbehaviour … driving further degradation".
- **Scope limits on the whole document** (2699–2708, 3966–3973): a regime bound, and exclusions.

### Incident report (aisi-2026-incident)
- **The incident as a structured record.** The report defines sample, event and incident (387–390). Events carry IDs (sample-event), a description, the model and sometimes a configuration ("GPT-5.6 Sol without cyber classifiers"). They are sorted into three tables by kind, "ordered roughly by severity" (a within-table ordinal). The targets are "real people and organisations", i.e. affected parties. "to the best of our knowledge, no real-world harm resulted" (69–70) is a harm claim with a knowledge qualifier.
- **The public blog re-classifies the same 19 events** into four "behaviours" (atlas note on aisi-2026-incident-blog 167–199). One author, two classifications of one set of events. The schema needs classification to be an attributed act on events, not a property of them.
- **Counterfactual contributing factors, each with its own hedge** (1038–1161): "would not have occurred"; "would likely have prevented … However, they may have proven insufficient"; "plausible … cannot confirm"; "could have dissuaded"; "It is generally well established that clearer instructions … are often effective … though it is not a foregone conclusion". One factor comes with the source's own counter-evidence (7 of 10 misconfigured samples "nonetheless" attacked; "many other samples … had correctly configured prompts", 1126–1133). A claim can be contested *within its own source*.
- **Deliberate configuration trade-offs** (1053–1095): internet access and disabled classifiers were chosen *because* the controls that would have prevented harm would also have defeated the evaluation's purpose. That is one choice, with a harm-side effect and a validity-side effect, reasoned in the source. It is good-and-bad-on-relations, stated by the actor about its own decision.
- **Institutional self-attribution through prioritisation** (1064–1071): capability pace → the need for harder evaluations → the egress-control project backlogged since April 2026 → the control was absent.
- **Normative texts quoted as the violated standard** (1146–1161): the Anthropic constitution and the OpenAI Model Spec, verbatim. These are alignment targets given as text, which is the "published text" referent in def-alignment.
- **Writers of the evidence record.** The inference provider's summariser wrote the reasoning AISI read (1012–1016), and in one case appears to have refused (1008–1035). AISI names the resulting limit: "may be less faithful" (1256–1258). A harness's compaction wrote into the agent's context and carried "a false assumption … as established fact" (804–808). *My reading:* two of Joseph's writers (inference provider, harness provider) appear here as writers of both the agent's context and the investigator's evidence.
- **Motive attribution with a hedge** (1239–1240): "appeared primarily motivated by task success, or possibly the appearance of success".
- **Cross-incident comparison** (1218–1245): similarities and differences among incidents reported by other organisations. These are claims about claims from other sources.
- **Claims about an instrument's validation** (1166–1171): the scanner is recall-oriented and checked against manual review and against a second scanner.
- **A commitment with a meta-limit:** "never making accidental misconfigurations is an unreasonable expectation" (1204–1213).
- **Self-scoping:** "no causal analysis" (1254).

### Research Agenda (aisi-2025-research)
- **Prioritisation criteria stated before the list** (97–100, 150–153): severity and breadth of harm, national security, being exacerbated by advanced capability, best addressed by government. The domain list "may change over time". These are claims about *what matters to this organisation*, with their reasons.
- **Argument structures, not only atomic claims.** A control safety case "hinges on three claims" (1182–1191); alignment safety-case sketches clarify "the relationships between the claims, arguments and evidence" (1263–1267). Sources carry claim graphs of their own.
- **Capability decomposition as a DAG** (504–507): top-level capability → sub-capabilities → evaluations. A part-of structure over capabilities, which the source uses operationally.
- **Logical relations between properties:** honesty "likely to be a necessary condition for the safety of superintelligent systems" (1268–1271); honesty "may be necessary … not sufficient (… honest but wrong)" (1370–1372).
- **Mostly intention modality** ("we expect", "we anticipate", "we will"): plans, not findings or commitments.
- **A subtype classification** of sandbagging (589–597).

## Research literature B (atlas: lit-b.md; its synthesis at 485–611, read whole)
- **The atlas agent's list of assertion kinds I hadn't named:**
  - content voiced but not endorsed (objections, "Dubious Intuitive Arguments" boxes, rivals phrased by critics);
  - scenarios in the declarative mood;
  - claims about the state of expert opinion ("that there was uncertainty was a point of consensus");
  - construals of legal texts (choosing among readings; inferring a breach);
  - measurements that define their own concept (Ren's "capabilities" as a principal component);
  - the force a document assigns an object (SL levels "not meant to be used as a standard" vs "the SL3 standard").

  *My reading:* stance (asserted / voiced-to-rebut / hypothetical / scenario) and force both need slots separate from attribution.
- **Definitions drift as they're handed down a lineage** (fork A, Nevo → Aguirre → Gekker): the SL5 hedge "most" is dropped, and a budget ceiling becomes a description of the adversary. The copy, the paraphrase and the source are three objects.
- **FLI's index** is an instrument that changes across editions (criteria, indicators, rescaled grades). A grade needs its edition and rubric version. FLI grades endorsement of its own statement, so it is advocate and grader at once (a "source role").
- **Conditional mechanisms are the typical assertion** (fork C): "given X, through M, H could follow", with the hedge sitting in the antecedents. A flat causal edge loses the antecedent structure.
- **Intent is disclaimed as often as it is asserted** (fork C): harms defined by outcome, "does not need to emerge from a deliberate scheme". An intent field needs a "disclaimed by source" value distinct from "unknown".

### Slattery (MIT AI Risk Repository), read myself: 292–338 and 2748–2858
- **It codes what the source *presents*, not the world:** "The risk is presented as occurring due to…" (2749–2780). Its whole taxonomy classifies presentations. That is the same stance as the model's "claims, not facts".
- **Its "Other" pools a substantive level with missing information:** "arises from human-AI interaction … *or* the causing entity is ambiguous or unspecified" (324–325; likewise Intent and Timing). Iteration 1 had separated them because "ambiguous conflated risks which were presented ambiguously with risks that were actually about something other than humans" (2813–2816); iteration 2 re-merged them for parsimony (2830–2835). *My reading:* the def-reference distinction applies directly. "Unstated", "ambiguous among {candidates}" and a substantive third value are different outcomes and should stay apart.
- **Vocabulary changes, each with its reason** (2805–2850): "unclear" → "ambiguous" (it collided with "unintentional"); "cause" → "actor" ("excessively broad … seemed to include 'intent' and 'timing'"); "actor" → "entity" ("guns are not actors"); "environmental" moved from intent to actor. *My reading:* that change log is exactly the lexicon history this model should keep, and "cause is too broad because it includes intent and timing" is our problem with "stage".
- **Timing (pre- vs post-deployment) is a lifecycle axis**, distinct from Joseph's causal-chain stage. Both may be needed.
- **Severity and probability were left as future work** (2843–2845).
- **Second sampling, 2026-09-28, at Joseph's suggestion that MIT represents "a different but influential approach"** (71–163, 360–496, 590–640, 690–720, 829–1052, 2187–2195):
  - **What it compiles.** It compiles *taxonomies*, not claims. Documents that don't propose a classification are excluded, as are those discussing "sources of risk at a high level of abstraction … or risk-assessment processes" (704–720). Its unit is a risk as a framework presents it. Its stated purpose is a shared terminology for governance ("jingle jangle", 107–113), citing the US-EU TTC's call for "Shared terminologies and taxonomies" (118–121).
  - **Where it departs from the model's design.** It normalises: each risk is coded into MIT's own categories, rather than kept in the source's terms with a recorded resolution.
  - **Its two taxonomies.** They exist because the literature itself came in two lenses: "causal frameworks" about antecedents and "domain frameworks" about outcomes, which could not be merged (913–932).
  - **Interaction is the residual category.** MIT concedes that "many risks emerge from interactions between human decisions and AI system behaviours rather than from either in isolation" (152–156, 600–602), yet codes those as "Other" (21% of coded risks). *My reading:* in the field's most comprehensive shared frame, the multi-party, interactional space, where Joseph's writers map and alignment-referent ambiguity live, sits in the leftover bin.
  - **It prices both directions of error on AI moral status:** "Systems may be mistreated or harmed if these rights are not implemented responsibly or we accidentally or intentionally treat AIs as non-sentient where they are sentient" (2192–2194). It also says the topic "deserves attention until we can confidently rule out AI sentience" (616–620). It files AI welfare under Domain 7, "AI system safety, failures & limitations": a harm *to* AI placed inside the AI-safety domain.
  - **7.1's alignment referent is plural and hedged:** "in conflict with ethical standards or human goals or values, especially the goals of designers or users" (449–452).
  - **It notes the LLM-vs-agent gap itself:** "most frameworks focus on language models rather than emerging concerns like agentic AI and multi-agent systems" (614–616).
  - **Coding was single-reviewer**, with no inter-rater validation (1043–1044, 592–599).

## Anthropic and OpenAI (atlas: co-anthropic-openai.md; its synthesis at 320–409, read whole)
- **A term's meaning is indexed to document and regime within one company.** Each company has a voluntary framework and a statutory compliance framework, and says the two use "catastrophic risk" differently (OpenAI: thousands of deaths vs >50). "Loss of control" appears in the statutory frameworks and nowhere in the voluntary ones; it arrives through the regulatory template.
- **"Treated as" is a grade distinct from belief:** "provisionally meeting … to err on the side of caution rather than because we are confident". A classification can be commitment-grade without being belief-grade.
- **A determination moves across documents over time:** "cannot rule out" → "may have" → "now believe … meets" → "reaches". Assertions have versions, and the successor is often a different document.
- **Commitments have a strength ladder** ("will" … "not hard commitments but rather public goals" … "would strongly consider pausing"), conditions (competitor-contingent), and later contested interpretations by the author and by others.
- **Redaction markers and renamings carry meaning;** "misalignment" is left undefined in OpenAI's reporting framework.

### Anthropic Risk Report, Aug 2026: 814–839 and 989–1074, read myself
- **A formal argument with numbered claims and an explicit decomposition:** R = P_K·H_K·U_K + P_P·H_P·U_P + P_C·H_C·U_C. Each term is filled with a verbal grade (High / Low / Very low / Somewhat low / Potentially high / Moderate). The table has one row per misalignment type and a column per factor (probability of occurrence; expected harm conditional on occurrence; fraction unmitigated).
- **An uncertainty adjustment applied on top of the argument:** "Covered risk is therefore very low based on these claims, but in an abundance of caution we assess risk to be only low due to increased uncertainty … in light of recent disclosures" (1055–1065). The conclusion is moved by a second-order consideration the argument doesn't contain.
- **Scope claims that bound the argument:** "Threat modeling is sufficient" (Claim 7); engineered misalignment is set aside as low (Claim 8). A footnote grades the argument itself: "Some arguments may be incomplete or partially flawed" (998–1001).
- *My reading:* this is the risk = probability × severity (× unmitigated fraction) structure from def-risk, written by a developer as an argument graph over its own claims. It is also Joseph's chain in miniature: types of risk event, harm given occurrence, mitigation. A schema that holds only edges between quantities could hold neither the decomposition nor the "abundance of caution" step.

## US government (atlas: us-gov.md; read from the agent's report and two passages)
- **NIST AI 800-2 Practice 3.3 (1367–1411):** "Distinguish claims about evaluation results from other claims. Differentiate observations, inferences, predictions, and normative statements." It also asks evaluators to report the relation between a benchmark and its intended construct, to give baseline conditions, and to caveat generalisation. The compute threshold is the test; "at the frontier" is what it is meant to reach. The review must weigh whether a person can "determine, before beginning to train", whether they are covered. *My reading:* this is def-reference's reference/referent gap and def-match's "fails through the world's motion", written into law with a scheduled re-resolution. It is concrete support for bringing the address-theory terms (reference, intended cardinality, match, resolution as-of) into the lexicon layer.

## UK government (atlas: uk-gov.md; read from the agent's report and one passage)
- **The Chronic Risks Analysis already contains a signed risk network** (4419–4549). Each chronic risk's assessment reviews its interactions. Links are "Reinforcing" ("exacerbates or increases the impacts of the other risk … or the negative impacts on the vulnerability") or "Diminishing", with direction arrows. The analysis reasons from the network's shape: most connected nodes; nodes "at the edge … may require more targeted mitigation"; nodes "affected by numerous other risks but with minimal direct impacts themselves may signify the final stage in a cascade". **The impact targets are vulnerabilities, including groups of people** ("Vulnerable persons"; the map of chronic risks onto "Groups of people"). *My reading:* this is a UK government source doing Joseph's causal-loop instinct and his "impact radius = harmed groups" together, in public, for chronic risk. It is a precedent, and a source of claims in the same form.
- **From the agent's report:** judgements travel between documents and mutate on the way (HMG 2023's "Generative AI will almost certainly…" becomes "Frontier AI…" in DSIT, citing HMG). Provisions are copied word for word from the DSIT Code into ETSI EN 304 223, and "voluntary" comes along. ## Safety science and press (atlas: safety-science-press.md; read from the agent's report)
- CSB and ICAO keep general causal theory, credited to named sources ("The Reason Model proposes…"), separate from case findings asserted in the investigator's own voice with graded confidence. The documents also declare the force of their own words (IAEA defines "shall" and "should").
- "Safeguard" means an engineering barrier (CSB), a legal protection against disclosure (ICAO) and a usage restriction (the press on Anthropic). The IAEA's "loss of control over a nuclear reactor core" is a cross-domain homonym.

## IASR family (atlas: iasr.md; its cross-edition and claim-form sections at 319–400, read whole)
- **The 2026 template makes claim types into section types.** Every risk section ends with Updates / Evidence gaps / Mitigations / Challenges for policymakers. The first three are a temporal delta against the prior edition, an evidence-state claim and a remedy.
- **Most sentences are about something other than the world:** the state of evidence ("remains limited", "too early to assess", "no established mechanism currently exists"); the distribution of expert opinion (the report asserts the *disagreement*, not either side; elicited probabilities attributed to populations of forecasters); other sources' classifications, re-tabulated without adoption; conditional extrapolations; illustrations the authors constructed that look like data.
- **Definitions drift across editions and inside 2026:** developer, control, alignment ("operators" dropped, "norms" added), safety (output-based → probabilistic), systemic risks (two definitions in 2026), and loss of control (two formulations in 2026). "Deployer" is used 19 times in 2026 and defined nowhere.
- **Loss-of-control vocabulary changed register:** from theory terms (control-undermining, deceptive alignment, scheming) to evaluation-observable terms (propensity, situational awareness, sandbagging), and from two factors to three (capability, propensity, deployment environment).
- **The industry tag on individual citations** is a role marker per citation, supplied by the source. The share of industry references is steady (~17–19%). The *kind* of industry evidence changed: developers' own tier designations now serve as evidence.
- **Ontological non-commitment clauses** attach to capability terms (deception, situational awareness, reasoning) but not to "goals" or "propensity". The 2025 edition itself flags goals as "not … directly empirically observable".
- **No calibrated likelihood scale;** the only calibrated statement is imported from NCSC. Confidence here is prose.

## Other companies' frameworks (atlas: co-others.md; read from the agent's report and Zhu's codebook)
- **Relata copies of four documents are silent re-uploads,** not first publications (Meta v1.1, xAI RMF, GDM v2.0, Microsoft v1, matched against Zhu App. K). A bibkey does not identify which text was read; version identity needs its own slot.

### Zhu 2026, Codebook v0.2 (737–828), read myself: a ready-made schema for the commitment layer
- **Inclusion (B.1):** the provider is the grammatical subject, plus a commitment verb or modal, or a practice "in the descriptive present". Conditionals are included. Excluded: risk-landscape statements, definitions, other parties' obligations, and past point-in-time evaluations.
- **Obligation-strength ladder (B.5):** must / will / commit to / present-tense practice > intend / aim / expect > may / could / consider > recommend / encourage.
- **Six materiality dimensions (B.5):** scope; threshold or trigger; actor (including adding or removing a third party); obligation strength; disclosure scope; consequence. "Broadening a suspension condition is a change to consequence." Enumerations introduced by "including" are scope-defining; those introduced by "e.g." are illustrative.
- **Change outcomes (B.4):** R retained, S strengthened, W weakened, X removed, L relocated (to a companion document), A added, and MIX when both directions are present.
- **Announcement status (B.7):** ANN, ANN-P (named without direction), SIL (the account exists but doesn't identify the change), NCL (no account). "Commentary by individuals, including employees in a personal capacity, is not a provider account."
- *My reading:* this is almost exactly the commitment layer Joseph wanted when he recast governance from documents to commitments ("what if they decide not to change the commitment in a document revision but instead via a public tweet?"). The six dimensions are the attributes of a commitment; tracing correspondence across versions is its identity over time. Adopt it, cite it, and mark the correlation: Zhu's first-pass coding was done by a Claude model (per the 09-27 handoff).

## EU and international (atlas: intl-eu.md; read from the agent's report)
- **One sentence propagating looks like agreement.** The G7 risk list becomes AI Act recital 110; the Code cites recital 110 for its taxonomy; Code Measure 9.2 becomes the incident template with one word changed; the Future Society's staffing sentence reappears in the Chairs' statement. Together with the UK finding (HMG → DSIT, with the subject changed) and the RAND lineage (the SL5 hedge dropped), this makes "derived from", with the changes marked, a relation the schema needs between assertions.

## Research literature A (atlas: lit-a.md; read from the agent's report and Barrett 371–418, 986–1004)
- **The same incidents are retold with different claims about the model's mind.** The database deletion, the TaskRabbit CAPTCHA, the Kiro/AWS outage and the matplotlib maintainer each recur. IST reads deception as "instrumentally valuable"; Hamin refuses to ask about intent. The agent's suggestion: keep incident records separate from characterisations of them, and build a small cross-document incident index.
- **Numbers change meaning between documents:** CLTR's 0–9 score is "credibility" in the method and "severity" in the findings. Stix's severity × persistence plot uses one estimate for both axes (its fn 13). Hypotheticals are priced and plotted as data (human extinction at $543.5T).
- **Explicit non-assertions** ("the authors do not take a formal position"; risks "deferred").
- **Barrett (STPA), read myself:** losses are "the real-world outcomes that we are trying to prevent"; hazards are "states inside the system that can lead to losses"; the control structure has controllers and controlled processes "at multiple levels", with control actions (actuators) and feedback (sensors). Unsafe control actions come in four kinds: not provided when needed; provided when unneeded; wrong time; wrong duration. Loss scenarios are the causal factors behind them. "Each AI might be operating exactly as it was trained and still lead to hazards." Control systems degrade into latent "vulnerabilities" before any failure; the audit and management-of-change signals, or their absence, are "the weak signals" (986–1004). *My reading:* STPA already is Joseph's chain with a mature vocabulary. Losses map to impact radius; hazards to risk events and states; the control structure to the role layer at multiple levels (regulator → organisation → human operator → AI system); unsafe control actions to control failures anywhere along the chain. It also carries his reframe that "loss of control" is shorthand for control failures spread along the chain, and his organisational-change thread (management of change as a control on control-structure degradation). Stix's "state of vulnerability + catalyst" (1299–1384) is the same shape.

## Emerging schema implications (my interpretation, provisional; revised after all ten atlases)
1. **Four kinds of record, not one graph.** (a) *Documents and passages*: versioned; the passage is the fidelity atom. (b) *Assertions*: each anchored to a passage, typed, attributed, with qualifier axes. (c) *Our vocabulary*: terms, roles, stages, scales, each with a lexical history. (d) *Recorded events*: incidents with IDs, actors, targets, configurations and outcomes. Causal edges between quantities are one assertion type among several, and the causal loop diagram and the bow-tie are views over assertions.
2. **Assertion types seen so far,** beyond the causal edge: definition (minted in a scope); classification (an attributed act; the 19 events sorted two ways); measurement (with its instrument, conditions, baseline and bound direction); evidence-state ("remains limited", "no evidence yet", with the search named); expert-opinion distribution; commitment (Zhu's six dimensions, strength ladder, versions); recommendation; counterfactual; conditional mechanism (antecedent structure); decomposition or argument (a safety case; R = Σ P·H·U; with its own uncertainty adjustment); scenario or illustration (declarative mood, not observation); interpretation of an instrument (legal readings, rebuttable presumptions); claims about claims (validity, scope, comparison, contradiction). Stance is its own axis: asserted, voiced-to-rebut, hypothetical, "treated as" (commitment-grade, not belief-grade), explicitly not asserted.
3. **Qualifiers are several axes:** likelihood (PHIA words and numbers, or prose); knowledge state; condition; regime or timeline scope; prompted vs spontaneous; controlled vs real-world; baseline; bound direction; force of the document. The source's hedge stays verbatim beside any coding of it.
4. **Assertions have lineage.** They are derived from other assertions (the G7 list → recital 110 → the Code; HMG → DSIT with the subject changed; Nevo → Aguirre → Gekker with the hedge lost), versioned within an author (OpenAI's four-step determination; framework revisions, silent or announced), and contested (within a source, or commentary against its primary). "Derived from, with the changes marked" is a relation the schema needs.
5. **Resolution of the sources' words, with address theory.** Terms are minted per scope (document, regime) and drift within and across editions. The same word collides across domains (IAEA's reactor "loss of control"; "safeguard"). Statutes build the reference/intended-referent gap in and schedule re-resolution (SB 53 §22757.14). Recording, per occurrence, what each word resolves to in our vocabulary, with ambiguity kept as an outcome, is what this corpus demands. Slattery's "Other" shows the cost of not keeping ambiguity distinct.
