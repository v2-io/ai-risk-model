## What you're taking over

Three pieces of work, built over two days with Joseph:
1. **`bin/source-search`.** It searches the corpus with verbs: `hybrid` (the default), `semantic`, `defs`, `outline`, `lexical`, `exact-phrase`, `exact-words` and `exact-bytes`. The **folded outline** (`outline`) is what Joseph called "the holy grail": a document's heading tree, with what matters opened as line ranges and anchors, and nothing folded silently. The index holds 466 texts, 61,559 passages and 4,030 definitions.
2. **A ranking model, decided but not built** (`search/RANKING.md`). Every ranking factor is written as a hypothesis, apart from its math. The combiner is to be a logistic regression on standardised features, fitted on whole-document judgments. That came from Joseph's warning that hybrid ranking "becomes spaghetti really quickly", and from three outside reviews (Fable, Gemini, Grok).
3. **Source experts** (`experts/`). These are Claude sessions that each read one Au5 source experientially and were then frozen, to be forked for any question about that source. All five Au5 sources have one, and all are prepared and stopped.

## How Joseph works, in what this session learned

- **When he floats an idea, discuss it before building it.** "slow down a touch-- don't feel obligated to immediately implement any of my ideas there-- I almost always need a little bit of pushback etc. first". Give the implications and your own opinion, with pushback. He'll often defer to your lean, and then you mark the decision as yours, supported by him, with its revisit triggers.
- **Attribute exactly.** Mark something as his only when he said it, and quote him. Keep agreed, supported and proposed apart. A side agent once caught me heading a list "Agreed with Joseph" when he had agreed to one item; he'd rather read "the agent took your silence as assent" than a stronger claim.
- **Math goes in `$…$`,** with displays as `$$…$$` on their own lines. Treat his sketched formalisms as impetus, and write the formal statement yourself, correctly.
- **He pushes main;** you commit locally. Experts may push their own branches.
- **Once he says an expert is ready, stop messaging it.** Every message changes the state a fork resumes from.

## Things that bit me, so they needn't bite you

- **The safeguards.** Experiential reading asks the reader to imagine implications, and on dangerous-capability passages that pushes towards what the safeguards stop. Two Opus experts were flagged and couldn't continue. A Sonnet fork was stopped later while writing about another expert's notes on a biological section. The brief now says the expertise is in the *document*, not the subject. Keep fork tasks on claims, evidence and status there too.
- **A Sonnet expert at its default effort was much thinner.** Joseph judged it low or medium. The scripts now pass `--effort xhigh`.
- **zsh doesn't split an unquoted variable into words.** I once passed 77 keys to `bin/canonicalize` as one key name. Use `xargs`, or `${=VAR}`.
- **`relata prep resume` and `relata prep -n` run the conversion worker in the foreground,** so anything chained after them waits for the whole queue. Joseph looks after relata himself.
- **I twice stated an inference as fact.** One was that experts in worktrees must be forked from their worktree; it's untrue, since resume finds a session from any directory. Test before writing a rule into the docs.
