# Source catalog

The main sources of this model. The core is the source list of the first compilation, `influx/model-alpha.md` §1 (its crosswalk sources and its organizational-factor sources), with model-alpha's columns, plus the press and commentary it cited for IPO pressure. Added to that core are sources read directly in later rounds that the source models (`influx/source-models/`) treat as main models of risk or as upstream of them:
- the company frameworks model-alpha saw only through METR's synthesis;
- UK AISI's and the UK government's risk models;
- NIST's AI RMF, America's AI Action Plan and New York's RAISE Act;
- the G7 code the EU text derives from;
- a few research papers the later compilation (`influx/model-beta/`) relies on.

A further set was added to relata on 2026-10-08 and has not yet been read in a verification round. Those documents enter the table where the repo shows others taking them up, or where they are authoritative in their own sphere (Set *relata-10-08*; `influx/catalog-update-proposal-2026-10-08.md` §4).

The table lists the model's main sources. The section after it, *The rest of the corpus*, lists every other document in relata that the repository cites, so that `relata emit bib` covers the whole corpus and `bin/canonicalize` knows what to do with each. Rows are grouped by **Influence**, newest first within each group.

**Columns** (as in model-alpha): *Date*; *Code* (the short name used in model-alpha; for added rows, the verification file's code where it has one); *Document*; *Kind*; *relata* (the local key, written with a leading @, as in `@california-2025-sb53`, so relata can read the catalog: `relata show <key>`, and `relata emit bib` writes `bib/refs.bib` from it; a key may carry a status tag, defined under *The rest of the corpus*); *Tag*:
- **P** primary, checked against the source text;
- **S** reputable secondary;
- **F** read through a web-fetch tool;
- **U** not verified against the source text;
- **C** commentary or opinion.

Added columns:
- ***Set***: where the row comes from.
  - **crosswalk**: model-alpha §1.1.
  - **organizational**: model-alpha §1.2 (and its footnotes, for the press rows).
  - **added**: not in model-alpha's list; read directly in a later round. The checking records are in `influx/verification/`:
    - `company-frameworks*.md` for the company frameworks and Zhu;
    - `uk-security-frameworks.md` for AISI's *Loss of Oversight* and incident report, the NRR and the CRA;
    - `us-gov-recent.md` for the Action Plan, RAISE, NIST AI 800-2 and CAISI's full DeepSeek report;
    - `remaining-literature.md` for the G7 code, Stix, Uuk, Kulveit, Davidson, Campos, the Singapore Consensus and the Digital Omnibus.

    NIST AI RMF 1.0 was searched whole for `absence-claim.md` and is mapped in `source-atlas/us-gov.md`. Buhl et al. (2025) is mapped in `source-atlas/co-others.md`.
  - **relata-10-08**: not in model-alpha's list, and not yet read in a verification round. Added to relata on 2026-10-08 (`influx/relata-aisi-sweep-2026-10-08.md`, `influx/relata-additions-2026-10-08.md`). The basis passages were checked against the source text in `influx/catalog-update-proposal-2026-10-08.md`; the rows are tagged U until a reading round checks the rest.
- ***Influence***: a declared grouping, proposed 2026-09-28 and open to revision. It is a statement about a document's reach in the field, not about its truth or its bearing on any hypothesis. Every *anchor* and *major* row gives its basis in the last column. File references there are relative to `influx/`; "OVERVIEW" is `source-models/OVERVIEW.md`.
  - **anchor**: the repo shows other documents in the set taking its wording, definitions, thresholds, lists or structure, by copying, adoption or declared basis. The basis names them. Copies are one lineage echoed, not independent agreement (OVERVIEW §3.1).
  - **major**: either authoritative in its own sphere (a law, official guidance, a government assessment, a developer's own governing framework), or a standing reference that the repo shows in use as one (a rating index, a widely cited taxonomy). The basis says which.
  - **supporting**: substantive, but narrower, or with a reach the repo does not show.
  - **context**: press, commentary and agency announcements. These are cited for dates, attribution, or how a primary was received, not as risk analyses.

## The rest of the corpus

Every other document in relata that this repository cites, by family. Listing a document here makes `relata emit bib` put it in the bibliography and tells `bin/canonicalize` what to do with it. It says nothing about the document's reach: the *Influence* column does that for the rows above.

**Status tags.** A key with no tag is canonicalized. A tag follows a key's closing backtick, on the same line, and applies to that key, here and in the table above:
- `[no-canon: <reason>]`: in the bibliography, not canonicalized.
- `[subsumed-by: <key>]`: a copy of the same text as the named key, so no separate copy is needed. In the bibliography, not canonicalized.
- `[superseded-by: <key>]`: a later version replaces it as the active document in its own lineage, and the named key is the next version. It is kept, canonicalized, and citable as history, but not as the active document. Following the tags from any version ends at the active one.
- `[canon-text: <path>]`: canonicalize this file, given relative to the repository, instead of relata's conversion of the PDF. It is used where relata's conversion failed or a better edition is held: IASR 2026 (the publisher's web edition) and the CSB report (converted by hand).

The named key is written in the same at-sign form, and is itself listed. A key may carry `no-canon` together with `superseded-by`, or `canon-text` together with `superseded-by`; `subsumed-by` stands alone. A key mentioned in prose is written bare, in backticks, so that it is not read as a listing. A key that moves into the table above comes out of this list, so that its status is stated once. The parsing contract is §2 of `influx/catalog-update-proposal-2026-10-08.md`.
