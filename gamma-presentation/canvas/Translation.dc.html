A translation row says what a source’s word can mean. A resolution says which meaning it had at one spot, and why we can or can’t tell. The standards only cover the first.

SSSOM says it outright: “Mappings themselves have no context (i.e. are always true)”. Inside one source, a word’s meaning moves, so the resolution record is ours to define.

One real record, in your notation

The thin pass’s one decisive ambiguity. OpenAI’s Frontier Governance Framework uses “severe harm” undefined; the same publisher’s Preparedness Framework defines it, about 20 to 200 times higher for casualties.

R-fgf-severe P-fgf-def "risks of severe harm" => { @{oai-fgf:severe-harm#own} | @{oai-pf:severe-harm} } lean #own, low-moderate
R-fgf-severe: the record’s id, in records/resolutions.yaml
P-fgf-def: the anchored passage where the word is used
{a | b}: one of these is meant, undetermined; never “either will do”
#own: a sense we split out of an unbound word, so it is attributed to us
lean: our preference and its confidence; the candidates stay

Which uses get a record

Recording every use is infeasible. So each scope (a document, section, code section or table) gets one default resolution of each key term, recorded once, and a separate record is made only where a use departs from that default, or where the use sits inside an assertion we record.

The heavily loaded terms are about a dozen: catastrophic, severe and systemic risk; loss of control; (mis)alignment; safeguard; hazard; developer, provider and deployer; threshold; incident; frontier.

Optional depth

Why outcome and reason are separate fields

It is the MIT “Other” lesson applied to our own records. The thin pass found the failure in its first draft: an imported binding we didn’t follow (“foreseeable”) read as zero referents, which mixes a fact about our reading into the outcome. Hence the separate status field.

Third parties resolve sources too

IASR resolves the EU’s “systemic risk”. Anthropic’s FCF files RSP thresholds under the statutory “loss of control”. A record has to say who did the resolving, because sometimes it isn’t us.

Under-specification is not ambiguity

From the spike: an absolute “aligned” (“our most aligned model to date”) is an argument place left unfilled, not a word with two senses. The spike proposes recording it as under-specification, with a use type: relational, anaphoric, absolute, activity, field. Those labels are the spike author’s, not established.

Decision 1What to call the per-source document

In Evans, a context map is the map across all contexts. The plan recommends keeping his sense for the across-sources map (the catalog plus lineage) and calling the per-source document a translation, the inside of an anticorruption layer. The cost of keeping “context map” for the per-source document is one recurring explanation to every DDD reader.

From my question A3. If source definitions become entries in their own namespaces (chapter 12), the per-source “translation” shrinks to those entries plus the resolution records for their uses. “Translation” stretches to cover that, loosely. Decide with Decision 1, so nothing is renamed twice.
