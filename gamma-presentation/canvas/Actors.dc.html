Two kinds of role, connected by conferral

Functional roles are defined by what a party does: NIST’s tasks, and your writer roles. Institutional roles are conferred by an instrument under conditions, Searle’s X counts as Y in C: the EU provider, the SB 53 frontier developer. The conditions are Hohfeld’s operative facts: SB 53’s compute threshold is operative; a company’s report of its compute is evidential.

The spike found the bases of division connect rather than compete. Instruments confer roles on operative facts of several kinds: training acts (SB 53), modification acts (the EU’s “significant change”), market acts, and use. So writing acts are already inside the institutional instruments.

So choosing a default decides which risks count, which is adjudication. Instead, the lexicon defines alignment as a relation with named slots (bearer, mode, claimant set, aspect, time, adjudicating standard, judge, domain, degree). Translations record the slots once per source definition. Referent-sensitivity is judged only for asserted claims where the alignment word carries the verdict, since most claims name their harm (“takeover”, “deceiving users”) and then the harm is what gets recorded. Those judgments use a declared, versioned set of admissible referents; a view may fix one referent as its own declared projection.
