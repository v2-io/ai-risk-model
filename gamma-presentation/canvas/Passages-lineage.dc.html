An anchor must match its passage exactly once, and a claim counts once per independent line of descent. In both, a copy is a record, never a vote.

Passages: the fidelity atom

The anchor: the PDF page (RFC 8118’s page=N), refined by a W3C quote selector (exact text, prefix, suffix), with the extraction line kept as an optional position and the document version as read. PDF pages survive re-extraction; line numbers don’t.

Where your theory corrects the W3C. The Web Annotation spec says several matches of a quote “SHOULD be treated as matching all”. A passage anchor intends exactly one match, {1,1}. Three matches means the anchor narrowed too little, and that should surface as a defect, not widen silently. So the match count is recorded when anchoring and on every re-check.

Tables are the gap. No standard addresses a PDF table’s row and column, and most framework thresholds live in tables that pdftotext -layout interleaves. That selector is ours to define.

Lineage: two rules for any corroboration view

1. Never inherit transitivity
AexactMatch BexactMatch C ⟹ A agrees with C

skos:exactMatch is transitive, and so is the micropublications model’s supports. A view built on either propagates agreement down chains, which is precisely the inference lineage-awareness exists to block.

2. Keep two axes
source reliability
how far to trust who said it
information credibility
how far to trust what was said

The intelligence “Admiralty” grading separates them, and defines its top credibility grade as “confirmed by independent sources”. If we grade sources at all, the grade is our attributed assertion, on two separate axes. (The Admiralty manual itself wasn’t reached; this rests on the research report.)

Claim identity is an assertion. The micropublications model: “identification of statements as being similogs is itself an assertion. Your ‘similarity’ may not be my ‘similarity’.” When we say two passages make the same claim, that judgment is a record with an author, not a merge.

Relations are mostly established: PROV’s derivation, revision, quotation, invalidation; PAV; CiTO’s cites-as-authority, includes-quotation-from, updates, retracts, shares-author-with; IFLA LRM’s work, expression, manifestation; Akoma Ntoso’s amends, repeals, and force against efficacy. Still ours: the direction of drift (a hedge dropped, a claim hardened), a summary that adds content, boilerplate, “tasks, fulfils”, “orders revision of”, announcement status, preemption across jurisdictions.

What the thin pass learned by getting it wrong (change 4)

The pass was checking lineage and still counted one template twice. Chapter 11 draws the graph.

Edges carry a claim

A document-level revision edge from xAI’s RMF to its 2025 framework would make the RMF an ancestor of the >50 bar, which it isn’t. The bar came from SB 53.

Templates get nodes

Anthropic’s FCF and OpenAI’s FGF share wording that is in neither SB 53 nor the EU Code. Their shared drift is plausibly one event, counted twice. Compare descendants with each other, not only with the root.

Drift needs frames

RAISE has SB 53’s exact words in its bearer slot and a different test, because RAISE defines “person” as nongovernmental. Comparing words finds no drift; comparing resolved tests does.

Moves are reported as moves

The companies move SB 53’s conduct list into a category table. Drift should report a relocated clause as moved, not as dropped and added.
