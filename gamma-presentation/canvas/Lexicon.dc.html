No term or field of ours may mix a thing in the world with a fact about our reading. Namespaces keep our own words from colliding with each other.

The first rule comes from the largest shared-vocabulary effort in the corpus, and what went wrong in it.

The MIT “Other” test

MIT’s AI Risk Repository codes 1,725 risks from 74 documents in a causal taxonomy. Its Entity value “Other”, checked at the source (Table 1), covers 21% of coded risks and merges three kinds of fact:

MIT causal taxonomy, Entity “Other” “The risk arises from human-AI interaction rather than either agent alone, or the causing entity is ambiguous or unspecified”
⟶
a thing in the world interaction a real causal category: human and AI together
a fact about our reading ambiguous a resolution outcome: the coder couldn’t tell which
a fact about the source unspecified the source was silent

Timing “Other” does the same with “across both pre- and post-deployment phases” and “presented without a clearly specified time”. This becomes acceptance test 3, and in chapter 5 it reappears inside our own resolution record, where an outcome and its reason have to be kept apart for the same reason.

What an entry is

An udon term group in def/, in your address-theory house style: a narrative opening, one-line definitions, relations with cardinalities, invariants, discussion, :synonyms and :avoid. Terminology science adds three things:

Intensional definitionsthe broader concept plus the delimiting characteristics that separate this one from its neighbours. Read |invariants as those, and “not to be confused with” becomes mechanical.
Acceptability statuspreferred, admitted, deprecated; :synonyms and :avoid already approximate the last two
Marked adoption[SOURCE: …, modified — what changed], so a borrowed definition says where it came from

Status per term uses your DECISIONS.ud ladder: steward, ratified, council, supported, defacto, proposed, transition. Everything an agent drafts starts at proposed.

Umbrellas and declared ambiguity. An umbrella is declared as containing named precise terms. A word used in several incompatible senses gets a declared ambiguity with its candidates named, never a silent pick.

Decision CBound vs match is two axesthin pass, proposed change 5

The plan classes a source term as either bound (the source defines it) or a match (used undefined). In your theory those are two axes: who maintains the sense (the source, an outside institution, or nobody) and how the sense reaches instances (almost always by match, since a statutory definition is a test run as of a moment). So SB 53’s “frontier developer” is a binding to a match. The plan’s §22757.14 example (“its test no longer reaches what it was meant to”) takes the dangle reading; the other reading is a match drifting with the world. The thin pass asks you to judge this: “You’ll judge this better than I can.”
