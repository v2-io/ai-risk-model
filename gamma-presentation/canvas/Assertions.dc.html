Draft 2 rebuilt most of speech-act, argument and norm theory under its own names. Gamma adopts the names, and with them one firm rule: record attacks, never defeats.

Four questions about any claim a source makes: what act it is, whose voice it is in, what it argues against, and, if it is a norm, which layer of a norm we are recording.

The act: point and strength are separate axes

Searle’s five illocutionary points (1975, read whole by the research agent). “hypothesizing that p and flatly stating that p are in the same line of business”: so “speculates” is not a different stance from “asserts”, it is the same act, weaker.

Definitions are declarations, of two kinds. An institutional one creates a status: a statute defining “frontier developer” changes every obligation that uses the term. A linguistic one fixes a word within a text and needs no institution (“I define, abbreviate, name, call, or dub”).

Determinations are assertive declarations. A Commission designation or a company’s capability-threshold determination is truth-apt and status-creating. The record can hold it as effective alongside another source’s claim that it is false, without contradiction.

Mode and indirect force. “To testify is to assert in one’s capacity as a witness” is the slot for who, in what capacity. Indirect force names draft 2’s “grammatical mood is not a safe guide”: NCSC writes norms in the indicative.

Illustrative. FactBank (Saurí and Pustejovsky 2008) records factuality as ⟨modality, polarity⟩ relative to a source, and the author is always the outermost: “Izvestiya according to the author”, not Izvestiya.

The proposal: draft 2’s stance decomposes into Searle’s point and strength, FactBank’s factuality per nested source, and argument relations. Draft 2’s first stance question had mixed evidentiality (how something is known, grammatical, and absent from English) with epistemic modality (how sure).

Other frames for voice

Penn Discourse Treebank separates claims (“X says”), beliefs (“X thinks”), presupposed facts (“X found that”) and an arbitrary source (“reports suggest”).

Goffman’s animator, author and principal is draft 2’s authorship-roles table. The corpus splits the principal further: IASR’s Chair holds “ultimate responsibility” for a report that “does not necessarily represent the views of the Chair”. Goffman was read via a secondary that quotes him.

Arguments: three kinds of attack, and none of them is a verdict

ASPIC+ (Modgil and Prakken 2013, read at the primary). Draft 2’s premise-attached “defeats” was undermining all along.

Attack is not defeat. An attack is a fact about what was argued. Defeat needs a preference ordering among arguments, and choosing one is adjudication. So attacks are recorded, typed; a contest’s standing (your logos-atom values: refuted, burden-shifted, standoff, open, conceded) is only ever some party’s attributed assertion, or a view computed under a declared ordering.

The architecture already exists. Inference Anchoring Theory and the Argument Interchange Format anchor an argument graph in the speech acts that carry it, which is draft 2’s two planes.

Norms: five layers, each with an owner

Draft 2 defined a commitment as “a norm where imposer = bearer”. Searle tried that assimilation and it failed: a promise commits the speaker, a request tries to move the hearer. The layering keeps them apart.

1The actdirective, commissive or declaration, with strength (Searle)
2The statement’s grammarInstitutional Grammar 2.0, already applied to legislation. Regulative: attribute, deontic, aim, object, context (activation condition, execution constraint), or else. Constitutive: the entity constituted, the function (including “the conferral of status”), its constituting properties.
3The positions it createsHohfeld: claim and duty, privilege and no-right; power and liability, immunity and disability. “Leadership can … make decisions without the SAG” is a power plus an immunity, which no obligation-strength ladder can say. And every duty asks who holds the correlative claim: for a voluntary framework the honest answer is often “no one with a legal claim”, the materiality question the sources keep asking.
4Its legal status over timeLegalRuleML: applicable, in force, efficacy, valid, with status-changing events; strong against weak permission. Most framework deployment conditions are strong permissions, derogations from a prohibition the same document sets.
5What we recorda norm-proposition (von Wright): a norm is neither true nor false; a report that a norm exists is. Compilation-not-adjudication, in deontic logic’s own terms.
The plan’s worked example

“To avoid catastrophic harm, developers of open-weight models should not release models without evaluating risks”

IASR 2026, line 2077 of the local text. Read as von Wright’s technical norm (if you want this end, you ought to do X), it presupposes an anankastic statement, that evaluation is necessary for the end, which is truth-apt and fits a descriptive report. Draft 2’s conflict becomes a typed ambiguity: technical norm or prescription.

The example narrows to a typed reading of one sentence, and it then supports the technical-norm reading.
