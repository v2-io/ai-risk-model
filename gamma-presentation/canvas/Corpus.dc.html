The sources model different things, copy each other, and are sometimes more precise than we are. A translation has to carry all three.

If the lexicon can’t say each of these separately, a translation of SB 53 loses what the statute says. That is why the lexicon has to be finer than any one source, and why SB 53 is acceptance test 1’s first case (chapter 13).

Findings 2, 4, 5 and 6

2. Much apparent agreement is copying

The EU Code’s loss-of-control formula (“reliably direct, modify, or shut down”) recurs in nine later documents. SB 53’s “>50 people or $1B” bar recurs in four, under two names. Shanghai AI Lab says its glossary is “primarily based on” IASR 2025’s. Agreement counts only as far as lineage allows.

4. Sources declare their own rules for reading their terms

The EU Code: “the AI Act definition applies, and such definition shall prevail”; “all grammatical variations of the terms defined in this Glossary shall be deemed to be covered”. SB 53 §22757.14 orders an annual review of whether “frontier model” still reaches “foundation models at the frontier”, weighing whether coverage can be determined before training and is “verifiable by parties other than the frontier developer”. These are resolution policies in your sense, written by the sources.

5. Terms are scoped finer than documents

SB 53 defines “catastrophic risk” twice: frontier models in the Business and Professions Code, foundation models in the Labor Code. IASR 2026’s glossary and body define loss of control differently (“no clear path to regaining control” against “extremely costly or impossible”).

6. Some sources are inconsistent with themselves, and some say so

Anthropic’s August 2026 Risk Report: “We acknowledge that the distinction between known and unknown is not crisply defined.” MIT’s causal taxonomy uses one “Other” value for three different things (chapter 4). IASR 2026 is inconsistent with itself on several key terms.

The nine recurrences are three relations

From the citation check’s grep over 214 extractions:

quoting it as the Code’s: Barrett, METR, Stix;
adopting it verbatim: xAI 2026;
embedding it: OpenAI’s FGF;
paraphrasing it: Microsoft 2026, Meta 2026 (which adds “contain”), Amazon 2026, GDM’s September 2025 blog.

That the paraphrases derive from the Code is inferred from shared wording; none says so. A tenth paraphrases GDM, a second-generation copy. Chapter 7 has the rules for counting these.

The four copies of the >50 bar are not identical

xAI 2025 quotes it with attribution and RAISE copies it. Anthropic’s FCF v2 and OpenAI’s FGF relabel it “systemic risk”, narrow it to “>50 fatalities” and make it an example (“including but not limited to”). For 60 deaths they agree; an injury-heavy event splits them. Chapter 11 follows this.

Reviewer is not maintainer

Under §22757.14 the Department of Technology only recommends; the Legislature changes the definition. In address theory’s terms the party that checks for a dangle and the maintainer of the binding are different. The citation check noticed this; the plan doesn’t use it yet.
