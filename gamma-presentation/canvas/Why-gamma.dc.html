Kept from beta

The claims ledger; the lexicon’s pooling checks; your address-theory idea that a source term resolves to ours with a recorded outcome, which can be “ambiguous”; draft 2’s two planes (what was said; what it is said about) joined by resolution.

The principles you have set since CLAUDE.md

These are yours, quoted from 6 October. Every later chapter leans on them, so they are worth one careful read.

Settled, 6 OctThe lexicon’s shape

“the union of all the most precise, scoped, bounded, and/or specific definitions (with some umbrella term containment as appropriate etc.)”

So no source’s precision is lost in translation, and broader terms exist only as declared umbrellas that contain the precise ones. Chapter 12 shows this sentence is now carrying a decision it didn’t know it was making.

Settled, 6 OctPriority

“One of the high priorities for me are our agreed-upon definitions for those actors (and agent parts) listed in the alignment-model/ directory.”

Settled, 6 OctRefinement is where the value is

“the value really only settles in during the last 5-10% of the refinement. First pass and lazily generated stuff, even from ample context, will be more likely to seed confusion and misunderstanding than truthful analysis and understanding.”

The plan reads this as a cost to budget, not a cap on entries: every agent-drafted entry stays a candidate until it has had repeated line-by-line passes, and nothing downstream leans on it before then. Your address-theory entries are the bar.

Standing ruleEstablished vocabulary over coinage, both ways

“a coinage where there is established academic vocabulary to adopt is a smell”

The plan adds the converse: don’t borrow an established word in a different sense. Chapters 3 and 4 list the collisions it found.

Optional depth

Beta, in domain-driven design’s terms

Adopting IASR 2026’s definitions wholesale (“The first four definitions are IASR 2026’s, adopted”, def-risk.ud) is Evans’s conformist pattern: our lexicon took one upstream model as its own. Translation is what is meant to prevent that.

Building the risk analysis and the lexicon it depended on at the same time is how beta began to read as a big ball of mud.

One more principle, the plan’s own

Neutrality lives in the assertions, not the vocabulary. The lexicon is our model (Evans: “a change in the language is a change to the model”). It is judged by whether it can express every source’s distinctions without loss, not by looking neutral. Compilation-not-adjudication governs what we say the sources claim.

Worth holding on to: it is the reason chapter 12’s question about where source definitions live matters.
