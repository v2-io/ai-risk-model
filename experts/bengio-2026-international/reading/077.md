# 077 · L908–914 · p.38 · OECD Scenario 2: Progress slows

**Predicted:** modest gains, day-long agent tasks. **Got:** "useful assistants". Deep knowledge, structured reasoning, computer and web use, "**limited interaction with people or services on behalf of the user**", memory, coherence and error correction for longer tasks; no robust learning of new skills; physical tasks only in controlled settings (factories, labs). No horizon numbers.
- **The pathway lists the capability frontiers in a useful order:** "continual learning, metacognition and agency, problem-solving, creativity, physical tasks, and social interaction". That's the OECD's capability taxonomy (I believe the OECD has an "AI capability indicators" framework with dimensions like these), so the scenarios are written in the OECD's capability vocabulary. A lineage note: the 2030 section's dimensions are the OECD's, not IASR's own.
- "reasoning systems fail to generalise as well as hoped": the generalisation question (065/069) decides between scenarios 2 and 3.
- **Analogue:** antibiotic discovery, the golden era 1940s–60s, then exhaustion of the "low-hanging fruit".

Wandering: the scenarios seem graded by the same capability dimensions. A risk-relevant reading: under Scenario 2, AI already acts "on behalf of the user" with services, i.e. agents with real-world authority, *even in the slow scenario*. So the agent-related risks of chapter 2 (reliability failures, delegated actions) are robust across scenarios; LoC-type risks presumably need scenarios 3–4. I'd expect the report to map risks onto scenarios somewhere. If it doesn't, that's an inference a fork can make from the scenario texts.
