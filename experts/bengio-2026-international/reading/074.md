# 074 · L888–892 · p.37 · "Commercial deployment often lags behind capability improvements"

**Predicted:** data and energy, or scenarios. **Got:** a deployment bottleneck, the "AI as normal technology" thesis (Kapoor and Narayanan's capability/diffusion distinction) in all but name, given its own subsection.
- Coding assistants adopted within 1–4 years; healthcare needing "**three to five additional years**" after research-grade accuracy; autonomous vehicles "will still be limited in 2030" (an expert forecast); SMEs ("employ **60%** of workers globally") face expertise, infrastructure and cost barriers; geopolitics: "**export controls on advanced semiconductors** and divergent regulatory frameworks". Export controls appear for the first time, as a *barrier to diffusion*, not as a policy instrument. Consistent with the no-recommendations posture.
- **The disagreement, with its policy implication:** "Policies designed for rapidly deployed AI capabilities may be premature, while those assuming slow adoption may be insufficient." The evidence dilemma restated on the diffusion axis, not the capability axis. Two independent uncertainties: how fast capabilities grow, and how fast they diffuse.

Wandering: for the risk side this matters a lot. Many systemic risks (labour, autonomy) scale with *diffusion*, while malicious use scales with *access to capability*, which open weights and APIs make fast regardless of sector adoption. So the deployment lag slows systemic risk but not misuse risk. The report doesn't make that distinction here; the project's causal model could, and IASR supplies its premises.
