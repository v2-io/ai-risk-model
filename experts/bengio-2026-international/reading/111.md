# 111 · L1176–1178 · p.55 · §2.1.2 Evidence gaps

- "limited understanding of _how_ AI manipulation works" (mechanism; italics in the source).
- **"whether AI systems are equally capable of inducing true and false beliefs"**. A sharp question: is persuasion truth-asymmetric? (Costello's conspiracy work suggests truthful counter-evidence persuades; does falsehood persuade as well?) A good should-ask.
- Realistic-condition studies, including **"AI systems that distribute content, such as social media platforms"**. Recommender systems are called "AI systems" here. That broadens "AI" beyond GPAI to ranking algorithms, the distribution layer.
- **"evaluating manipulation in realistic settings can be challenging due to ethical concerns"**: an evidence gap with an *ethical* cause. You can't ethically manipulate people at scale to measure it. A third kind of gap beyond 049's epistemic/access split: **ethically constrained evidence**.
- "as AI systems are trained to adapt to people's psychology": adaptive systems.

**Next, I predict:** Mitigations (disclosure, detection of manipulation, training against sycophancy, limits on companion design) with mixed evidence; then Challenges.
