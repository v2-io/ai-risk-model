# 072 · L868–874 · p.36 · "Economic returns from additional compute may diminish"

**Predicted:** data, energy, chips, capital. **Got:** capital first, framed as an *economic* bottleneck, not a physical one. That ordering is interesting: after arguing physical inputs are feasible to 2030 (068), the report locates the most immediate brake in *revenue*.
- Costs: "Current frontier AI training runs already cost approximately **$500 million** in computational resources alone, with next-generation models projected to require **$1–10 billion**." (§1.1's "hundreds of millions", made precise.)
- "consumer trust in AI systems is still low on average, and many enterprises are struggling to adopt": demand-side weakness. Investments are "a bet on uncertain returns". Figure 1.8 (Epoch revenue estimates) is an image I can't view.
- **The mechanism:** if revenue fails → investment cuts → the 5×/yr compute growth slows → progress depends on algorithms. A causal chain from economics to capability, the "plateau" scenario's economic route. It corresponds to the slow branch of 025.

Wandering: the report's own risk framing gives "trust" two roles. Low trust slows adoption, hence investment, hence capability (here). And in the ES, risks "can erode trust and impede adoption" (024). So risk management, by building trust, *speeds* capability growth. The report doesn't say this, but its causal claims imply that safety measures that build trust are capability accelerants. It's the kind of feedback loop the project's causal graph should hold, and IASR supplies both edges.

**Next, I predict:** data scarcity, energy and chips, maybe geopolitics (export controls).
