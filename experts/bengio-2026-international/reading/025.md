# 025 · L446–452 · p.11 · ES 1: "Capabilities are improving rapidly but unevenly"

**Predicted:** IMO, coding, agents, jaggedness, 2030 scenarios. **Got:** less concrete than I predicted. No IMO, no METR here. Three bolded claims:
1. Gains from **"'inference-time scaling'**: allowing models to use more computing power in order to generate intermediate steps before giving a final answer". This is a definition in passing, and a term of art (quoted). Note how it's defined: by mechanism ("generate intermediate steps"), so reasoning models are equated with inference-time scaling. "Post-training" (023) and "inference-time scaling" are related but distinct; here they're elided ("new techniques that enhance performance after initial training" → inference-time scaling). Inference-time compute isn't strictly post-*training*; it's at inference. A small conceptual slip, or deliberate grouping as "after initial training". Lexicon: separate `post-training`, `inference-time scaling`, and `reasoning model` if the report later defines it.
2. **"Jagged"**, defined: "leading systems may excel at some difficult tasks while failing at other, simpler ones". The failing examples are counting objects in images, spatial reasoning, "**recovering from basic errors in longer workflows**". The last bears directly on agents. Reliability over long horizons is the bottleneck, which ties to the 2.2.1 "reliability challenges".
3. **2030**: "current trends are **consistent with** continued improvement". A careful phrasing: consistency, not prediction. Three scenarios named: **slow/plateau** (data or energy bottlenecks), **continue at current rates**, **accelerate dramatically** ("if AI systems begin to speed up AI research itself"). That's recursive improvement or AI R&D automation, named as the accelerant. Evidence offered: developers are "**betting**" ("hundreds of billions of dollars in data centre investments"). Revealed preference as evidence about expectations, not capabilities.

Jaggedness is presented as the caveat, not a refutation of capability. That frames the sceptical "AI as normal technology" view (Kapoor and Narayanan are in the room) as compatible with fast progress. They aren't contradictory: jagged and fast.
