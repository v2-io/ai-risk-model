# 002 · L28–36 · the landing blurb

**Predicted:** a title and an about-paragraph (second report, mandate, discretion, not the governments' views).
**Differed:** the title and a promotional blurb, with no disclaimer yet. "Comprehensive review of latest scientific research on the capabilities and risks of general-purpose AI systems"; "over 100 AI experts"; "backed by over 30 countries and international organisations"; "the largest global collaboration on AI safety to date".

- **The scope is set by its object, "general-purpose AI systems".** It continues 2025's framing, as I expected. Note that the object is "capabilities and risks", not mitigations. I'd bet risk management still gets a part of its own, as in 2025, but the blurb doesn't promise one.
- **"Backed by" is doing a lot of work.** Backing isn't endorsement of content. The 2025 report made a point of the writers' independence. If this text later says the countries don't endorse it, "backed" will turn out to mean something like "sponsored the process". Worth watching, because downstream documents cite IASR as if it were an intergovernmental consensus. Question for the file: *does IASR represent government consensus?*
- **The translations, into the other five UN languages, and the separate "Extended Summary for Policymakers"** are part of my lineage. The Extended Summary is a distinct document, so the summary in *this* text may be shorter.
- **"Largest global collaboration on AI safety to date"** is a self-description and a superlative. It's harmless, but it tells me the web page is partly advocacy for the report itself.

**Next, I predict:** either the start of the report proper (a foreword by Bengio, or "About this report" with the panel and the writing team) or more web chrome, such as a table of contents.
