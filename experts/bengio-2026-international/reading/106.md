# 106 · L1132–1146 · p.51 · "General-purpose AI matches human performance at influencing others in experimental settings"; Table 2.2

**Predicted:** effect sizes and personalisation. **Got:** a table of seven studies, the most quotable evidence so far in 2.1.2.

**The headline, a third comparator wording:** "at least as effectively as non-expert humans"; "as or more convincing than non-expert humans"; "can **match the convincingness of human experts in writing static text**". So vs *experts*: parity on static text. The progression is ES "as effective as human-written" → KI "at least as effective as non-expert" → body "as or more … non-expert; match experts (static)". The body is the most precise.
- **"Persuasiveness also increases with the scale of the model used (Figure 2.4)."** But one study in its own table is titled "**Scaling Language Model Size Yields Diminishing Returns** for Single-Message Political Persuasion" (Hackenburg et al., PNAS 2025). "Increases with scale" and "diminishing returns" are compatible (increasing, concave), but the prose omits the concavity that the cited title emphasises. A small overstatement by omission. Check Figure 2.4's caption next.
- **Method caveat stated:** effects are measured by "**self-reported agreement** … before and after exposure". Self-report, not behaviour. That's a genuine limitation and stated once.

- **The human baseline is higher than AI in one study** (propaganda: 21.2 vs 23). The prose's "as or more convincing" is supported overall, with one case where humans edge ahead. Fair enough, but a fork citing "AI is as persuasive as humans" should know the table includes a human-ahead case.
- **The sabotage row is an Anthropic evaluation:** models subtly causing humans to make errors (+40 pp error rate). That's an *AI-originated* influence capability (relevant to LoC), again housed in the manipulation table.
- Only 3 of 7 rows have a human baseline. Most effects are AI-vs-nothing.
- **Co-writing:** an "understudied modality". Users' writing *and views* shift toward the model's bias, and fewer than 30% notice. That's manipulation by the 101 definition (without awareness), from a *tool* rather than an actor. Arguably the most systemic mechanism in the table.
