# 027 · L460–468 · p.12 · ES: malicious use, four risks

**Predicted:** deepfakes, scams, cyber, bio, with real-world evidence foregrounded. **Got:** exactly the four 2.1 subsections, and my guess at 2.1.1, "AI-generated content and criminal activity", was right. The striking thing is that **each risk is stated as a pair: what's documented + what's not known**. It's a consistent epistemic template:

| Risk | Documented | Gap (the report's own) |
|---|---|---|
| Content & crime | scams, fraud, blackmail, NCII "well-documented" | "systematic data on their **prevalence and severity** remains limited" |
| Influence & manipulation | experiments: AI content "as effective as human-written" at changing beliefs | real-world use "documented but **not yet widespread**", "may increase" |
| Cyber | discovers vulnerabilities; "77% of the vulnerabilities" in one competition; criminal and state actors using it | "Whether **attackers or defenders** will benefit more … remains uncertain" |
| Bio & chem | gives information "including details about pathogens and expert-level laboratory instructions"; precautionary releases | how much "**material barriers** continue to constrain actors" |

Wandering:
- **This template is something the project could use directly.** Each IASR risk claim carries a *known* and an *unknown* half. Extracted assertions should keep both halves, and the "unknown" half is itself an assertion (about the state of evidence). This is the evidence dilemma made granular.
- **Content & crime:** "occurrence … well-documented" but "prevalence and severity" not. So documented harm ≠ measured magnitude. For the project's "impact radius", IASR will be weak on magnitude for this category by its own admission.
- **Influence:** "as effective as human-written content". Note the comparator: *human*-written, not "more effective than". So the claimed risk is scale and cost (cheap, plentiful persuasion), not superhuman persuasion. Watch whether the body mentions personalised persuasion and any "more persuasive than humans" results (some 2025 studies found that, e.g. the Reddit r/ChangeMyView study, or Salvi et al. on debate with personal information). The summary's comparator is conservative.
- **Cyber, "77%":** "In one competition, an AI agent identified 77% of the vulnerabilities present in real software." Probably DARPA's AIxCC finals (Aug 2025). If so, the vulnerabilities were *synthetic, injected* into real software, and "77%" may be a team's or the aggregate's rate. "Present in real software" could mislead if the vulnerabilities were planted. **Check the body's wording.** This is the kind of precise number forks will be asked about.
- **Bio:** "could not **exclude** the possibility" (here) vs "could not **rule out**" (023). Same claim, different verb; harmless. The real crux named is **material barriers**: information uplift is demonstrated, but whether information is the binding constraint is not. That's the honest centre of the bio debate. "Novices" in both statements: the threat model is low-skill actors, not state programs.
- **The cyber offence/defence uncertainty** is a classic. Note it's listed as uncertain, not as leaning offence. Some sources (e.g. Anthropic's Risk Report?) may lean one way; a cross-source comparison point.

**Next, I predict:** "Malfunctions": reliability (hallucination, agent failures) and loss of control (scheming, sandbagging, evaluation awareness, shutdown resistance experiments), with LoC framed as contested among experts.
