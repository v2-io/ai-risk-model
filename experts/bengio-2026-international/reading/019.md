# 019 · L340–360 · p.6 · Bengio's foreword, "A new scientific assessment of a fast-moving technology"

**Predicted:** pace, growing evidence, the value of a shared scientific basis. **Got:** pace and shared understanding, yes. But the foreword's real content is **two declared changes**, and one of them answers my question from 005.

1. **Scenarios and forecasts.** "to help policymakers better understand the range of potential outcomes despite the uncertainty involved, we have drawn upon new research conducted by the **OECD** and **Forecasting Research Institute** to present more specific scenarios and forecasts." So "1.3 Capabilities by 2030" is built on commissioned outside work: OECD scenarios and FRI forecasts (FRI runs expert and superforecaster elicitation). **Lineage note:** the 2030 claims are the OECD's and FRI's, reported by IASR. A fork asked "what does IASR say about 2030?" must keep that attribution.
2. **Narrowed scope: "emerging risks".** "following extensive consultation, we have narrowed the scope to focus on **'emerging risks': risks that arise at the frontier of AI capabilities**." Hence the two-item systemic section. Environment, privacy, copyright and the R&D divide, which are mostly *established* risks of present AI broadly, fall outside. The stated reason has two parts: rigour is most valuable under high uncertainty, and complementarity, "to complement other efforts, including the **United Nations' Independent International Scientific Panel on AI**." That's a division of labour between scientific bodies, the IPCC analogy coming true. **Key definitional fact: "emerging risks" is a defined scope term,** in single quotes, glossed as "risks that arise at the frontier of AI capabilities". Check the Glossary and "About this Report" for a fuller definition.

Wandering:
- **"Emerging" in this definition is about the frontier, not about time.** A risk is "emerging" because it arises *at the frontier of capabilities*, not because it's new or growing. But the word "emerging" connotes newness. A risk can be old and still frontier-located (bio uplift has been discussed since 2023), or new and not frontier-located (deepfake nudification apps run on small open models). Where does "AI-generated content and criminal activity" (presumably 2.1.1) sit? Deepfakes are mostly not frontier-driven, so including them stretches "frontier". Watch how they justify it.
- **The narrowing is a consequential choice for this project's map.** Someone using IASR 2026 as "the" scientific consensus on AI risk will find nothing on environment or bias, not because the evidence says those risks are small but because of scope. Absence in IASR 2026 is a scope fact, not a finding. That belongs in the expert's should-ask questions: *"Does IASR 2026 say anything about X (bias, environment, privacy, copyright)?"* The answer begins with scope.
- **"This remains the most rigorous assessment of AI capabilities, risks, and risk management available."** A superlative in the Chair's voice. It's advocacy for the institution, unlike the cautious register I expect in the body. And "risk management" is listed among the things assessed, so the object is capabilities, risks *and* risk management, unlike the blurb's "capabilities and risks".
- **The US again:** "experts nominated by over 30 countries and intergovernmental organisations". Not mentioned. The foreword is silent on the US.
- **The tone is restrained hope:** "working with the many experts that produced this Report has left me hopeful", "we are making progress towards understanding". Compare Bengio's public alarm. Here he's the chair of a consensus document, and the foreword reads like it.

**Next, I predict:** a second foreword, perhaps from the Indian summit host (the Minister of Electronics and IT) or the UK Secretary of State. It will be about inclusivity, the Global South and "AI for all", given the India summit framing ("impact").
