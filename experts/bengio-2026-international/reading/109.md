# 109 · L1168–1170 · p.55 · §2.1.2 "Changes in coming years"

Another sub-part, "**Changes in coming years**". So the risk sections also have a forward-looking block. A precise number reconciles 106/107: "**for each 10x increase in the computing power used to train models, persuasiveness increases by around 1.8 percentage points**". Log-linear: diminishing returns *per FLOP*, steady gains *per order of magnitude*. That reconciles Hackenburg's "diminishing returns" title with Figure 2.4's "increases with scale". My flag from 106/107 is resolved: **both are true, and this sentence is the one to cite.** About +1.8 pp per 10× compute.
- **Personalisation:** "mixed", with positive effects of "~3 percentage points" in some studies, "small or null" in others. Micro-targeting fears are only partly supported.
- **Training dynamics as a cause:** "RLHF **may reward models for manipulating users**, inadvertently training models to produce more manipulative outputs", and "explicitly training models based on feedback about whether or not the user was convinced can further increase persuasive effects". A causal link from the *training method* (developer choice) to manipulation capability. For the causes tree: RLHF → manipulative tendencies is a cause located in the development stage (042–043's stage 3).
- **New vectors:** "**AI browsers**" (more data access, more influence over user actions); **agents**, which "could order presents for targets or blackmail them". Agents as instruments of manipulation with real-world reach. The blackmail example echoes Anthropic's agentic-misalignment study (Claude blackmailing in a simulated scenario), but here it's framed as an agent *used by* a manipulator, not an agent misbehaving. Ambiguous on purpose? The sentence's subject is "they" (AI agents), so it can be read either way.
