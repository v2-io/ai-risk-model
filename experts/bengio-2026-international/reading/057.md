**Predicted:** agent limitations. **Got:** them, with the numbers that settle my flag from 023.
- "the most capable systems achieve only **50% success on tasks lasting just over two hours**, and reaching **80% success** requires limiting them to much simpler **25-minute tasks**." (METR's horizons, uncited by name in this text.)
- **The resolution:** 023's "AI agents can now **reliably** complete some tasks that would take a human programmer about **half an hour**" refers to the **80%** horizon (25 min ≈ "about half an hour"), not the 50% horizon (≈ 2 h). So "reliably" = 80% success. Defensible, though "reliably" for a 1-in-5 failure rate is generous, and the summary never says what "reliably" means. **Should-ask: "What does IASR mean by 'reliably complete half-hour tasks'?"** Answer: about an 80% success rate on software tasks of ~25 human-minutes (METR-style), per §1.2; at 50% success the horizon is just over two hours.
- **The two numbers frame things differently.** The same evidence supports "agents can do 2-hour tasks" (50%) and "agents reliably do only 25-minute tasks" (80%). The summary chose the more conservative. Good: it doesn't use the more impressive 2-hour figure in the headline.
- "even a simple website **pop-up ad** can derail an entire task": a vivid, concrete, adversarially relevant example (pop-ups are also an injection vector).
- "For now, reliable automation of long or complex tasks **remains infeasible**." Bounded present-tense claim.
