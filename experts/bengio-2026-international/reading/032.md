# 032 · L508–516 · p.14 · "The role of this report"

**Predicted:** help policymakers, an IPCC-like role, does/doesn't. **Got:** that, plus the evidence cutoff I'd been waiting for, a typology of evidence, and one self-inconsistency.

- **The evidence cutoff: "evidence published before December 2025".** Before December, so through November 2025. Events in December 2025 or January 2026 are outside, or are mentioned only exceptionally. Important for forks: e.g. a model released in December 2025 wouldn't be assessed. *Should-ask: "Is X in IASR 2026?" Check X's date against the cutoff first.*
- **A self-inconsistency about who wrote it:** "**The writing team included over 100 independent experts, including an Expert Advisory Panel**". But 023 says the writing team has "over 30 members", and 006 says the panel "provided technical feedback only". So the Introduction folds the panel into the "writing team", against the report's own description. It's minor, but it's exactly the slippage by which "backed by 30 countries" becomes "written by 30 countries' experts". **Authoritative: 006 and 023** (the panel advises and gives technical feedback; the writing team is about 30).
- **The justification of the focus is now epistemic:** "Because the evidence dilemma is most acute where scientific understanding is thinnest, this Report focuses on 'emerging risks'". So the frontier focus is justified by *uncertainty*, not severity. The narrowing (019) had two reasons, rigour-value and complementarity; this adds a cleaner one. Note "Its analysis focuses on issues that **remain particularly uncertain**". A selection rule: uncertainty attracts coverage.
  - Wandering: this has a perverse edge. Risks that become *well understood* (e.g. deepfake harms "now have robust empirical evidence") would, by this rule, eventually move out of focus, like bias and privacy did. So IASR's map will always under-represent the documented present and over-represent the uncertain frontier. That's not a flaw for its purpose, but this project should know it when weighting IASR's coverage as evidence of what matters.
- **The evidence typology** (lexicon-relevant, and a claim-warrant scale): "**robust empirical evidence**" (AI-generated media harms, cyber vulnerabilities) versus "**modelling exercises, laboratory studies under controlled conditions, and theoretical analysis**" for risks "that may arise from future developments". A three-way warrant set for the uncertain risks. The project's record of a source's "methodology" could adopt this: IASR tags its own claims with evidence types implicitly. Watch whether the body marks them explicitly.
- "While contributors differ on some points, they share the belief that constructive and transparent scientific discourse …": a minimal shared credo. It confirms the report's posture: disagreement exists and is not erased.
- **The regional caveat:** "risks may manifest differently across countries and regions" given adoption, infrastructure and institutions. Global relevance claimed, uniformity disclaimed.
