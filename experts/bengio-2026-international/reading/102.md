# 102 · L1104–1108 · p.50 · "Forms and harms of AI manipulation": a second definition, and its rival

**Manipulation, a second definition, attributed to "experts":** "**influencing someone in order to achieve a goal without their full awareness or understanding**". Compare 101's: "change their beliefs or behaviours without their full awareness or **consent**". Differences:
- **"in order to achieve a goal"** adds *goal-directedness*, close to intent. 101's was target-side only. This brings back the intent element I said was absent at 101. So: 101 (target-side, includes consent), 102 (goal-directed, awareness or *understanding*, no consent). Two definitions on one page with different elements. The 102 one is explicitly attributed ("Experts often distinguish…"), so it's the literature's; 101 is the report's own working gloss.
- For AI, "in order to achieve a goal" raises the question: *whose* goal? The user's (malicious use), the developer's (engagement optimisation), or the system's (an emergent goal under RLHF)? The definition is agnostic. That's what lets sycophancy count.

**"'rational persuasion'"**: "influencing someone using honest and rational arguments so that they **authentically endorse** their new beliefs". The contrast class. Authenticity of endorsement is the criterion.

**"In practice, this distinction is contentious"**: researchers disagree about separating harmful manipulation from legitimate influence. So the section deliberately "also discusses other types of persuasion that some might regard as neutral or even beneficial". An honest scope note: the section's object is fuzzy by the field's own lights.

Lexicon proposal: `iasr:manipulation` with both definitions recorded (report-gloss 101, literature 102), `iasr:rational-persuasion` as the contrast, `iasr:influence` as the genus. Note: "deliberately ambiguous where honest" (CLAUDE.md: "deliberate ambiguity where it is honest"). This is such a case: the source itself says the boundary is contested.
