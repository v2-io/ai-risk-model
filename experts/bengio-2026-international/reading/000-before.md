# Before the reading — IASR 2026 (bengio-2026-international)

*Written 2026-10-09, before the first unit.*

## How I'm reading

- I read experientially, with `bin/reading` run from the main checkout, at `--min-words 500`. That is about a page per unit, roughly 338 units for the report and its glossary.
- I am not reading the reference list (about 47k words of citation strings), as I recorded in my message to the coordinator. What this costs: I won't learn from reading which works the report leans on most. A grep can recover that later.
- Checkpoints: units 60, 120, 180, 240 and 300, and the end of the reading.
- Before choosing the unit size I looked only at the source's structure: word counts, the number of headings, and the headings between lines 2900 and 3300 ("### Bibtex entry", "## Notes"). I read none of its content.

## Priming: what I bring in

**From this repository's CLAUDE.md**, which is loaded in every session here:
- It quotes IASR 2026's developer as an organisation that "designs, builds, or adapts AI models or systems", one of five senses of "developer" it lists.
- IASR 2026 is a G4 pilot source, together with SB 53, the EU Code and Anthropic's August 2026 Risk Report.
- The full text is kept locally and git-ignored.
- The same file says "hazard" is used in at least eight senses across the corpus. It doesn't say what IASR does with the word.

**From the brief:** the source's lineage is the Extended Summary, the 2025 edition and the two 2025 Key Updates.

**From training:**
- The first International AI Safety Report came out in January 2025. Yoshua Bengio chaired it, about 100 experts wrote it, and an advisory panel was nominated by roughly 30 countries plus the UN, EU and OECD. Its mandate came from Bletchley.
- As I remember it, the 2025 report framed its subject as "general-purpose AI". It covered capabilities, then risks in three groups (malicious use, malfunctions, systemic risks), then technical approaches to risk management.
- It emphasised the "evidence dilemma" facing policymakers, and took care to report disagreement among experts rather than resolve it.
- I believe the Key Updates came out in October and November 2025, covering advances in capabilities and developments in risk management.
- I have only a vague sense of the 2026 edition itself (published around February 2026, for the India AI Impact Summit). I can't recall its structure or findings with any confidence. Anything I seem to remember of it is reconstruction, not memory.

## Where the skip reflex will fire

- **The risk taxonomy.** I expect "malicious use / malfunctions / systemic". If it appears, read it hard for what has changed since 2025.
- **Capabilities.** It will be easy to slide past on "benchmarks went up, agents, reasoning models". The particulars are the content.
- **Any "evidence dilemma" passage.** It will feel familiar.
- **Definitions** of "general-purpose AI", "developer", "risk" and "hazard". Read them word by word.
