# 049 · L677–679 · p.25 · §1.1 Evidence gaps

**Predicted:** challenges and gaps. **Got:** "Evidence gaps", and no "Key challenges for policymakers" before it (perhaps it comes after, or §1.1 has none). The "policy decisions shape development" subsection promised in 038 hasn't appeared. Maybe it was the stage-sensitivity remarks in 042, or it got cut. I'll note it as a possible unkept promise if it never comes.

**The gap is transparency.** "a lack of publicly available information regarding how they are developed". "Some developers are highly transparent … However, in general, there is a limited degree of public and policymaker knowledge about how most advanced models are developed, safeguarded, evaluated, and deployed."
- **Internal deployment is named as the blindest spot:** "**internally deployed AI systems** that are used within AI companies but not used or understood by outside stakeholders". That's the second mention of internal deployment (after 043's definition). Charlotte Stix (Apollo, writing group) has published on internal deployment governance; probably her influence. **This is something SB 53 and the EU Code differ on** (SB 53's catastrophic-risk framework covers internal use, I believe; the Code's "placing on the market"). A should-ask for cross-expert talk: *"Does IASR treat internal deployment as a risk locus?"* Yes: it's an evidence gap here, and probably 2.2.2 or 3.x.
- **A balanced line on trade secrets:** "Limitations to external disclosure are **sometimes necessary** to protect companies' trade secrets and intellectual property. At the same time, low transparency makes it more difficult for independent researchers and policymakers". This pairs with 030's "developers have incentives to keep important information proprietary". The structural incentive is named, and its legitimate side granted.
- Wandering: the evidence gap here is *about the evidence-gathering system itself*, not a scientific unknown. It's a political-economy gap: information exists but is withheld. The report treats it as an "evidence gap" in the same register as "we don't know if LoC is possible". Two very different kinds of gap, epistemic (no one knows) and access (someone knows, the public doesn't), under one heading. **This project might want to distinguish them in assertion records**: IASR's "evidence gaps" are of both kinds.
