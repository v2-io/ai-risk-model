# 081 · L942–950 · p.40 · §1.3 Evidence gaps

As predicted. Three gaps: (1) forecasting science is weak ("cannot reliably predict when AI systems will have certain capabilities"), and even predictable benchmarks wouldn't predict real-world impact; (2) real-world constraints (data beyond 2030, energy, chips, capex); (3) "**minimal empirical understanding of feedback loops from AI automating its own research and development**", in particular "how much **human oversight** will be needed in this process" and whether slow experimental feedback constrains acceleration.
- **The two pitfalls**, the evidence dilemma in its capability form: "**underestimating** rapidly emerging capabilities … and **overreacting** to technical advances that may not translate into practical applications". The overreaction side is now about advances that *don't translate*, the evaluation gap.
- **"This makes contingency planning across multiple scenarios essential."** The nearest thing to a recommendation so far, phrased as an implication ("makes … essential"). A general methodological prescription (scenario planning), not a specific policy. It sits within the "no *specific* recommendations" boundary, at its edge.
- No "Key challenges for policymakers" in chapter 1 at all. The 033 promise applies, it seems, to chapters 2–3.
