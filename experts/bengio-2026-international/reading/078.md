# 078 · L918–924 · p.39 · OECD Scenario 3: Progress continues

**Predicted:** multi-day projects; Moore's law. **Got:** Moore's law, correctly guessed. And **month-long** tasks, longer than I predicted.
- **The 2030 state: "expert collaborators"**: "many professional tasks in digital environments that might take humans **a month** to complete"; humans give "high-level directions", AI works "with high autonomy … including **autonomously interacting with a range of stakeholders**"; memory; "'learn on the job' to some extent"; some physical tasks beyond controlled environments.
- **Tension with §1.3's own extrapolation.** 065 and 069: "If capabilities continue to improve at their current rate, by 2030 … tasks that would take human engineers **multiple days**" (METR trend). Scenario 3, "continued rapid progress" (which reads as current rates: "Compute and data inputs continue to scale and do not hit substantial limits"), has **month**-long tasks. Days vs a month is a 5–10× difference. Explanations: the OECD's scenario isn't calibrated to METR; or Scenario 3 assumes innovations beyond trend ("new algorithmic innovations … overcome … continual learning"); or "successfully perform many professional tasks" isn't the 80% metric. **Should-ask: "Under current trends, what can AI do by 2030 according to IASR?"** Answer: the IASR text says multi-day software tasks (METR extrapolation); the OECD's "Progress continues" scenario says month-long professional tasks. Two framings, not reconciled in the text.
- "'learn on the job'": continual learning arrives in Scenario 3, having been "a key area of development" (060).
- **"Autonomously interacting with a range of stakeholders"**: AI agents acting socially on people's behalf, which is the substrate for the autonomy and manipulation risks.
