# 061 · L780–782 · p.31 · §1.2 Evidence gaps

**Predicted:** real-world measurement, contamination, low-resource evaluation. **Got:** a more fundamental gap.
- **"difficult to prove with high-confidence that an AI system cannot perform certain – potentially dangerous – tasks."** This is the *absence-proof problem* at the heart of capability-threshold governance. A negative result depends on "the specific test examples and prompt used". **It's in tension with the ES's flat absence claim** (028: "Current systems lack the capabilities to pose such risks", i.e. LoC). By the report's own epistemology, that statement should carry a confidence qualifier. Unless 2.2.2 grounds it in something stronger than evaluation negatives (e.g. no autonomous replication *in the wild*), it is the kind of claim this paragraph says is hard to establish. **Should-ask: "How confident is IASR that current systems can't cause loss of control?"** Look at 2.2.2's basis, and set it against this paragraph.
- **"There is no single, comprehensive, and continuously updated synthesis of AI capabilities"** and **"no widely accepted taxonomy for capabilities"**. The capabilities equivalent of this project's problem with risk vocabulary. "Policymakers must navigate a patchwork of benchmarks and sources" ("must" as description of a predicament, not a prescription).
- **Self-reference:** "Existing reviews, **including this Report**, provide valuable summaries but are **static snapshots** in a rapidly moving field." Honest, and a reason for the Key Updates between editions (not mentioned again).
- No "Key challenges for policymakers" in §1.2 either. The structure promised in 033 seems to be "updates + evidence gaps" in chapter 1, with "challenges" perhaps reserved for chapters 2–3.
