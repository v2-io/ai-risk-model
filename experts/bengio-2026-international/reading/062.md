# 062 · L784–786 · p.31 · "Benchmarks often fail to predict real-world performance"

Still in §1.2's evidence gaps (a sub-subsection). "'data contamination'", the second definition ("trained using data from these same benchmarks"), with a new claim: "**most developers do not currently track or disclose**" it. Developer conduct is characterised as a fact; the evidence is presumably a survey of model cards. Lab testing "**often overestimates** AI systems' practical utility": the directional evaluation gap again (utility side). The example study: agent code "functional" but needing "significant human effort" on documentation, formatting and quality (METR's 2025 note on algorithmic vs holistic scoring, I believe). **"'evaluation science'"** is named as an emerging field "advocating for rigorous methodologies that ensure **external validity**". New benchmarks of "economically valuable tasks and real-world **remote labour**" (GDPval? the Remote Labor Index?). Lexicon candidates: `evaluation science`, `external validity`.

Wandering: this is the third mechanism of the evaluation gap (contamination, lab-vs-world, narrow tasks). All are about *overstatement of utility*. The risk-side mechanisms (evaluation awareness, sandbagging) would be *understatement of danger*. They'll presumably appear in 2.2.2 and 3.x. The report's chapter split keeps the two directions in different chapters, so no single passage confronts the fact that evaluations err *both* ways at once, and in the direction least convenient for each purpose.
