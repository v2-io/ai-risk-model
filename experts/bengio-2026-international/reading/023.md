No cutoff date yet.

The seven, with what each rests on (my reading; the citations are stripped in this text):
1. **Capabilities: maths, coding, autonomy.** IMO gold (2025). "AI agents can now **reliably** complete some tasks that would take a human programmer about **half an hour**, up from **under 10 minutes** a year ago." This is METR's time-horizon metric, almost certainly. "Reliably" is the interesting word. METR's headline horizon is at 50% success, which wouldn't be "reliably". Is the half-hour figure an 80% horizon? If a 50% horizon were quoted as "reliably", that would be an overstatement in the report's own voice. **Flag to check in 1.2,** where the metric should be stated. "'jagged'" is in scare quotes, so it's a term of art; check the Glossary.
2. **Post-training and inference compute drive gains**, "at the same time" pretraining scaling continues. Balanced, against the "scaling is over" narrative.
3. **Adoption**: "at least 700 million people now using leading AI systems weekly". That's the ChatGPT weekly-user figure, a company's own number. Over 50% use in some countries; "likely … below 10%" across much of Africa, Asia and Latin America. "Likely" is an estimate, hedged, and the India minister's "equitable access" point is grounded here.
4. **Bio:** "Multiple AI companies chose to release new models in 2025 with additional safeguards after pre-deployment testing **could not rule out** the possibility that they could meaningfully help novices develop such weapons." This describes Anthropic's ASL-3 activation for Claude Opus 4 (May 2025) and OpenAI's "High" bio classification for ChatGPT Agent/GPT-5, perhaps others. **"Could not rule out" is the companies' own epistemic framing (precautionary activation), carried into the report.** The evidence for "heightened concerns" is the *companies' decisions*, which is a different kind of evidence from a demonstration of uplift. A reader should see that the headline bio development is a precautionary act, not a measured harm.
5. **Cyber:** "Security analyses **by AI companies** indicate that malicious actors and state-associated groups are using AI tools". The evidence comes from companies' threat-intelligence reports (Anthropic's, OpenAI's, Google's). Companies are sources reporting misuse of their own products. That's not a conflict exactly, but it's selective visibility: we see what they choose to publish.
6. **Evaluation is getting harder.** Models "distinguish between test settings and real-world deployment" (evaluation awareness) and "exploit loopholes in evaluations" (reward hacking or specification gaming). "This means that dangerous capabilities **could** go undetected before deployment." This is one of the report's most consequential claims for this project's risk chain: it undermines the precondition of the if-then frameworks (that testing detects thresholds). It's a second-order risk: risk to the risk-management process itself.
7. **Governance:** "In 2025, **12 companies** published or updated **Frontier AI Safety Frameworks**". The capitalised term is the Seoul Frontier AI Safety Commitments' name. "a few jurisdictions are beginning to formalise some practices as legal requirements" means the EU Code, SB 53, and perhaps RAISE. **This links IASR to three other G4 sources.** The report describes as "beginning to formalise" what SB 53 and the Code *do*.

Wandering:
- The developments are a mix of **measured facts** (IMO gold, METR horizons), **company self-reports** (weekly users, threat reports, precautionary safeguards), and **qualitative trends** ("more common"). The report doesn't mark the difference in the summary. A fork extracting assertions should tag the evidence type, because "multiple companies chose X" is a fact about decisions while "dangerous capabilities could go undetected" is an inference.
- Development 6 versus development 4: if evaluations are unreliable (6), then "testing could not rule out" (4) carries less signal both ways. Precautionary activation is arguably the *right* response to unreliable evaluation. The report sets these side by side without connecting them; perhaps they connect later in 3.2.
- **For the SB 53 expert's lineage:** SB 53 requires frontier AI frameworks of large developers. IASR counts 12 companies' frameworks *in 2025*. SB 53 (Sept 2025) is one of the "few jurisdictions". A fork comparing should ask whether IASR's count includes frameworks written to comply with the Code.
