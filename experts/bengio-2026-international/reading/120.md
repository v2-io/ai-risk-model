# 120 · L1256–1268 · p.60 · "Even with AI assistance, humans remain in the loop"; Table 2.3 (threat activity × AI involvement)

- **"human–AI collaboration remains the dominant paradigm for cyber operations"**: humans set strategy, decompose tasks and handle errors; AI automates subtasks.
- **Table 2.3**, an assessment matrix: threat activity → observed trend (quoted from threat reports) → confirmed AI capabilities (quoted) → **IASR's own judgement of AI's contribution, in graded language**:

| Threat activity | Trend | IASR's judgement of AI's contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Phishing & deepfakes | Increase (identity attacks +32% H1 2025; phishing up 2024) | "**very likely** to have contributed": within capabilities + multiple actors reported |
| Influence operations | Sustained high | "**likely** to have contributed": several sources, multiple actors scaling |
| Data & credential stealing | Increase (exfiltration +92.7%; ransomware +87%; credential theft +23%) | "**limited and likely secondary** to other factors. However, **some malicious actors would be unlikely to launch their attacks without AI**" |
| Attack development & weaponisation | Increase (faster exploit weaponisation) | "**appear to have contributed, but … likely secondary**. **Unclear** whether AI enabled substantial attacks beyond the sophistication level of the attackers." |

- **This is the report's most explicit causal-attribution apparatus so far**: graded likelihood words ("very likely", "likely", "limited… secondary", "unclear") applied to *AI's contribution to observed trends*. Is there a defined likelihood scale (like the IPCC's calibrated language)? I haven't seen one. If not, "very likely" and "likely" are uncalibrated. **Should-ask: "Does IASR use calibrated likelihood language?"** Check the Glossary and methods.
- **The data-stealing row is the most important nuance:** AI's aggregate contribution is "secondary", yet "some malicious actors would be **unlikely to launch their attacks without AI**". That's the *uplift-to-the-unskilled* claim, distinct from aggregate impact. The supporting quote is from **Anthropic's August 2025 Threat Intelligence Report**: "The actor [...] relied heavily on Claude for [malware] implementation". So a key piece of evidence for uplift is an Anthropic self-report about Claude.
- **The evidence base is overwhelmingly industry.** I count the citations by their "[industry]" tag: Microsoft (×5 uses, two reports), CrowdStrike (×2), Fortinet, Zscaler, Dragos, Anthropic, Google GTIG, Check Point, Palo Alto Unit 42, OpenAI are [industry]. Non-industry: WEF, ODNI, EEAS. So **~13 of 16 citation uses in this table are industry-authored**, and mostly from security vendors, who have a commercial interest in threat escalation (they sell defences). This isn't a conflict for AI labs alone: *security vendors* are another interested party. The report doesn't comment. **A clear case for the project's derivation and interest recording.** IASR's cyber trend evidence leans heavily on threat-intelligence marketing reports, by necessity, since that's where the data is.
- Specific incidents named in the quotes: the "**Shai-Hulud**" npm worm (Unit 42; AI-generated content in a worm); Google GTIG's malware using AI mid-execution (the experimental malware of 118); "**APT INC** deployed a likely LLM-authored data destruction script" (CrowdStrike).
