# 034 · L530–543 · p.15 · "How this Report is organised", and **the systemic-risk definition, with the source flagging the EU collision itself**

**Predicted:** chapters and how to read the sub-parts. **Got:** the three central questions, chapter by chapter, and then the thing I'd flagged in 029, settled by the source.

**"In this report, systemic risks are risks that result from widespread deployment of highly-capable general-purpose AI across society and the economy. Note that the EU AI Act uses the term differently, to refer to risks from general-purpose AI models that pose "risks of large-scale harm"."**
- The prediction confirmed, and better: **IASR names the collision itself.** It's a rare case of a source doing this project's work for it, a declared homonym with another bounded context. Lexicon: `iasr:systemic-risk` ≠ `eu:systemic-risk`, with IASR's own note as the evidence.
- IASR's definition has two elements: (a) **caused by widespread deployment** (diffusion across society and economy), and (b) of **highly-capable** GPAI. So systemic risk is tied to the frontier focus via "highly-capable", consistent with "emerging risks". The individual-level autonomy harms (029) qualify through (a), scale.
- The EU characterisation in quotes, "risks of large-scale harm", needs verifying. That's not, I think, the AI Act's definition wording (Art. 3(65): "a risk that is specific to the high-impact capabilities of general-purpose AI models, having a significant impact on the Union market due to their reach, or due to actual or reasonably foreseeable negative effects on public health, safety, public security, fundamental rights, or the society as a whole, that can be propagated at scale across the value chain", from memory, not verified). Where does "risks of large-scale harm" come from? Possibly a recital, or the Code. **Should-ask: is IASR's quotation of the EU accurate?** The EU Code expert could answer; I can't from this text. If it's a paraphrase in quotation marks, that's a fidelity defect in IASR worth recording.
- **The asterisk on "systemic risks,*"** in the chapter-2 summary is the pointer to this note. That resolves the asterisks seen earlier (on Advisers to the Chair, and the UN Panel): IASR uses asterisk footnotes, which the web edition renders inline or as separate paragraphs.

**Clarifications of the subsection scopes** (useful for tagging):
- 2.1.1 "the use of AI systems for **criminal activities**"; 2.1.2 "manipulation"; 2.1.4 "**developing** biological or chemical weapons" (development, not deployment or attack).
- 2.2.1 reliability = "**operational failures**".
- 2.3.1 "disruptions to labour markets"; 2.3.2 "**threats to** human autonomy".
- 3.3: "techniques **developers** use to make AI models and systems more robust and resistant to misuse". So 3.3 is developer-side by definition. 3.5: "efforts to make society more resilient to potential AI **shocks and harms**".
- 3.1 is "the distinctive **policymaking** challenges that general-purpose AI poses". The ES called them "technical and institutional challenges" to *risk management*; here they're *policymaking* challenges. The audience framing shifts.
