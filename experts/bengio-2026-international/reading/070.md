**Predicted:** AI R&D, then scenarios. **Got:** domain expansion and emergent capabilities.
- GPQA Diamond: "leading models now **exceed PhD-level experts**". Stronger than 031's "as well as subject-matter experts". "Research-level performance across specialised scientific domains in the next few years", per "trend extrapolations".
- **"'emergent capabilities'"**, defined: "sudden jumps" in specific capabilities "even as overall performance improves steadily". **The example is odd:** adding large numbers jumped "once they were **prompted to work step-by-step**, rather than gradually improving at this as models scaled". That's an *elicitation* jump (a change of prompting method), not emergence with scale, which is how "emergent capabilities" is usually meant (Wei et al. 2022). The example conflates two phenomena: (a) capabilities appearing abruptly as scale grows, and (b) latent capabilities appearing abruptly when elicited differently. Both are planning hazards, and (b) is arguably the more important for evaluation, since it's the same model with a different prompt. **For the lexicon:** IASR's `emergent capabilities` covers sudden jumps from any cause, with an elicitation example. Note the conceptual blur.
- "difficult to anticipate when AI systems will suddenly acquire **strategically relevant cognitive abilities**": the planning challenge.
- "they **disagree on how unpredictable** these capability leaps truly are". That nods to the "mirage" critique (that emergence is an artefact of discontinuous metrics), without naming it.
