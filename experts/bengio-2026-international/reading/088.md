# 088 · L992–998 · p.44 · Chapter 2 "Risks" opens: **the three category definitions and two reading rules**

**Predicted:** the categories, how they were selected, evidence types. **Got:** the categories with definitions, and two reading rules that change how every chapter-2 claim should be read.

**The definitions, the canonical statement:**
1. "**Risks from misuse, where actors deliberately use AI systems to cause harm**". Note the name: "misuse", not "malicious use" (TOC, ES). Same category, two names. The defining feature is *deliberate use to cause harm*, intent attached to the user.
2. "**Risks from malfunctions, where AI systems fail or behave in unexpected and harmful ways**". The defining feature: the *system's* behaviour diverges ("fail" or "unexpected"). LoC falls here (028).
3. "**Systemic risks, which arise from widespread deployment across society and the economy**". Consistent with 034 (minus "highly-capable").
- This confirms my 026 reading: the basis of division is mixed (user intent / system behaviour / scale of deployment).

**Reading rule 1: the categories overlap.** "These categories are **not exhaustive or mutually exclusive** – risks may cut across multiple categories – but they provide a structured way to analyse different **mechanisms of harm**." This **contradicts the ES's flat "General-purpose AI risks fall into three categories"** (026), which implied exhaustiveness. The body is authoritative: *a structuring device, not a partition*. **Should-ask: "Is IASR's triad a taxonomy?"** The source says no: non-exhaustive, overlapping, organised by mechanism. Lexicon: record `iasr:misuse / malfunction / systemic-risk` as *analytical lenses by mechanism*, not mutually exclusive kinds. That suits the project's CLAUDE.md principle that "stage in that chain is read as a role … not a fixed property of a kind".

**Reading rule 2: inclusion ≠ importance.** "inclusion here does not necessarily imply a risk is likely, severe, or requires policy action." A strong disclaimer. A fork or a translator must not read IASR's risk list as a ranking or as a call to act. And it flips: *exclusion* doesn't mean unimportant either (the scope narrowing, 022).

Other points:
- "already causing real-world harm": fraud, harmful outputs from errors, and "deployment is impacting labour markets, **information ecosystems**, and cybersecurity systems". "Information ecosystems" has no subsection. Maybe it's within 2.1.2 or 2.3.2.
- "mechanisms, severity, and likelihood": the three dimensions of understanding a risk. Mechanism is first-class, alongside severity and likelihood, beyond the two-factor formula of 024. That fits the categories being "mechanisms of harm".
- "In some cases there is clear evidence of harm **and effective ways to address it**. In others, both the effects … and the effectiveness of mitigations remain uncertain." Evidence about the risk and evidence about mitigations are two separate axes.
