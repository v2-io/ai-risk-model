# 096 · L1060–1068 · p.47 · §2.1.1 Updates (detectability)

Figure 2.1's axis text: "Number of **incidents and hazards**", monthly with a six-month moving average, Nov 2021 to Nov 2025, up to ~250/month. So the figure counts *incidents and hazards together* (OECD's two classes). A reader of IASR could mistake "incidents" for harms; the axis says incidents *and hazards*.

- **The 77% figure, contextualised:** "after a **five-minute conversation**, participants misidentified text generated by OpenAI's **GPT-4o** model as human-written 77% of the time." That's a Turing-test design (probably Jones & Bergen; Cameron Jones is in the writing group). The ES-level "misidentified AI-generated text as human-written 77% of the time" (091) loses the conversational setting. A conversation-based Turing test isn't the same as detecting a static AI-written article. **A fork should give the setting.**
- Deepfakes: humans "often performing **no better than chance**". Audio voice clones mistaken for the real speaker in **80%** of cases: "heightened risks of impersonation".
- **Counter-finding:** "multimodal AI outputs combining video, audio, and text appear **easier to detect** than text or audio alone". The report includes a finding that cuts against the trend. Even-handed.

**Next, I predict:** Evidence gaps (prevalence data, detection effectiveness), then Challenges for policymakers (detection vs generation race, attribution, CSAM harm regardless of labelling).
