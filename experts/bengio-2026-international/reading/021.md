# 021 · L378–398 · p.8 · India's foreword (Vaishnaw), and the start of "About this Report"

**Predicted:** MeitY; inclusion, the Global South. **Got:** that, plus the shortest and most content-dense summary of the report so far, from a minister.
- "This edition focuses on rapidly evolving general-purpose AI systems, **including language, vision and agentic models**." "Agentic models" is named as a class alongside modalities, which is a little odd: agency is a mode of deployment, not a modality. Watch how 1.1 handles it.
- "wider impacts on labour markets, human autonomy **and concentration of power**". **Concentration of power isn't a heading in the TOC.** So it lives inside some section, perhaps 2.3.2 or a box. That supports my guess from the Forethought writers (Davidson, Hadshar). A fork asked "does IASR 2026 discuss power concentration?" should say yes and find where; I'll mark it when I meet it.
- "practical approaches of model evaluations, **dangerous capability thresholds** and **'if-then' safety commitments**". These are the vocabulary of company frontier safety frameworks (if-then is Karnofsky's framing). The minister names them as the report's practical highlights.
- "Our global risk management frameworks are still immature, with limited quantitative benchmarks and significant evidence gaps. These gaps must be addressed alongside innovation." Policy talk in the minister's voice ("must"), which the report itself disclaims. Forewords aren't the Report: they're attributable to the ministers. **For citation:** a sentence from a foreword is not "IASR 2026 says"; it's "the UK/Indian minister's foreword says". Mark that distinction in any extraction.
- "**Responsible openness** of AI models, fair access to compute and data": India's interest in open models and access. A counterweight to the risk framing of 3.4.

Then "About this Report" begins. "the capabilities and risks associated with **advanced AI systems**" — "advanced AI" again, as in the disclaimer. Panel nominees from "over 30 countries and international organisations, including the OECD, the EU, and the UN". The examples named are international organisations, not countries. A careful choice, avoiding naming which countries (and so who isn't there)?

**Next, I predict:** "About this Report" continues: the independence of the writers, the report's purpose (not to make policy recommendations), its scope (general-purpose AI, emerging risks), the timeline (literature up to some cutoff, perhaps December 2025), and the relation to the Key Updates.
