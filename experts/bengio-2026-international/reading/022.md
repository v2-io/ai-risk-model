# 022 · L400–408 · p.9 · "Scope, focus, and independence": the report's own definitions

**Predicted:** independence, no recommendations, scope, a literature cutoff, the Key Updates. **Got:** three labelled definitions, with no cutoff date yet. This is one of the most important units for the lexicon work.

**Scope**: "'general-purpose AI': **AI models and systems** capable of performing a wide variety of tasks across different contexts. These models and systems perform tasks like generating text, images, audio, or other forms of data, and are frequently adapted to a range of domain-specific applications."
- Both "models and systems". The 2025 report, I think, distinguished models from systems carefully. Here the definition straddles both, and generality ("wide variety of tasks across different contexts") is the criterion. No threshold, no compute, no "frontier". So the *scope* object is broad: any general-purpose model or system. The *focus* narrows it.
- The examples are generative ("generating text, images, audio, or other forms of data"). Generality is defined by task variety, but every example is generation. A general-purpose robot policy fits the definition, not the examples.
- "frequently adapted to a range of domain-specific applications": the downstream adaptation story, close to the EU AI Act's GPAI ("can be integrated into a variety of downstream systems or applications"). Compare word for word in the lexicon phase.

**Focus**: "'emerging risks': risks that arise at the frontier of AI capabilities." Anchored in **the Bletchley Declaration**: "particular safety risks arise at the 'frontier' of AI", including "risks from misuse, issues of control, and cybersecurity risks". The triad misuse/control/cyber is Bletchley's. Broader impacts ("human rights, fairness, accountability, and privacy") are acknowledged and handed off to the UN Panel (with an asterisked note).
- **So the scope/focus split is a two-level object.** Scope = general-purpose AI (broad); focus = risks at the *frontier* of capabilities (narrow). A risk from a non-frontier general-purpose model, such as deepfakes from small open-weight image models, is in scope but arguably out of focus. Watch how 2.1.1 handles it. I bet they justify it by "frontier capabilities enable more realistic X" or treat it as emerging.
- **"Frontier" is used without definition here.** It's quoted from Bletchley in scare quotes. Does the Glossary define "frontier AI"? The term the report *chooses* is "general-purpose AI"; "frontier" is borrowed vocabulary used to locate risks. That's a deliberate choice: lexicon candidate `iasr:emerging-risk`, defined by reference to the frontier.

**Independence**: "the independent writing team jointly had full discretion over its content … It does not make specific policy recommendations." "Specific" is a hedge: it leaves room for general findings about what works (e.g. "layering multiple approaches offers more robust risk management").

**The narrowing, stated by the source**: "this focus makes the scope of this Report narrower than that of the 2025 Report, which also addressed issues such as **bias, environmental impacts, privacy, and copyright**."
- This confirms half my memory from 005: bias, environment, privacy, copyright. It doesn't mention market concentration or the global R&D divide, which I also remembered from 2025. "Such as" leaves room. Check against the 2025 text.
- Odd tension: "Note that this focus makes **the scope** of this Report narrower". The report defined *scope* (GPAI) and *focus* (emerging risks) as distinct, then says the focus narrows the scope. Loose usage of its own terms within a paragraph. Minor, but it's the kind of collision this project records.

**Next, I predict:** more of "About this Report": the evidence base and its limits (preprints, company reports, a literature cutoff), how disagreement is handled, the Key Updates, perhaps the asterisked note on the UN Panel.
