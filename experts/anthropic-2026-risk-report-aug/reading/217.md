# Unit 217 — §5.1.1.1 Distillation: introduction, L2789–2808, p.159

- Distillation (training a weaker model on a stronger one's outputs) is legitimate and common; a **"distillation attack"** = unauthorised, systematic extraction of transcripts to train a separate model, circumventing controls (e.g. regional access restrictions), violating the usage policy.
- Risk: a distilled model inherits risk-relevant capability but is served without safeguards.
- **Successful attack** = **Volume** (~10,000+ transcripts, the rough benchmark from prior observed attempts) + **Uplift** (transcripts capture reasoning well enough that training produces real improvement on high-risk agentic evals: **coding, cyber-offense, biology**).
- New defences on both axes, applied (beyond pre-existing thinking summarisation) **only to Fable 5**.
- fn: the commercial-interest point is "less so for preventing distillation, which we currently believe mostly consists of competitors with much less capable models … (while still being unlikely to compete directly for most of our customers)."
