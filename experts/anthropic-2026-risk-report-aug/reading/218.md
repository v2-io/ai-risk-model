# Unit 218 — §5.1.1.2 Thinking and connector-text summarisation, Figures 5.1.1.2.A–B, L2810–2840, pp.160–161

- Existing defence: **summarising internal reasoning** before showing users (default for longer reasoning since Claude Opus 4).
- **Connector text** = visible reasoning-like text between tool calls; **connector text summarisation (CTS)**: all connector text of 50–500 tokens rewritten by a smaller model (**Claude Haiku 4.5**) into a shorter paraphrase. A minor bug weakened it on one surface for a few days after Fable 5's launch (fixed).
- Efficacy test: fine-tune base models (strong and weak) on production-teacher transcripts with no mitigation vs with thinking summarisation + CTS; evaluate on agentic coding, cyber, biology.
- **Figures (opened):** mitigations generally lower the distilled student's scores (e.g., SWE-bench, Mythos 5 teacher → strong student: 0.793 → 0.728; TerminalBench: 0.515 → 0.326), with a few non-reductions (e.g., a Mythos Preview-teacher biology case where the mitigated student scored higher; a weak-student TerminalBench case roughly flat). Students mostly land well below the teacher models' own scores (dotted reference lines).
- Report: "fairly clear reductions in uplift across agentic coding and cyber evaluations", mixed on some biology evals (one judged unusually noisy). Rough guess: the absolute uplift shown is representative of (perhaps slightly weaker than) what real-world threat actors would see with comparable teacher/student models (fn 84).

Differed: the anti-distillation mechanism is the *same* thing the misalignment section relies on staying legible — the model's reasoning. Users (and distillers) see a summary; Anthropic's monitors see the raw CoT. That's a coherent design: raw reasoning is visible to the developer for oversight and hidden from outsiders against extraction. An expert should connect §2 (CoT monitorability as a safety barrier) and §5.1 (CoT hidden from users as an anti-distillation barrier): one artefact, two controls, different audiences.

Wandering: summarisation by Haiku 4.5 is another AI-in-the-loop control — a smaller model mediating what users see of a larger model's reasoning. It also affects users' ability to verify the model's work (they see paraphrased reasoning). That's a cost to transparency toward users for the sake of proliferation control. Not discussed by the report; mine, marked.

Mitigations reduce but don't eliminate distillation uplift; and students of Mythos 5 still gain substantially over their base. The defence raises the cost of distillation; the "volume" defences (next) are the other half.
