# Unit 99 — §2.23.1 Asynchronous monitoring (intro), L1339–1341, p.76

Asynchronous monitoring is justified for actions that would likely be detected and are revertible, "or for which the impact is small compared to the upside of catching misaligned actions (which helps mitigate future risk)."

Differed: a new justification element: catching misaligned actions has *upside* — information that mitigates future risk — which can outweigh small uncaught harms. So asynchronous (after-the-fact) monitoring is framed partly as an intelligence-gathering instrument, not only a harm-reverting one.

Wandering: that framing quietly accepts some realised harm as the price of learning, which is a reasonable trade at low stakes and a principle worth recording: *letting a small harmful action happen in order to detect a misaligned model* is preferred to blocking it unseen. Blocking would prevent the harm but also hide the signal. Classic control-research trade-off (detection vs prevention), stated plainly.
