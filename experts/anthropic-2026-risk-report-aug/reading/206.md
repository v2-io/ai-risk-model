## Wandering

**Footnote 75 undercuts part of the review's triage.** The review set aside flagged transcripts from "exercises where we asked red-teamers to deliberately stress-test our systems" and manually checked only a random 30 of the external red-teaming transcripts — while all 62 non-red-teaming ones were checked. But fn 75 says a threat actor could fairly easily have been hired *as a vendor red-teamer*. A red-teaming label, then, was not good evidence of benign intent during the gap, and the bulk of flagged external red-teaming transcripts got only sampled review. The report states both facts; it doesn't connect them. An expert should: *the review's manual coverage was complete for non-red-team flags but sampled for red-team flags, and the report itself notes threat actors could have been hired as red-teamers.*

**The previous report had a blind spot it now names.** The February Risk Report didn't treat human-feedback platforms as a risk surface; the gap was live at its publication. That's a concrete instance of Claim-7-style incompleteness (threat-surface enumeration missing a surface) — in the CB domain. An expert comparing the two reports should mention it: the Feb report's CB assessment omitted a surface where safeguards were off.
