7. **Claim 4:** $P_C H_C$ low (less confidence; $H_C$ "only moderate should it occur").

## Wandering

So the formal argument is new in this report (February's was less structured). A self-declared first attempt, "may be incomplete or partially flawed". That disclaimer should travel with any quotation of the argument's conclusion.

The algebra makes the dependency structure checkable:
- $R_K$ small needs $H_K$ low (Claim 2) and/or $U_K$ low (Claim 5); $P_K$ is high.
- $R_P$ small needs $P_P$ very low (Claim 3) — Claim 6 will presumably give $U_P$ "somewhat low" as backup.
- $R_C$ small needs $P_C H_C$ low (Claim 4) and $U_C$ low (Claim 5).
- $R_{\text{total}} \approx R$ needs Claim 7 (pathways sufficient) and Claim 8 (engineered misalignment low).
- Claim 1 feeds Claims 3 (if covert, pervasive misalignment could hide from assessment), 5 (models can't evade mitigations), and the definitions.

A product of three qualitative factors summed over three types is a clear model, and the step-4 factorisation is exact by definition (P·H·U is the expectation decomposed). The hard part is entirely in the factor estimates, which are qualitative. "Very low", "low", "moderate", "potentially high" have no numbers so far. An expert asked "what probability does Anthropic assign to misalignment catastrophe?" must say: *none numerically; qualitative levels only*, unless a later section gives numbers.

The decomposition treats the three types as additive — reasonable since they're defined as a partition (by the known/unknown and pervasive/context-dependent definitions). But a model could have known misalignment *and* unknown context-dependent misalignment at once; additivity of expected harm still holds by linearity, so no error there.

The catastrophic qualifier entered at step 1 ("expected total unmitigated *catastrophic* harm") though §2.5's definition said "harm". Minor drift; the section consistently confines itself to catastrophic harm, so it's coherent.
