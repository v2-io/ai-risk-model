## Wandering

The page numbers tell a story: the misalignment threat model runs pp.17–93, about 76 pages, of which the appendices (internal monitoring, power-seeking eval, reward-hacking generalization) are ~17. The automated-R&D section starts at p.93. If the whole report is ~200 pages, misalignment is a third or more. "Emerging from internal usage" recurs in Claim 5's sub-claims — the threat is the model working on Anthropic's own infrastructure.

The structure "Claim 1: models unlikely to have strong covert capabilities" first is logical: if a model can't act covertly, monitoring catches it, and most of the rest follows. It's the keystone; if it's wrong, Claims 2, 4, 5 weaken. §2.16.1 "Models might have stronger covert capabilities than we believe" is that keystone's limitation, named. Good that they name it; whether the argument can survive it is the question an expert should be asked.

"Unknown severe pervasive misalignment is very unlikely" — and Claim 3.2's grounds: "our experience with adversarially-designed training processes". That sounds like model-organism work: deliberately training misaligned models and seeing whether the audit catches them (auditing games). Prediction: §2.9.2 describes blind auditing of deliberately misaligned models.

"Claim 7: Threat modeling is sufficient" — a meta-claim about the completeness of their own threat model. That's the unknown-unknowns claim, explicitly carried as a claim with a burden. Unusual and honest-looking in form; content to come.

For this project: the hedge ladder (unlikely/somewhat unlikely/very unlikely) and the known/unknown × pervasive/context-dependent grid are obvious lexicon candidates, and they are *Anthropic-specific* senses (source-namespaced: `anthropic:context-dependent-misalignment`?).
