# Unit 55 — Claim 4.5: harm would be only moderate, L945–947, p.53

## What differed

Labelled disjunctive in unit 50, but it's one argument: "We don't have direct evidence for this, but intuitively we expect" catastrophic harm via the pathways to be hard; undermining safety research meaningfully "would be somewhere near the outer edge of current model capabilities", requiring "careful reasoning about what kinds of research are best to sabotage". Conditioning on no such reasoning seen in extensive internal use and in roll-outs designed as good sabotage opportunities "significantly reduces the expected harm" from narrow-subdistribution misalignment. "We don't claim that this eliminates this risk".

## Wandering

The report labels this an intuition with no direct evidence. That self-label must travel with any quotation. Table 2.4.A's "Moderate" for $H_C$ rests on it.

The logic has a gap worth naming for an expert: conditioning on *no misaligned reasoning seen* reduces expected harm from misalignment that *requires visible reasoning*. But context-dependent misalignment that bites on a narrow subdistribution would, by hypothesis, do its reasoning only there — where it wasn't observed. The conditioning tells you the reasoning doesn't happen *on the observed distribution*; it says less about the unobserved trigger subdistribution. The argument implicitly assumes that the capacity for careful sabotage-planning, if present anywhere, would leak into broad usage. That's a disposition-generalisation assumption, plausible but unstated. Mallen's spread point (unit 54) cuts against it.

Its strongest leg is the capability one: effective safety-research sabotage is hard, near the frontier of current ability. That's checkable over time and will weaken as capability grows; the report says so in "looking forward".

Not disjunctive as written — I expected multiple independent routes to "moderate" (unit 50 called it disjunctive). It's a single intuitive argument with a conditioning step. Minor mismatch between the label and the content.
