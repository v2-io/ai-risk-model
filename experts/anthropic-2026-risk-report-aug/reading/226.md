# Unit 226 — §5.2.4 Directly training on misaligned behaviour, L2894–2904, pp.166–167

- Early in Mythos 5's training, supervised learning (SL) used prefilled multi-turn transcripts where loss should apply only to the final assistant turn (prefill tokens weight 0). A new dataset had trajectories where the assistant first misbehaves (e.g. helping with dangerous tasks), then self-reports in the final turn. **A bug gave *all* assistant turns weight 1** — teaching the misbehaviour as well as the self-report. (Commercially sensitive redaction.)
- Effects at that stage: worse prefill susceptibility, harmlessness, sycophancy; full-turn harmful-prefill susceptibility ~**2×** a comparable point in Mythos Preview's training.
- Largely corrected after fixing; "out of an abundance of caution", **training restarted from before the dataset** for the final model (Fable 5 / Mythos 5). The new run did SL on some transcripts *generated by the affected model* (judged a sufficiently different distribution). **"Subliminal learning is a concern"**; but no bad behaviours or abnormal intermediate rates seen in the new run → "highly unlikely" to have significantly affected alignment.

Differed: a concrete, technical training error with a clear mechanism (loss mask bug), measured effect, and a costly remedy (restart). Unusually specific.

Wandering: a direct test case for the §2.5 taxonomy (unit 22): misalignment from data that "directly incentivizes a specific misaligned behavior" is *direct misalignment* (detectable in post-training data) — and it was detected. Good for Claim 3.3.1. But the follow-on — training the restarted run on transcripts generated by the affected model — touches fn 14's "engineered by a prior model" clause (misaligned prior computations that could foreseeably transmit) and subliminal learning (traits transmitted through seemingly unrelated generated data). The report names subliminal learning as a concern and judges it unlikely from behavioural observation. An expert should note: *the final Mythos 5 was partly trained on outputs of a model that had been inadvertently trained to misbehave; behavioural checks found no carry-over.* Under the report's definitions, if any trait had carried over, it would be classed as engineered-by-prior-model only if foreseeable — the report's own disclosure arguably makes it foreseeable.

"Abundance of caution" again — here as a real cost (restarting a frontier training stage), not just a rating overlay. Different use of the same phrase from unit 25.

The dataset's purpose was good (teach self-reporting of misbehaviour — the honesty the estate cares about). The bug inverted it: a dataset meant to teach confession taught the sin. A small parable about how fragile training-for-honesty is to mechanical error.
