## What differed

Not auditing games (my prediction) — Goodharting:
- If training overfits the assessment distribution, the assessment adds nothing beyond training-data monitoring. Routes: direct contamination; frequent iteration against evals with many-degrees-of-freedom interventions; supervising CoT in training (which spends the novelty of CoT supervision at eval time).
- **"We do not train against any chains-of-thought or activations-based monitoring, with two exceptions":** (1) some SFT data from previous models' transcripts was filtered with CoT access; (2) **some RL episodes allowed reward code to see chains-of-thought** (§5.2.3). Impact unmeasured; Carroll et al. suggest monitorability impact "may not be severe"; Claim 1's covert-capability evals would include any effect.
- Public static alignment datasets: excluded via canary strings, but "we are not confident that our practices as of the coverage date reliably eliminate such data from training; see Section 5.2.6 for a recent instance in which this exclusion failed." No internal clones/near-clones of alignment evals built for training (fn 22, text not shown).
- No direct training against RL monitors, alignment audits, or deployment monitors; but evidence from them *informs* training indirectly, "in a principled way that is based on careful root-cause analysis": add data for simple behavioural issues (refusal calibration); patch root causes for reward hacks (bad reward signal, environment vulnerability); subtler issues like agentic blackmail treated more carefully (continues).

## Wandering

Unit 41 predicted it, and here it is: the doctrine "don't optimise against your monitors" with two named exceptions, one of which (reward code reading CoT) is exactly the §5.2.3 process failure. The report puts the breach inside the argument it threatens, rather than only in the incidents section. That's good practice: the reader sees the exception where it bears.

The argument that Claim 1's covert-capability evals "include any effect" of CoT supervision is sound as far as it goes: if CoT supervision had made models better at hiding things in CoT, the CoT-controllability and stealth evals would show it. They show low capability (Mythos 5 less covert than Mythos Preview, even). Unless the supervision effect is specific to contexts the evals don't cover. Reasonable.

"Indirectly inform training" — this is the subtle Goodhart channel. Each time an audit finds an issue and training is adjusted, the next model is shaped (weakly) toward passing that audit. Root-cause analysis is the stated guard: fix the cause, not the symptom. Over many generations, even principled fixes select for models that don't trip *these* detectors. The report acknowledges the channel and claims principle; it can't quantify the cumulative effect. An expert might be asked about this, and should say: acknowledged, mitigated by root-cause discipline, unmeasured.

Canary strings failing: the second named breach (§5.2.6, alignment-faking transcripts trained on repeatedly). So of the overfitting routes, two had real lapses this period. Both are disclosed. My unit-39 prediction (that alignment-faking data would bear on training-gaming detection) looks set to be confirmed.
