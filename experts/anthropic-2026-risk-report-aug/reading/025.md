# Unit 25 — §2.6 Claims and core argument (part 2), L541–550, p.26

## What differed

As predicted, nearly to the letter:
- **Claim 6:** $U_P$ "somewhat low" — mitigations give "less protection than might be intuitively expected" against pervasive misalignment, because conditioning on its presence "would undermine some important beliefs (e.g. that models have poor covert capabilities, Claim 1)".
- **Step 10:** covered risk "very low based on these claims, but in an abundance of caution we assess risk to be only low due to increased uncertainty." Each designation (K, P, C) raised very low → low "in light of recent disclosures".
- **Claim 7:** $R_{\text{total}} = R + a + b$; $a$ = risk from naturally-emerging misalignment via non-covered pathways, argued low.
- **Claim 8:** $b$ (engineered misalignment) low.
- **Step 13:** "Risk is therefore low."

## Wandering

One phrase to flag: "abundance of caution" frames the upgrade as conservatism. But step 10 and §2.1 say the reason is *uncertainty from recent disclosures* whose implications the methodology hasn't yet absorbed. Those are different: caution says "we're probably fine but rounding up"; methodology-under-revision says "our method may be missing something these incidents reveal". The report says both. An expert should report both and not let "abundance of caution" stand alone.

Claim 6's reasoning is a nice piece of conditional-probability honesty: conditioning on a surprising event (pervasive unknown misalignment) should lower your trust in the beliefs that made it surprising, including belief in your controls. Formally: $U_P$ is evaluated *conditional* on pervasive misalignment, and under that condition $P(\text{strong covert capability})$ is higher than its marginal. The report does this conditioning explicitly. Good practice, and rare.

The conclusion "low" is one rating for $R_{\text{total}}$, assembled qualitatively from $R_K, R_P, R_C, a, b$ — five terms each "low" or "very low". Sum of five lows is plausibly "low", but qualitative sums can creep: five "low"s could be "moderate". The report doesn't address aggregation of the ratings across terms. A small point an expert could raise if asked about the argument's weakest step.
