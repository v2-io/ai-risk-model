# Unit 238 — §6.1 Threat model criteria, L3030–3056, pp.175–176

- Other risk work is listed outside the RSP's scope: **Safeguards** research (child safety, political bias, suicide/self-harm, encouraging user delusions); **Economic Index** (labour); **Threat Intelligence** ("how AI models are being used in cyberattacks so that we can develop the relevant cyberdefense capabilities"); **Alignment** research (sycophancy, reward hacking, alignment faking).
- For the RSP and risk reports: focus on few risks for robust, partly generalisable safeguards.
- **Criteria:** (1) high potential damages × high likelihood — fn 89: **"expected" damages** (probability × size, summed), sometimes estimated roughly, sometimes reasoned informally; (2) a clear role for AI beyond other technologies/background conditions; (3) survive sanity checks against historical analogies (lower priority for historically rare/less damaging attacks unless AI changes the pattern); (4) additional factors: whether addressing one threat model positions them for others; whether early warning is hard (favouring prevention).

Wandering: **Cyber, finally, by the criteria's lights:** cyber appears only under Threat Intelligence ("cyberdefense capabilities"), outside the RSP threat models. Applying the stated criteria: cyber has a clear AI role (Mythos Preview's "leap forward in offensive cyber capability", unit 230); high potential damages are plausible; but criterion (3)'s historical sanity check and criterion (4)'s early-warning factor favour it less — cyberattacks have historically been costly but rarely catastrophic in the existential/COVID-plus sense, and, per §4's contrast, defences can be mounted and AI can help defenders. So cyber falls below the catastrophic bar of footnote 1 as the report applies it. That's my reconstruction from the stated criteria plus the report's contrasts; the report never applies the criteria to cyber explicitly.
