# Unit 168 — §4.4.1 How evidence is weighed, L2168–2177, p.123

Informative evidence: real-world difficult biological tasks (not multiple-choice proxies); long-horizon multi-step end-to-end projects with real-world failure stages; evaluations with expert baselines (especially rare, world-leading expertise); with/without-AI comparisons (uplift trials). Also "significant weight on subjective impressions from our internal biology experts and from expert red-teamers … gestalt expert opinions … some of the most informative signal we have … even though it is difficult to make legible."

Differed: an explicit evidence hierarchy — a methodology statement the project needs (source's own rules for weighting evidence).

Wandering: two contrasts with other sections. (1) §3.4.2 deprioritised researcher self-reports as biased; here, gestalt expert impressions are weighted heavily. Different domain, different judgement: experts eliciting bio help are judging the *model's* capability from extended adversarial use, whereas researchers in §3 judged *their own* productivity — arguably more biased. Defensible distinction; worth an expert keeping in mind if asked about consistency. (2) "Difficult to make legible" — evidence that can't be shown publicly. The CB conclusions will partly rest on illegible expert gestalt. For the project's assertion records: the warrant type "expert gestalt, illegible", recorded as such.
