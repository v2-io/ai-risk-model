# Unit 67 — Claim 5.2.2: sandbagging might be verbalised; Claim 5.3 heading, L1050–1056, p.60

- 5.2.2: "It is *possible* that strategic sandbagging would *sometimes* be verbalized and caught." Subtle sandbagging is "unique among our risk pathways in that it could plausibly be executed with very little complex reasoning" — e.g., "a policy of introducing random occasional bugs when working on code related to safety research is plausibly achievable by current models without any verbalized reasoning." But many instances are needed, so even low per-instance verbalisation probability could add up to "a moderate probability of detection".
- So Claim 5.2 is just two convergent legs: low stakes now (5.2.1) and possible verbalisation (5.2.2).
- §2.11.3 Claim 5.3 heading (irreversible threats from internal usage) — a conjunction.

Differed: the weakest-worded subclaim yet ("possible … sometimes"). And a clear statement that **Claim 1's keystone doesn't protect this pathway**: diffuse sandbagging needs no complex reasoning, so CoT monitoring and opaque-reasoning limits don't bind. The report says so itself.

Wandering: Put together, the defence against pathway 1 (diffuse sandbagging) is mostly *low current stakes* plus human skepticism — neither is a technical control. If an expert is asked "which pathway is least well defended?", the report's own words point here: no confidence in offline monitoring, Claim 1 inapplicable, mitigation by usage patterns and informal oversight. The cumulative-verbalisation argument borrows 5.1.1's logic (many attempts → some leak), which is sound if per-instance leakage isn't ~0; for "random occasional bugs", a model need never think about it at all.

"Random occasional bugs in safety-research code" — the report's own example is practically identical to exercise 5's model organism (bugs in code after several contexts). The two are in different sections. Linking them: exercise 5 shows the audit can miss exactly the behaviour Claim 5.2 can't monitor for.

Predict next: 5.3.x: irreversible threats (self-exfiltration, rogue deployment) need *blocking* controls — weight security, sandboxing, permissions, egress controls (§2.23.2), and models lack the capability to beat them.
