# Unit 175 — §4.5.1 Robustness levels, L2319–2335, p.132

(High-level register.)

- **Level 1:** essentially the previous report's classifiers, patched for specific known jailbreaks but not redesigned; still "a significant barrier to novice threat actors". Used on Opus 4 and 4.1 (deprecated; to become unavailable "later this year"), and on one hard-to-update product surface for Sonnet 4.5/4.6 and Opus 4.5/4.6 (upgrade to Level 2 expected "in the coming months").
- **Level 2:** updated Constitutional Classifiers design (January 2026 paper); two-stage (streaming linear-probe first stage → fine-tuned-model second stage); final decision a weighted combination of both stages' scores.
- **Level 3:** like Level 2 with a lower blocking threshold (more conservative) — for **Opus 4.7, Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5, Mythos Preview, Mythos 5, Fable 5** (Fable 5 also higher *coverage*).

Differed: this fills the blank cells of unit 174 — Opus 4.7, 4.8 and Mythos Preview are Level 3. My conversion note stands as a warning; the answer is here.

Wandering: the levels are defined by *design generation and threshold*, not by measured robustness against a stated adversary. "Level 3" = Level 2 architecture with a stricter threshold. So the levels are an internal engineering taxonomy, not a performance standard like RAND SL levels for security. For the project's lexicon: `anthropic:robustness-level` is design-defined; translating it to a performance claim would be an error.

Two axes now cleanly separate: robustness (how hard to get around the classifier) and coverage (what topics it watches). Fable 5 differs from Mythos 5 on coverage, not robustness — which is the operational meaning of "Mythos 5 with additional safeguards".

The legacy product surface still at Level 1 for four models: a disclosed lagging control. An expert should mention it if asked about weakest current safeguards on deployed models.
