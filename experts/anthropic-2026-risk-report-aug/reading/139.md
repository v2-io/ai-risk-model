# Unit 139 — §3.5.2 Conclusions on acceleration, L1815–1819, p.103

Leading indicators: "meaningful acceleration starting in early-to-mid 2025, though by less than a factor of 2." 2025 acceleration attributed "fairly confident[ly]" to factors other than AI use, but "our AI models have been a key factor in the faster trends *continuing* through the coverage date." Conclusion: below the RSP threshold, "uncertain and subject to some lag (such that we would have difficulty measuring very recent acceleration)."

Differed: more than the AECI plot showed — the (redacted) leading indicators *do* show meaningful acceleration since early-to-mid 2025, <2×. And AI is "a key factor in the faster trends continuing". So acceleration is real, partly AI-sustained, below threshold.

Wandering: the claim structure is delicate: acceleration started for non-AI reasons (2025), and AI keeps it going (2026). Criterion 2 requires the doubling be "substantially attributable to the automation of research and/or engineering". If the *continuation* is AI-driven, attribution gets murkier over time: the acceleration's origin wasn't AI, its maintenance is. An expert should keep both halves: started non-AI; continuing with AI as a key factor; magnitude <2×; measurement lagged.

"Difficulty measuring very recent acceleration" — the lag means the early warning may arrive late. An early-warning threshold that's measured with lag is less early. The report says this plainly.

All public readers get the conclusion; the indicators themselves are redacted. Claim-on-redacted-evidence; mark it so.
