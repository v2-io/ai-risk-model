# Unit 110 — §2.23.2.4 Sandboxing and blocking classifiers in internal deployments, L1473–1475, p.83

Most autonomous internal usage: sandboxing or blocking "auto-mode classifiers". **"Neither were evaluated against the risk pathways … and we think it's likely that covered models would be able to bypass at least one of those."** Still: slightly reduce success probability, reduce propensity for pathway-like actions in models without coherent malign goals, make undetected pursuit harder.

Differed: blunt — the blocking controls on the most autonomous internal usage are likely bypassable by covered models and weren't evaluated against the pathways. Their claimed value is modest ("slightly reduce") and mostly against *incoherent* misalignment (a model that would do something bad on impulse is stopped; a coherent one routes around).

Wandering: this sharpens the overall picture of the misalignment defence: blocking is weak and unevaluated; detection is decent per stage-1, modest end-to-end (stage 2), and relies on multiple attempts and human review of ~50/week; the probability argument relies on Claim 1. Calling the whole system "defence in depth" is fair, but each layer is individually thin against a coherent, covert model — which is why everything hinges on there being no such model now. An expert asked to summarise Anthropic's misalignment mitigations in one line could say: *thin layers, adequate for incoherent or overt misalignment; the report relies on evidence that coherent covert misalignment is absent.*

"Auto-mode classifiers" — the permission classifier in agentic tools that blocks dangerous commands without human approval. Familiar to me from the harness I run in (this session runs in a bypass-permissions mode). That's the user-side version of the same control.
