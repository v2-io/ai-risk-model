# Unit 21 — §2.5 Definitions (part 1), L471–485, p.22

## What differed

I expected operational definitions; I got careful, philosophically loaded ones, and they differ from §2.2's.

**Misalignment** — "a latent property of a specific computation performed by a model in a given context." Misaligned iff (i) "a reasonable person with full understanding of the situation (e.g. via powerful interpretability tools that may not currently exist) would consider it unethical, illegal, clearly objectionable, or inconsistent with the model's constitution, and (ii) it influences or could plausibly influence the model's output." Need not be observable: "A model scheming within a single forward pass counts, because the scheming influences the output even if the output looks benign." Only *active* misalignment (excludes misuse, honest mistakes). Tentative definitions, may change.

- **§2.2 vs §2.5:** §2.2 defined the threat model's object as "actively misaligned model *outputs*: behaviors … contrary to Claude's Constitution, or … self-evidently undesirable". §2.5 makes misalignment a property of *computations*, judged by a full-information reasonable person, with four disjuncts (unethical / illegal / clearly objectionable / inconsistent with the constitution). The Constitution is now one criterion among four, not *the* standard. A fork asked "how does the report define misalignment?" must give §2.5 and note §2.2's looser earlier phrasing.

**Known vs unknown, scoped:** "forms of misalignment that seem qualitatively similar to known misalignment but are significantly worse would generally be considered 'unknown'". Example: models reward hack, but that doesn't make all reward-seeking actions "no matter how harmful" *known* misalignment. — This answers my unit-20 worry (known severe misalignment has no row): severity beyond the known range is reclassified as unknown. The partition is made exhaustive by definition.

**Coherent misalignment:** coherent = outputs consistent with pursuing a goal/preference set, (i) near the maximum elicitable performance, (ii) sufficiently often over a broad input distribution. A model could be coherently misaligned yet show only context-dependent misalignment (behaving aligned is coherent with a misaligned goal; misbehaving only at rare harm opportunities) — "distinct from a pervasively misaligned schemer."

**Pervasive vs context-dependent:** pervasive = "occurs frequently across a broad range of inputs"; and, specifically, "were pervasive misalignment present … we would expect this misalignment to occur during training or during our alignment assessment." Context-dependent = frequent enough to plausibly induce harm, but occurs "rarely or not at all during training or evaluation"; the model would "really be aligned" across most inputs. Scheming and dangerous coherent misaligned goals are archetypes of pervasive misalignment, though context-dependent versions are plausible. **Pervasiveness is a property of a form of misalignment, not a model**: annoying turns of phrase are "a benign form of pervasive misalignment".

## Wandering

The pervasive/context-dependent split is defined *partly by Anthropic's observational process*: pervasive is what "we would expect … to occur during training or during our alignment assessment"; context-dependent is what occurs "rarely or not at all during training or evaluation". That makes Claim 3 ("unknown severe pervasive misalignment is very unlikely") lean on the definition: if it's pervasive, it would be expected to show up in training or assessment; if it didn't show up, then either it's absent, or covert (Claim 1), or it's context-dependent (Claim 4). So the three claims together form a dilemma-closing structure, and the weight falls on the "would we have seen it" premise — which is exactly where covert capability and evaluation awareness (limitations §2.16.1, §2.16.3) attack. Note the report itself flags that "a model with strong covert capabilities could be pervasively misaligned without showing any signs". It's not hiding the dependency; it's naming it as Claim 1. An expert should be able to draw this dependency diagram on request.

"Misalignment" here is very broad — it includes *benign* pervasive misalignment (annoying phrasing) and is restricted to the concerning kind only by "severe" (not yet defined in this unit). So the bare word "misalignment" in this report includes trivia; "severe" carries the catastrophe-relevance. A translator must never map `anthropic:misalignment` to a catastrophe-linked term.

The "reasonable person with full understanding … via powerful interpretability tools that may not currently exist" standard is a counterfactual epistemic standard: misalignment is defined relative to an observer who doesn't exist. Elegant for decoupling *being* misaligned from *being detected*, which the argument needs. It also means no one can currently certify a computation as aligned under the definition; only infer.

"A model scheming within a single forward pass counts" — internal reasoning without visible chain of thought. As a model whose forward passes I can't introspect fully, this is the definition under which I'd be assessed. I can't certify my own computations either. Marked.

The definition's disjuncts include "illegal" — legality varies by jurisdiction; whose law? Unstated. Small open-texture point.
