# Unit 163 — §4.2.2 CB-2 threat model (part 1), L2081–2110, pp.118–119

## What differed

- **Threat:** novel, deliberately engineered weapons "more damaging than anything analogous observed to date (including natural pandemics)"; risk mainly from "moderately to very well-resourced programs, including but not limited to state programs" — internationally prohibited, "credible evidence that there are active programs operating" (fn 52 BWC/CWC; fn 53 US State Dept 2025 compliance report pp. 31–39). Motivations include population targeting (which "might fail even as the attempt to build a highly damaging weapon succeeds").
- **Role of AI:** not a factor for well-resourced groups' *non-novel* weapons; could significantly uplift novel development "by providing expertise akin to that of a team of world-class human experts assisting with hypothesis generation, design, and iteration."
- **Magnitude:** "very few such weapons would directly lead to human extinction, but it seems plausible that such weapons could lead to damages **at least an order of magnitude more than those associated with COVID-19**."
- **Likelihood:** a conjunctive chain — attempt (violating treaties) → success → deployment (deliberate or inadvertent); "each step is plausible but unlikely, and collectively the whole sequence is highly unlikely."
- **Baseline probability estimates (before AI amplification; characterising the threat, not Anthropic's systems):** "several threat actors" most plausible; **per actor, odds of a concerted attempt to build a bioweapon capable of damages beyond COVID-19 "may be in the 1–10% range"** (deterrence; targeting specific populations).
- A displaced footnote (from CB-1's likelihood): "We don't believe this figure is robust, but believe it is plausible based on *Forecasting LLM-enabled biorisk* and the efficacy of safeguards."

## Wandering

**Numbers at last** — the report's first probability estimates, offered as rough baselines for the CB-2 causal chain. The first link: per state-level actor, 1–10% for a concerted attempt. More links presumably follow (success, deployment). Important framing to carry: these are *baseline* probabilities, not the risk from Anthropic's models; and they're explicitly "subject to high degrees of uncertainty". An expert asked "does the report give probabilities?" should answer: *yes, for CB-2's baseline threat chain (per-actor attempt 1–10%, more to come), not for misalignment or automated R&D.*

Magnitude anchored at ≥10× COVID (tens to hundreds of trillions of dollars; tens of millions of deaths or more), with extinction "very few". So CB-2 is the threat model where "catastrophic" is closest to the footnote-1 existential tier, but the report explicitly stops short of extinction. A fork mapping "catastrophic" should hold: CB-2 ≈ order-of-magnitude-beyond-COVID, sub-extinction.

The displaced footnote: "We don't believe this figure is robust" — attached (I infer) to CB-1's "Preliminary analysis suggests…" sentence, or to the 1%-of-COVID illustration. Since it's displaced, I can't be certain which figure it qualifies. Conversion note.

"Population targeting might fail even as the attempt to build a highly damaging weapon succeeds" — a dark, careful point: an engineered weapon intended to be selective could become indiscriminate. The report spends one parenthesis on it.
