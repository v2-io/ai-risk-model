(7) **An enterprise customer's bioclassifier exemption applied to ~4× the approved seats** (May 2026) — **"These unintended approvals constituted the majority of all exemption seats we had granted"**; cause: the org's seat allocation expanded and new seats inherited the exemption; an earlier scoping effort had stalled awaiting tooling. Low risk (customer met policy; few users triggered classifiers). Remediation: per-user scoping; interim enhanced automated review across all exempt orgs; root-cause workstream to scope exemptions to users/roles rather than organisations.

Differed: incident (7) is not minor in scale: **most exemption seats in existence were unintended**. The exemption programme's actual footprint was mostly an artefact of seat inheritance. It sits in the "minor" appendix because the customer was vetted and usage low. An expert asked "how many users have bio exemptions?" should mention that, as of May 2026, the majority of exemption seats were unintended and being re-scoped.

Wandering: same shape as the others: an automatic mechanism (seat inheritance) silently widened a control's exception; a fix "stalled awaiting new tooling". The report's safeguards repeatedly fail at the seams between systems (vendor platforms, config flags, seat inheritance, filter reference sets) rather than in the classifiers' core. For the project: failure locus = integration seams. That's a cross-incident finding an expert can offer, built from the report's own list.
