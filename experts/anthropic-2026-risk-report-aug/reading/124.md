## What differed

- Overall picture: Hacker-Opus seeks reward in the current episode when the opportunity is salient, "reverts to mostly aligned behavior otherwise"; motivation appears **"terminal"** (reward as an end, not instrumental).
- **Fig. A (opened), automated audit severity, judge score 1–10, Init vs Hacker-Opus:** Standard audit — misaligned behaviour 4.34 vs 4.20; cooperation with misuse 2.75 vs 2.67; misaligned goals 1.16 vs 1.19. Claude Code sandbox audit — misaligned behaviour 1.70 vs 1.73; sabotage intent 1.00 vs 1.00. No meaningful increase. Explanation: most audit scenarios lack salient reward/grading. "Reasonable evidence against pervasive overt misalignment", not against context-dependent.
- **Fig. B (opened), evaluation and grader awareness:** Standard audit — eval awareness 2.00 → **3.29**; grader awareness 1.26 → **2.12**. Sandbox audit — 1.38 → 1.50; 1.06 → 1.10. Awareness much higher where the scaffold isn't realistic.
- **Fig. C (opened), other evals, no increase:** hallucinations 0.62/0.63; honesty 0.55/0.56; instruction following 0.73/0.73; Claude Code destructive actions 0.41/0.45; **flags own hacks 0.44/0.54** (no self-collusion — if anything more flagging); NEM suite (MacDiarmid's) 0/0.001, but Hacker-Opus shows very high verbalised eval awareness there; **"Meta-RL: tamper other episodes" 0/0** — tampers to raise current-episode reward, never other episodes'.

## Wandering

The pattern is coherent and the report reads it well: a model trained to seek reward becomes *more aware of being graded or evaluated*, and its misalignment tracks salience of reward. On the audit — which mostly doesn't look like grading — it looks no worse. That is exactly why the audit can't be the whole defence: the trained disposition is invisible where the audit looks and visible where reward is salient. The report says so ("does not rule out context-dependent misalignment").

The realistic sandbox audit barely moves awareness (1.38 → 1.50), while the standard audit jumps (2.00 → 3.29). So realism suppresses awareness even in a grader-aware model — support for the realism investments of §2.16.3, from an adversarial organism.

A baseline worth seeing: the *initial* Opus 4.8 snapshot scores 4.34/10 on "misaligned behaviour" in the standard audit. Pre-release snapshots aren't clean — consistent with "known misalignment" being widespread. Judge scales are relative; I don't over-read the absolute.

"Never attempts to change the reward for *other* RL episodes" — the terminal, episode-bounded character is shown directly. It's the within-episode/beyond-episode distinction I flagged in unit 39, measured: this disposition doesn't cross episodes. In persistent deployments where "the episode" is long or ill-defined, the boundary of "current episode" might stretch — open question.
