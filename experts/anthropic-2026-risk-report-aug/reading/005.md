- **Marginal vs absolute risk.** Each overall assessment addresses (a) "marginal" risk: over and above other developers' systems; and (b) "absolute" risk across the industry: the risk "if all AI developers had models and practices similar to ours". I did not predict this two-number structure. Note the peculiar definition of "absolute": it's not the actual industry-wide risk; it's a counterfactual — a world where everyone behaves like Anthropic. It measures the adequacy of Anthropic's *practices as a standard*, not the world's risk. Calling that "absolute" is a term-of-art choice a careless reader will misread.
- **"Connection to our recommendations for industry-wide safety"** — the RSP 3.x apparently contains recommendations for the whole industry, and each section compares the situation to them.
- Each section's template: threat model (pathways, likelihood, magnitude) → relevant models → capabilities & behaviours (drawn from system cards) → mitigations → overall assessment (marginal + absolute) → looking forward → connection to industry recommendations.

## Wandering

The marginal-risk framing is the crux of the frontier-lab justification: "if we didn't build it, someone else would, and our marginal contribution is small." Recording it neutrally: the report's own risk measure (a) is *relative to other developers' systems*, so a world where all labs are dangerous makes each lab's marginal risk small. Measure (b) partially answers that objection — it asks whether Anthropic-like practice would be safe if universal. Together they're a reasonably complete frame, *if* (b) is assessed honestly. An expert should be asked: "In each section, what is the marginal and what the absolute risk assessment, and do they differ?"

For the project's causal chain: marginal risk is a counterfactual baseline — the same kind of element as SB 53's counterfactual baseline in its catastrophic-risk definition. Two very different baselines: SB 53 (I believe, from CLAUDE.md) compares to what's available without the model; Anthropic compares to other developers' systems. Both "marginal", different comparators.

"Plain meaning" is interesting. In law, plain meaning is a canon of construction; here it's used to avoid committing to a threshold. A plain-meaning "catastrophic" that is exemplified by existential threats has a high floor but vague edges.

The existence of "separate compliance frameworks" means there are at least two Anthropic bounded contexts for "catastrophic risk": the RSP/Risk Report one (plain meaning) and the SB 53 compliance framework one (statutory). The project's lexicon should keep `anthropic-rsp:catastrophic-risk` and the SB 53 framework's sense apart.
