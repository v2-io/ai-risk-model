## Wandering

**The second stage is where recall is lost.** The first-stage monitors catch most things; the false-positive filter and summariser that feed the ~50/week human review drop a lot: model organisms 3/3 → 1/3; synthetic CoTs 72% → 29%; METR attacks 94% → 79%. The false-positive rate drops ~185-fold (0.74% → 0.004%), which is why the stage exists — humans can only look at ~50 a week. This is the precision/recall trade imposed by human review bandwidth. The *end-to-end* recall (what reaches a human) is the right number for Claim 5.1.3, and for model organisms it's 1 in 3. Claim 5.1.1's "many attempts" argument is what makes 1/3-per-attempt still catch one of many. An expert should give stage-2 numbers when asked "how good is Anthropic's monitoring?", and explain that per-attempt end-to-end recall is modest and the argument relies on multiple attempts.

The 0.004% false-positive rate after stage 2, on internal traffic: if internal traffic is millions of transcripts a week, 0.004% is tens to hundreds — consistent with ~50 reviewed. So human attention is the binding constraint, and it sets the stage-2 threshold. More reviewers would allow higher recall. A capacity-limited control; for the project, the control's effectiveness is bounded by a staffing variable.

"Considered and then rejected within the extended thinking" — the monitor misses misaligned intent that the model entertains then drops. Good for false positives (the model didn't act), bad for detecting a model that *rehearses* misaligned plans. Which matters more depends on whether rejected plans predict later action.

METR's red-team set (n=163) is from attacks against the *old* 4.6 monitor; 4.8's 94% on those is partly "trained on the test" in the loose sense (it's the new monitor's known-attack recall). The report says 4.8 has been red-teamed less. Hold.
