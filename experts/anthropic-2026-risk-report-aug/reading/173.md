# Unit 173 — §4.4.4 CB-2 evidence for Opus 4.8 and other models, L2253–2277, pp.128–129

(High-level register, as in 171.)

- Evidence from system cards on Opus 4.8, 4.7, 4.6 (Table 4.4.4.A): expert red-teaming scores mostly in the middle of the uplift scale, none at the top ("world-leading expert" level); uplift trials not uniformly concerning; benchmark results mixed, with some older models outperforming Opus 4.8 on the subsets the report thinks most threat-relevant.
- Conclusion by **dominance**: these models are "significantly less capable at risk-relevant tasks" than Mythos-class models, which fall short of CB-2 → "fairly high confidence" they don't cross it either.
- But, as for Mythos-class: more uncertainty about *expert uplift* than about *substitution*, and **"the expert uplift provided by Opus 4.6 and later models is slightly higher than what we concluded in the system cards for those models at the time"** (improved elicitation and red-teaming; §4.6.2).

Differed: a retrospective upward revision of older models' expert-uplift estimates. Release-time system-card judgements were too low, by the report's own later evidence.

Wandering: this is a pattern worth an expert's attention across the whole report: later evidence revises earlier risk judgements *upward* (misalignment very low → low; CB-1 up; expert uplift of Opus 4.6+ up), never (so far) downward — except the Hong RCT's modest downward update for Opus 4/4.1 (unit 170). That asymmetry fits a world where elicitation improves over time, so release-time evals are systematically lower bounds. For the project: a *revision* relation between assessments, with direction, is worth recording; the Risk Report is partly a vehicle for revising system cards.

Opus 4.8 performing worse than older models on some threat-relevant subsets — non-monotonic capability across versions in specific domains, perhaps from safety training or data changes. Same non-monotonicity seen with Mythos 5 vs Mythos Preview elsewhere.
