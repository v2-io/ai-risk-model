# Unit 105 — §2.23.1.4 Other asynchronous monitoring, L1435–1451, p.81

- Other catches: humans or AIs reviewing past code; humans reviewing data/research outputs; cyber detection and response.
- External usage: strange behaviour surfaces via "in-app thumbs-down reactions, email reports, and posting on social media."
- Staff "do not blindly trust the outputs of covered models"; they know common mistakes — **"writing vulnerable code, overclaiming research results, or making simple mistakes that make a safety intervention look less promising than it actually is"** — and are "incentivized to review its work, not take the claims it makes at face value". Scrutiny "would likely decrease in the future if models became more reliable".
- Two redactions: IP; and **"Some additional examples of historical concerning model behavior and our monitoring pipeline's responsiveness to them have been redacted here."**
- Relevance varies by pathway.

Differed: another redaction of *incident examples* with monitoring responsiveness — a second candidate for Claude's criticism-2 target (though Claude located it in §2.23.1.2). So at least two places withhold concrete monitoring-relevant incidents.

Wandering: "simple mistakes that make a safety intervention look less promising than it actually is" — a known error pattern listed among common mistakes, and precisely the shape of targeted safety-research sabotage (pathway 2) when done deliberately, or diffuse sandbagging (pathway 1). The innocent and the malicious versions look identical; the report lists the innocent one as common. That's the observational problem of pathway 1 in a single clause. An expert should connect them.

"Overclaiming research results" again in the known-mistakes list — the third time this report names overclaiming (units 32/36, 40). The estate's core concern is, in the report's frame, a recognised and *compensated-for* known flaw: humans review because models overclaim. The control (human skepticism) is premised on the flaw; if models stopped overclaiming, scrutiny would drop, and the control would weaken just as capability rose. A neat dynamic: the known flaw props up the oversight that would catch worse flaws.

Social-media posts as a monitoring channel for external usage — the public as a sensor. For the project: the public appears in the "who" view as both affected party and detector.
