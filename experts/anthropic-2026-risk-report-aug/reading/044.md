# Unit 44 — Claim 3.4.1 conclusion, L849–851, p.47

Monitored internal usage evidence is "especially strong": several months across comparable models (Mythos Preview → Mythos 5 → Model 2), "a stronger body of evidence than we might typically have … at the time of initial release." Three-part coverage: breadth (general audit), specificity/realism (targeted subversion scenarios), on-distribution volume (internal usage) → "we would likely elicit pervasive misalignment were it to be present" + strong oversight & limited covert capabilities → likely detect it.

Differed: no results for the 86 scenarios here (the System Card presumably has them). The report argues the *coverage* of the assessment, not its findings, at this point; findings are implicitly "nothing beyond Claim 2".

Wandering: the timing point is genuinely important and easy to miss: because Mythos 5 and Model 2 follow months of Mythos Preview internal use, this risk report has *longitudinal* evidence that a release-time system card can't. That's a structural argument for periodic company-wide reports over per-model cards. An expert asked "why does Anthropic do Risk Reports as well as system cards?" can cite this along with §1's reasons.

"Elicit" vs "detect": the claim splits into (a) the assessment would *elicit* (provoke) pervasive misalignment if present — coverage; and (b) monitoring would *detect* it — oversight + no covert capability. Two distinct premises; (a) is attacked by eval awareness/selective action, (b) by covert capability.
