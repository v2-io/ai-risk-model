# Unit 8 — §1.3.1 Updated threshold for automation of AI R&D, L299–317, p.13

## What differed

My guess (a ~2x acceleration criterion) landed on half of it. Changed twice (v3.1 and v3.4); the table compares v3.0 with the current text only.

- **v3.0:** "trigger this risk threshold at the point where we determine that a model could compress two years of 2018–2024 AI progress into a single year." A model-capability criterion ("a model could").
- **Current (v3.4):** met if either
  1. "our models would be able to fully substitute for our entire set of Research Scientists and Research Engineers, at competitive costs (i.e., within a factor of 5)"; or
  2. "dramatic acceleration" of AI progress "for reasons that likely relate to the automation of AI R&D", operationalised as (a) observing *or expecting* "double the rate of progress in AI aggregate capabilities compared to both the rate we'd expect and the fastest rate of extended progress [we've] observed in the absence of significant AI contributions to AI R&D" [fn 4: "Over at least three model generations."] **and** (b) it is "plausible" the doubling is "substantially attributable to the automation of research and/or engineering (as opposed to … headcount, compute, or general productivity), such that continuation of the trend … seems likely to lead to even greater acceleration."
- **Footnote 5 defines "double the rate of progress"**: "as much progress in one year as one would see in two years at baseline". Worked example: 3× compute × 3× algorithmic efficiency = 9× effective scaleup at baseline → doubling means ~81× in a year. And: "not the same idea as 'doubling researchers' productivity,' since doubling inputs does not necessarily double the rate of progress."

## Wandering

The shift is from *a model could* (counterfactual capability) to *we observe or expect* (an observed-world trend) plus *our models would be able to substitute* for staff. Criterion (2) is now partly an *outcome* criterion — acceleration in the world, attributed to automation — rather than a pre-deployment capability test. Outcome thresholds trigger after the fact by design; the "or expect" softens that, letting a forecast trigger it.

Criterion (1) is striking: full substitution for *the entire set* of Anthropic's research staff, within 5× cost. That's a very high bar, and an *Anthropic-relative* one — it's indexed to Anthropic's own workforce. Two labs with different staff would have different thresholds. For the lexicon: `anthropic:ai-rd-threshold` is internally indexed, not a universal capability level.

The footnote-5 arithmetic is the kind of precision a summary loses: "double the rate of progress" in *log* space — 9× per year becomes 81× per year, not 18×. A reader who thinks "2× speedup" means 18× would underestimate the bar by a factor of 4.5 per year. An expert should be asked: "What exactly does 'double the rate of progress' mean in the RSP?" and answer with the 81× example.

"At least three model generations" for the baseline window — so the baseline is the fastest rate sustained over three generations without significant AI contribution. Which generations count as "without significant AI contributions" is a judgment the report will have to make in §3.5 ("AECI capability trajectory" — I'd now guess AECI = something like "AI Effective Compute Index" or an aggregate capability index).

Was the change a loosening or tightening? v3.0's "compress two years into one" is roughly the same as "double the rate"; but v3.0 compared to 2018–2024 progress, while v3.4 compares to *the fastest* extended rate and *the expected* rate — the higher of two baselines, which raises the bar. And it adds condition (b), attribution, which also raises the bar. So on its face v3.4 is harder to trigger than v3.0, while (1) is a new alternative route. The report itself doesn't (yet) characterise the direction. Worth an expert being asked directly, and worth noting that this is my reading, not the report's.
