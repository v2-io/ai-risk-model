# Unit 170 — §4.4.2.1 RCT on uplift (Hong et al. 2026), L2191–2206, p.124

- **Hong et al. (2026)** RCT: novice uplift from the mid-2025 public LLM frontier (incl. Opus 4/4.1, no blocking bio classifiers); real lab tasks over several weeks. Finding: "mid-2025 LLMs did not substantially increase novice completion of complex laboratory procedures but were associated with a modest performance benefit."
- More reflective of CB-1 real-world uplift than Anthropic's own system-card evals.
- Caveats: participants with minimal lab experience (lower than some threat actors); **post-hoc ~1.42× uplift on a "typical" task, wide error bars**; moderate LLM use (only one participant >1M tokens); **underpowered — 36% power to detect an odds ratio of 2.0**.
- Update: Opus 4/4.1 "likely to pose somewhat lower risk than we worried" when stringent safeguards were first enabled; classifiers stay, but this affects prioritisation of further safeguard improvements.

Differed: external, real-world RCT evidence — and the report weighs it fairly, listing caveats that cut both ways. The update is modest and specific to Opus 4/4.1 (mid-2025 models), not to Mythos-class.

Wandering: an expert must carry the power caveat: 36% power to detect a doubling means the null result is weak evidence against substantial uplift; the 1.42× post-hoc estimate is a plausible effect the study couldn't confirm. And the models tested are a year old relative to Mythos 5 — the evidence speaks to the CB-1 era, not the CB-2 question.

The resource-allocation consequence is interesting and honest: weak evidence of low uplift for older models → less effort improving their safeguards. A real decision driven by an external study, disclosed.

For the project: Hong et al. 2026 is an external primary source worth cataloguing; this report's reading of it (with caveats) is the Anthropic context's interpretation.
