# Unit 11 — §1.3.4 Redaction scope and transparency, L349–353, p.15

## What differed

- Redaction governance is about **internal** audiences, not external reviewers. RSP v3.4: (a) publicly disclose, at a high level, when redactions are made; (b) fully unredacted reports shared with **"at least 200 Anthropic employees"** — *rather than* all regular-clearance staff, as before. A narrowing of internal access, disclosed as such and justified by sensitivity and "the company's ongoing growth".
- Practice this time: all staff got a "minimally-redacted" version whose only redactions are in §3.5 (AI R&D process, "internally compartmentalized and commercially sensitive"). Public redactions to be "noted below".
- No government or external reviewer named yet.

## Wandering

The unredacted-readership rule is a governance control whose function is internal accountability: many eyes inside the company. Going from "all regular-clearance staff" to "at least 200" weakens that control as written, while the practice (minimal redaction to all staff) kept most of it. The project's distinction between a *commitment* and a *practice* applies: the commitment loosened; the practice reportedly held. An expert should keep the two apart when asked "who can see the full report?".

Two of the redacted things (§6.2, §6.3) are whole appendices; §3.5 is redacted even internally in part. "Commercially sensitive" sits beside safety-sensitive as a grounds for redaction — a reason category worth noting neutrally.
