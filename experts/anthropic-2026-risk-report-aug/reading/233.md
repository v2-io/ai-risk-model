# Unit 233 — §5.4 Overall risk assessment and risk-benefit determination, L3003–3012, p.173

As of the coverage date:
- **Autonomy risks low** ("likely our arguments support … 'very low' for alignment"). "Because of our models' relatively weak covert capabilities, we believe they lack the behavioral propensities—as well as the capabilities—to present substantial risk of catastrophic harm."
- **CB risks low**: safeguards make persistent significant uplift hard for low-resource actors; better-resourced actors may manage it but get less uplift; "significant room for debate".
- **Likely contributing to industry acceleration** (others using models for R&D; distillation); safeguards improved.
- **Benefits**: leadership on information sharing, policy advocacy, risk-reduction measures, beneficial deployments — "major benefits".
- **"Our view is that these net out to our continued AI development and deployment to date passing a societal cost-benefit test."**

Differed: the final determination is a *societal cost-benefit test*, passed, for development and deployment "to date". Not a prospective licence.

Wandering: **a reasoning slip in the summary.** "Because of our models' relatively weak covert capabilities, we believe they lack the behavioral propensities" — weak covert capability doesn't imply absent propensities; §2's actual argument was that weak covert capability makes propensities *detectable*, and detection found no severe ones (Claims 1 and 3). The summary compresses an evidential chain into a causal-sounding "because" that the body doesn't support. The body's version is the one to quote. An expert should flag this as summary-vs-body drift (the third I've noted: units 83, 128).

Also absent from this summary: the forecasts (AI R&D threshold may be crossed within a year; CB-2 by near-future models) and the admitted gaps (no "eyes on everything" until Jan 2027; no state-robust security in time). The determination is about "to date"; the forward-looking warnings live in §§3.8–3.9, 4.7–4.8. A reader of §5.4 alone would miss that the report expects both major thresholds to be reached soon with mitigations not fully ready. An expert must carry both: *passes the test to date; forecasts near-term threshold crossings with acknowledged gaps.*

"Societal cost-benefit test" is the report's top-level normative claim, made by the party whose continued operation it justifies. The report earlier disclosed this (§5.3 interest framing). For the project: the source's terminal assertion, recorded with its role (self-assessment by an interested party) and its scope ("to date").
