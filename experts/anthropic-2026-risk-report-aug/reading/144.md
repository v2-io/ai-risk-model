# Unit 144 — §3.6.3 Biotechnology, L1855–1867, pp.105–106

7 interviews (synbio, drug development, biotech investment, immunology, genomics, biosecurity, protein design). Non-LLM ML (structure prediction) transforming protein design; LLMs save time (analysis, literature, experiment setup, figures); LLMs as orchestrators of partly automated labs, early. Not yet significant breakthroughs; reliable lab robotics the bottleneck; drug-to-patient timelines (9–12, 10–15 years) unaffected; in vivo validation and trials bottleneck; one: no shown impact on speed, cost, headcount. **One dissenter**: AI already capable of automating full teams, citing **Chinese automated-synthesis operations and academic virtual-lab work**; boost ~2–5× by workflow. Next two years critical; robots doing ~25% of biology experiments reliably ~a year away.

Differed: a named dissent, with a geopolitical example (Chinese automated synthesis). The report records the minority view rather than dropping it. Good practice; for the project, a minority assertion inside a "litmus test".

Wandering: this domain links directly to §4 (CB weapons). The CB threat model concerns uplift to *actors*; this section concerns acceleration of the *field*. Lab automation (robots doing 25% of experiments within a year; LLM orchestration of labs) is exactly what would erode the "wet-lab bottleneck" that CB-2's "functional substitution for scarce expertise" relies on — the end-to-end pipeline in the CB-2 threshold includes "verification and validation, formulation" (unit 9), which are lab tasks. If lab robotics advance as these interviewees expect, the CB-2 threshold's physical components could be met by automation rather than expertise.
