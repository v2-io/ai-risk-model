Wandering: "Correct any severely mistaken conclusions" assumes the conclusions are traceable to the undermined work. In research, a wrong conclusion gets cited, built on, absorbed into intuitions and decisions (e.g., a flawed eval result that justified a deployment). Reversal of *beliefs* across an organisation is harder than reverting code. The reversibility premise is strongest for artifacts (code, data) and weakest for conclusions. The report treats them alike here. An expert could note that reversibility of epistemic harm is assumed, not argued.
