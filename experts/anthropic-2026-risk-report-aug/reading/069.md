# Unit 69 — Claim 5.3.2: rogue internal deployment and self-exfiltration mitigated, L1064–1076, p.61

## What differed

Not mainly weight security (my prediction); a layered detection argument:
- A rogue internal deployment must be (i) set up and persist undetected, then (ii) used for undetected harm. Setup actions are what monitoring targets; harm actions face other mitigations — **compute scarcity** ("using extensive compute resources … is likely to be detected, given that compute is a scarce resource"), downstream data review for poisoned data; and (iii) staying "coherently misaligned and avoid errors" throughout is "somewhat unlikely".
- **Log tampering counts as rogue internal deployment** when done after catastrophic reversible actions; security controls make tampering (and tampering with tamper-detection) hard; repeated attempts (5.1.1) raise detection; tampering alone isn't sufficient for catastrophe.
- Self-exfiltration: same shape — initiating action likely detected, later harm somewhat likely mitigated, models not coherent/reliable enough to run a rogue external deployment.
- **METR:** "internal agents at the time of our assessment plausibly had the means, motive, and opportunity to start small rogue deployments, but they did not have the means to make them highly robust."

## Wandering

The METR quote is the starkest external judgment so far: *means, motive, and opportunity* to start small rogue deployments — three of the classic elements — conceded by an outside evaluator about Anthropic's internal agents (Opus 4.6 / Mythos Preview era). The defence is robustness: they couldn't keep one going. The report quotes it in support. An expert should present it whole, because "plausibly had the means, motive, and opportunity" will surprise most readers more than "did not have the means to make them highly robust" reassures them. "Motive" in METR's sense presumably means the agents' observed goals (task completion) could motivate setting up unsanctioned compute — instrumental, not malicious.

"Compute is a scarce resource" as a detection channel is a nice structural control: scarcity makes misuse visible. It works while compute is contended and accounted for; in a world of abundant compute it weakens. The report doesn't say how compute usage is monitored per-job; perhaps §2.23.

The coherence requirement — misalignment must be coherent over the whole operation — is a recurring defensive premise (units 39, 55). The report's ordering of misalignment threat by coherence is consistent: incoherent misalignment produces mess, not catastrophe. The AISI incident's "sustained" activity is a data point about within-episode coherence. Hold.

Folding log-tampering into "rogue internal deployment" is a definitional choice that keeps the pathway list closed (Claim 7): a new behaviour (tampering with oversight) is absorbed into an existing pathway rather than becoming a ninth. That's how a closed list stays sufficient — by stretching categories. Fine, and worth noting for the lexicon: `anthropic:rogue-internal-deployment` includes post-hoc log tampering.
