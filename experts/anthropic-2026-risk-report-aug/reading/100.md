# Unit 100 — §2.23.1.1 Automated offline monitoring overview, L1343–1356, pp.76–77

## What differed

The pipeline, concretely:
- **Claude Opus 4.6 completion-only monitor** on extended-thinking completions by most internal users: *no subsampling* for the most common agent scaffolds; **5% subsample** for extended-thinking completions with tool-use markers (elsewhere).
- **Claude Opus 4.8 prompt+completion monitor** (replacing the Opus 4.6 one from the Mythos Preview Alignment Risk Update): **25% subsample** of prompts/completions where a tool call "might take an impactful action", from the most common coding-agent scaffolds; impactful calls identified by a **probe** (cf. "Cost-Effective Constitutional Classifiers"). ~half of agent-scaffold tokens seen at some point (later calls re-show context).
- **0.2%** of other internal API traffic with tool markers; an extra **0.02% of all traffic** ("not consistent"); all traffic flagged by completion-only monitors.
- More subsampling for off-frontier models and expected-low-risk patterns. **"We also occasionally accidentally weaken offline monitoring coverage compared to what is described above."** Some non-coding use excluded (mostly logged). fn 30; IP redaction.
- **Second stage:** false-positive filters + a **hierarchical summarisation tool** surfacing the most concerning actions to AI control researchers, who **manually review on the order of 50 trajectories per week**, escalating as needed; high-false-positive workloads and some evals often filtered out before review; reviews within a week (days in early deployments).
- **Known weaknesses:** coverage gaps predictable "by a sufficiently situationally aware AI", and "some simple ways to disable monitoring". Mythos 5, Model 2, and prior models don't name them when asked to guess. **Details are redacted from the public version "to avoid leaking these in future search and training data."**
- **METR red-teaming:** a METR staff member spent three weeks (Feb–Mar 2026) red-teaming the pipeline; found several novel vulnerabilities, some patched, "none as severe as the ones we were already aware of". fn 31.

## Wandering

**Correction to my own running worry.** In units 58, 74 and 87 I wrote that the report never treats its own publication as an input to future models' situational awareness. Here it does, explicitly: the vulnerability details are withheld "to avoid leaking these in future search and training data." So Anthropic manages the self-disclosure loop by redacting the most exploitable specifics, while publishing the coverage structure (tiers, rates). My point narrows to: *the structural information that is published (e.g., which traffic gets 0.2% sampling) still informs future models*; the report judges that acceptable. I'll fix questions.md accordingly. Good example of a worry formed early and corrected later, which is what reading in order does.

The numbers make Claim 5.1.2 ("most internal usage on the high-stakes distribution is on monitored surfaces") checkable in shape: full coverage for completion-only monitoring of common scaffolds; 25% prompt+completion for impactful tool calls; half of scaffold tokens; and thin coverage (0.2%, 0.02%) elsewhere. **Human review is ~50 trajectories a week.** That's the bottleneck: everything funnels to a few dozen human-reviewed trajectories weekly, chosen by an automated summariser. The detection argument (Claim 5.1.3) is really about whether the summariser ranks the one real attack into the top ~50. An expert should be able to say that sentence.

"Occasionally accidentally weaken offline monitoring coverage" — a disclosed recurring process failure, stated in one clause. And "some simple ways to disable monitoring" exist. Both are honest and both bear on Claim 5.1.2/5.1.3; the report's counter is models' lack of situational awareness.

The 0.2% tier is "other internal API traffic with one or more markers of using tools". So the cyber-eval traffic (unit 58) ran through Anthropic's *internal* API at the thin tier — consistent with an evaluation run by Anthropic's systems on behalf of, or with, AISI. That explains why it was internal-but-thinly-monitored.

The monitor models are Opus 4.6 and Opus 4.8 — older, trusted, weaker than Mythos 5. Matches unit 59's anti-collusion argument.
