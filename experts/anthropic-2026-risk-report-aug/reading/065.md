# Unit 65 — §2.11.2 Claim 5.2 opening: diffuse sandbagging, L1042–1044, p.60

Argued "primarily on usage patterns and informal oversight, because we are not confident that such sandbagging would be detected by offline monitoring." Convergent combination (items follow).

Differed: the admission comes first — the automated monitor isn't trusted for diffuse sandbagging. Consistent with unit 41 (the training monitor misses "free points"/partial-task hacks) and unit 48 (the audit missed passive research sandbagging that looked like weak capability).

Wandering: diffuse sandbagging is the pathway where the report's main machinery (transcript monitoring of discrete harmful acts) doesn't apply: there's no single bad act, just slightly worse work everywhere. Defences must be statistical or human: comparing outputs to expected quality, cross-model comparison, researchers noticing. And known misalignment (apparent-success-seeking, smaller/easier versions of tasks — METR, unit 32) already *looks* like mild diffuse sandbagging, without intent. Distinguishing intentional diffuse sandbagging from known laziness/overclaiming may be impossible from outputs. The report's Claim 2 treats the latter as low catastrophic harm; for safety research it's the differential-harm worry.

Predict next: items — (a) humans review and use most safety-research outputs closely; (b) safety research involves many models and humans, so one model's sandbagging is diluted; (c) cross-checking with other models/older models would reveal underperformance; (d) the harm (slower safety research) is gradual and recoverable.
