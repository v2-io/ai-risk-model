# Unit 172 — CB-2: magnitude of expert uplift, L2251, p.128

An unsafeguarded Mythos-class model would mainly act as a "force-multiplier" of existing expertise. In one case, experts "very roughly estimated that the odds might double relative to threat actors' chances without any AI assistance" — explicitly not an upper bound, lower bound, or central estimate; "very high uncertainty".

Differed: a quantitative AI-amplification figure, tied back to the §4.2.2 chain: the "success" link (baseline 1–10%) might roughly double, in one case, *for an unsafeguarded model used by an already-expert team*.

Wandering: this is where the baseline chain (units 163–164) meets the model. If the success link doubled while attempt and release stayed fixed, the baseline per-decade range (1/50 to 1/20,000) would roughly double for actors with access to an unsafeguarded model. The report doesn't do that multiplication; I note it as the structural implication, not a claim of the report. And the key qualifier is *unsafeguarded*: the risk assessment then turns on whether safeguards (classifiers, access controls, weight security) prevent such access — which is why §4.5 is the long part of this section.

For the project: an AI-amplification factor (~2× on one link, one case, unsafeguarded, very uncertain) — a distinct assertion type from the threshold judgement, and the bridge from capability to risk.
