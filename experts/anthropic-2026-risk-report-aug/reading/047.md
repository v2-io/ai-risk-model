## Wandering

The overrefusal-on-safety-research example connects three threads: §5.2.2 ("partial refusals on safety work undermining stress-testing research"), Claim 2's differential-harm worry, and the proxy-misalignment category (biased data → misgeneralisation from stylistic features). A safety-motivated dataset caused refusal of safety research — a control producing a hazard to another control. And the fix avoided training on the very distribution (safety research requests) that the eval measures — the held-out discipline in practice.

The train/val/test framing is a clean, citable methodology statement; the project's "methodology recorded with the source" requirement would capture it as Anthropic's *alignment mitigation methodology*. Its integrity rests on the outer layer staying held out. But the outer layer's *findings* seed val sets, so information does flow from test to training — via humans, filtered by root-cause analysis. It's held out from optimisation, not from influence. The report is explicit about that distinction; a summariser might flatten it to "never influences training", which would be false.

The "cancellation" risk — alignment that depends on one part of training correcting another part's push toward misalignment — is an elegant, rarely-stated failure mode: fragile alignment by balance. The report asserts it didn't occur, with a footnote I haven't seen. The assertion is unevidenced in-unit.
