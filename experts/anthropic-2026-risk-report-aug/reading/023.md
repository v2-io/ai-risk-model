# Unit 23 — §2.5 Definitions (part 3), L505–517, p.24

## What differed

Covert capabilities still not defined (maybe in §2.7). This unit:

- fn 15 (on "expected future harm"): "Intended in the sense of expected value."
- **Unmitigated:** safeguards can mitigate harm-inducing outputs by blocking, or by "detecting it with offline monitoring and mitigating the harm later". A computation is unmitigated "if its harm-inducing effects are realized without mitigation."
- **Misalignment risk (the quantity the section estimates):** "the expected total unmitigated harm induced by misaligned computations produced by covered models." An expectation, over covered models only.
- **Prior models:** by default, Anthropic models behind the frontier "which have seen significant usage."
- **Subclaim aggregations** — each decomposition annotated *conjunctive* (≈ product of subclaim strengths), *disjunctive* (≈ P(at least one holds)), or *convergent* (accumulation of partially independent evidence). "guides … rather than formal statements of joint probabilities"; correlated subclaims make naive conjunctions underestimate and naive disjunctions overestimate.

## Wandering

"Misalignment risk" is now a defined quantity: E[total unmitigated harm from misaligned computations of covered models]. That's a clean definition of risk as expected harm — note it is *expected value*, which treats a small chance of catastrophe as equivalent to a certain small harm with the same expectation. The ratings ("very low", "low") are qualitative summaries of this expectation, but the table in §2.4 decomposed it multiplicatively, so it's consistent. For the project: `anthropic:misalignment-risk` = expected unmitigated harm; *not* probability of catastrophe. A reader comparing with sources that define risk as probability × severity with risk-aversion, or as probability of exceeding a threshold, should notice.

"Unmitigated" includes after-the-fact mitigation ("mitigating the harm later") — so offline monitoring that catches a backdoor weeks later and removes it counts as mitigation. This is why reversibility matters (Claims 5.1 vs 5.3): mitigation-later only works for reversible threats.

The aggregation vocabulary (conjunctive/disjunctive/convergent) is argument-mapping language from safety-case practice (GSN/assurance cases, Toulmin-style). The correlation caveat is sophisticated and honest. The project's gamma research on "argument" should map these; they're source-defined argument-structure terms, with a declared non-formal status.

"Prior models" defined by being behind the frontier *and* well-used — evidence from prior models (Claim 3.1) depends on their having had enough usage to reveal misalignment. That's a sensible base-rate argument; its weakness is that prior models are, by construction, less capable — so what was absent in them may emerge with capability (limitation §2.16.2).
