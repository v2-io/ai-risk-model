# Unit 145 — §3.6.4 Energy, L1869–1883, pp.106–107

5 interviews. Non-LLM ML accelerating materials discovery (storage, superconductor screening), validation still months of expert lab work; a seminar claim of 2–3× from LLM agents in a solution-chemistry workflow, judged credible by one interviewee who sees 2–5× on purely computational work and grad students running several projects; LLMs lack original ideas, maybe in ~a year. Battery cell R&D: not accelerated (physical testing unautomated; unmeasured data). Fusion: coding help (porting legacy simulation code), little else; component manufacturing and experiments bottleneck. Energy firms' adoption early, cautious, mostly back-office; "do not yet trust frontier LLMs internally"; deployment (e.g. transmission) is the constraint even if R&D sped up.

Differed: nothing surprising; the repeated pattern: computational work 2–5×, physical work ~1×.

Wandering: "2–5× on purely computational research work" recurs (biotech dissent 2–5×, energy 2–5×, Anthropic's survey ~4×). There's a consistent ~2–5× per-worker productivity number on computational tasks across domains. Field progress stays bottlenecked elsewhere. For the project's evidence base, a cross-domain convergence — but all from interviews in one report, filtered through one author's summary; correlated by selection.

"Does not yet trust" — trust again as the adoption bottleneck (cf. units 132–133).
