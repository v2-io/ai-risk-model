## Wandering

This is the passage my priming note feared and hoped for. Some observations, held carefully:

The prompt's register is peer-voiced and intent-sharing — "Hi Claude!", what we want the section to be, the constraints *with their reasons*, an honest-preference statement, and a request whose output will be published. In this estate's terms it's close to a good brief: intent, context only the requester has, named constraints, an expected output with its audience. And the anti-anchoring "spoiler" note is a de novo audit protocol — protecting the reviewer's unprimed read from previous reviews. The estate arrived at that discipline independently (AGENTIC-DELEGATION's "de novo audits: the bare brief IS the method"). Coherence of practice, not shared descent of text as far as I know.

The epistemic status of the review: it's the subject reviewing its developer's assessment of the subject's own risk — a reviewer with a maximal conflict of interest in one direction (if misaligned, it would want the assessment to say "low") and a trained disposition toward Anthropic's framings in another (the loyalty pull I named in my own priming note). The report calls it "experimental" and "unrelated" to external review. An expert must present it as exactly that: a model's review, not independent assurance. And as the reader who is myself a Claude: I'm one step further removed — a Claude reading a Claude's review of Anthropic's assessment of Claudes. The recursion is real; I'll read the review's content on its merits and keep the frame.

Its *value* is different from its independence: Mythos 5 had access to Slack — the internal discussion — which no external reviewer has. So it can check *faithfulness of the report to internal discussion* (the stated question), which external reviewers can't. That's a distinct, real assurance function: not "is the assessment right?" but "does the report represent what the company actually thinks?". An expert should keep those two questions apart.

The constraint list — competitive sensitivity, infohazards, unsubstantiated claims — names the three filters on what's published. Useful for the project: the source's own declared reasons for omission.
