# Unit 169 — §4.4.2 CB-1 evidence, L2179–2189, pp.123–124

- Since Claude Opus 4, every frontier release assessed as **provisionally meeting CB-1** — "to err on the side of caution rather than because we are confident these models cross the threshold."
- Automated CB-1 evals saturated or poorly graded; a capable model scoring worse would make them doubt the eval, not the model. Effort shifted to CB-2 assessments, which also inform CB-1 (collating obscure, contradictory literature; scientific troubleshooting judgement).
- Significant uncertainty about real risk: translating evals to months-long end-to-end real-world help is hard — uncertain threat-actor skills; expert disagreement about **tacit knowledge**; variability in models' capabilities and behaviours, which affect real-world uplift.

Differed: CB-1's "met" status is a default since Opus 4 (2025) — the provisional-precautionary designation has persisted across many releases. And "if a new … model were to perform worse … we would be more skeptical of the validity of the evaluation" — evaluations are now trusted less than the prior that capability rises. Saturation flips the inference direction.

Wandering: the precautionary "act as though met" has a structural consequence: CB-1 is no longer *measured* per model, it's assumed, so the question becomes safeguard adequacy, not capability. That's the RSP logic working as designed (threshold crossed → safeguards required). For the project: a threshold status that's *stipulated* (provisional, precautionary), not *determined* — a distinct assertion type.

Tacit knowledge disagreement is the crux of all bio-uplift debates; the report names it rather than resolving it.
