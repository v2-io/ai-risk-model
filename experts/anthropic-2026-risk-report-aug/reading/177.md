# Unit 177 — §4.5.2.1 Coverage extended since the prior report, L2339–2349, p.133

(High-level register.)

- Prior report: classifiers covered uses associated with CB-1. Now extended to "a large (but not comprehensive)" set of uses associated with CB-2, validated on a new dataset (fn 61: 30 CB-2-but-not-CB-1 seed examples expanded with synthetic jailbreak transformations into thousands of variants; the old CB-1 classifiers already flagged 97.5% by generalisation; the improved classifiers caught most of the rest).
- **Inherent limit:** "significant portions of novel bioweapon development involve queries that are indistinguishable from non-harmful research biology use", so targeted blocks on clear harmful pursuit, "even extremely robust ones, are insufficient" — motivating the broader-coverage classifiers (Fable 5).

Differed: an explicit statement of the dual-use limit on content classifiers — the clearest acknowledgement that the CB-2 threat can't be fully addressed by refusing obviously harmful requests.

Wandering: this is the core policy tension the project's "controls" view should capture: for CB-2, the harmful queries look like legitimate research. The control options then are (a) block a broad swath of legitimate research (Fable 5's broader coverage — at a cost to researchers), (b) gate access by user identity (exemptions, vetting), or (c) accept residual risk. Fable 5 = option (a) for general access; Mythos 5 via Glasswing = option (b) presumably. That's the design rationale for the two-regime release of one model (unit 6), now explained by the dual-use limit. An expert asked "why is Mythos 5 released two ways?" can give this.

The validation set is small at the seed level (30 examples) though large after transformations. Generalisation from 30 seeds is a thin basis for "broadly covering"; the report hedges ("believe", "not comprehensive").
