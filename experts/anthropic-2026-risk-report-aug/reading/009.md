## Wandering

**The COVID-19 anchor was dropped.** v3.0 gave the only concrete magnitude so far: damages "far beyond… COVID-19" (~7M+ officially recorded deaths, trillions of dollars). v3.4's CB-2 says only "potential for catastrophic harm". With footnote 1's plain-meaning "catastrophic", the magnitude bar became less concrete. Whether that's a loosening or tightening of the magnitude element isn't determinable from the text; it's an anchor removed. An expert should flag that the old CB-2 had a magnitude anchor and the new one doesn't.

**The criterion changed kind:** from *significant help* (uplift, graded) to *functional substitution* for a scarce expert (a replacement test with a crisp counterfactual: would you otherwise need to recruit "one of a small number of world-leading specialists"?). Substitution is a higher bar than significant help. Under v3.0, Table 1.2.C's statement "models may provide significant uplift" would arguably have *met* the old CB-2 threshold ("ability to significantly help"). Under v3.3+, it doesn't. So the threshold change may be what keeps the CB-2 status at "not met". That is my inference, not the report's claim, and an important question for an expert: *"Would Mythos 5 have met the v3.0 CB-2 threshold?"* — I'll watch §4 for whether the report addresses this. The report promises to discuss "the ways in which we consider mitigations related to this threat model despite not having met the current threshold" — suggesting they apply CB-2-ish mitigations anyway.

**Actor drift:** the threat model in Table 1.2.C says "Moderately resourced threat actors (including… expert-backed teams)"; the new threshold says "a well-resourced team". Moderately vs well-resourced. Possibly a footnote (7 is glued to "team") qualifies it. If not, the threat model's actor and the threshold's actor differ in resourcing. Hold.

"End-to-end agent design and deployment (including… design, verification and validation, formulation, and dissemination)" — a pipeline decomposition of weapons development. The substitution must cover the *whole chain*, end to end; a model that substitutes for the expert in design but not formulation would not meet it. Conjunctive thresholds are easy not to meet.

Footnote 6 arrived in unit 10: "In particular, weapons with the potential to cause events with consequences comparable to or worse than those of COVID-19."
