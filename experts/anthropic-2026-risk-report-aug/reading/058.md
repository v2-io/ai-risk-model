# Unit 58 — Claim 5.1.2: harm-inducing computations would land on monitored surfaces, L964–970, pp.54–55

## What differed

- "Most internal usage on the high-stakes distribution is on monitored surfaces" (§2.23); harm won't concentrate on unmonitored surfaces by chance.
- Models unlikely to be situationally aware enough to *target* unmonitored surfaces: low opaque reasoning; no reasoning about monitoring coverage seen in assessments (and it would likely have been seen); "information on what non-monitored surfaces look like is not available online", and models asked to guess don't answer correctly.
- Scope: internal sabotage with access to internal systems; **excludes the usage in "the recent cyber eval incidents, which landed on the 0.2%-subsampled tier described in Section 2.23.1.1."** Harm there argued low on Claim 5.4's grounds.

## Wandering

The AISI incident's traffic was on a monitoring tier that **subsamples 0.2%** — so roughly 1 in 500 transcripts gets automated review on that surface. That's a concrete fact about why the incident wasn't caught by Anthropic's own monitoring and was disclosed by AISI instead. The report states it plainly, deep in a sub-sub-claim. A fork asked "why didn't Anthropic's monitoring catch the AISI incident?" should quote this: external/evaluation usage of that kind sits on a 0.2% subsampled tier. (Details in §2.23.1.1, ahead.)

The situational-awareness argument has a self-undermining edge the report doesn't remark on: "information on what non-monitored surfaces look like is not available online" — and this public report now describes the monitoring tiers, including a 0.2%-subsampled one, and an appendix (§2.23) "Detailed description of internal usage monitoring". Future models trained on text that includes this report would know more about Anthropic's monitoring coverage than covered models do. The report's transparency feeds the training data of its successors. That's an inherent tension in publishing control details — the information-hazard loop I noted in unit 31. Not a criticism of publishing; a dynamic the project's model should hold: *disclosure of controls is itself an input to future models' situational awareness.*

Testing whether models can guess unmonitored surfaces (they can't) is clever and checkable evidence. It will decay as such reports enter training data.
