# Unit 165 — §4.2.3 Timescale and scope of uplift, L2135–2147, pp.121–122

- Bioweapon development takes months (serial real-world iteration).
- Substantial uplift likely needs "substantial and persistent guidance, involving dozens of queries over substantial periods of time (weeks if not months), and spanning a wide range of topics."
- Uncertain whether a single-session plan could suffice; expectation: sustained iterative assistance matters far more; uplift trials suggest even top models are "particularly weak at correcting for failures in complex multi-step plans".
- If wrong, safeguards still help: classifiers aim to block brief guidance too, and "many jailbreaks appear (based on limited testing we've done) to degrade capabilities, often very significantly."

Differed: the "persistent guidance" assumption is load-bearing for the safeguard design — it's the CB analogue of §2's "many attempts" (Claim 5.1.1): uplift needs many queries, so per-query classifiers get many chances. Structurally the same argument in two threat models.

Wandering: the assumption is explicitly flagged as uncertain ("We are not confident about this"). It also has a capability expiry: the stated weakness (correcting failures in multi-step plans) is a judgement/self-monitoring weakness (cf. §3.4.1's calibration and self-monitoring gap). As models improve at self-correction and long-horizon planning (§3's trajectory), single-session or agentic end-to-end assistance becomes more plausible. And an agentic model running for weeks inside a scaffold is "persistent guidance" without dozens of human queries. The two threat models connect: automated R&D capability (long-horizon autonomy) erodes the CB safeguard's premise. An expert might be asked; the report doesn't link them here.

"Jailbreaks degrade capabilities" — a jailbroken model is a dumber model. A useful, empirical (limited-testing) mitigating factor. Its strength depends on jailbreak technique; boundary-point jailbreaking (TOC §4.5.3.2.1) may or may not degrade.
