# Unit 209 — §4.6.2 CB-2 overall risk, L2674–2692, pp.151–152

## What differed

- **"low, with high uncertainty."**
- Plausible (far from assured) that models ≥ Opus 4.6 give "substantial operational uplift" to actors already capable — e.g. well-resourced state programs — though much less than Mythos 5. Multiple developers have models at or above Opus 4.6 level.
- **A revision of the February assessment** (then: uplift to sophisticated actors "likely insubstantial"): better red-teaming and evaluations show the earlier capability gaps "can be substantially ameliorated by better expert steering and elicitation" → slightly greater force multipliers than estimated.
- **"highly plausible that a concerted effort by a well-resourced actor (e.g. state-sponsored programs) could result in theft of our model weights, breach of our access controls, or development of highly effective, universal jailbreaks."** Not unique to Anthropic; not assessed as higher risk than other generally available models; more capable models harder to misuse this way; Mythos-class "particularly challenging".
- Overall: low, "but also recognize that this is a judgment call."
- Especially strong protections for Mythos-class not because a bright line was crossed, but as "a policy of especially strong protections for especially strong models".
- **"Our assessment of low risk above is a description of the absolute risk we believe Anthropic's actions impose on the world, relative to a world without powerful AI models."** Marginal: Mythos-class are "currently the world's most capable models"; sub-Mythos models "add limited risk to the ecosystem, on a marginal basis."

## Wandering

**Correction to unit 200:** I wrote that the report "doesn't assemble this chain in one place" (state actor → weight theft → unsafeguarded use). It does, here: it states plainly that a concerted state-sponsored effort could "highly plausibly" steal weights, breach access, or find universal jailbreaks, and still judges risk low because of the low baseline likelihood and uncertainty about uplift. My earlier note was premature — the assembly came later in the order of reading. Leaving 200 as written, with this correction beside it.

**A definitional seam — "absolute risk" means two different things.** §1.1 defined "absolute" as "the level of risk that would be posed industry-wide, if all AI developers had models and practices similar to ours". Here: "absolute risk … Anthropic's actions impose on the world, relative to a world without powerful AI models." These are different counterfactuals: (a) a world where everyone is Anthropic-like; (b) a world without powerful AI. An expert must flag that §4.6.2 uses a different definition of "absolute" from §1.1, and that §§3.8 and 4.6.1 use §1.1's phrasing. Lexicon consequence: `anthropic:absolute-risk` is not stable within the document.

**Mythos-class are "the world's most capable models"** — a competitive claim that also sets the marginal-risk logic: for Mythos-class, marginal risk ≈ their full risk (no one else offers equivalent capability); for sub-Mythos, marginal ≈ small (equivalents available elsewhere with weaker safeguards). This is the clearest worked use of the marginal-risk frame in the report, and it explains the extra-strong Mythos safeguards: they're the one tier where Anthropic's marginal contribution isn't diluted.

"A judgment call" — the report names the epistemic type of its CB-2 conclusion. That's what a fork should say too.
