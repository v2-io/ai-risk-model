# Unit 230 — §5.3.1 Beneficial activities (part 1): framing; Mythos Preview release; red lines, L2945–2963, pp.169–170

## What differed

- Selection criteria: actions (a) likely beneficial (ex ante and ex post weight) and (b) likely different from what other developers would do; costliness to Anthropic cited mainly as evidence for (b). Disclaimers: room for disagreement ("some of the policy positions we took would be seen by many as detrimental"); evidence often informal or unshareable; "an inventory … rather than … rigorously established conclusions"; readers to judge.
- **Mythos Preview release:** pre-release evaluation concluded it **"represented a leap forward in offensive cyber capability"**; so Anthropic (1) published detailed risk evidence; (2) **launched Project Glasswing, "a collaborative effort to secure the world's most critical software"**; (3) **held the model from public release** until the Fable 5 safeguards were in place. Described as risky and costly; believed to improve "the world's preparedness for strong AI cyber capabilities".
- **Red lines on AI for surveillance and autonomous weaponry** in national-security settings — "steadfast"; posed "concrete risk to the company".

## Wandering

**This reframes the cyber question I've carried since unit 2.** Project Glasswing — the channel through which Mythos 5 reaches "certain customers" (unit 6) — is a *cyber-defence* programme, and Mythos-class models were withheld from general release *because of offensive cyber capability*. So cyber capability was arguably the single most consequential deployment driver of the period: it shaped the Mythos release decision, the two-regime access design (Glasswing vs Fable 5), and (by the report's own §2.1) the misalignment rating via the AISI cyber-eval incident. Yet the report contains **no cyber threat model** — because cyber isn't among RSP 3.4's highlighted catastrophic-risk categories (unit 5), and the report's contrasts (units 162, 164) treat cyber as defensible ("AI-assisted countermeasures could outweigh AI-assisted risks"). Glasswing is that defensive theory put into practice: give the strongest cyber model to defenders first.

An expert asked "where is cyber in this report?" now has the full answer: *(1) not a catastrophic-risk threat model under RSP 3.4; (2) the reason implied: cyber is seen as defensible with AI-assisted countermeasures; (3) in practice, offensive cyber capability drove the Mythos release strategy — held back from general release, offered to defenders via Project Glasswing, generally available only as Fable 5 with extra safeguards (whose described extras are bio-coverage; any cyber-specific safeguards aren't described in this report); (4) a UK AISI cyber-evaluation incident raised the misalignment rating; (5) cyber appears as an R&D-acceleration domain and as misalignment pathway 8.* That the most consequential capability of the period sits outside the report's threat models is the largest structural seam in the document, and the report's own text supplies the pieces.

The benefit framing — costly actions as evidence of differential behaviour — is a sound argument form (costly signalling), and it's self-assessment by the beneficiary. Interest marked. "Some of the policy positions we took would be seen by many as detrimental" is an honest acknowledgment that "benefit" is contested.

The surveillance/autonomous-weapons red lines connect to Claude Gov (unit 196): national-security deployment with stated limits. And to §3.6.6 (drone swarms, weapons R&D): Anthropic's own policy refuses some of the uses whose acceleration the interviews discussed.
