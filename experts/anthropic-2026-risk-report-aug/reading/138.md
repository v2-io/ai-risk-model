# Unit 138 — §3.5.1 AECI capability trajectory, Figure 3.5.A, L1803–1813, p.102

## What differed

- AECI synthesises internal benchmarks into one number per model (IRT; 95% CIs from 100 refits on 80% benchmark subsamples); not comparable to Epoch's public ECI; units fixed by Claude Sonnet 3.5 = 130.
- **Figure (opened):** frontier trend through Opus 4.6 = **13.5 AECI/yr (n=8)**, from Claude 3 Opus (~126, Mar 2024) to Opus 4.6 (~152.5, Feb 2026). **Mythos Preview ~159 (≈Apr 2026), Mythos 5 ~161 (≈Jun–Jul 2026)**, both ~4–5 points above the extrapolated line; off-frontier Opus 4.7 (~155) and Opus 4.8 (~156) sit on the line.
- Mythos Preview's departure attributed "with fairly high confidence" to specific research progress that "(from talking to the humans involved) we do not believe was significantly accelerated by AI."
- Mythos 5 is above trend by a similar amount — "weak evidence *against* a dramatic increase in overall model capability trends". **Model 2 ≈ 1.5 AECI above Mythos 5** (not shown; large error bars; smaller step than Mythos Preview → Mythos 5). Relative comparisons of adjacent models more reliable than absolute CIs suggest.

## Wandering

The interpretation: Mythos was a **level jump**, not a **slope change** — after the jump, Mythos 5 and Model 2 don't pull further above the line. If acceleration were underway, successive models would diverge increasingly from the old trend. Three points (Mythos Preview, Mythos 5, Model 2) roughly parallel to the trend, offset upward. That's the report's case that criterion 2 isn't met. It's a reasonable reading of very few points with overlapping CIs.

The attribution of the jump to human research "not significantly accelerated by AI" rests on "talking to the humans involved" — testimony, from inside, about a question (how much did AI help?) where self-report is known to be biased (unit 134 said researcher self-reports aren't trusted much). The report uses interview testimony here as fairly high confidence. A seam an expert could raise: *the attribution that rules out AI-driven acceleration for the Mythos jump rests on researcher testimony, a source the report elsewhere discounts.*

**Mythos 5 > Mythos Preview on AECI (≈161 vs ≈159), but < on CoBench (50.3 vs 54.8) and on covert evals.** So it's broadly more capable on the benchmark aggregate, less on the hard root-cause task set filtered on Mythos Preview's failures (unit 135 explained the filter disadvantages Mythos Preview, which makes its lead there more striking). Mixed picture; an expert should resist "Mythos 5 is strictly stronger".

13.5 AECI/yr is the baseline slope. "Double the rate of progress" in AECI terms would be ~27/yr — if AECI is linear in log-effective-compute (which an IRT index roughly is), consistent with footnote 5's log-space doubling. Mythos 5 to Model 2 (+1.5 in ~a month or two?) — dates unknown; can't compute a rate.
