# Unit 141 — §3.6.1 Introduction: other domains, L1823–1839, pp.103–104

- Not met for non-AI domains: AI R&D acceleration would precede others; "we would be generally aware (via e.g. anecdotes)" of dramatic acceleration elsewhere.
- **31 semi-structured interviews** — academics, scientists, industry experts, government officials, tech executives, frontier practitioners — across robotics, energy, biotech, semiconductors, weapons development, neurotech, nanotech; mostly external. "rough litmus test, rather than a rigorous or comprehensive assessment."
- "overwhelmingly consistent": neither full automation nor geopolitically significant acceleration; some report dramatic acceleration "in a normal sense".
- Exposure varied (daily users to second-hand: supervisors, executives, investors, funders).
- Most automation in coding: one estimate "one day's worth of work per week"; another "coding agents enable teams to be 5–10× smaller" for the same software output (not the same as multiples of progress). Time savings on figures, analysis, planning. Models "handle their fields' established knowledge well but still lack research taste, or the ability to generate novel ideas or high-quality hypotheses."

Differed: **no cyber domain** in the interview list (robotics, energy, biotech, semiconductors, weapons, neurotech, nanotech) — though §3.2 named "cyberwarfare" as a key domain. Cyber is named in the threat model and absent from the assessment. An expert should flag this gap: *§3.2 lists cyberwarfare among key domains; §3.6's interviews don't cover it* (unless inside "weapons development" — I'll check in §3.6.6).

Wandering: the evidence is self-described as a litmus test, and the sample mixes direct users with people reporting second-hand. "Anecdotes" as an awareness mechanism for geopolitically significant acceleration is a weak sensor; the report owns that.

"5–10× smaller teams for the same software" — a striking labour-substitution figure, distinct from acceleration. Displacement without acceleration: same output, fewer people. That's a societal impact (employment) the report's catastrophic scope excludes; it's here as a methodological aside. For the project's "who is affected" view: an observed labour effect, reported by an interviewee, in a document not assessing it.

"Lack research taste … novel ideas" — the same judgement gap as §3.4. Consistent picture across internal and external evidence.
