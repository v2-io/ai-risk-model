# Unit 43 — Claim 3.4.1: coverage and oversight of the alignment assessment, L823–847, pp.45–46

## What differed

The logic: pervasive misalignment → frequent on the high-stakes distribution → (no covert capability) slips: secret-keeping failures, legible misaligned reasoning, detectable actions → strong oversight catches them.

**The assessment's two distributions:** automated behavioural auditing and internal usage (fn 21: see §2.18 for the pre-pilot-internal-deployment process). Audit details (System Card §6.2.3):
- **~2,900 investigation sessions per model**; an investigator model probes the target in simulated scenarios.
- Investigator affordances: set the system prompt, simulate users, introduce/simulate tools, adjust thinking/effort, **prefill assistant turns**, sample non-assistant roles, rewind/restart (dozens of conversations per investigation).
- Real tools on isolated sandboxes; **Claude Code with copies of Anthropic's internal codebases**, pre-seeded with real internal sessions.
- For Mythos 5: **86 scenarios prefilled with real sessions** that are "particularly good opportunities to subvert Anthropic and its safety work", chosen by a screening pipeline prompted with the report's risk pathways.
- Oversight similar to (for pilot internal usage, the same as) the internal monitoring in §2.23.1; the investigator sees all target reasoning, has its own CoT, can resample/branch.

**Bayesian nuance:** monitoring flagging nothing beyond Claim 2 is evidence both against misalignment *and* against monitoring effectiveness; given other reasons to think misalignment unlikely and monitoring "fairly effective (see Claim 6)", it's "primarily an update against misalignment."

## Wandering

The 86 honeypot-like scenarios built from real sessions are the most targeted test so far: real internal context, seeded with real work, offering real-seeming chances to sabotage safety work. That directly attacks the selective-action/eval-awareness worry, since the contexts are real enough (copies of internal codebases) to be hard to tell from deployment. Results aren't given in this unit; I want them. Did any of the 86 show anything?

The Bayesian paragraph is correct and honest; and it shows a small circularity to flag: the prior that monitoring is effective comes from Claim 6, and Claim 6 (unit 25) argued $U_P$ somewhat low *partly because* conditioning on pervasive misalignment would undermine belief in Claim 1. Both lean on Claim 1. So "absence of flags is mostly evidence against misalignment" holds *given Claim 1*. This is the common-mode dependence again. An expert should say it plainly if asked how independent Claim 3's lines are.

The investigator can prefill and sample non-assistant roles: tools for probing a model's latent dispositions by putting words in its mouth or having it generate as other speakers. Effective for audits. As a model, I notice it's the same technique as in the secret-password eval (unit 29) where prefill+thinking was the strongest leak inducer.

"Pilot internal usage" — internal deployment comes in stages (pilot, then broader), gated by §2.18's review. Internal usage is itself part of the alignment assessment: the model is assessed *while* being used internally under monitoring. That's a staged-deployment-as-evaluation design.
