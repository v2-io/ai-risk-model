# Unit 186 — BPJ limitations, L2424–2431, p.138

(Status-only.) (1) Robustness evidence rests on Anthropic's and AISI's versions of the attack; a stronger attacker version is possible, judged not very likely. (2) Monitoring limitations; one not expected to be resolved: **surfaces with zero data retention (ZDR)** — less confidence in flagging there; no known mitigation for CB-2 actors "other than refraining from deploying sufficiently capable models on ZDR surfaces in settings at risk of use by such actors (e.g. enterprise deployments without both strong KYC requirements and very strong security guarantees against compromise of individual users' access)." (3) One limitation redacted, weak evidence it isn't effective.

Differed: a structural trade-off named — privacy commitments (zero data retention for enterprise customers) disable the monitoring control. The stated mitigation is a deployment restriction tied to KYC.

Wandering: this is a clean example for the project of two legitimate values in tension within the controls stage: customer data privacy (ZDR) vs misuse detection (monitoring). The report resolves it by conditioning deployment on KYC and access security. An expert asked "can misuse be monitored on every surface?" should cite ZDR as the named exception. Whether Mythos 5 is offered on ZDR surfaces isn't said here.
