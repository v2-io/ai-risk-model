# Unit 212 — §4.8 Industry recommendations: CB-1 row, L2724–2730, p.155

CB-1 threshold text (RSP): systems that can "significantly help individuals or groups with **basic technical backgrounds (e.g., undergraduate STEM degrees)** create/obtain and deploy chemical and/or biological weapons with serious potential for catastrophic damages." Standard: "a strong argument that individual users and relatively small teams will not become significantly more likely to cause catastrophic harm" via product use or weight theft — likely requiring behaviour restrictions and/or fast detection/action on Usage Policy violations with a strong case against reliable, sustained jailbreaking; precautions against opportunistic weight theft (centralised controls on third-party applications and software updates).

Differed: CB-1's actor is defined concretely here: basic technical background, "undergraduate STEM degrees" — a useful anchor absent from the earlier summaries ("individuals or small groups with limited resources"). Compare CB-2's fn 7 (millions of dollars, recent top PhDs). An expert can now give both actor anchors.

Wandering: the CB-1 standard's weight-security requirement is *opportunistic* theft — the bar is low-resource attackers, consistent with unit 200's claim of strong protection against such actors. The four-part CB-1 argument (unit 208) is this standard applied. For the project: thresholds ↔ standards ↔ self-assessment form a triple per threat model in this report.
