## Wandering

Reason 4's parenthetical — deployment lacks "elicitation information" — means: in evals, the model is told to attack and sometimes helped (prompt optimisation, fine-tuning); in deployment, a misaligned model would have to self-elicit. So eval ability is an *upper bound* on deployment ability, the opposite of the usual worry (that evals underestimate). It's a fair point for capability *given instruction*; it's less clear for a model whose misalignment supplies its own motivation and which might try harder than an instructed-but-reluctant model (the "ethically-motivated" sandbagging seen in unit 28). Evals of reluctant models may underestimate a motivated one. The report's NLA evidence (no sandbagging motivated by consequences) partly addresses this.

The explicit flag — "if this claim were invalidated it would reduce confidence in Claim 5.1.3 overall" — is the report building its own sensitivity analysis into the text. For the project's argument records: dependency edges with stated sensitivity. Rare and valuable.

The report marks one claim as non-load-bearing ("Our overall claim does not depend on this holding"). Distinguishing load-bearing from supporting claims in-text is something an expert should preserve when extracting assertions.
