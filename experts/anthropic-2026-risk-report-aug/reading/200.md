# Unit 200 — §4.5.7 Model weight security, L2582–2586, p.146

Weights of models ≥ Opus 4 under enhanced security — sufficient that theft "would be difficult for most attackers—**excluding sophisticated insiders and attackers with nation-state backing**". Nation-state-robust security is "extremely difficult"; **"we do not believe any frontier AI developer currently meets this bar; we do not either"**; heavy investment (Frontier Safety Roadmap); more in Appendix 6.4.

Differed: answers my unit-157 question: Anthropic does **not** currently meet nation-state-robust weight security — so the §3.10 industry standard for the AI R&D threshold ("security roughly in line with RAND SL4" against top-tier-state-backed actors) is not met today, by Anthropic's own statement. An expert should connect the two passages.

Wandering: **the CB-2 threat actor and the weight-security gap coincide.** CB-2's actors are "moderately to very well-resourced programs, including but not limited to state programs" (unit 163); weight security is explicitly not robust to "attackers with nation-state backing". And unit 172 said an *unsafeguarded* Mythos-class model might roughly double an expert team's odds of success. So the pathway "state program steals Mythos-class weights → uses unsafeguarded model with its own experts" is, on the report's own statements, not blocked by weight security; the report's CB-2 "low risk" must rest on other links (baseline improbability of attempt/success/release; state actors' own capabilities). The report doesn't assemble this chain in one place; it's assembled from units 163, 172, 200. An expert should be able to state it carefully, with each link's source, and note that the report rates CB-2 risk low "with substantial uncertainty".

The admission that *no* frontier developer meets the bar is also a claim about competitors — and supports the marginal-risk framing (Anthropic isn't worse than others). Interest marked.
