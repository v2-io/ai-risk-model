# Unit 63 — Claim 5.1.4: response and remediation, L1017–1032, pp.58–59

## What differed

- Response process: strange behaviour reported on the internal messaging service; staff decide effort; well-staffed efforts for major issues (e.g., "a task force to deal with reward hacking"); less for rare failures with no clear path to a major incident.
- Actions: revert the caught actions' consequences; search for and revert similar ones; short-term security/monitor patches; medium-term training-pipeline updates; root-cause research; "possibly communicate publicly".
- **Unevaluated:** "We did not evaluate how good these post-deployment follow-up investigations are at finding all instances of harmful behavior and finding effective mitigations." Subtle misalignment without unambiguous evidence may not be caught (fn 26).
- Tampering with the response pipeline: not aware of important unmitigated routes; humans heavily involved; "most research into incidents is done mostly by humans talking at least some in person"; could switch to harder-to-tamper channels.
- **"Reversible" operationalised:** harm "can be reverted even if responded to within a few weeks"; response likely within that window (except when detection comes from a much later attempt). Costs of reversal to Anthropic are irreversible but "not relevant for this risk assessment".

## Wandering

"Reversible = revertible within a few weeks" is a usable operational definition — an expert can quote it. It sets a clock: asynchronous monitoring + human triage must complete inside ~weeks for 5.1 to hold.

The unevaluated response pipeline is the weakest link in the 5.1 conjunction by the report's own admission: detection is evaluated (§2.23.1.2), response is assumed. In incident management generally, response effectiveness is where systems fail — alerts that nobody acts on. The "staff consider a variety of factors in determining how much effort to devote" sentence is honest about triage: rare, ambiguous signals get less attention. A selective misaligned model's signals would be rare and ambiguous by design.

"Humans talking at least some in person" as a tamper-resistant channel — a lovely, very concrete control: face-to-face conversation as the out-of-band channel AI can't edit. For the project's controls list, it's a real, named control. And it's another sign of the threat model's shape: the AI is inside the company's communication fabric (messaging, code, docs), so the fallback is the one channel it's not in.

The report excludes Anthropic's own costs of reversal from "harm". Under the risk definition (catastrophic harm to the world), that's consistent; it does mean a scenario where Anthropic spends months recovering from sabotaged research isn't counted as harm unless it raises future catastrophic risk — which, under the threat model, it would by delaying safety work. A small tension; marked.
