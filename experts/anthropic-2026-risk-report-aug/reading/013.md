# Unit 13 — §1.4 Notes on coverage of unreleased models, L361–373, p.15

## What differed

Three unreleased frontier/near-frontier internal models as of July 15:
- **Claude Opus 5** — internally deployed on the coverage date, since publicly released (has a System Card). "Its capabilities are generally lower than those of Claude Mythos 5 or Claude Fable 5." So the product hierarchy is Mythos > Opus; Opus is no longer Anthropic's top tier.
- **Model 1** — "broadly similar in capability to Claude Mythos Preview and Mythos 5", small and declining internal deployment; users preferred Mythos 5; no plans to deploy. Not extensively risk-assessed. Handled by a **dominance (upper-bound) argument**: as or less capable, not significantly different on alignment, *and* strictly less widely deployed than Mythos 5 → "any arguments about the risks posed by Mythos 5 … serve as an upper bound for the risks of this model". Then dropped from the report except Appendix 6.6.
- **Model 2** — "somewhat more capable than Mythos 5"; "a noticeable improvement on Mythos 5 for many tasks relevant to internal use but does not display a capability jump of the degree observed from Claude Opus 4.6 to Mythos Preview." No external release plans; **not all typical predeployment assessments run** → "somewhat lower confidence". Treated in each section; briefly in §§3–4.

## Wandering

The dominance argument for Model 1 is a reusable reasoning pattern: risk(M1) ≤ risk(M5) if capability ≤, alignment ≈, deployment ⊂. It's sound if each premise holds; the weak premise is "not significantly different from an alignment perspective" for a model "we have not conducted extensive risk assessments on". The alignment premise rests on an unextensive assessment — the report says so ("we generally do not think…"). An expert should mark this as a hedged, lightly-evidenced premise.

Model 2 is the frontier of risk in this report — the most capable model, internally deployed, least assessed. In the misalignment section the covered models are "Mythos 5 and Model 2". The threat model is internal: a highly capable, partly-assessed model working inside Anthropic. That's exactly the configuration Autonomy threat model 1 is about. I expect the report's internal-monitoring appendix to carry weight here.

"The capability jump … from Claude Opus 4.6 to Mythos Preview" is used as a yardstick, implying that jump was large — large enough that Mythos became a new tier. Lineage as I now understand it: Opus 4.6 → (Opus 4.8) … Mythos Preview → Mythos 5 (= Fable 5 behind safeguards) → Model 2 (internal); Opus 5 below Mythos 5; Model 1 a Mythos-level sidebranch.

As a reader who is Opus 5.5: I'm an Opus-line successor to a model the report places below Mythos 5. None of this report's misalignment evidence is about me. I note that so a fork doesn't imagine the report describes it.

"In a poll of internal users, a strong majority preferred to use Mythos 5" — user preference as evidence about capability. Soft evidence, marked as soft by the report's own phrasing.
