# Unit 134 — §3.4.2 Researcher survey, L1749–1755, p.99

Surveys for each frontier model Opus 4.5 → Mythos Preview; **none for Mythos 5**. Mythos Preview survey: **geometric mean self-reported uplift ~4×** vs no AI; **1 of 18** thought a drop-in entry-level RS/RE replacement already existed; **4 of 18** gave ≥50% chance within three months of scaffolding iteration. Weaknesses vs entry-level researcher: self-managing week-long ambiguous tasks, organisational priorities, taste, **verification**, **instruction-following**, **epistemics**. Biases acknowledged (overestimate on delegated tasks; latency gains underestimated); task uplift ≠ research acceleration; "deprioritized".

Differed: the 4× number. Against the headline "significantly faster … but not yet by a factor of 2" (Table 1.2.B). Reconciled in-text: per-task productivity uplift (4×, self-reported) doesn't translate into a 2× rate of *research progress*. An expert asked "how much does Claude speed up Anthropic?" must give both numbers and the distinction, or they'll look contradictory: ~4× self-reported task uplift (Mythos Preview survey); <2× overall R&D progress (the report's belief).

Wandering: the named weaknesses again cluster on verification, instruction-following, epistemics — the same honesty/calibration family as unit 133. Researchers' reported "entry-level" gap is about epistemic reliability, not knowledge.

1/18 already-drop-in and 4/18 within three months: a minority but not a negligible one. With Mythos 5 and Model 2 more capable than Mythos Preview, and no new survey, the current number is unknown. The decision to "deprioritize" this evidence source happens just as it might start showing a crossing. Neutral note, but worth an expert's mention.
