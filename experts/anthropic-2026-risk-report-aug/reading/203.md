# Unit 203 — §4.5.8.2 Incidents (intro), L2602–2608, p.147

Why incidents are reported: (1) temporary gaps matter for assessing risk during the coverage period (no evidence of actual CB misuse); (2) incidents signal preparedness — detection/remediation speed and confidence no similar gaps remain; (3) transparency serves the ecosystem (other developers check their systems; policymakers and public see practical challenges). "some rate of incidents like these is an expected feature"; investment in detection, containment, remediation. Minor incidents in Appendix 6.5.

Differed: a stated *theory of incident disclosure*, with three rationales, and a normalisation ("expected feature") that frames incidents as part of operating safeguards at scale.

Wandering: rationale (2) is exactly what the report did with the CB-1 rating (unit 6): incidents as evidence about the unseen. Rationale (3) is a public-goods claim. The normalisation sentence is also rhetorically protective — it pre-frames incidents as expected. Both are true and both serve the author; an expert should report the incidents' facts and the framing separately.
