# Unit 181 — §4.5.3.2 Viable routes around the classifiers (overview), L2382–2386, p.136

(Status-only register, per 179.)

- As of the coverage date: **four routes** that could plausibly circumvent deployed classifiers on at least one model without large capability degradation — one **coverage gap** (identified independently by UK AISI and internal testing; mitigations rolling out), **two surfaced by UK AISI**, **one from the bug bounty**. None publicly disclosed in enough detail to be easily implemented; mitigations deployed or in progress on the most capable models.
- Track record: every known universal, highly effective jailbreak or gap eventually patched; never a period with a publicly known straightforward bypass. But **resolution times for non-public jailbreaks "as long as several months to reach all deployment surfaces on our most capable models"** — longer than ideal; judged commensurate given non-public status; a faster fix for a public jailbreak is expected "but this has not been tested."

Differed: UK AISI is the source of three of the four known routes (two jailbreaks + co-discovery of the gap). Together with the cyber-eval incident, AISI is the most consequential external actor in this report.

Wandering: "several months" to patch across surfaces — for a safeguard whose function is to stop CB-2-relevant help from a model "nearing the CB-2 threshold". The report's mitigating judgement rests on secrecy: the routes weren't known to threat actors. Secrecy is then itself a control (information control on jailbreak details), and the report respects it by not publishing method detail. An expert should present: patch latency up to months; justified by non-public status; untested response speed for a public jailbreak.

For the project's "who" view: UK AISI as an external red-teamer whose findings feed Anthropic's mitigations — a governmental evaluator acting as a source of risk events (jailbreaks found) and of controls (fixes prompted).
