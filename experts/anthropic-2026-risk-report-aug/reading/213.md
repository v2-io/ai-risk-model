# Unit 213 — §4.8 CB-2 row and Anthropic's self-assessment, L2734–2760, pp.156–157

## What differed

- CB-2 row: the threshold text (with its footnotes again: COVID-comparable, millions-of-dollars teams, 2023 baseline, "hundreds"); standard: a strong argument that threat actors won't become significantly more likely to cause CB-2-type harm via product use or weight theft — "to a higher standard", even against well-resourced, well-staffed actors; **"security roughly in line with RAND SL4"**, depending on the strongest plausible actors not bound by a credible governance regime.
- **Self-assessment:**
  - Many models "possibly meet" the CB-1 threshold (high uncertainty).
  - **"We currently do meet the right column's criterion for CB-1"** (the safety-case standard).
  - Models do not meet the CB-2 threshold — "significantly more difficult and uncertain than it was in our previous Risk Report"; "a reasonable case" that the ideal ecosystem would require greater restrictions, "particularly on our recent Opus-class models".
- **The forecast and the admitted gap:** "we will declare near-future models to meet the RSP threshold for CB-2, or at least fail to rule it out. We expect to meet the planned mitigations described in our RSP for the CB-2 threshold at that time, but **we do not expect to meet our ambitious industry-wide recommendations (specifically, security that can reliably stop attacks from well-resourced state actors) in time, absent a change in the rapid pace of model capability improvement.**" Moonshot R&D and security work ongoing; update next report.

## Wandering

This is the report's most consequential self-assessment for CB, and an expert should be able to give it in one breath: *CB-1 standard met; CB-2 threshold not yet met but expected to be met (or not ruled out) by near-future models; the RSP's own CB-2 mitigations expected to be met; the industry-wide standard for that tier — security robust to state actors (≈ RAND SL4) — not expected to be met in time, unless capability progress slows.*

So the report explicitly forecasts a period in which its models cross a threshold whose *recommended* industry standard (its own) it will not meet. The RSP's binding commitments ("planned mitigations") are lower than its industry recommendations. The difference between *what Anthropic commits to* and *what Anthropic says an ideal ecosystem requires* is stated openly. For the project's "policies & decision-making" stage: two tiers of normative statement from one source — commitments (RSP planned mitigations) vs recommendations (ideal ecosystem) — with an announced gap between them.

"Absent a change in the rapid pace of model capability improvement" — names the race as the cause, implicitly the industry's pace, not only Anthropic's. Interest marked: the frame places the gap's cause in the competitive environment.

Same structure as §3 (AI R&D): threshold forecast within a year; mitigations not ready ("eyes on everything" target Jan 2027; SL4 not in time). Both major threat-model sections end on the same shape.
