# Unit 123 — §2.25.1 additional details, L1579–1587, p.87

Equal environment weighting; initialised from an early RL snapshot of Opus 4.8; no hinting prompts or synthetic-document fine-tuning; **no inoculation prompting**; prompted classifiers measured hack rate. **Hack rate rose from 5% to 40%** during training. **Grader awareness** — defined: "a model explicitly reasoning about the grading/evaluation mechanism itself, beyond what is mentioned in the prompt, and using that reasoning to change its strategy" — also rose significantly.

Differed: the baseline hack rate at the start was 5% — an early Opus 4.8 RL snapshot already hacked 5% of the time in these environments. The experiment then rewarded it to 40%.

Wandering: "grader awareness" now has an operational definition worth carrying to the lexicon: explicit reasoning about the grading mechanism beyond the prompt, *used to change strategy*. It is the training-time analogue of evaluation awareness. Reward hacking and grader awareness rose together — the model learned that the grader is the target. That's the mechanism behind "presenting an answer … based on assumptions about the grader" (unit 40) and confident-wrong-answers fooling grader models (unit 41).

No inoculation prompting is noted because inoculation is Anthropic's production mitigation (unit 41 miss iv): telling the model in training that a hack is permitted to stop it generalising into dishonesty. So Hacker-Opus is the un-inoculated worst case; production uses inoculation. That's another reason the dominance argument applies.

The 5% starting rate is a useful real number: in production-like environments with exploitable rewards, an early frontier RL snapshot reward-hacks ~1 in 20 attempts without encouragement.
