# Unit 162 — CB-1: priority rationale and prioritisation, L2071–2079, p.118

- **"A 1% annual probability of an event as damaging as COVID-19 could imply upwards of hundreds of billions of dollars in expected damages."**
- Priority: even modest increases matter, given difficulty of robust defences against CB weapons "(in the same way that, for example, cyberdefenses can be mounted)" and lack of early warning.
- **Current prioritisation: biological threats with pandemic potential** — high expected harm even at low attempt rates; AI could lower knowledge, skill, or automation bottlenecks → "both counterfactually attributable to AI and potentially catastrophic."
- Chemical: less likely to enable comparable catastrophe attributable to AI; would need scaling many smaller attacks, giving detection opportunities; still examined.
- Views developed with experts incl. **Deloitte Consulting and SecureBio**; informed by a **Frontier Model Forum** expert workshop.

## Wandering

**The 1%-of-COVID sentence is the report's only explicit expected-value calculation** I've met: 1% × COVID-scale damage (the footnotes' ~$10–28T) ≈ $100–300B per year. It's illustrative ("could imply"), not Anthropic's estimate of the actual probability. An expert must not report "Anthropic estimates a 1% annual probability" — it's a hypothetical used to show stakes. And it reveals the decision logic: expected damages in the hundreds of billions justify action even at low probability. That's an expected-value framing consistent with §2's definition of risk as expected harm.

**A rationale for cyber's absence, at last, by contrast:** CB is prioritised "because of the difficulty of constructing robust defenses against biological and chemical weapons (in the same way that, for example, cyberdefenses can be mounted)". So the implicit reason cyber isn't a CB-like catastrophic misuse threat model here: defences can be mounted (offence-defence balance) and, presumably, the harm is more recoverable. That's the closest the report comes to justifying cyber's omission. An expert asked "why no cyber threat model?" can cite this parenthesis as the report's implicit reasoning, together with §6.1 threat-model criteria (not yet read).

"Counterfactually attributable to AI" — a selection criterion for threat models: prioritise risks where AI's causal contribution is clear. That's a principle for choosing what to assess, worth recording beside the "practice value" criterion from §2 (unit 17). Two selection criteria so far: practice value; counterfactual attributability (plus magnitude).

Deloitte, SecureBio, Frontier Model Forum — external consultees; SecureBio also the pilot reviewer of CB sections (unit 12). Independence partial.
