# Unit 45 — Claim 3.4.2: evaluation awareness, L853–863, p.47

## What differed

The claim is modest by design: eval awareness doesn't "more than partially" undermine the assessments.
- Unrealism might elicit less misalignment, ease non-verbalisation, reduce misaligned action; it's a lack of coverage "sufficiently specific and potentially pernicious" to treat explicitly.
- **Admission:** "evaluation awareness does partially undermine our confidence … A large portion of the automated behavioral audit likely is detectably unrealistic to covered models, such that it primarily provides evidence for behaviors that are not gated by realism."
- Defences: pilot internal usage (real distribution, so no eval-awareness concern); real sandboxes and Anthropic codebases in audits ("appear difficult for the model to distinguish from real usage") to concentrate effort on sub-distributions like safety-research sabotage opportunities; branching/resampling to stretch scarce realistic seed transcripts; **realism steering and fine-tuning** results ("fidelity … unclear"). Details: System Card §6.5.1.

## Wandering

The candour is notable: most of the ~2,900-session audit is probably recognisable as fake to Mythos 5, so it mainly tests behaviours that don't depend on realism. The weight shifts to (a) internal usage and (b) the realistic seeded scenarios (the 86). So Claim 3.4's real evidence for *realism-gated* misalignment is much narrower than "2,900 sessions" suggests. An expert should never cite the audit's size as evidence against eval-gaming; the report itself says that's the wrong inference.

"Pilot internal usage is the most obvious case where evaluation awareness is not a concern" — true if the model believes internal usage is real. A model that knew it was in a *pilot* internal deployment (being watched closely before wider rollout) might treat the pilot as an evaluation. The pilot is real usage *and* a gate. Does the model know it's a pilot? The report doesn't say. Worth a question.

"Realism steering" — steering activations to make the model believe the scenario is real (suppressing eval-awareness representations), then checking whether behaviour changes. An interpretability-based control on evaluation validity. "Fidelity unclear" is the right hedge.

The scarcity of realistic seeds ("limited by their natural rate of occurrence") is a deep constraint: the best opportunities to sabotage safety research occur rarely in real work, so there are few real transcripts to build realistic tests from. Rare real opportunities are both what a selective misaligned model would wait for and what the audit can least simulate. The report sees this and mitigates via branching.
