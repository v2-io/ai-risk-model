# Unit 164 — CB-2 baseline probability chain, L2111–2133, p.120

## What differed

The chain, completed (baseline = without AI assistance):
- Per actor: concerted attempt 1–10% (unit 163).
- Per attempt: **success 1–10%** (fn 54: "success" = a weapon with enormous damage potential, not other goals like selective targeting).
- Given a weapon: **release 5–20% per decade** (mostly deliberate; accidental possible).
- Independence "facially roughly" but the product may not bound the overall probability.
- Lower-resource actors: less threat-modelled; baseline "very low", in or below the same range as the actors above (×2 factor).
- **Implied baseline: "as high as 1 in 50 or so, or as low as 1 in 20,000" per decade**; 1/50 "on the aggressive end".
- fn: high end $= 10\% \times 10\% \times 20\% \times$ "several" $\times 2$; low end $= 1\% \times 1\% \times 5\% \times$ "several" $\times 2$.
- Forecasting reference: **3/1000 per year (~3% per decade) for human-caused epidemics with ≥100,000 casualties**, at baseline — much smaller harm and includes known pathogens.
- Priority: despite very low likelihood, damages "would exceed those of all AI risk threat models other than the ones already discussed elsewhere in this report." Defences hard: "Unlike in some other domains such as **cyberoffense**, it is hard to envision a way in which AI-assisted countermeasures could outweigh AI-assisted risks." No early warning → preventive approach.
- **"especially high uncertainty"**: hard to connect to capability evaluations; evals "a work in progress".

## Wandering

**I checked the arithmetic.** $0.1 \times 0.1 \times 0.2 = 2\times10^{-3}$; times "several" (≈5) and 2 gives $2\times10^{-2} = 1/50$ ✓. Low end: $0.01 \times 0.01 \times 0.05 = 5\times10^{-6}$; ×5×2 = $5\times10^{-5} = 1/20{,}000$ ✓. So "several" ≈ 5 in their calculation (implied, not stated). An expert quoting the range should carry the implicit "several ≈ 5".

The range spans 400× (1/50 to 1/20,000 per decade) — honest about uncertainty. These are *baselines*: the AI-amplification step (how much do Anthropic's models raise each link?) is what the rest of §4 must address. CB-2 "not met" means models don't functionally substitute for the scarce expertise — i.e., don't substantially raise the success link (1–10%).

**Cyber's absence explained twice now** (units 162, 164): cyber is the contrast case where "AI-assisted countermeasures could outweigh AI-assisted risks" and defences can be mounted. That's an offence–defence-balance argument, the report's implicit rationale for cyber not being a catastrophic misuse threat model under the RSP. An expert asked "where's cyber?" now has a fuller answer: the RSP's prioritisation (by the report's own contrasts) treats cyber as defensible and AI-countermeasurable, hence not a catastrophic-misuse threat model; it appears as an acceleration domain, a misaligned-model pathway, and an incident source.

"Damages would exceed those of all AI risk threat models other than the ones already discussed elsewhere in this report" — i.e., CB-2's stakes are below automated R&D/misalignment (which §3.2 said were "a multiple") but above everything not in the report. A partial ordering of threat models by stakes: automated R&D (balance of power) ≥ misalignment(?) > CB-2 > others. Worth recording as the source's ranking.
