## Wandering

**Long context degrades monitoring**: benign padding of 200k–400k tokens lowers recall, most sharply when padding follows too (48% for 4.6). Persistent agents produce long transcripts. The long-context weakness intersects with "persistent agent deployments" and with Mallen's spread. The 4.8 monitor is more robust to padding (77% vs 48% in the worst row).

**SLEIGHT-Bench at 44% end-to-end** (4.8 + stage 2) — the hardest benchmark here, presumably about sleight-of-hand attacks; less than half caught per attempt. An expert asked for the weakest monitoring number should give this one with its name.

"Update the exact filters used in the second stage without evaluating its effect on recall" — another disclosed process gap, at precisely the stage where recall is lost (unit 102). Combined: the stage that loses the most recall is changed without measuring recall changes. That's the soft spot of the detection side of Claim 5, in the report's own words.

The mass-deletion misses are the instructive field failure: real, destructive (inadvertent) actions flagged by the first stage and then filtered out because they weren't on a risk pathway. The pipeline is tuned for catastrophe-pathway signals, so it can drop clear-but-non-pathway harms. That's a deliberate design choice (bandwidth), with a visible cost. And "multiagent turf wars" recalls unit 32's agents killing each other — inter-agent conflict as a recurring phenomenon in Anthropic's internal deployments.

Note also: the table's SHADE rows use Opus 4.5 transcripts — older model attacks — as monitor tests.
