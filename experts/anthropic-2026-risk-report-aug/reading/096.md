# Unit 96 — §2.21 Looking forward, L1311–1325, p.74

Goals in the frontier safety roadmap; improving training alignment "with the Constitution", training-data monitoring, assessments, deployment monitoring. On the keystone: "We do not have confident views on how the strength of this claim will change over time" — secret keeping and opaque-reasoning task complexity may improve, but the *relative* gap with/without legible reasoning "may persist", and monitoring "might keep pace". Plans: better measurement (elicitation, evals more faithful to the high-stakes distribution) and assessment/control even for covertly capable models (interpretability, validated). Also: audit realism, stress-testing safety systems, adversarial training studies, better risk-assessment structure. Cyber incidents under review; future reports may engage §2.17 (differential progress).

Differed: as predicted — interpretability, elicitation, realism. The specific new thought: the *relative gap* between performance with and without legible reasoning may persist even as absolute capabilities rise. That's a hopeful structural hypothesis for CoT monitoring: if reasoning keeps helping a lot, models will keep needing to reason legibly to do hard things, so monitoring keeps working. Its failure mode would be training pressure on CoT (§5.2.3) or architectures that reason in latent space.

Wandering: nothing in "looking forward" addresses staged deployment (Model 2's pilot) or the employee-spun custom deployments (unit 90) as things to improve. Nor the self-disclosure tension. Nor the absolute-risk statement missing from §2.19.

"Alignment of our training process and data with the Constitution" — alignment defined, again, as conformity to the Constitution (§2.2's framing), not §2.5's four-criterion definition. The two definitions coexist in the section; the forward-looking language uses the Constitution one.
