# Unit 129 — §3.2 Threat model, L1668–1695, p.94

## What differed

- Premise: "in the next few years" AI may exceed human capabilities broadly; most or all R&D work in key domains — "from robotics to energy to **cyberwarfare** to AI R&D itself" — may become automatable.
- Three concerns: (1) power structures within/between nations — e.g. "an authoritarian regime built a self-reinforcing lead in AI", compounding into advantages in war, surveillance, and powerful technology; (2) AI with dangerous goals heavily automating R&D → "unbounded harm—up to and including humanity losing control over civilization entirely"; (3) automating AI R&D → extreme acceleration, broad risks.
- Role of AI: acceleration "contributing to unexpected power imbalances (between different humans or groups of humans, or between humans and AIs)."
- **Magnitude:** "major, enduring changes in the global balance of power (either between humans or passing to AI systems themselves)"; value at stake "could be a multiple of what's at stake in any of the other threat models".
- **Likelihood:** "difficult to assess, and we do not have consensus on a specific likelihood"; impacts "at least plausible if and when AI becomes capable of automating nearly everything humans do in advancing R&D".
- Priority: "our best working guess is that the automation of R&D will be the one most likely to lead to globally transformative impacts".

## Wandering

**Cyberwarfare appears**, as a domain whose R&D could be automated — the fourth place cyber surfaces (after units 6, 34, 73). So the report does treat cyber capability as a *domain of acceleration* (§3.6 lists "weapons development"; cyber may be inside it or absent from §3.6's list — I'll check). For an expert: cyber is in the report as (a) an acceleration domain, (b) a misaligned-model pathway, (c) an incident source; not as a misuse threat model.

The magnitude claim — this threat model's stakes are "a multiple" of the others' — is a comparative valuation across threat models, unusual and important: Anthropic ranks power-balance shifts (including loss of control to AI) above CB catastrophes and above misalignment-sabotage in value at stake. And yet its likelihood is explicitly unassessed ("no consensus"). Highest stakes, least quantified. For the project's model: an assertion of relative magnitude without likelihood.

"Passing to AI systems themselves" — loss of control named as a balance-of-power outcome, i.e., the report's framing of loss of control is political (power), not only technical (control). Compare the EU Code's loss-of-control formula ("reliably direct, modify, or shut down", per CLAUDE.md). Different conceptual home: Anthropic places it in a power-balance threat model; the EU Code (as I understand from CLAUDE.md only) in a controllability definition. Not to be merged.

"Authoritarian regime" as the paradigm human-actor concern: geopolitical framing — the democratic-lead argument familiar from Anthropic's public positions. Record as the source's stated concern; note it's also a policy position Anthropic holds publicly (interest marked).
