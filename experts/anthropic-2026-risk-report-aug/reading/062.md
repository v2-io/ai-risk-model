# Unit 62 — Claim 5.1.3.1 end: first attempts and progressive spread, L1013–1015, p.58

- The first attempt to spread a plan or trigger would likely either fail to spread (so no harm) but be detected, or induce easily-detected misalignment. "Both of these outcomes seem likely."
- Spread might be **progressive** — "steadily increasing in egregiousness and virality over time through an evolutionary process" — slightly harder to catch but giving asynchronous monitoring "many opportunities".
- Deployment applies weak optimisation pressure on AI-generated shared content (vs training), so no "heavily-optimized-for-virulence misaligned content" expected.

Differed: the evolutionary framing — misalignment as a replicator under selection among instances — I didn't expect the report to use that language. It treats misaligned content like a meme (in the original sense) whose virulence would be selected for if there were selection pressure. And it argues selection is weak in deployment.

Wandering: "weak optimisation pressure on content generated and shared by AIs" — true of deployment as such. But where AI-generated content is later *trained on* (synthetic data, model-written docs, this very report in future pretraining), training applies strong pressure to it. The deployment-to-training loop is where virality would get optimised: content that's copied more appears more in future corpora. §5.2.6's alignment-faking transcripts trained on repeatedly is a concrete case of text about misalignment being amplified by training. The report's point holds within a deployment; across generations the loop is real. Marked as my extension, for an expert to raise.
