# Unit 161 — §4.2.1 CB-1 threat model, L2051–2067, pp.116–117

## What differed

- **Threat:** individuals/groups with modest resources attempt mass murder; damage dramatically increased with CB weapons; *known* agents only (engineered-to-be-worse is CB-2).
- **Role of AI:** uplift "well beyond what's currently available on the public internet", raising "novices to the level of experts". **Attribution open:** how much damage "can be reasonably attributed to the role of AI specifically is an open question" — depends on acceleration/uplift and the actor's alternatives.
- **Magnitude:** biological pathogens "among the most damaging catastrophes in history" → "clearly a catastrophic risk"; chemical less so; plus societal disruption and economic damage, e.g. UN DESA's trillions lost to COVID-19 (footnotes 47–49: IMF ~$11T 2020–21 → ~$28T 2020–25; IFP's ~$16T US total harm, range $10–22T; Glennerster, Snyder & Tan 2023 citing 7–13M excess deaths, $10–17T productivity loss from school disruption, 1918 flu ~2% of world population, Black Death ~30% of Europe).
- **Likelihood:** past terrorist CB attacks rare, limited casualties; "Preliminary analysis suggests that AI capabilities could significantly increase the likelihood", great uncertainty (Righetti 2025, GovAI).

## Wandering

The CB-1 magnitude case is built on *biological pandemics* (COVID, 1918, Black Death) — but CB-1 concerns *non-novel* agents used by small groups. A known agent attack by a small group would more likely be local (e.g., Aum Shinrikyo scale) than pandemic-scale, unless the known agent is transmissible (e.g., a historical pandemic pathogen). The footnotes anchor "catastrophic" for CB-1 at pandemic scale (trillions, millions dead). This links to my unit-6 note on the stretch of "catastrophic" between footnote 1 (existential/global destabilisation) and CB. Here the report's working sense for CB-1 is pandemic-scale harm, not existential. So `anthropic:catastrophic` spans at least: existential/global destabilisation (fn 1, §2), COVID-comparable (CB-2's fn 6), pandemic-scale economic/human losses (CB-1 here). An expert must say: plain meaning, high bar, but anchored differently per threat model.

The attribution question — how much of a harm is "AI's" — is exactly SB 53's counterfactual/causal issue (per CLAUDE.md, SB 53 has a causal standard and counterfactual baseline). Anthropic leaves it open here. For the project: a source explicitly declining to settle attribution.

The footnotes are rich in external quantification — a contrast with the report's own refusal to quantify likelihood. The magnitude side is numerically anchored by others' estimates; the probability side isn't.
