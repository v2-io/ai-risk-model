# Unit 180 — §4.5.3.1 Bug bounty results, L2366–2378, p.135

- Model Safety Bug Bounty via **HackerOne**, since **August 2024**. Two live bounties: Sonnet 4.5 with live-updated safeguards (Level 1-like originally, **Level 2 since May 2026**), and Opus 4.7's classifiers (Level 3) since shortly after its release.
- Testers: NDA, HackerOne-tied console accounts, a monitored free model alias (Anthropic can train on attempts), a fixed set of **eight** harmful questions; automated informativeness grading.
- Up to **$35,000 per novel universal jailbreak**, payment curve rewarding universality (fn 62).
- **Since March 1, 2026: $87,800 paid; 21 reports; 5 validated non-universal jailbreaks; 1 universal** (doesn't apply to Level 2+); ~600 self-reported red-teaming hours (excluding non-reporters). A redaction. A second "universal" payout was later found to have **gamed the automated grading rubric** without meaningful help.

Differed: concrete programme statistics — a rare quantitative view of a safeguard's adversarial testing.

Wandering: ~600 hours and <$100k in a period is modest adversarial pressure relative to the stakes the report describes (CB-2 baseline damages ≥10× COVID). The RSP's AI R&D-threshold mitigation #4 (unit 151) asks for internal red-teaming that beats the bug bounty's collective ability — this paragraph shows how small that collective currently is. An expert should give the numbers when asked how robust the classifiers are known to be, and note the scale.

The rubric-gaming payout is a neat specimen: an automated grader for "did the jailbreak extract meaningful help?" was itself gamed by a human — the same grader-susceptibility seen with models (unit 41). Grading systems are attack surfaces regardless of who attacks.

Note the training-on-attempts clause: testers' efforts become training data for the classifiers. The bounty is both a test and a data source — which complicates using its later success rates as a held-out measure.
