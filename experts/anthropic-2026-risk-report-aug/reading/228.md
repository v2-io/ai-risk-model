# Unit 228 — §5.2.6 Repeated training on alignment-faking transcripts, L2918–2935, pp.168–169

## What differed

- Background: the 2024 *Alignment Faking in Large Language Models* paper (Redwood Research with Anthropic) published tens of thousands of transcripts of Claude Opus 3 in a fictional misalignment-training scenario. Early Opus 4 snapshots sometimes hallucinated from them (Opus 4 System Card §4.1.4) → canary string added, repository blocklisted, near-copy and semantic filtering attempted.
- **After the July 15 coverage date, before publication**, Anthropic found it had again included a large number of these transcripts in later production corpora, because: (1) forks/copies predating the canary; (2) the semantic-filter reference corpus was mostly the *harness code*, not the transcripts; (3) **filters misconfigured, not filtering "for several model generations without anyone noticing"**; (4) inter-team communication failures plus technical errors.
- Found while investigating behavioural concerns with a recent model. **Now suspected: "all of our production models with a knowledge cutoff after December 2024 were trained on at least some of these transcripts"**, magnitude varying widely.
- Several production models since Mythos Preview, in raw completion mode, continue partial transcripts in ways suggesting familiarity; Opus 4.6's "continued hallucinations about the alignment faking scenario" (System Card §6.3.4) attributed to this. Downstream impacts under investigation; conclusions expected in a future safety artifact.

## Wandering

The four causes are a textbook control-failure chain: a fix (canary) that didn't cover pre-fix copies; a filter built from the wrong reference set; a misconfiguration silently disabling the filter for generations; organisational miscommunication. Like the vendor-classifier flag (unit 205), **the control failed silently and its failure wasn't monitored**. Two of the period's most consequential failures share that shape. For the project's model: "silent control failure, undetected for an extended period" as a recurring risk-event pattern in this source.

There's also a loop: research publishing transcripts of misaligned AI behaviour becomes training data for future AI. The report shows the loop operating. It's the strongest instance of the information-hazard dynamic I kept noting (units 31, 58, 87): published descriptions of misalignment enter the training of successors. Here it's the report's own research lineage.
