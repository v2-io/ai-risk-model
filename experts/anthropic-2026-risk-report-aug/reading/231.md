## What differed

- **Policy:** supported **California SB 53**; opposed "premature and overly blunt" federal preemption of state AI regulation (others largely supportive/neutral); **first frontier developer to endorse Illinois SB 315** (signed July), testified for it, opposed a competing liability-shield bill; endorsed **Massachusetts** legislation. **June: "Advanced AI Framework"** — "the most ambitious regulatory proposal put forward by any AI developer": **independent evaluation of risk reports** and enabling the **US federal government to block or deter the release of dangerous models**. Rationale: a field-leading developer has unique credibility with policymakers.
- **Robust safeguards:** believes it leads the industry ("based in large part on internal testing as well as informal reputation"). **Plan to require 30-day data retention on its most capable models** — expected to be unpopular vs zero retention and a business risk — "essential to detect and prevent sophisticated attacks that span multiple requests", especially **offensive cyber and biological weapons development, "where no single request looks harmful on its own"**, and attacks on customers via compromised accounts/keys (seen in the wild, caught via cross-request analysis). Retained data not used for training without explicit customer approval.
- **Model character, alignment and welfare:** "very important to the company that Claude follows its Constitution"; heavy resourcing of immediate alignment issues and alignment science (especially interpretability); believes it resources and prioritises alignment more and produces models with fewer concerning behaviours; few public metrics — on **Petri 3.0** (open-source alignment audit built by Anthropic, now maintained by the nonprofit **Meridian Labs**, run cross-lab by Meridian and **UK AISI**), Mythos 5 tied with other Claude models as best-aligned publicly accessible (sentence continues).
