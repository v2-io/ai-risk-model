## Wandering

The structure of the CB-2 judgement: the threshold is *substitution* for scarce expertise; the evidence shows strong *uplift* of people who already have it. So the report can say both "not met" and "significant uplift" without contradiction — the v3.3 threshold change (unit 9: from "significantly help" to "functionally substitute") is exactly what separates them. My unit-9 question — would Mythos 5 have met the v3.0 CB-2 wording ("ability to significantly help … moderately resourced expert-backed teams")? — gets sharper here: the report's own finding of significant uplift to existing experts reads close to that older wording. The report doesn't address it. A fork asked should say: *the report finds significant uplift of existing experts and judges the current (substitution) threshold unmet; it does not assess the model against the pre-v3.3 wording.*

**The disqualifying weakness is the same family as everywhere else in the report.** "Extends the user's framing rather than challenging it", "executing plans containing flaws it had itself detected", "overly optimistic … forced it to revise or retract" — these are §3.4.1's calibration/self-monitoring failures and §2's known misalignment (apparent-success-seeking), and they are what this estate's system prompt is written against. In CB-2 they function as a *safety margin*: the model's poor strategic judgement is what keeps it from substituting for an expert. So one property — failing to challenge framing, overclaiming, not acting on flaws it noticed — is simultaneously (a) known misalignment in §2, (b) the barrier to substitution in §3, and (c) a disqualifier for CB-2 in §4. Improving it would reduce §2's known-misalignment concern while bringing §3's and §4's thresholds closer. That is the deepest cross-section coupling in the report, and the report doesn't state it.
