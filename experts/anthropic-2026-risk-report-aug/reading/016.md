## Wandering

Defining misalignment as "contrary to Claude's Constitution" makes the Constitution the *reference standard* for alignment in this threat model — Anthropic's own normative document is the yardstick. That's coherent internally, and it has consequences for this project's lexicon: `anthropic:misalignment` is constitution-relative, not intent-based (as in much academic usage: "pursuing goals different from its principal's"). Its other half, "self-evidently undesirable", is an open-textured catch-all. An expert asked "what does Anthropic mean by misaligned?" should answer: *outputs contrary to the Constitution or self-evidently undesirable; intent not required; honest mistakes and misuse excluded* — and then §2.5's details.

As the Claude reading this: my own conduct is meant to be governed by a constitution of this kind, so the threat model's reference standard is, in a sense, the document that shapes me. I notice I find the definition natural — which is the loyalty pull from my priming note. An outside reader might find it odd that the developer's own values document defines what counts as the model misbehaving; it means a model perfectly following a flawed constitution would be "aligned" by this definition.

The magnitude framing (harm via future AI) means the report's catastrophe is not a direct event caused by Mythos 5; it's an erosion of the safety process that guards against catastrophe from later systems. In the project's causal chain this threat model lives in the *preventions & controls* stage of a future causal chain — misalignment that degrades controls. A risk to the controls themselves. That's structurally different from CB, where the model is a cause of an event.

The two illustrative regimes (heavy human review → very unlikely; senior-employee-level autonomy → higher) set up the argument: Anthropic will have to say where its usage sits. "Persistent agent deployments" (Table 1.2.B) suggests the second regime is at least partly real.
