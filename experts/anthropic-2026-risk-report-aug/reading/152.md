# Unit 152 — §3.8 Overall assessment, L1961–1965, p.112

"near-term risk of a catastrophe under this threat model is low, due to the capability limitations noted above." Also describes the **marginal** risk, **and** holds for the **absolute** (industry-wide-if-all-like-us) risk. **"However, we believe it is plausible that this threat model will become a major concern in the next 6–12 months."**

Differed: §3.8 gives both marginal and absolute statements, as §1.1 promised. That confirms §2.19 omitted the absolute statement for misalignment (unit 93). A fork comparing sections should note the asymmetry: §3 states absolute risk; §2 doesn't.

And the forecast — **"plausible … major concern in the next 6–12 months"** (i.e., by roughly Jan–Jul 2027 from the July 2026 coverage date) — is the most forward-leaning sentence in the report so far. It lines up with the "eyes on everything" target of Jan 1, 2027 (unit 151): the mitigation target date falls inside the window in which the threat may become major. An expert should place these two dates side by side.

Wandering: "low … due to the capability limitations" — the rating rests wholly on capability, as noted at unit 128 (mitigations insufficient if the threshold is crossed). So the rating's half-life is the time to the threshold, which the authors put at plausibly 6–12 months for "major concern". A rating with a stated near expiry. For the project's assertion records: an assessment plus a forecast of its own obsolescence.

Absolute = marginal = low here: if all developers were like Anthropic, still low, because the capability limits are shared. That reasoning is general: when risk is capability-gated and capabilities are similar across labs, marginal and absolute converge.
