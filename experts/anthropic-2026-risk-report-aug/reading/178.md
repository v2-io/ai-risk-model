# Unit 178 — §4.5.2.2 Wider coverage for Fable 5, L2351–2360, p.134

- Fable 5 = "our most capable widely deployed model **nearing the CB-2 threshold**". Its classifier blocks "the vast majority of potentially dual use usage relevant to research biology"; as of the coverage date "particularly conservative, and in practice blocks almost all queries relevant to biology" — broader than ideal; since then more targeted classifiers with fewer false positives deployed.
- Coverage evaluations: (1) researcher-style questions generated from recent biology literature in offensive-relevant topic areas: blocked 100% (vs 17% for the harmful-biology classifier); (2) sampled production traffic filtered for research-biology relevance: 94% flagged, the remaining 6% judged mostly benign non-research. (3) Internal biology experts testing for over an hour each couldn't get research biology in the covered domains — "our strongest evidence about the coverage", but testers weren't selected for red-teaming skill, so evidence about coverage, not robustness.

Differed: "nearing the CB-2 threshold" — a phrase stronger than anything in the executive summary (which said models "do not yet meet" CB-2). An expert should quote it: the report itself describes Fable 5 (i.e., Mythos 5) as *nearing* CB-2.

Wandering: the control's cost is stated plainly: at the coverage date, general-access Fable 5 refused nearly all biology. That's a large capability withheld from legitimate users — biologists, students, clinicians — as the price of CB-2 risk control under the dual-use limit (unit 177). For the project's "who is affected" view: legitimate research-biology users are affected *by the control*, not by the hazard. Since then, narrower classifiers reduce false positives — the trade-off is being tuned.

The evaluation design is clean in what it claims: coverage (does it fire on the topic?) vs robustness (can a skilled adversary evade it?). The report keeps them apart; a fork should too.
