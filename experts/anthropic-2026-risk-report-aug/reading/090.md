# Unit 90 — §2.18 Pre-internal-deployment review process, L1234–1258, pp.70–71

## What differed

- **New since the previous report.** Before approving a new model for "easy or broad internal use" (e.g. availability in the most-used internal tools). fn 29: includes **non-frontier models** ("a less capable model with very concerning behavioral propensities could pose significant risks"); later snapshots aren't re-reviewed unless the pipeline changes substantially or the prior review was limited (e.g. "only the next N training steps").
- **Who:** asynchronous discussion + a live meeting of researchers in alignment training, alignment testing, control, security, interpretability; the alignment stress-testing team; others by discretion. **Live meeting skippable for low-risk models.**
- **Evidence gaps vs this report:** (1) based on testing during training, not the exact snapshot deployed — the meeting judges representativeness, sometimes with short evals on the precise model; (2) much shorter, fewer hours than full audits; human-intensive evidence not reliably collected; **infrastructure issues have sometimes limited data**; (3) no pilot-usage evidence yet (a key part of the assessment) → lower confidence before internal deployment.
- Two pre-release Mythos 5 versions reviewed; both approved.
- **Model 2:** piloted a **staged deployment** — first on internal surfaces "with stronger blocking controls against dangerous actions", then unrestricted.
- **"for most models, including all early snapshots of models intended for eventual broad public release, we do not have strict technical safeguards on internal deployment, and individual technical employees can and occasionally will spin up small-scale custom deployments for evaluation or manual testing."** The review gates only easy/broad use.

## Wandering

The last paragraph is a governance fact an expert must keep: internal deployment of unreviewed snapshots by individual employees is possible and happens ("occasionally"), small-scale. The review is a gate on *broad* use, not on *any* use. For the threat model (misaligned model with powerful internal affordances), small-scale custom deployments by employees are a surface outside the gate — presumably still under monitoring? Not said here. Question for an expert: *are employee-spun custom deployments of unreviewed snapshots covered by offline monitoring?* (Appendix 2.23 may say.)

"Who decides" is not named: the review produces approval, but no named decision-maker (RSO? CEO? a committee chair?). The participants are listed; the authority isn't. For the project's "who decides" view, that's a gap in the source, not something to fill by inference.

Note that Claim 3.4 relied heavily on pilot internal usage evidence — which, by item (3), doesn't exist at review time. So the decision to deploy internally is taken on weaker evidence than the report's assessment; the report's assessment is retrospective, after months of use. A model's first internal weeks are its least-assessed period, and it's then that it has the affordances the threat model worries about. Model 2's staged deployment with stronger blocking controls is the response to exactly this, piloted for the first time. The report is honest that this is a pilot.

"Infrastructure issues in our alignment testing pipeline which limited the range of data we could collect" — a process failure stated in passing. Not in the §5.2 list (yet).
