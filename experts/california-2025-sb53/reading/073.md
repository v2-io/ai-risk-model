# Unit 73 · L133 · p.5

> (6) Revisiting and updating the frontier AI framework, including any criteria that trigger updates and how the large frontier developer determines when its frontier models are substantially modified enough to require disclosures pursuant to subdivision (c).

## What differed

My recall was exact apart from the cross-reference: it reads "subdivision (c)", not "subdivision (c) of Section 22757.12". It's an in-section reference.

## Wandering

- **The developer defines its own trigger for re-disclosure.** Subdivision (c), presumably the transparency report at deployment, applies to "new" or "substantially modified" frontier models. Who decides what is "substantially modified enough"? The framework describes *how the developer determines* it. So the transparency-report trigger for updated models is **self-defined**. That adds to the pattern from units 55 and 58, where scope turned on the developer's stated "primary purpose" or "design". Now the reporting trigger does too. A lab that ships frequent model updates (weekly checkpoints, silent version bumps) can set its own bar for when a transparency report is owed. **A question someone should ask: who decides whether a model update needs a new transparency report?** The developer, per its own framework criteria. The state enforces only consistency with those criteria.
- "Criteria that trigger updates": the framework must say when *it* gets revised. With (a)'s "comply with", the developer is bound to revisit on its own stated triggers. And the annual review (likely later: "review and, as appropriate, update its frontier AI framework at least once a year" [recall]) is the floor.
- This item answers my unit-69 worry in part: revisions aren't banned or ratcheted, but their *criteria* must be stated. Weakening a framework is allowed, if done by the stated process. Whether a weakening must be *disclosed and justified* is probably elsewhere (e.g., "publish any material modification and a justification within 30 days" [recall]).
