# Unit 120 · L187 · p.7

> (h) The Office of Emergency Services may adopt regulations designating one or more federal laws, regulations, or guidance documents that meet all of the following conditions for the purposes of subdivision (i):

## What differed

As recalled: the federal-equivalence mechanism.

## Wandering

- **Federal laws can stand in for state requirements.** OES *may* designate federal laws, regulations, or even **guidance documents** whose incident-reporting standards meet conditions (to come). Then, under (i), a developer complying with them is presumably deemed compliant with SB 53's incident reporting. This is a **deference valve**: if the federal government builds an equivalent regime, California steps back. The politics are visible. The bill anticipates federal preemption pressure and offers to give way on equivalence, not on demand.
- **"Guidance documents"** are not law. A federal *guidance document* (non-binding agency guidance) could be designated. Then a developer following voluntary federal guidance could satisfy a binding state duty. Unusual. It means California's requirement could be met by something the federal government doesn't itself require. The conditions will matter: presumably the designated document must "impose or state standards", and the developer must actually comply with it.
- "**May**": OES has discretion, and needn't designate anything.
- "**For the purposes of subdivision (i)**": the effect is in (i). This is another forward reference inside a section, normal enough.
- This is also the first rulemaking authority I've seen in the TFAIA: OES "may adopt regulations". Narrow, only for designation.
