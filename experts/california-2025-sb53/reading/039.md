# Unit 39 · L91–93 · p.3

> 22757.11. For purposes of this chapter:
> (a) "Affiliate" means a person controlling, controlled by, or under common control with a specified person, directly or indirectly, through one or more intermediaries.

## What differed

My first alphabetical option. It's the standard securities-law affiliate definition (Rule 405, SEC).

## Wandering

- **"For purposes of this chapter."** The definitions are scoped to B&P Chapter 25.1. They don't automatically reach the Labor Code chapter. So the whistleblower chapter must either cross-reference ("'catastrophic risk' has the meaning defined in Section 22757.11 of the Business and Professions Code") or define the terms itself. Unit 4's question becomes more pointed: **either the Labor Code imports these definitions, or SB 53 contains two definitions of catastrophic risk.**
- Why is "affiliate" defined? Probably for revenue aggregation. A "large frontier developer" likely counts "annual gross revenues in excess of $500,000,000" *together with its affiliates* [recall]. Without that, a lab could split revenue across entities and stay under the bar. A "frontier developer" may also be defined to include an affiliate that trained the model. The definition is anti-evasion plumbing.
- "**Person**" is used but not yet defined. Probably defined later (alphabetically, "Person" would come after "Large frontier developer"). In California statutes "person" often includes corporations, partnerships, LLCs and so on. If it includes natural persons, then an individual could in principle be a "frontier developer". Unusual, but it matters for the "developer" collision CLAUDE.md lists: NIST counts individual people as "developers". In SB 53, can a natural person be a frontier developer? **Watch the definition of "person".**
- "Directly or indirectly, through one or more intermediaries" covers corporate chains. Relevant to, say, an AI lab that is a capped-profit subsidiary of a nonprofit (OpenAI's structure as of 2025), or that is controlled by a larger tech company (DeepMind under Alphabet). Under this definition Alphabet and DeepMind are affiliates, so Alphabet's revenue could count toward "large". Interesting for the question "is lab X a large frontier developer?". Usually trivially yes for big tech; less trivial for pure-play labs with smaller revenue.
