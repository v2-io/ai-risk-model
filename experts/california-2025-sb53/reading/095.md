## Wandering

- **A hortatory norm inside operative text.** "Encouraged, but not required" is neither a duty nor a permission. It's an exhortation, legally inert. For the project's norm modelling (the gamma research on speech acts and norms), this is a clean specimen of a third deontic category: **recommendation / encouragement**, distinct from obligation ("shall") and permission ("may"). An assertion extractor that maps every statutory sentence to obligation-or-permission will mis-type this one. And "not required" explicitly negates obligation, a self-declared non-duty.
- What exactly is encouraged? The disclosures "described in this subdivision" are already *required* by (1) and (2). So the encouragement attaches to the *quality* qualifier: "consistent with, or superior to, industry best practices". It means: you must disclose these items, and we'd like you to do it at least as well as the best practice in the industry. So the standard of disclosure *quality* is encouraged, not required. Required content, encouraged quality. That's precise, and it answers "does SB 53 set a quality standard for transparency reports?": no, it encourages one.
- "Industry best practices" again treats industry practice as the benchmark, even for the *better* ("superior to"). The law imagines that industry sets the ceiling as well as the floor.
