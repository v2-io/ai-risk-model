# Unit 52 · L109 · p.4

> (3) Loss of control of a frontier model causing death or bodily injury.

## What differed

My recall was exact. And the watch item from last unit is confirmed: the incident list says **"loss of control"**, while the catastrophic-risk scenario said **"evading the control of its frontier developer or user"**.

## Wandering

- **Two control concepts in adjacent definitions:**
  - (c)(1)(C), catastrophic risk: the model is "*evading* the control of its frontier developer or user". The model's act, agentive.
  - (d)(3), critical safety incident: "*loss of control* of a frontier model". The controller's state, passive, with no agent named and no controller named (whose control?).

  These differ in agency, and in *whose* control (named in (C), unnamed in (3)). "Loss of control" in (3) would cover a runaway model with no evasive intent, which (C) arguably wouldn't. So a non-evasive runaway causing one injury is a critical safety incident (3), while the risk of a non-evasive runaway causing mass harm isn't, on a strict reading, a catastrophic risk under (C)! (It might fall under (B) if it's a cyberattack or crime-equivalent.) **That's an inversion:** the smaller event is covered more broadly than the larger risk. I'd mark this as my reading, plausibly contestable. A court might read "evading" broadly. But it's the kind of seam the project exists to find: the same document using "control" two ways, one word apart.
  - Lexicon, then: SB 53 has two concepts, *evading control* (a model act; scenario in catastrophic risk) and *loss of control* (a controller state; incident kind). Neither is defined further. Plus the finding (j)'s "loss of control" in register.
- "**Causing** death or bodily injury": "causing", where (1) said "results in". Different causal verbs again. "Causing" may be read as stricter (proximate cause) than "results in" (consequence). Probably a stylistic variation, but a careful source expert notes that the incident items use three causal phrasings: *results in* (1), *resulting from* (2), *causing* (3).
- Again "bodily injury" with no count: one person suffices.
- A thought on what's missing: loss of control *without* any injury (a model that escapes its sandbox, copies itself out, but hurts no one) isn't covered by (3). Self-exfiltration by a model is arguably "exfiltration of the model weights" under (1), unauthorized, but (1) also requires injury. So **a model self-exfiltrating and running loose, with no injury yet, may not be a critical safety incident under (1) or (3).** Item (4) (deception to subvert controls), if it reads as I recall, might cover it, through "subvert the controls or monitoring".
