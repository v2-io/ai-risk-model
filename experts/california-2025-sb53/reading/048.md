# Unit 48 · L105 · p.4

> (C) Harm caused by a frontier model in combination with other software if the frontier model did not materially contribute to the harm.

## What differed

As recalled. So the exclusion list is exactly three items.

## Wandering

- **Logically redundant with the stem.** The stem already requires that the developer's activity "will materially contribute". If the frontier model "did not materially contribute", the stem fails and there's no catastrophic risk to exclude. So (C) excludes nothing new. It's a **clarification**, probably to reassure developers that a frontier model used as one small component in a larger software system (an agent scaffold, a third-party application) won't be blamed for that system's harms. Statutes often carry belt-and-suspenders clauses for political reasons. A source expert should say so plainly when asked: (C) restates the stem's causal standard for a combination context.
  - One subtle difference, though: the stem says "will materially contribute to the death … or … damage" (prospective), and (C) says "did not materially contribute to the harm" (past tense). (C) is written from the incident's perspective, after the fact, while the stem is prospective. In a definition of *risk*, a retrospective exclusion is a little incoherent. How do you assess *before* harm whether a model *did not* contribute? It reads as if drafted with litigation over an actual incident in mind.
- **Category slip in the list.** The exclusions are "a … risk **from** any of the following". (A) information outputs and (B) federal activity are *sources* of risk. (C) is "**Harm caused by** …", a harm, not a source. "A risk from harm caused by …" is grammatically odd. The drafters meant "risk of harm caused by …" or "risk from a frontier model's use in combination …". It's a small drafting seam where the genus slips from *source of risk* to *harm*. An assertion extractor that types each exclusion as "excluded risk source" will mistype (C).
- **"In combination with other software"**: only *software*. A frontier model in combination with *humans* (human-AI teams) or *hardware* (robots, lab automation) isn't addressed by (C). That's no gap, because the stem's materiality requirement still applies. But it shows what the drafters imagined: the model inside a software stack, the agentic-scaffold case.
- **"Caused by"** sits next to "contribute". (C) talks about harm "caused by a frontier model in combination", then about whether the model "materially contributed". So harm can be *caused by* the combination while the model doesn't materially contribute. Causation sits with the combination, contribution with the component. That's a clean enough distinction in the end.
- The definition is complete now. Running inventory of its concepts:
  1. genus: risk;
  2. knowledge: foreseeable;
  3. materiality of the risk;
  4. conduct: development/storage/use/deployment by the frontier developer;
  5. causal: will materially contribute;
  6. harm thresholds: >50 people death-or-serious-injury (combined), or >$1B **property** damage/loss;
  7. counting: single incident (scope ambiguous);
  8. scenario: closed list of model doings (CBRN expert assistance; autonomous cyberattack or crime-equivalent conduct, no meaningful oversight; evading control);
  9. baseline: otherwise publicly accessible information excluded (info outputs only);
  10. actor carve-out: lawful federal activity;
  11. combination clarification.

  That's at least ten or eleven separable concepts on my count, more than CLAUDE.md's "at least seven" (consistent with "at least"). Where CLAUDE.md says the definition "reaches into two other provisions", I haven't seen that yet. It may be that "catastrophic risk" is used *inside* another definition ("critical safety incident" (2): materialization of a catastrophic risk) and in the whistleblower trigger. I'll watch.
