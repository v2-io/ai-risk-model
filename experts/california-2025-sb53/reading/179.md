# Unit 179 · L259 · p.10

> (B) Engaging in conduct with no meaningful human oversight, intervention, or supervision that is either a cyberattack or, if committed by a human, would constitute the crime of murder, assault, extortion, or theft, including theft by false pretense.

## What differed

**It isn't identical.** Comparing word by word with the TFAIA's (c)(1)(B) (u43):

- TFAIA: "… that is either a cyberattack or, **if the conduct had been committed by a human**, would constitute the crime of …"
- Labor Code: "… that is either a cyberattack or, **if committed by a human**, would constitute the crime of …"

"The conduct had been" is gone.

## Wandering

- **Does the change alter the meaning?** Probably not. Both describe a human doing the same conduct and ask whether it would be a crime. The TFAIA's version is explicitly counterfactual ("had been", subjunctive). The Labor Code's is compressed, with "committed" left to attach to "conduct" implicitly. A court would read them the same.
- **What it shows is that the copy was edited.** The Labor Code definition wasn't pasted from the final TFAIA text. Either one of them was revised after the copy was made, or someone tidied one and not the other. So the two definitions have **separate drafting histories**, and they may diverge elsewhere too. **Every remaining item in §1107(a) needs the same word-by-word comparison.** An extractor that assumes "same as TFAIA except foundation/frontier" would miss this kind of drift, even where it's harmless.
- For quoting: anyone who quotes "SB 53's (B)" needs to say which version. The human-counterfactual phrasing differs by provision.
- Rules of statutory interpretation create a small trap here. When a legislature uses different words in parallel provisions, courts sometimes presume it meant different things. A litigator *could* argue that "if committed by a human" (the Labor Code) differs from "if the conduct had been committed by a human" (the TFAIA). It's a weak argument here, but this is how textual drift turns into legal argument.
