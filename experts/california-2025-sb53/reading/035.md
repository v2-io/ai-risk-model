# Unit 35 · L83 · p.3

> (o) The recent release of the Governor's California Report on Frontier AI Policy and testimony from legislative hearings on artificial intelligence before the Legislature reflect the advances in AI model capabilities that could pose potential catastrophic risk in frontier artificial intelligence, which this act aims to address.

## What differed

The report is named in full this time: "the Governor's California Report on Frontier AI Policy", the title I recalled in unit 23. A second evidentiary basis is added: "testimony from legislative hearings". And there's an explicit aim clause: "which this act aims to address".

## Wandering

- **The statute's purpose, in its own words:** "advances in AI model capabilities that could pose potential catastrophic risk in frontier artificial intelligence, **which this act aims to address**." So for liberal construction, the aim is catastrophic risk from frontier capability advances. That's narrower and more safety-focused than the trust, competition and innovation purposes in (a)–(g). The findings now hold two purpose registers: **purposes of transparency** (trust, accountability, competition, evidence) and **the purpose of the act** (address potential catastrophic risk). A court construing liberally "to effectuate its purposes" would probably look to (o) first, as the only finding that says "this act aims".
- **Two evidentiary bases:** the JCPWG report, now named twice (c, o), and legislative hearing testimony. The testimony isn't in the text, of course. Which hearings? The Senate Judiciary and Assembly Privacy committees, I'd guess. Their committee analyses on leginfo ("Bill Analysis" tab) are another source layer around SB 53, with their own claims about what it does. The corpus could hold them if anyone wants the legislative history.
- **"Could pose potential catastrophic risk"** makes three modal layers: *could*, *potential*, *risk*. This is how the findings consistently talk about catastrophe: always at two or three removes from assertion. With (j)'s "there is concern that", the findings never assert in their own voice that frontier AI *does* pose catastrophic risk. They assert only that it could, potentially, and that there is concern. If anyone asks "Does SB 53 claim that frontier AI poses catastrophic risk?", the precise answer is: **it asserts the possibility and acts on it, but never claims the risk is actual.** That's the honest register for a precautionary statute, and the hedge should be recorded with it.
- "Frontier artificial intelligence" without a following noun is the field, not a model. Yet another variant.
- "Recent release" dates the bill: the final JCPWG report came out in June 2025, and SB 53 was amended heavily after it. The amendments in July–September likely imported the report's recommendations. That's a lineage fact best checked against the version history, which my next readings (earlier versions) could do.
