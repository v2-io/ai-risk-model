# Unit 219 · L306 · p.12

> (c) The duties and obligations imposed by this act are cumulative with any other duties or obligations imposed under other law and shall not be construed to relieve any party from any duties or obligations imposed under other law and do not limit any rights or remedies under existing law.

## What differed

It's a cumulation clause for the **whole act**, not the preemption clause I predicted.

## Wandering

- **This corrects unit 215.** There I said the TFAIA lacks a cumulative clause, which made it asymmetric with the Labor Code chapter. SEC. 5(c) supplies one for **the whole act**. So the asymmetry was only at the chapter level. At the act level, both chapters are cumulative. That's another case of concluding about part of a document before reading the rest (as in u216). I'm noting the pattern in myself: **twice in the last few units I drew structural conclusions from a chapter's end that the bill's general sections then overturned.** In a statute, the general provisions come last, so any conclusion of the form "X lacks Y" made mid-bill is provisional until the end. That's a useful rule for future forks.
- **Two consequences:**
  1. **Tort duties survive.** "Shall not be construed to relieve any party from any duties … imposed under other law". Unit 36's tort-shadow point gets stronger. SB 53 doesn't displace the common-law duty of care, and finding (p) acknowledges it. So **SB 53 is a floor, not a safe harbour**: complying with the TFAIA doesn't shield a developer from negligence claims.
  2. **Other remedies survive.** "Do not limit any rights or remedies under existing law". The §17200 question from u143–144 leans toward *available*. An unfair-competition action predicated on a TFAIA violation is not limited by this act, though the AG-only clause in §22757.15(b) still restricts *the civil penalty in that section*. My reading: a §17200 "unlawful" claim based on a TFAIA violation is likely still possible, by whoever has §17200 standing. That's my legal inference, not settled text. A court could read §22757.15(b)'s "only by the Attorney General" as showing intent to exclude other enforcers. It's genuinely contestable, and I'd mark it so.
- "**Any party**" includes the state agencies too, presumably.
