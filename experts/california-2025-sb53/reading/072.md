## Wandering

- **This partly corrects the JCPWG-divergence note I made in unit 23.** There I said SB 53 might not have third-party evaluation. It does, but only as something a framework must *describe how it approaches*. A developer whose framework says "we approach using third parties by engaging them when we judge it useful" satisfies (5) and must then comply. A framework saying "we do not use third parties" might also satisfy (5), since it describes an approach (none). So: **SB 53 requires disclosure of the developer's approach to third-party evaluation, not third-party evaluation itself.** A precise answer to "does SB 53 mandate third-party audits?": no, it mandates describing one's approach to using third parties, and then following that description. The JCPWG pushed harder on third-party assessment, as I recall it, so the divergence holds in substance, softened in form. I'm keeping the u23 note as written and adding this refinement.
- "Effectiveness of mitigations" is the closest thing to an adequacy check yet, and it's routed to third parties.
