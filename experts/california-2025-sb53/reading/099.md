## Wandering

- **A conjunctive safe harbour**: the statement must be made in **good faith** (subjective, honest belief) **and** be **reasonable under the circumstances** (objective). Both are needed to escape. So a materially false statement is a violation unless the developer both believed it and was reasonable to. In effect a **negligence standard** for false statements: honest-but-unreasonable errors are violations, and honest, reasonable errors aren't.
- **The epistemic-standards family in SB 53 is now four members:**
  1. "*reasonable cause to believe*": whistleblower anti-retaliation (per the digest, u14), objective;
  2. "*believes in good faith*": internal anonymous channel (per the digest, u15), subjective;
  3. "*good faith and … reasonable under the circumstances*": false-statement safe harbour, both;
  4. "*foreseeable*": catastrophic risk's knowledge standard, observer unspecified.

  Plus "*reasonable*" schedule (u96). A source expert asked "what knowledge or belief standard does SB 53 use?" should answer: **four different ones, by provision.** This is a cross-provision distinction a summary would almost certainly flatten.
- Applied to the system-card scenario (u97): a lab that reports in good faith, after reasonable evaluations, that a model lacks a dangerous capability, which later turns out false, is protected. A lab that skipped the evaluations its framework promised and made the same claim is not, because unreasonable. So the safe harbour rewards *process*, which ties false-statement liability to framework compliance.
- "This subdivision" means (e) as a whole, so both (A) and (B).
