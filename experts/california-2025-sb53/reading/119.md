# Unit 119 · L186 · p.7

> (3) The Office of Emergency Services shall transmit a report pursuant to this subdivision to the Legislature, pursuant to Section 9795, and to the Governor.

## What differed

This answers my open question from u117–118: the annual report goes to the **Legislature and the Governor**. It isn't published to the public by statute.

## Wandering

- **The public alert is indirect.** "Section 9795" is Government Code §9795, the standard procedure for agency reports to the Legislature (filed with the Legislative Counsel and the Secretary of the Senate, typically in electronic form). Such reports are usually available publicly through the Legislature's reports database, I believe. But SB 53 doesn't direct OES to publish. So: the public sees an anonymized, aggregated, annual report, *if* it finds it in the Legislature's report filings. The (k) purpose "alert … the public" is served at three removes: anonymized, aggregated, filed with the Legislature. A source expert asked "does SB 53 publish incident data?" should answer: **no, not directly. It requires an anonymized aggregate report to the Legislature and Governor, which is likely publicly retrievable through the Legislature's report filings.** The last part is my inference about §9795's practice, not the bill's text.
- Recipients: the Legislature (which can amend the definitions, consistent with (k)/(n)'s "additional legislation may be needed") and the Governor. The information loop is closed through law-making, not public pressure.
