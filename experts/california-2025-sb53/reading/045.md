## Wandering

- The exclusion's grammar repeats the definition's genus, "a foreseeable and material risk", and excludes such risks "from" certain sources. So the exclusions are carved out of the *risk* concept: a risk that *is* foreseeable and material, and *would* otherwise count, is excluded if it comes "from" a listed source. That structure means the exclusions don't deny the risk exists; they deny that SB 53 treats it as catastrophic risk. That matters for the project's own vocabulary. **SB 53's "catastrophic risk" is a regulatory category, not a description of what is dangerous.** Something may be dangerous and catastrophic in ordinary terms and still not be "catastrophic risk" under SB 53. A crosswalk that maps SB 53 catastrophic risk to a general "catastrophic risk" concept should record the exclusions as scope narrowing, which is a declared narrowing of the sort CLAUDE.md's lexicon design anticipates ("declared scope narrowing and widening").
- "Risk *from* any of the following" means the excluded items are risk *sources*. Expected: public information, federal government activity, combined-software harms.
