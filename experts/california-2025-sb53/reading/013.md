# Unit 13 · L48 · p.2

> (3) Existing law prohibits employers and their agents from making, adopting, or enforcing a rule, regulation, or policy preventing an employee from disclosing information to certain entities or … testifying before, any public body … if the employee has reasonable cause to believe that the information discloses a violation of a law … and prohibits retaliation …

## What differed

As predicted: item (3) gives the Labor Code 1102.5 baseline. The general whistleblower statute protects disclosure of *violations of law*.

## Wandering

- The baseline is protection for reporting **illegality**. A frontier-AI danger is often not illegal; a model with dangerous capabilities may break no law. So SB 53's addition is meaningful exactly where it extends protection from *violations of law* to *dangers*, such as "specific and substantial danger … resulting from a catastrophic risk". That's the conceptual jump: whistleblowing about *risk*, not wrongdoing. The digest's choice of baseline makes that contrast visible. The *conjunction* of "danger" with "catastrophic risk" may make the extension narrow, though: the danger has to be specific and substantial, and it has to come from a catastrophic risk as SB 53 defines it. So the >50-deaths/$1B bar quietly gates whistleblower protection. An employee who reports a danger that could kill 30 people may get no protection under this new chapter. They'd fall back on 1102.5, which requires a violation of law. If my recall of the structure is right, that's a non-obvious consequence, and worth checking when I reach the Labor Code text.
- "Employers and their agents", "employee". The new chapter will narrow this to "covered employee" and widen it on the employer side to the "frontier developer". So the same relation, employer to employee, is renamed at both ends. Another candidate lexicon pair: *employee (1102.5)* against *covered employee (SB 53)*.
- "Testifying before … any public body". Will SB 53's version include the Attorney General and federal authorities explicitly? I recall the AG, "a federal authority", and internal managers.
