## Wandering

- **A duty tucked inside a list item.** The frame is "shall take into account all of the following:", followed by "(1) Similar thresholds … **and shall align with** a definition adopted in a federal law or regulation …". The list item carries a *second* duty of a different kind: not "consider" but "align". Grammatically it doesn't fit: the item should be a noun phrase (a thing to take into account) and it's half noun phrase, half command. That's the fourth type-inconsistent enumeration (after u48, u53, u122). SB 53's lists are a repeated drafting weak spot, and an extractor should expect them.
- **The substance is a federal-alignment preference.** In its recommendations, the Department "shall align" with a federal definition (law or regulation, *not* guidance this time), insofar as that's consistent with the chapter's purposes. So if Congress or a federal agency defines "frontier model" in binding law, California's recommendations must follow it, unless that contradicts SB 53's purposes. Here the anti-patchwork politics sit inside the review mechanism. Note the asymmetry with §22757.13(h): incident-reporting equivalence accepts federal *guidance*, while definitional alignment requires federal *law or regulation*.
- "**For the management of catastrophic risk**": a third use of this quasi-term (after the false-statement rule and preemption). It's clearly a stable phrase in the drafters' vocabulary, and it's never defined.
- "**International standards**": the EU's 10^25 is a "similar threshold used in international standards"? The EU AI Act is law, not a standard. ISO or OECD would be standards. Whether the EU threshold counts is a classification question the text leaves to the Department.
