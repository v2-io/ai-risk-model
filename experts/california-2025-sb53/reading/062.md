# Unit 62 · L119 · p.4

> (i) (1) "Frontier model" means a foundation model that was trained using a quantity of computing power greater than 10^26 integer or floating-point operations.

## What differed

My recall was exact, and the exponent survived conversion as "10^26". The web capture kept the superscript as a caret. So the text is safe on this point, though a downstream tool that strips "^" would turn it into "1026", and that's a mistake to watch for. The source text itself is fine.

## Wandering

- **"Greater than 10^26"** is strictly greater. A model trained with exactly 10^26 operations isn't a frontier model. Trivial in practice, but "≥10^26" in a secondary source would be a small misstatement.
- **"Was trained"**, past tense. A model mid-training isn't yet a frontier model. That confirms my reading of (h): the "intends to use" prong is what makes someone a frontier developer *during* training. (h)'s compute clause is redundant for completed models and does real work only for runs in progress.
- **"Integer or floating-point operations"**: both are counted. That closes the loophole of training at integer precision (INT8 and so on) to get under a FLOP-denominated threshold. EO 14110 said "integer or floating-point operations" too, I believe. A lineage edge: **EO 14110 §4.2(b) (10^26 integer or floating-point operations, reporting threshold, revoked Jan 2025) → SB 53 §22757.11(i)(1).** The same number and the same operation type. SB 1047 used 10^26 *plus* a $100M cost threshold. SB 53 drops the cost prong here, and uses revenue instead at the "large" tier. The EU uses 10^25 FLOPs for the systemic-risk presumption, an order of magnitude lower. So:
  - **EU GPAI with systemic risk:** >10^25 FLOPs (presumption, rebuttable, plus Commission designation);
  - **SB 53 frontier model:** >10^26 operations (bright line);
  - **EO 14110:** >10^26 (reporting; revoked).
- **Compute threshold, not capability.** Finding (k) wanted to "track the frontier", but the definition is a fixed number. As efficiency improves, a 10^26 model represents less of the frontier, so more developers cross over time (as compute costs fall). The (n) finding admits small or behind-frontier models may become dangerous. Together they make **a threshold that grows more inclusive over time (more developers cross 10^26) while capturing less of the capability frontier.** Both drifts at once. A review provision would be the mechanism to adjust.
- **"A quantity of computing power"**: compute counted as operations, not FLOP/s. "Computing power" would usually mean a rate. Here it's used for a total count. A small misuse of terms, harmless but imprecise. (EO 14110 had the same, I think.)
- "(1)" again means a (2) follows: the counting rule.
