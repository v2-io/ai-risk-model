## Wandering

- Taken together: a foundation model is an AI model (a system) that is trained on a broad data set, designed for generality of output, and adaptable to a wide range of distinctive tasks. There's no parameter count (EO 14110 had "tens of billions of parameters") and no self-supervision requirement. It's close to the CRFM's original concept, and to the EU AI Act's GPAI model definition in spirit ("displays significant generality and is capable of competently performing a wide range of distinct tasks"). Lexicon near-neighbours:
  - **SB 53 foundation model:** broad data, designed for generality of output, adaptable to a wide range of distinctive tasks;
  - **EU GPAI model:** "trained with a large amount of data using self-supervision at scale, that displays significant generality and is capable of competently performing a wide range of distinct tasks …";
  - **EO 14110 dual-use foundation model** (revoked Jan 2025);
  - **AB 2013 generative AI system or service.**

  SB 53's is *design-based* ("designed for", "adaptable") where the EU's is *capability-based* ("displays", "capable of competently performing"). That's a real difference for the edge cases.
- "Distinctive tasks" against the EU's "distinct tasks". A small wording drift, probably meaningless.
- The definition does almost no narrowing work in practice. Any model above 10^26 FLOPs is a foundation model by any reasonable reading. Its real effect is to exclude specialized models, as I noted.
