# Unit 113 · L180 · p.7

> (d) The Office of Emergency Services shall review critical safety incident reports submitted by frontier developers and may review reports submitted by members of the public.

## What differed

As recalled.

## Wandering

- **There's an asymmetry:** OES *shall* review developers' reports and *may* review the public's. So public reports can sit unread. The public channel exists, but OES has discretion over its use. A realistic choice (public reports may be noisy), and it means the public's role is advisory at best.
- "**Review**" isn't defined: read? investigate? respond? Nothing here gives OES power to compel more information or act. OES reviews, and then what? Perhaps the next subdivision says.
- Picturing it: a small unit at OES, perhaps a few staff, reviewing a handful of incident reports a year from about five large labs and some smaller frontier developers. Each report has four fields. Most would be (d)(4) deception-in-deployment or (d)(3) loss-of-control-with-injury events, if any occur at all. In most years there might be *zero* reportable incidents, given how high the definitions set the bar. **The incident regime's practical yield could be near zero**, which is either reassuring (no incidents) or a sign that the definition is miscalibrated (incidents happen but don't meet the bar). There'd be no way to tell which from the outside.
