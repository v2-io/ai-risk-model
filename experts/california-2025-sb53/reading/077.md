## Wandering

- **A third control concept: "circumventing oversight mechanisms".** Now SB 53 has:
  1. *evading the control of its frontier developer or user* (catastrophic-risk scenario (c)(1)(C));
  2. *loss of control of a frontier model* (incident (d)(3));
  3. *using deceptive techniques … to subvert the controls or monitoring* (incident (d)(4));
  4. *circumventing oversight mechanisms* (framework item (a)(10)).

  Four phrasings for a family of related ideas: evade, lose, subvert, circumvent; control, monitoring, oversight. **None is defined.** For the lexicon, this is a cluster to map carefully, not merge. They differ in agency (the model evades / circumvents / subverts, against the controller losing), in object (control / monitoring / oversight mechanisms), and in method (deception named only in (d)(4)). A crosswalk from the EU's "reliably direct, modify, or shut down" would land on (2), the controller's state, most closely.
- **Why internal use and circumvention are paired.** Internal use is where models have most access (to code, infrastructure, their own training pipelines), so it's where circumvention risk concentrates. That's the AI-R&D-automation threat model: a model used internally to do AI research could sabotage safety work or exfiltrate itself. SB 53 names it. This item and incident (d)(4) are SB 53's two places for **misalignment-risk** concepts. The rest is misuse (CBRN, cyber) and security.
- "Catastrophic risk resulting from the internal use" is the framework-side companion to the digest's internal-use assessment summaries to OES (u8). So the chain is: the framework describes how it assesses internal-use catastrophic risk, it must comply, and it sends summaries of those assessments to OES (to come).
- The list ended at (10). Ten items, all present as recalled, in order. The full table of contents: standards, thresholds, mitigations, deploy and internal-use review, third parties, updates, weight cybersecurity, incidents, governance, internal-use risk.
