# Unit 206 · L289 · p.11

> (g) In a civil action brought pursuant to this section, once it has been demonstrated by a preponderance of the evidence that an activity proscribed by this section was a contributing factor in the alleged prohibited action against the covered employee, the frontier developer shall have the burden of proof to demonstrate by clear and convincing evidence that the alleged action would have occurred for legitimate, independent reasons even if the covered employee had not engaged in activities protected by this section.

## What differed

My prediction was nearly verbatim. That's the standard 1102.6 burden-shift template, which explains it.

## Wandering

- "**A civil action brought pursuant to this section**" settles the private-right question from u205: an action *pursuant to this section* exists. The right to sue is presupposed throughout.
- **The burden shift is the employee's biggest advantage.** The employee shows, by a preponderance, that protected activity was a *contributing factor*, which is a low causal bar. Then the developer must prove, by *clear and convincing* evidence, that it would have done the same anyway. This mirrors Labor Code 1102.6. SB 53 adopts the existing California whistleblower burden structure.
- **This section has its own epistemic standards**: preponderance, contributing factor, and clear and convincing. Add these to the four from u99 and SB 53 uses about seven belief, proof or knowledge standards. Ordered roughly by strength:
  - good faith;
  - reasonable cause;
  - foreseeable;
  - good faith + reasonable (false-statement safe harbour);
  - preponderance;
  - clear and convincing.

  That's a good table for the lexicon's epistemic-standards group, with each standard tied to its provision.
- "**An activity proscribed by this section was a contributing factor in the alleged prohibited action**": a slightly garbled phrase. "Activity proscribed" should be "activity protected" (the employee's protected disclosure), since a *proscribed* activity is the developer's retaliation. Read literally, it says the developer's proscribed activity contributed to the prohibited action, which is circular. The 1102.6 template reads "activity proscribed by Section 1102.5", where 1102.5 proscribes the retaliation. So the phrase is inherited from the template, and it's confusing there too. The second half ("activities protected by this section") uses the right word. **Two words for the same thing, "proscribed" and "protected", within one sentence.** It's a template artifact. A court reads it as intended.
