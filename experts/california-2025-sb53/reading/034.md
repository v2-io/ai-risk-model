# Unit 34 · L82 · p.3

> (n) In the future, foundation models developed by smaller companies or that are behind the frontier may pose significant catastrophic risk, and additional legislation may be needed at that time.

## What differed

None of my predictions. This is a candid statement that the statute's scope is limited: the threshold design leaves out something real.

## Wandering

- **The Legislature declares its own blind spot.** Finding (k) said "avoid burdening smaller companies behind the frontier". Finding (n) admits that those same companies' models "may pose significant catastrophic risk" *in the future*, and that SB 53 won't cover them. It amounts to saying: our threshold is deliberately underinclusive, we know it, and someone may have to fix it later. For this project that's valuable. **SB 53's coverage is declared, in its own text, to exclude catastrophic risk from sub-frontier models.** It's a self-stated scope boundary, and a view of "coverage by each source's declared scope" (CLAUDE.md's method, step 4) should use it.
  - It also answers a likely question honestly: "Does SB 53 cover dangerous open-weight models from smaller labs?" By its own findings, not if they fall below the thresholds, and the Legislature knows it.
- **"In the future"** places the risk from small models in the future. The implicit claim is that, *at present*, catastrophic risk tracks the frontier, which is capability-scaling logic. That's a falsifiable assumption, and some would dispute it now: algorithmic efficiency gains, distillation, fine-tuning of open weights. The statute dates the problem to later; critics would date it to now.
- **"Additional legislation may be needed at that time"** goes with (k)'s "track the frontier". The bill's answer to drift is the Legislature acting again, not automatic adjustment. That's consistent with my recall of a definition-review provision that *recommends* rather than updates. If that provision exists, the pair (k)+(n) is its rationale.
- **"Significant catastrophic risk"** stacks two qualifiers: "significant" on top of "catastrophic". Is there an insignificant catastrophic risk? If "catastrophic risk" is defined by probability-laden standards (foreseeable, material), "significant" would be redundant in operative text. In a finding it's emphasis. Count of risk qualifiers in the findings so far: "material risks" (d), "catastrophic risks" (j), "serious risks and harms" (k), "risks to public safety" (m), "significant catastrophic risk" (n). Five phrasings. Only the defined one will matter operatively, but a lexicon crosswalk that picks up findings passages could mistake these for distinct concepts.
- "Foundation models developed by smaller companies **or that are behind the frontier**" makes two separate axes: company size (revenue) and model position (compute). It anticipates the two-dimensional threshold: frontier model (compute) × large developer (revenue). So the four quadrants:
  - a large company with a sub-frontier model: no frontier-model duties;
  - a small company with a frontier model: frontier-developer duties only;
  - a large company with a frontier model: everything;
  - a small company with a sub-frontier model: nothing.
  The finding names two of the excluded quadrants.
