# Unit 27 · L75 · p.3

> (g) Greater transparency can also advance accountability, competition, and public trust.

## What differed

It's still not harms. Transparency's purposes are now enumerated: accountability, competition, public trust. "Accountability" is new; "competition" is surprising.

## Wandering

- **Competition** as a purpose of a safety-transparency statute? Transparency about frontier practices helps competitors benchmark each other, and it may lower barriers for smaller entrants who see what the leaders do. It also creates a "race to the top" on safety practices if frameworks are compared publicly ("recognize leading safety practices", unit 25). An antitrust-flavoured purpose in a catastrophic-risk statute is a real oddity, and good to know when someone asks "what are SB 53's stated purposes?". The full list so far:
  - innovation and leadership (a);
  - benefits (b);
  - sound policy (c);
  - balance (d);
  - an evidence environment that protects consumers, leverages industry and recognizes safety leaders (e);
  - public trust through information (f);
  - accountability, competition and trust (g).
- "Accountability" is the first word that implies someone could be held to something. Even so, "accountability" stays abstract: accountable to whom, for what? The findings haven't yet said *for what harms*.
- "Can also" is modest. The Legislature finds transparency *can* advance these things, not that it will. Findings are often hedged like this. It's an honest hedge, and an instance of the register this project likes to record: the source's own hedge.
