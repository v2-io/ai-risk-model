# Unit 22 · L67 · p.2

> (b) Artificial intelligence, including new advances in foundation models, has the potential to catalyze innovation and the rapid development of a wide range of benefits for Californians and the California economy, including advances in medicine, wildfire forecasting and prevention, and climate science, and to push the bounds of human creativity and capacity.

## What differed

My first prediction, nearly word for word. The differences: "foundation models" (I'd guessed "generative artificial intelligence"), and specific domains — medicine, wildfire, climate science.

## Wandering

- **"Wildfire forecasting and prevention"** is a California-specific benefit, and it links to OES: the agency that fights wildfires will also receive AI incident reports. That may not be a coincidence of drafting, but a reader can feel the resonance. The state that names AI's wildfire benefits has routed AI's catastrophic incidents to its wildfire agency.
- "Foundation models" now appears in the enacted text, not only in the digest. The statute's base noun is settled: *foundation model*, not "generative AI system" (AB 2013's) or "general-purpose AI model" (the EU's). Lexicon note: the near-neighbour set is {CA *foundation model*, EU *GPAI model*, CA AB 2013 *generative AI system or service*, US EO 14110's *dual-use foundation model* (revoked)}. "Foundation model" itself comes from the Stanford CRFM's 2021 coinage. A legislature adopting an academic coinage is a lineage edge of its own kind.
- "Push the bounds of human creativity and capacity" is the most expansive phrase so far. Benefits are named concretely. I'll see whether harms are named as concretely: bioweapons, cyberattacks, loss of control? Or abstractly ("catastrophic risk")? A useful comparison for the source model: how does SB 53 describe benefits, and how does it describe harms?
