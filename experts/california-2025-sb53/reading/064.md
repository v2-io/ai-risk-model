# Unit 64 · L121 · p.4

> (j) "Large frontier developer" means a frontier developer that together with its affiliates collectively had annual gross revenues in excess of five hundred million dollars ($500,000,000) in the preceding calendar year.

## What differed

My recall was exact.

## Wandering

- **"Together with its affiliates collectively"** is why "affiliate" was defined (u39). Revenue aggregates across the corporate group. So a small AI subsidiary of a big tech parent is "large" by virtue of the parent's revenue. DeepMind counts Alphabet's revenue, and Meta's AI lab counts Meta's.
- **Any revenue, not AI revenue, and not California revenue.** "Annual gross revenues", with no limit to AI products or to California. So:
  - a company whose AI revenue is zero (a research lab funded by its parent) is "large" if the group's total revenue is over $500M;
  - a pure-play lab with $400M revenue (in 2025 terms, a mid-tier lab) is not "large", even if its models are the most capable on Earth.

  The revenue tier measures **corporate size, not AI capability or AI-related risk.** That's the proportionality design (finding (k)'s "avoid burdening smaller companies"). Note what follows from it: **a startup with a breakthrough frontier model and little revenue escapes the framework, internal-use summaries and the internal whistleblower channel.** It still has frontier-developer duties (incident reporting, the anti-retaliation rule, and transparency reports at deployment if those attach to all frontier developers). Consider a frontier model built by a well-funded startup that hasn't commercialized: venture funding isn't revenue. Such a lab could train a 10^27 model and owe no framework.
- **"Gross"** means before costs. "**Preceding calendar year**": status is re-evaluated yearly, so a developer can become large or stop being large. Edge cases: a developer whose revenue crosses $500M mid-year becomes large only from the following January. Duties have an annual cadence.
- "**In excess of**" means strictly greater, as with the other thresholds. The three bright lines: >10^26 operations, >$500M revenue, >50 people / >$1B property. All are strict inequalities.
- Lexicon: "developer" in SB 53 is now at least four things:
  - "frontier developer" (h): a training act plus compute (actual or intended);
  - "large frontier developer" (j): plus group revenue;
  - "the developer" in (i)(2): undefined, lowercase;
  - "the major AI developers" in finding (l): descriptive.
