# Unit 198 · L281 · p.11

> (c) A covered employee may use the hotline described in Section 1102.7 to make reports described in subdivision (a).

## What differed

This was my 'fuzzy' second guess from u196, the hotline.

## Wandering

- **There's an existing state whistleblower hotline.** Labor Code §1102.7 is California's whistleblower hotline. It's run by the AG's office, I believe, though possibly the Labor Commissioner; I'm unsure. It routes calls to the right agency. So SB 53 plugs into existing infrastructure instead of building its own. The routing is: covered employee → hotline → appropriate agency, which is probably the AG for TFAIA matters. That fits the AG being the whistleblower hub (u140).
- "**May**" makes the hotline permissive, one channel among several.
- The full set of reporting channels for a covered employee is now:
  - the AG;
  - a federal authority;
  - a supervisor or an authorized co-employee;
  - the 1102.7 hotline;
  - the internal anonymous channel at large developers (still to come).

  OES isn't one of them, even though employees could arguably report incidents to OES as "members of the public" under §22757.13(a). Is a disclosure to OES protected? Not under this list. **So a covered employee who reports a critical safety incident to OES through the public mechanism may not be protected against retaliation.** That's a strange gap, since OES is the incident hub, and an employee might naturally go there.
