# Unit 127 · L194 · p.7

> (B) The failure by a frontier developer to meet the standards of, or comply with the requirements stated by, the federal law, regulation, or guidance document designated pursuant to subdivision (h) shall constitute a violation of this chapter.

## Wandering

- **Federal guidance becomes binding state law.** For a developer that opts in, failing to meet a federal *guidance document's* standards is a **violation of the TFAIA**, enforceable by the California AG. So non-binding federal guidance becomes binding through state law, for the opted-in developer. That's an elegant transformation, and a legally interesting one: it's the same move as "comply with your own framework", but the borrowed standard is federal instead of the developer's own. **SB 53's general technique is to make external or self-written standards enforceable by incorporating them.** Framework (self) → enforceable; federal guidance (other) → enforceable; industry best practices → described, encouraged.
- The pattern of SB 53's enforcement: the state writes almost no substantive standards itself. It enforces standards authored by developers (frameworks), by federal agencies (designated documents), and its own *reporting* standards (deadlines, fields). Its own substantive definitions (catastrophic risk, incidents) set *scope*, not conduct.
- Note "comply with the requirements **stated by**" here, where (i)(1) and (2)(A) said "imposed or stated by". A small variation between the paragraphs.
