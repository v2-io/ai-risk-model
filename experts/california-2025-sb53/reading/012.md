# Unit 12 · L46 · p.2

> This bill would establish within the Government Operations Agency a consortium … to develop a framework for the creation of a public cloud computing cluster … "CalCompute" … safe, ethical, equitable, and sustainable … The bill would require the Government Operations Agency to, on or before January 1, 2027, submit a report from the consortium to the Legislature … and would dissolve the consortium upon submission of that report. The bill would make those provisions operative only upon an appropriation in a budget act, or other measure, for its purposes.

## What differed

- My recall was close to verbatim for the first sentence. That's more evidence I've seen this text, or the digest at least.
- Two things I didn't have:
  - **the consortium dissolves on submitting its report**;
  - **the CalCompute provisions are operative only upon an appropriation.**
- The consortium sits in the **Government Operations Agency**, not the Department of Technology. Item (2) opened with the Department of Technology, so the baseline and the new home don't quite line up: GovOps is the parent agency, and the department sits inside it.

## Wandering

- **CalCompute may never exist, legally speaking.** The provisions take effect only if money is appropriated. Without a budget appropriation, the consortium is never formed, and the report is never due. Even with one, what's mandated is a *framework for the creation of* a cluster: a report, not a cluster. So "SB 53 creates CalCompute" is an overstatement in two ways, and a common one, I'd guess, in press coverage and in other documents. The accurate form: SB 53 establishes, contingent on appropriation, a consortium to develop a framework for a public cloud computing cluster. Two contingencies, and one deliverable that's a document. A fork asked "what does SB 53 do about public compute?" needs to say that.
- So the January 1, 2027 date is conditional on the appropriation happening first. Is that date still meaningful if the appropriation comes late? A real interpretive question, the kind the statute may not answer.
- "Safe, ethical, equitable, and sustainable" is the values vocabulary of state AI policy, very different in register from the B&P part's "catastrophic risk". So the bill has two vocabularies of goodness: an *aspirational* one (safe, ethical, equitable, sustainable) for public compute, and a *threshold* one (catastrophic, critical) for frontier developers. "Safe" in the CalCompute sentence and "safety" in "critical safety incident" are probably not the same concept. The lexicon shouldn't merge them.
- The consortium "dissolves on submission". A sunset built into the institution keeps it from becoming a standing body. The design is "study, report, go away", and the Legislature keeps the decision about actually building anything.
