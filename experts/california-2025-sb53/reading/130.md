## Wandering

- **The review states the definition's *target* in words.** The target is "foundation models at the frontier of artificial intelligence development". So the 10^26 number is a *proxy* for that, and the statute says so: the operational definition (compute) serves a conceptual one (at the frontier). That's a helpful split for the lexicon:
  - **SB 53 frontier model (operational):** a foundation model trained with more than 10^26 integer or floating-point operations (§22757.11(i));
  - **SB 53 frontier model (intended):** "foundation models at the frontier of AI development" (§22757.14(a)(1)).

  When the two come apart (as compute efficiency improves), the statute itself tells you which is the aim. A source expert can answer "what is SB 53 trying to capture with 'frontier model'?" in the statute's own words, not by guessing from the number.
- "At the frontier" is relative. The frontier moves, so "at the frontier" picks out a moving set. A fixed number can't track it, hence the review.
