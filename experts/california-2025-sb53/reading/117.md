# Unit 117 · L184 · p.7

> (g) (1) Beginning January 1, 2027, and annually thereafter, the Office of Emergency Services shall produce a report with anonymized and aggregated information about critical safety incidents that have been reviewed by the Office of Emergency Services since the preceding report.

## What differed

My recall was exact.

## Wandering

- **This answers unit 31's question.** How does SB 53 "alert … the public to serious risks" (finding (k))? Through an **annual, anonymized, aggregated** report, beginning 2027. No specific incident, no developer, no model is named. The public learns *how many* and *what kinds*, a year late. The gap between purpose and mechanism I suspected is real.
- "**Produce**" a report, not "publish". Produce it to whom? (2) may say. If it goes to the Legislature and Governor, not posted publicly, the public alert is weaker still.
- "**That have been reviewed by OES**": only reviewed incidents. Developer reports must be reviewed ("shall", u113); public reports needn't be ("may"). So public reports OES chose not to review drop out of the aggregate. The aggregate shows only what OES reviewed.
- "Since the preceding report": the first report (Jan 1, 2027) covers incidents from when? The statute is operative Jan 1, 2026, so the first report covers 2026. If the CalCompute report is also due Jan 1, 2027, that's a date with two deliverables in different agencies.
- Anonymized *and* aggregated: two separate protections. Even a single incident in a year would be "aggregated" into a count of one, and anonymity might be hard to keep then ("one deception incident at a large frontier developer" when there are five of them). Small-n anonymity is a known problem.
