# Unit 24 · L72 · p.3

> (d) Targeted interventions to support effective artificial intelligence governance should balance the technology's benefits and the potential for material risks.

## What differed

Nearly my words, and that bothers me a little. I wrote "targeted interventions to support effective AI governance should balance the technology's benefits and material risks". The text has "the **potential for** material risks". Recall again, I think, and probably of JCPWG phrasing the finding paraphrases.

## Wandering

- **"Material"** appears for the first time in the enacted text, here in a finding: "material risks". CLAUDE.md lists a *materiality standard* among the seven concepts in the catastrophic-risk definition. So the findings set up the vocabulary the definition will use. Courts reading "material" in the definition could look back here. This finding gives no test for materiality, though. It's register, not a standard.
- "The potential for material risks" is a doubled modality: a *potential* for *risks*, a possibility of possibilities. The digest did the same with "the potential for catastrophic risk" (unit 8). So the doubling isn't only the digest's looseness: the enacted findings share it. That makes it likelier that the operative text's "catastrophic risk" is a probability-laden concept and that "potential for" is idiom, not a distinct layer. Something to settle at the definition.
- "Balance" means proportionality: this is the finding that justifies light touch. "Targeted" is a JCPWG word, I think, and the opposite of SB 1047's broader duty of care. Legislators who say "targeted" usually mean "please don't call this burdensome".
- The findings so far are ordered *innovation → benefits → expert principles → balance*. A finding on risk itself is still to come. I'm curious whether the harms will be named concretely, as wildfire and medicine were for benefits.
