# Unit 25 · L73 · p.3

> (e) In building a robust and transparent evidence environment, policymakers can align incentives to simultaneously protect consumers, leverage industry expertise, and recognize leading safety practices.

## What differed

Not transparency-as-accountability, and not harms. It's the "evidence environment" and the alignment of incentives. That's JCPWG language, I'm fairly sure; "evidence environment" sounds like the report's.

## Wandering

- **"Protect consumers."** This is the first protected class named in the enacted text, and it's *consumers*, not "the public", "Californians" or "humanity". That's a consumer-protection frame, which fits B&P code placement and "clearly and conspicuously". But catastrophic risk (mass casualties, $1B) isn't a consumer harm in the ordinary sense: the victims of a bioweapon aren't consumers of the model. So there's a **frame mismatch**: the statute regulates catastrophe through consumer-protection machinery. The findings' "consumers" may reflect where in California law this bill had to live, more than what it is about. A question someone should ask: **who does SB 53 protect, according to its own text?** So far: "Californians and the California economy" (benefits, b), "consumers" (e), and in the digest "the public health or safety" (whistleblowers). Three different protected-party framings.
- "Leverage industry expertise" and "recognize leading safety practices" are deference to industry. The frontier AI framework will incorporate "industry-consensus best practices" (digest, unit 8). The theory: industry knows best how to make models safe, and the state's job is to make it say so publicly and to reward the leaders. That's soft-law-to-hard-law: frontier labs' voluntary frameworks (RSP, Preparedness Framework, FSF) become legally required documents. **So SB 53 codifies a genre that already existed**, the "frontier safety framework" published voluntarily by Anthropic (Sept 2023), OpenAI (Dec 2023) and Google DeepMind (May 2024), and the Seoul commitments (May 2024). The lineage edge goes *from industry practice into law*. A reader should know that "frontier AI framework" in SB 53 names something the labs already produced.
- "Robust and transparent evidence environment": the epistemic purpose is to generate evidence. That's an honest framing for a transparency statute: it doesn't claim to make AI safe, only to make the risks *knowable*. It ties to this project's own aim. SB 53 is, in a sense, a machine for producing the kind of source documents this project compiles: frameworks, transparency reports, incident data.
