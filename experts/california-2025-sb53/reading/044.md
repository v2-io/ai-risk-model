## Wandering

- **SB 53's loss-of-control item doesn't use the EU Code's formula.** "Evading the control of its frontier developer or user" has no "reliably direct, modify, or shut down". So SB 53 isn't among the documents that copy the EU Code here. That's a useful negative for the lineage map: SB 53 (Sept 2025) postdates the Code (July 2025), and on loss of control it doesn't borrow from it. The finding (j) said "loss of control", and the operative item says "evading the control". The finding's term and the operative term differ; the operative one is narrower and more agentic.
- **"Evading"** is an active, almost intentional verb. A model that *loses* control through a bug (it spirals, runs away) — is that "evading"? To evade suggests pursuing escape. So passive loss of control (a malfunctioning model the developer can't stop, without any evasive behaviour) may not fit (C). That's a gap between "loss of control" in (j) and "evading control" in (C). Compare the EU formula, "reliably direct, modify, or shut down", which is about the *controller's capacity*, not the *model's behaviour*. SB 53 frames it from the model's side (it evades), the EU from the human's side (we can't control it). **A real conceptual difference, worth a lexicon note: *evading control* (model act) vs *loss of control* (controller state).**
- **"Its frontier developer or user"**: either party's control. "User" probably isn't defined (watch). A model that evades a *user's* control counts, so an agent deployed by a company that ignores its operator's instructions or resists shutdown counts. But "user" against "deployer" or "operator"? SB 53 doesn't seem to have the deployer/operator distinction the EU and NIST draw. Two parties only: developer and user.
- Now the full shape of (c)(1): stem + (A) CBRN expert assistance + (B) autonomous cyberattack or crime-equivalent conduct + (C) evading control. **There's no item for systemic, economic or societal harms, and none for harms to democratic processes, mass manipulation, critical-infrastructure disruption without property damage, or concentration of power.** It's a closed list of three. And (C) combines with the stem: the evasion must be part of a single incident causing 51+ deaths/injuries or $1B property. So evasion alone, with no casualties, is *not* a catastrophic risk materializing. But a *risk* of evasion leading to such harm is a catastrophic risk.
- Does (C) apply only to the current developer? "Its frontier developer". So a model stolen from its developer, run by a thief, and evading the thief's control: the thief is a "user", arguably. Probably fine.
