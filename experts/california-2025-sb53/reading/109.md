# Unit 109 · L173 · p.6

> (c) (1) Subject to paragraph (2), a frontier developer shall report any critical safety incident pertaining to one or more of its frontier models to the Office of Emergency Services within 15 days of discovering the critical safety incident.

## What differed

My recall was exact.

## Wandering

- **The clock runs from discovery.** "Within 15 days of **discovering** the critical safety incident". That settles part of my u103 question: the clock starts at discovery, not occurrence. But "discovering the critical safety incident" means discovering that something *is* a critical safety incident. For (d)(1) (weight theft resulting in injury), that's discovering both the theft and the injury link. For (d)(2), discovering that harm resulted from a catastrophic-risk materialization, which requires a judgment. So the developer controls when "discovery" happens, by when it concludes an event qualifies. Another self-set parameter: **the developer's own classification judgment starts the clock.**
- **"A frontier developer"**: all frontier developers, not only large. Incident reporting is universal across the tier.
- **"Pertaining to one or more of its frontier models"**: only *its* models. A developer that learns of an incident involving a *competitor's* model has no duty to report it. A member of the public could.
- **15 days** is long for an emergency. Hence paragraph (2), presumably the 24-hour imminent-danger rule. And it fits the OES framing: 15 days for a record, 24 hours for an emergency.
