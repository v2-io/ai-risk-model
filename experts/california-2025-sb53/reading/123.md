## Wandering

- **"*The* catastrophic risk"**, with a definite article, as if referring to one specific catastrophic risk. Which one? There's no antecedent. "Catastrophic risk" is defined as a kind, not an instance. "The catastrophic risk" reads like a leftover from an earlier draft where a specific risk had been mentioned, or it means "catastrophic risk (as defined)". Small, but a definite article without an antecedent is the kind of seam that shows amendment history.
- **"Intended to"**: the federal document's *intent* matters, not its effect. A federal regime aimed at, say, cybersecurity incident reporting generally (CIRCIA, for critical infrastructure) wouldn't qualify unless it's "intended to assess, detect, or mitigate" catastrophic risk. That's a purpose test applied to someone else's law.
- **"Assess, detect, or mitigate"**: three verbs, against the framework's "manage, assess, and mitigate" (g). Similar verb families, slightly different (detect against manage).
- So the conditions for designation are: (1)(A) substantially equivalent or stricter incident reporting standards; (1)(B) the non-condition (no CA reporting needed); (2) intent to address catastrophic risk.
