## Wandering

- **A correction to unit 41.** There I wrote that SB 53's $1B threshold is about "damage to, or loss of, property", not economic loss, so a market crash with no damaged or lost property might not count. Now that property includes **intangible** property, the gap is much narrower. Intangible property covers money in accounts, securities, cryptocurrency, data, intellectual property, contractual rights. So a $1B theft of crypto (intangible property lost) counts, and so does destruction of $1B worth of data, if data's worth can be valued. What *still* might not count is pure economic loss where no property is damaged or lost, only reduced in value or never gained: lost profits, business interruption, a decline in market prices. Is a share-price decline "damage to" intangible property (the share)? It's a stretch, but a plaintiff-friendly reading under liberal construction might say yes. So: **the property/economic distinction stands, but it's narrower than I said in unit 41.** I'm keeping both notes. Unit 41 records what I thought then, this one corrects it, and the correction is part of what the reading taught me.
  - The crosswalk against OpenAI's "economic damage" stays a real difference, but a smaller one: (intangible) property damage or loss against economic damage. A careful mapping records both, not "same concept".
