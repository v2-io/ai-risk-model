> (2) If a frontier developer redacts information in a document pursuant to this subdivision, the frontier developer shall describe the character and justification of the redaction in any published version of the document to the extent permitted by the concerns that justify redaction and shall retain the unredacted information for five years.

## What differed

My recall was exact.

## Wandering

- **Redaction must be visible and kept.** It must be visible in kind ("describe the character and justification"), as far as the justifying concerns allow, and the unredacted original must be **retained for five years**. The retention duty is what makes later enforcement possible: an AG investigation, or a whistleblower's claim, can be checked against the unredacted version. It's a quiet but important design element, a sealed record behind each public document.
- "**To the extent permitted by the concerns that justify redaction**": the description itself can be limited by the same concerns. A redaction for national security might be described only as "redacted for national security". Recursive, but sensible.
- Five years from what? Publication, presumably. So frameworks and transparency reports from 2026 must be retained in full until 2031. If the corpus wants the *unredacted* versions someday, they exist, held by the developers.
- §22757.12 is perhaps ending. It has been the heart of the TFAIA: (a) framework, (b) review and modification, (c) transparency report, (d) OES internal-use summaries, (e) false statements, (f) redaction.
