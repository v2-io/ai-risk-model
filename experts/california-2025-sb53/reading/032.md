# Unit 32 · L80 · p.3

> (l) While the major artificial intelligence developers have already voluntarily established the creation, use, and publication of frontier AI frameworks as an industry best practice, not all developers are providing reporting that is consistent and sufficient to ensure necessary transparency and protection of the public. Mandatory, standardized, and objective reporting by frontier developers is required to provide the government and the public with timely and accurate information.

## What differed

None of my predictions. The statute confirms in its own voice what I inferred in unit 25: SB 53 turns a voluntary industry genre into a legal requirement. And it gives the problem as **inconsistency and insufficiency** among developers.

## Wandering

- **The lineage edge, now explicit:** "the major AI developers have already voluntarily established … frontier AI frameworks as an industry best practice." So SB 53's own account of where frontier AI frameworks come from is that industry invented them and the state is codifying them. Any source model should record this as the statute's self-declared genealogy. (Note "creation, use, and publication": three activities, roughly mapping to "write, implement, … publish" from the digest. Still no "comply with".)
- **"Not all developers are providing reporting that is consistent and sufficient"** is the closest the findings come to an accusation. Who isn't? It doesn't say. By September 2025 there had been public criticism of labs releasing models without system cards, or with thin or late ones (for example, Google's Gemini 2.5 Pro model card arrived weeks after release; there were the OpenAI–GPT-4.1 complaints; xAI published no framework for some time) [recall, soft]. The finding is diplomatic enough that each lab can assume it means another lab.
- **"Mandatory, standardized, and objective"** names three properties. *Mandatory*: yes, the statute makes it so. *Standardized*: will it? If each developer writes its own framework on its own terms, describing "how it approaches" listed topics, reporting is standardized only in its **topics**, not its **content**. *Objective*: a self-authored framework isn't objective in any strong sense. So the finding may promise more than the mechanism delivers. Check: does the statute prescribe formats (OES forms for incident reports, required fields for transparency reports)? Required fields would be standardization; topic lists alone are weaker. **A question someone should ask: how standardized is SB 53 reporting, really?**
- "Timely and accurate". *Timely* points to deadlines (15 days / 24 hours [recall]); *accurate* points to a false-statement prohibition [recall]. Each property should map to a mechanism. That's a nice audit for later: findings (l)'s five properties (mandatory, standardized, objective, timely, accurate) against the operative text's mechanisms.
- "Protection of the public" is the fourth protected-party framing: Californians / consumers / the public health or safety / the public.
- "Frontier developers" appears in the findings in the operative noun. And "the major AI developers" is a non-defined descriptive phrase. In the findings, "developers" ranges over industry; in the operative text it will be defined.
