## Wandering

- **The military/national-security carve-out.** A frontier model used by the US government lawfully, for example in offensive cyber operations by DoD or the NSA, or in a military context where deaths result, produces no "catastrophic risk" under SB 53. The state can't regulate federal activity (Supremacy Clause, intergovernmental immunity), so this is partly constitutional necessity written out. But it has a consequence: **a frontier developer's framework need not address risks from its federal government customers' lawful uses.** That's relevant to frontier labs' 2025 government contracts (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and xAI each had DoD and other federal deals). And because "lawful" is decided by federal law, the federal government defines the carve-out's edges.
- **Only the federal government.** Not state governments, not foreign governments, not California's own agencies. Lawful activity of a *foreign* government using a frontier model to cause mass casualties is still catastrophic risk. Lawful activity of California's own police? Not excluded. Odd, but California's own use would rarely reach these thresholds.
- "Lawful": unlawful federal activity (a rogue agency program) isn't excluded.
- Is it the risk *from the activity* that's excluded, or the risk *to* federal activity? Grammar: "does not include a … risk from … lawful activity of the federal government". So it's risk arising *from* federal activity. A cyberattack *on* federal systems by a model is still included.
