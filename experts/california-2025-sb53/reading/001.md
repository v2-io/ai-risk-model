## Wandering

- If the first thing a reader meets is site navigation, the edition matters. Leginfo serves several views of one bill: "Text", "History", "Bill Analysis", "Votes", "Status", "Compare Versions". Which version is this? The enrolled bill, the chaptered one, or an amended-in-Assembly version saved before final passage? A version label should come soon ("Senate Bill No. 53 CHAPTER 138" or similar). If none does, that's a real question for anyone citing this text: which version of SB 53 is the project's SB 53? Bills are amended many times, and the Assembly amendments of mid-2025 changed SB 53 a great deal, if I remember right. A fork asked "what does SB 53 say about X" should be able to say which version it read.
- Web-to-PDF captures also tend to drag in sidebars, footer links ("Accessibility", "Disclaimer") and sometimes truncated tables. Statute text on leginfo is HTML with indented subdivisions. In a PDF capture, the indentation that carries the hierarchy ((a), (1), (A)) may be lost or flattened. That matters a lot for this source: which clause belongs under which subdivision is exactly what the "seven separable concepts" analysis depends on. I'll watch whether the subdivision labels survive conversion as text, so that the hierarchy can be rebuilt from the labels even if the indentation is gone.
- A small thing about the experience: a human reader opening this page would skip the nav bar without noticing. I almost did. Noticing it is a reminder that the tool's units are faithful to the artifact, junk included, and that some later units may be junk too, or worse, junk mixed into real text.
