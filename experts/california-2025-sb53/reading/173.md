# Unit 173 · L249 · p.10

> (j) If CalCompute is established within the University of California, the University of California may receive private donations for the purposes of implementing CalCompute.

## What differed

This isn't the operative-on-appropriation clause. It's a **private-donation pathway**, which I didn't predict.

## Wandering

- **The statute does think about CalCompute actually existing.** "If CalCompute is established within the University of California". So far I'd read SB 53 as creating only a study (u12, u147). This clause reaches past the study to a CalCompute that has been established, and allows UC to accept private donations for implementing it. So the bill does more than plan: it pre-authorizes one funding route for a real cluster. My earlier "it's only a study" needs a small qualification: **mostly a study, plus one forward-looking permission.**
- **Private donations, possibly from the regulated labs.** Who would donate to a public AI compute cluster? Tech companies, frontier labs among them (with compute credits, hardware, money), and philanthropies. So the companies regulated by the TFAIA could become CalCompute's funders. Same companies, now in a third role: regulated party, potential vendor (u164), potential donor. The bill has no conflict-of-interest provisions for donations. (UC has its own gift policies.)
- This also partly answers u160's "recommendations for potential funding sources": the bill already names one.
- "May receive" is permissive. UC can already accept gifts generally, so this clause is mostly clarification and signalling.
