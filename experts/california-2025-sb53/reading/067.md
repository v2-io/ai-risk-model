## Wandering

- **The open question from unit 8 is closed.** The statute requires a large frontier developer to *comply with* its own framework. Consequences:
  1. **Self-commitments become enforceable.** A framework says what the developer will do (thresholds, evaluations, mitigations), and failing to do it is noncompliance with §22757.12(a), so noncompliance with the TFAIA, so subject to the AG's civil penalty (digest, u10). This is SB 53's real conduct lever: **the state doesn't set the standard, but it enforces whatever standard the developer sets itself.**
  2. **Whistleblower prong (b) is broad** (u14). "The frontier developer has violated the TFAIA" includes not following its framework. So an employee who reasonably believes the lab isn't doing what its framework says is protected, whatever the casualty count. That's a much wider whistleblower shield than the catastrophic-danger prong.
  3. **A perverse incentive.** If compliance with your own framework is enforceable, a developer gains by writing a vague or weak framework, since it's easier to comply with. "Describes how [it] approaches" (a disclosure verb) leaves room for a framework that says little. The incentive pulls toward vagueness, and only reputation (publicity) and the topic list push against it. Findings (l)'s "standardized and objective" hope runs into this.
- **The digest dropped the most consequential verb in the bill.** A careful, authoritative summarizer, Legislative Counsel, compressed "write, implement, comply with, and … publish" to "write, implement, and … publish". It's a pure instance of CLAUDE.md's "precision that a summary loses", inside one document. And it's a retrieval lesson: a fork asked "does SB 53 require compliance with frameworks?" that retrieves only the digest would answer *no*. **The search tool must rank §22757.12(a) above digest passages for that query.** I'll put it in questions.md as a test case with a checkable answer.
  - My own recall got it right and the authoritative digest got it wrong. That's a useful calibration: my recall of the *operative text* has been near-perfect so far. My recall of the *digest* was less sure, but I hadn't recalled the digest's omission, I'd assumed it. Still, near-perfect recall is no excuse for skimming. Every surprise so far (the weight-theft injury condition, the materialization ambiguity, the per-developer compute hinge, evading vs. losing control) came from reading slowly text I "already knew". **Recall gives the words; only reading gives what follows from them.**
- "**Applies to the large frontier developer's frontier models**": one framework for all its frontier models, not per model. "Describes how [it] approaches **all** of the following": a list follows, and it must address every item. Watch for the words "approaches" and "describes": description of approach, not performance.
- "Clearly and conspicuously publish on its internet website": consumer-protection vocabulary, as in the digest.
