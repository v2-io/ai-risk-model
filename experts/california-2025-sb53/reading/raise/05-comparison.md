# RAISE (S8828) against SB 53: the comparison

*Lines L51–257 of `ref/canonical/ny-2026-raise-s8828.md`, read whole after units 1–4. Section numbers are GBL Art. 44-B, as replaced by S8828 §2. "SB 53" means the chaptered text. Each point is marked **[text]** (what the texts say) or **[reading]** (my interpretation).*

## The headline

**RAISE copies the TFAIA's definitions, not the Labor Code's.** It says "frontier model" throughout. Its weight-theft incident requires "death or bodily injury" only. Its catastrophic-risk (ii) keeps the TFAIA's "if the conduct had been committed by a human", and its (iii) keeps the TFAIA's "if" (L87, L92). So the source is B&P §22757.11 specifically **[text]**. SB 53's broader whistleblower definitions (u177–190) don't carry over. RAISE has **no whistleblower chapter at all** in this article.

The coordinator's "copies word for word" holds for the definitions, with the exceptions below. The operative duties are mostly copied too, but New York made real departures in reporting speed, agency powers, registration, scope and exemptions. In several places RAISE is **stricter or more complete than SB 53**, and it fixes some of the gaps I found. In a few places it's more industry-protective.

## What's copied (nearly verbatim)

- **Definitions** (§1420): affiliate; AI model; catastrophic risk with its exclusions; critical safety incident; deploy; foundation model; frontier AI framework; frontier developer; frontier model with its compute count; large frontier developer; model weight. Number formatting differs: "more than fifty people", "one billion dollars" with no numeral **[text]**.
- **The equity exclusion** (§1423): verbatim, and codified as its own section, as in SB 53 **[text]**.
- **The framework's ten topics; annual review; modification published with justification in 30 days; the transparency report, both tiers, and the system-card safe harbour; the false-statement rule and its safe harbour; redaction with 5-year retention** (§1421) **[text]**.
- **The incident intake fields; the 24-hour imminent-danger disclosure; optional amendment; must-review / may-review** (§1422.1, .3(b)–(c), .4) **[text]**.
- **Federal equivalence** (§1422.8–10) **[text]**, with one addition (below).
- **Cumulation** (§1424). This is SB 53's uncodified SEC. 5(c), now **codified** **[text]**.
- **The findings** (S8828 §1): 10 of SB 53's 16, with edits (units 3–4).

## Where RAISE is stricter or more complete

1. **Incident reports within 72 hours, not 15 days** (§1422.3(a)). The clock runs "from a determination that a critical safety incident has occurred **or** within seventy-two hours of the frontier developer learning facts sufficient to establish a **reasonable belief** that a critical safety incident has occurred" **[text]**. The reasonable-belief trigger answers the problem I raised at SB 53 u109, where the developer's own classification judgment starts the clock. In New York, an objective reasonable-belief standard starts it too **[reading]**.
2. **The framework must be "in detail" and say how the developer "handles" each topic** (§1421.1), where SB 53 has "describes how … approaches". That's a stronger disclosure standard: "handles" asks what the developer does, and "approaches" only how it thinks about it **[reading]**.
3. **The internal-use schedule needs the office's agreement** (§1422.2(a)): "requested by the large frontier developer … and agreed upon by the office". SB 53 lets the developer set it unilaterally (u96) **[text]**.
4. **Federal equivalence doesn't cut off the state's data** (§1422.9(b)(iii)). Developers relying on federal standards must send copies of their federal incident reports to the office at the same time. That closes the gap I noted at SB 53 u122, where California could end up receiving nothing **[text]**.
5. **"Person" is defined** (§1420.14): it includes an individual and excludes governments ("any other **nongovernmental** organization"). SB 53 leaves "person" undefined (u66). So in New York an individual can be a frontier developer, explicitly, and the state can't be **[text]**.
6. **Territorial scope is stated** (§1425): the act applies "only … to frontier models that are developed, deployed, or operating in whole or in part in New York state". SB 53 has no scope clause. Even "operating" partly in NY is enough. It's broad, and stated **[text]**.
7. **Registration and funding: a regime SB 53 doesn't have** (§1428). Large frontier developers must:
   - file a disclosure statement with the Department of Financial Services before developing, deploying or operating a frontier model in NY;
   - identify themselves and every name they do business under, their addresses, and **beneficial owners** (5% or more for private companies, including owners from the previous 5 years; 50% or more for public ones);
   - name three contacts;
   - renew every 2 years, or when ownership or material facts change;
   - **pay pro rata assessments to fund the office**.

   Failure costs $1,000 a day plus the assessments owed. DFS publishes a list of registrants **[text]**. That's a licensing-like layer, and an industry-funded regulator, the model DFS uses for banks and insurers. Its ownership disclosure isn't about catastrophic risk at all. It's about who controls frontier labs, possibly with foreign ownership in mind **[reading]**.
8. **Rulemaking powers** (§1429). The office may adopt rules "as needed" and **may add reporting or publication requirements**: post-incident information, sharing of plans and protocols, and transmission of frameworks to the office **[text]**. SB 53's OES can only designate federal equivalents. New York's regulator can grow the regime without new legislation.
9. **The annual report covers more** (§1422.7). Beginning **2028** (not 2027), it covers incidents, "any information that the office deems relevant to frontier model safety", **recommended updates to this article**, and developments. So one report folds together SB 53's OES aggregate and its Department of Technology definitions review. Its recipients are the Governor, the legislative leaders, and the chairs and ranking members of the two technology committees. It isn't published to the public either **[text]**.
10. **Penalties are higher** (§1427.1): up to $1M for a first violation and **$3M for each subsequent one**. The same four violation types; **large frontier developers only**, as in SB 53 **[text]**.

## Where RAISE is weaker, or protects industry more

1. **No whistleblower protections in the article** **[text]**. SB 53's whole Labor Code chapter has no counterpart here. (New York has general whistleblower law, Labor Law §740, but this act adds nothing frontier-specific.)
2. **No private right of action, stated explicitly** (§1427.2) **[text]**. In SB 53, the B&P chapter's AG-only penalty is the counterpart, but SB 53 doesn't say there's no private action (u144, u219).
3. **A causation-defence clause** (§1427.3): nothing prevents a large frontier developer "from asserting that another person, entity, or factor, may be responsible for any alleged harm". SB 53 has no such clause **[text]**. It reads like a clause negotiated to protect developers in litigation **[reading]**.
4. **Weaker consideration of confidentiality interests, which helps transparency** (§1422.5(b)). The office "**may** consider, at its discretion" trade secrets and the rest when transmitting reports. SB 53 has "**shall strongly consider**" (u115) **[text]**. That's less protection for developers' secrets when reports move between agencies.
5. **Security precautions are lower**: "all **reasonable** precautions … personnel **authorized** to know", against SB 53's "all **necessary** precautions … specific **need to know**" (u108) **[text]**.
6. **Academic research and the Empire AI consortium are exempt** (§1426) **[text]**. Accredited NY universities doing academic AI research, and Empire AI (NY's public compute consortium, the counterpart of CalCompute), are outside the act. That answers in New York the question I raised at SB 53 u153 about a state-built cluster being regulated, here by express exemption **[reading]**.

## SB 53 elements that RAISE drops

- **Every SB 53 SEC. 5 clause except cumulation and severability:** liberal construction, the federal-contract override, deference to federal preemption, and local preemption. **No liberal-construction clause in RAISE** **[text]**. So CLAUDE.md's "liberally construed" point is California-only.
- **The two "encouraged, but not required" norms:** better-than-best-practice disclosure, and voluntary reporting on non-frontier models **[text]**.
- **The Department of Technology's definitions review**, with its five threshold-design criteria (u133–138). It's partly replaced by the office's "recommended updates" **[text]**.
- **CalCompute** (with Empire AI exempted instead) and **all the whistleblower provisions** **[text]**.
- **The findings on harm categories and scope gaps:** SB 53's (j) list (malicious use and malfunction; hacking, bio, loss of control), (k) "track the frontier", (n) "future smaller-company risk", and the JCPWG grounding (units 3–4) **[text]**.

## Text fidelity (for search and quotation)

- **"10º26"** (L106). The canonical text has an *ordinal indicator or degree sign*, not "10^26". The superscript was mis-converted. **An exact-phrase search for "10^26" won't find RAISE's threshold, and a quotation copied from the canonical text would be wrong.** This needs fixing in `bin/canonicalize` or noting.
- NY's numbered-line layout leaves line numbers and broken hyphens in the text ("fron- 18 tier", "inju- ry", "Assem bly"), and runs of `pdf-text` line-number blocks. Phrase matching across line breaks will fail.
- The bold markers (`**`) on the whole new article reflect NY's "matter in italics … is new" convention, rendered as bold.

## What this means for the lexicon and lineage

- **Lineage:** RAISE (S8828) §1420 ← SB 53 B&P §22757.11, copied verbatim apart from number formatting. Not from the Labor Code §1107 variant. RAISE §1 ← SB 53 SEC. 1, with selections and edits; "wildfire forecasting" is the tell.
- **Namespacing:** `ny-raise:catastrophic-risk` = `ca-sb53-tfaia:catastrophic-risk` (identical, apart from spelled-out numbers). `ny-raise:critical-safety-incident` = the TFAIA version. Both ≠ the `ca-sb53-labor:` variants. And `ny-raise:person` is defined, so it's ≠ SB 53's undefined "person".
- **"Corroboration" by copying.** RAISE's agreement with SB 53 on the >50 / $1B bar is copying, not independent corroboration. That's exactly CLAUDE.md's "agreement that is really copying". RAISE keeps "serious injury", so it isn't one of the documents that drop it.
- **A question to keep open:** where RAISE departs, which way was each departure negotiated (Governor Hochul's chapter amendment against the legislature's original)? This text can't say. The 2025 original (S6953-B) would show what New York gave up in order to align with California.
