## Wandering

- **One word, "but" to "and", flattens SB 53's key finding.** In u36 I called SB 53's (p) the most important finding. Its "but" marked the gap between what the act does (transparency) and what safety needs (due care, which the act doesn't impose). NY's "and" turns the contrast into a list. The intent is transparency, *and* safety depends on due care, with no admission that the act leaves the second undone. A small edit with a real interpretive effect: SB 53's finding disclaims a duty of care, and NY's reads more like endorsing one. In tort litigation (u36's tort shadow), NY's version may be the friendlier text for plaintiffs. That's my reading. It also shows **a copy drifting in exactly the way this project tracks**: the same sentence, one conjunction changed, a different stance.
- **Dropping "assess, and respond".** SB 53's government monitors, assesses and responds. NY's only monitors. That may be honest, since NY's receiving office probably has no response powers. Or it may reflect a NY agency that isn't an emergency-management body. I'll see which agency it is.
- **What else NY dropped:** "track the frontier" (k) and "future smaller companies" (n), so the two findings about the threshold's limits are gone, along with the JCPWG grounding (c, o). **SB 53's self-declared scope gap (n) doesn't carry over into RAISE.** A source model of RAISE that copied SB 53's "declared scope" would be wrong on this point.
- "Reporting" → "disclosures": NY's vocabulary is shifting from *reporting* to *disclosure*. Watch whether the operative text says "transparency report" or something else.
