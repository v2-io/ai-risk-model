# Unit 216 · L303 · p.12

> 1107.2. The loss of value of equity does not count as damage to or loss of property for the purposes of this chapter.

## What differed

**My inference in units 191 and 193 was wrong.** There I concluded that the Labor Code chapter has no equity exclusion, so its catastrophic risk "probably includes loss of equity value". It does have one: §1107.2 repeats §22757.16 word for word, as the chapter's last section. I reasoned from the definitions list ending at (f) and didn't wait for the rest of the chapter. It's the forward-reference problem from the other side: I drew a conclusion about a chapter before I'd read all of it.

## Wandering

- **The correction.** Both catastrophic-risk definitions exclude loss of equity value, each through its own separate section (§22757.16 for the TFAIA, §1107.2 for the Labor Code). On equity, the two definitions **agree**. What stays different:
  - the model noun (frontier vs foundation);
  - critical safety incident (1)'s property harm (added in the Labor Code);
  - the two stylistic drifts.
- **"Property" is still undefined in the Labor Code.** The TFAIA's "tangible or intangible property" (§22757.11(l)) has no counterpart in §1107. So the Labor Code's "property" takes its ordinary meaning (which in legal usage includes intangible property, so the practical difference is probably nil). That's a smaller remaining asymmetry, and I'll state it at that size.
- **The same drafting rule shows up at the section level.** The Labor Code mirrors the TFAIA's structure: definitions, duties, then an equity exclusion as a separate final section. The drafters placed the equity rule the same way in both chapters. That suggests §22757.16 and §1107.2 were added together, probably in one late amendment, perhaps in response to a concern that market crashes might count. Version history could confirm.
- For the reflections as a record: **u191's and u193's equity claims are wrong, and this unit corrects them.** I'm leaving them as written, so that a fork reading the reflections sees both the error and the correction. The error is the useful part. It's exactly the kind of mistake a summary-reader makes: the definitions section looks complete, and the rule that modifies it sits elsewhere. I just made it myself, after having caught the same pattern at u145.
- In practice, then, CLAUDE.md's "reaches into two other provisions" most likely refers to §22757.16 (the equity rule) and the incident definition (d). Or, counting across the bill, the two equity sections. I still can't be sure which two CLAUDE.md meant.
