# Unit 15 · L52 · p.2

> This bill would require a large frontier developer to provide a certain internal process through which a covered employee may anonymously disclose information to the large frontier developer if the covered employee believes in good faith that the information indicates that the large frontier developer's activities present a specific and substantial danger … resulting from a catastrophic risk or that the large frontier developer violated the TFAIA. The bill would specify provisions particular to the enforcement of those whistleblower protections and would authorize attorney's fees to a plaintiff who brings a successful action for a violation.

## What differed

- "A **certain** internal process", not the "reasonable" one I recalled. The digest withholds the detail. My recalled "monthly update" isn't here, so it may be in the statute, or my memory may be wrong.
- Remedies: "provisions particular to the enforcement" and attorney's fees. No burden-shift detail in the digest.

## Wandering

- **Two epistemic standards in two adjacent provisions:**
  - the anti-retaliation prohibition (unit 14) protects disclosure where the employee "has **reasonable cause to believe**";
  - the internal process (this unit) is for an employee who "**believes in good faith**".
  These aren't the same. "Reasonable cause" is objective: would a reasonable person believe it? "Good faith" is subjective: does this person honestly believe it? The good-faith standard is easier to meet. That fits: internal anonymous reporting should be open, and it costs the developer little. Retaliation protection carries legal consequences, so it has the stricter objective standard. But if the statute uses the same pair, a fork asked "what belief standard does SB 53's whistleblower protection use?" has to say: **it depends which protection**. That is precisely the "one word, many things" problem, the different-standards-under-one-label kind, inside one chapter. And the digest is the summarizer here, so check whether the statute actually varies.
- **Tier shift:** the prohibition applies to "a frontier developer" (unit 14); the internal-process duty applies to "a **large** frontier developer". So a small frontier developer, one that trained a frontier model but is under the revenue bar, can't retaliate but needn't build an anonymous channel. A tidy proportionality design. A careless summary ("frontier developers must provide anonymous reporting") would be wrong.
- "Pose" (unit 14) versus "present" (unit 15). A synonym swap. Probably meaningless, but it's the kind of thing that, in a statute, a court might be asked to distinguish. Watch whether the statute itself varies.
- "Indicates" (unit 15) versus "discloses" (unit 14): "the information **indicates** that" against "the information **discloses** that". "Indicates" is weaker evidence; "discloses" suggests the information reveals it. Same direction as the belief-standard shift: the internal channel is looser on every axis.
- Attorney's fees "to a plaintiff who brings a successful action" is one-way fee-shifting, a standard pro-employee device. It makes litigation affordable for an individual employee against a lab.
