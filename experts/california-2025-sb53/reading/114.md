# Unit 114 · L181 · p.7

> (e) (1) The Attorney General or the Office of Emergency Services may transmit reports of critical safety incidents and reports from covered employees made pursuant to Chapter 5.1 (commencing with Section 1107) of Part 3 of Division 2 of the Labor Code to the Legislature, the Governor, the federal government, or appropriate state agencies.

## What differed

The AG appears beside OES, which I hadn't expected. And the TFAIA explicitly cross-references the Labor Code chapter.

## Wandering

- **The two chapters cross-reference each other.** The TFAIA (B&P) points into the Labor Code chapter ("reports from covered employees made pursuant to Chapter 5.1"), and the Labor Code chapter points back to the TFAIA ("violated the TFAIA", per the digest). So the bill's two main parts are wired to each other. That's relevant to unit 4's question: the Labor Code chapter probably *imports* the TFAIA's definitions (catastrophic risk, frontier developer) by reference, so there would be one concept, not two. To confirm when I get there.
- **Who receives covered-employee reports?** The AG, presumably (one of the whistleblower recipients in the digest, u14). So the AG holds whistleblower reports, OES holds incident reports, and either can pass them to the Legislature, Governor, federal government or state agencies. "**May**", discretionary. There's no mandatory onward reporting of individual reports.
- "**The federal government**" is unspecified. Federal partners are left to the agencies to choose. This is the path by which California's incident data could reach CAISI, DHS or DoD.
- (e)(1) implies (e)(2): probably a confidentiality consideration for those transmissions.
