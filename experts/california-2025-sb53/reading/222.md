# Unit 222 · L309 · p.12

> (f) This act preempts any rule, regulation, code, ordinance, or other law adopted by a city, county, city and county, municipality, or local agency on or after January 1, 2025, specifically related to the regulation of frontier developers with respect to their management of catastrophic risk.

## What differed

The local preemption is here, in uncodified SEC. 5(f). The words match the digest (u16) closely. There's no "matter of statewide concern" phrase, which I'd predicted.

## Wandering

- **This answers unit 16's question about where preemption is codified.** It isn't codified at all. It sits in the uncodified SEC. 5, like liberal construction (u218). So anyone reading the codified B&P or Labor Code sections won't see that local laws are preempted.
- **The preemption clause uses "catastrophic risk" in a section where the term is undefined.** "Frontier developers … their management of catastrophic risk". Both definitions of "catastrophic risk" are scoped "for purposes of this chapter": the TFAIA's to B&P Ch. 25.1 (u39) and the Labor Code's to Ch. 5.1 (u177). SEC. 5 is in neither chapter. So **in the preemption clause "catastrophic risk" is formally undefined.** A court would almost certainly borrow the TFAIA definition, since the preempted subject (developers' *management* of catastrophic risk) is the TFAIA's subject. But it's a third, implicit use of the term, defined by neither chapter. Which definition applies changes the preemption's scope: the Labor Code's foundation-model version would preempt more local law than the TFAIA's frontier-model version. **An honest expert answer: it isn't stated, the TFAIA's is the likely referent, and liberal construction could argue either way.** Liberal construction "to effectuate its purposes" applies to preemption too, but here broad preemption *narrows* local protection. Which way does liberal construction cut? Unclear.
  - "Frontier developers" is likewise formally undefined here, though it's the same term in both chapters (the Labor Code cross-references the TFAIA), so there's no practical ambiguity.
- **Charter cities.** Without "matter of statewide concern" language, a charter city (San Francisco is one) might argue home-rule protection for a municipal-affairs ordinance under Cal. Const. art. XI §5. Courts decide statewide concern themselves, so the label isn't decisive, but drafters often include it. Its absence is a small vulnerability, if San Francisco ever wanted its own frontier-AI rule. Speculative.
- "**On or after January 1, 2025**": retroactive to before enactment, as I noted at u16.
