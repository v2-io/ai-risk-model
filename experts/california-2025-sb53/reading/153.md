## Wandering

- "Fully owned and hosted": owned by whom? The state, or UC, presumably. This rules out a pure arrangement of rented commercial cloud credits (as the US NAIRR pilot largely uses). So it's public infrastructure, not vouchers. That's expensive, which is part of why it's contingent on appropriation. Only the statute's word "fully" stands between this and a cheaper credits model.
- A thought on tension: an owned public cluster able to train frontier-scale models would make *California itself* a potential frontier developer, if it trained a model over 10^26 operations. The TFAIA's "person" is undefined. Is a state agency or UC a "person" subject to the TFAIA? Probably not, since statutes usually don't bind the sovereign unless they say so. A curious edge: the state could build what it regulates and possibly be outside its own regulation. Very hypothetical; CalCompute won't approach 10^26 soon.
