## Wandering

- **The stated target exposes the revenue proxy's gap.** The intended target is "**well-resourced**" frontier developers; the operational test is group revenue over $500M. In unit 64 I noted that a venture-funded lab with billions in capital but little revenue escapes "large". Now the statute's own target says such a lab *should* be covered: it's well-resourced. So the review provision effectively invites the Department of Technology to recommend a resource-based test (capital raised, compute spend, valuation) instead of, or beside, revenue. **A good question for a source expert, with a text-grounded answer:** "Does SB 53 intend to cover a well-funded pre-revenue lab as 'large'?" Its stated aim says yes ("well-resourced"); its operational test says no (revenue only). The gap is acknowledged in principle and left to future legislation.
- **The list has three definitions: frontier model, frontier developer, large frontier developer.** Catastrophic risk and critical safety incident aren't on it (if the list ends here). So **the review can't touch what counts as catastrophic** (51 / $1B / the scenario list) or what counts as an incident. Only *who and what is covered* is reviewed. The Legislature expects drift in scope, not in the harm concepts. The $1B in nominal dollars will erode with inflation, and no review is directed at it.
