# Unit 46 · L103 · p.4

> (A) Information that a frontier model outputs if the information is otherwise publicly accessible in a substantially similar form from a source other than a foundation model.

## What differed

My recall was exact. This is the **counterfactual baseline** CLAUDE.md mentions: risk measured against what is already available without the model.

## Wandering

- **The unit of comparison is "the information".** Excluded: information the model outputs *if that information* is otherwise publicly accessible in substantially similar form. So the comparison is output-to-public, per item of information. The hard case is **synthesis**. Every fact in a bioweapon protocol may be public, scattered across papers, while the assembled, troubleshot, step-ordered protocol is not public "in a substantially similar form". Under this exclusion, the *assembled* output is "information" not publicly accessible in that form, so it's not excluded. That's the right reading for safety, and I think the drafters intended it ("substantially similar *form*" is exactly the synthesis-catching phrase). But a looser reading ("each piece is public, so the information is public") would hollow out (A) of the definition. **Interpretive hinge: does aggregation of public facts count as public?** "Form" says no, I'd argue.
- **The exclusion bites only on information outputs.** It says "information that a frontier model outputs". The (B) autonomous-conduct and (C) evasion categories aren't information outputs. So the public-availability baseline applies to (A)-type assistance, not to autonomous action. A model autonomously running a cyberattack using only public exploits isn't excluded: the risk is *conduct*, not information. Clean design: the counterfactual baseline is scoped to uplift.
- **"A source other than a foundation model"**, foundation, not frontier. So information available *only* from some other AI model, even a small open-weight one, doesn't count as publicly accessible for this purpose. This blocks a circularity: "our model's bio output is public because other models also say it". It also means the proliferation of capable open models doesn't erode the baseline. A careful drafting choice. Note "foundation model" is used here before it's defined (alphabetically it's later). A forward reference inside the definitions, normal in statutes.
- "**Publicly accessible**": to whom, at what cost? Behind a paywall? In a specialist journal? On the dark web? In a library in another language? Undefined. A developer arguing exclusion would want "accessible" read broadly (any paper anywhere). A regulator would want "readily accessible to a member of the public". The text says only "publicly accessible".
- "**Otherwise**" has no timing element, so it's evaluated at the time of output, presumably. Information made public *after* the model outputs it (by a leak, say) wouldn't retroactively exclude… or would it? The risk is assessed prospectively, so the exclusion is too. Edge case.
- For the expert reader: this is SB 53's version of the "marginal risk" or "counterfactual uplift" concept (NTIA's 2024 open-weights report spoke of "marginal risk"; Anthropic's RSP and OpenAI's PF reason in terms of uplift over existing resources). Lexicon near-neighbours: *marginal risk* (NTIA), *uplift* (labs), *otherwise publicly accessible in a substantially similar form* (SB 53). SB 53's is a **binary exclusion** (public → excluded), not a *degree* of marginal uplift. Different structure: the labs ask "how much uplift?"; SB 53 asks "is it already public?".
