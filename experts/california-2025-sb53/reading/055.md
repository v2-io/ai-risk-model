## What differed

## Wandering

- **Third-party evaluation is not deployment.** So pre-release testing by UK AISI, CAISI, METR, Apollo and the like doesn't trigger the transparency report. Sensible, since otherwise the duty to report "before deploying" would arrive before testing could happen.
- But "**primary purpose**" invites a loophole question. A "research preview" to 10,000 trusted testers, framed as evaluation, could be argued not to be deployment. A model made available to enterprise partners "for the primary purpose of evaluating" it in their workflows? A broad early-access program? "Primary purpose" is a purpose test, so the developer's characterization matters, and the boundary between a beta and a deployment becomes contestable. **A question someone should ask: are staged rollouts, trusted-tester programs and research previews deployment?** The text gives a purpose test, not a scale test.
- "Developing **or** evaluating". Making a model available to a contractor who helps *develop* it (fine-tuning vendors, data annotators using the model) isn't deployment. That covers the RLHF-contractor case. Also, possibly, a cloud provider who trains or hosts the model for the developer.
- This pairs with internal use. A model in internal use or third-party evaluation is "not deployed", yet still subject to (c)'s "development, storage, use" conduct for catastrophic risk, and to internal-use assessments for large developers. So SB 53's regime splits into a *deployment* layer (transparency reports) and a *pre-deployment and internal* layer (frameworks, internal-use summaries, catastrophic-risk definition's conduct). That's a more complete lifecycle coverage than "deployment-only" laws.
