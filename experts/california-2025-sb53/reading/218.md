# Unit 218 · L305 · p.12

> (b) This act shall be liberally construed to effectuate its purposes.

## What differed

It's here at last: SEC. 5(b), an **uncodified** section, applying to "**this act**".

## Wandering

- **This settles unit 146's question.** "This act" means the whole bill: the TFAIA, CalCompute and the Labor Code chapter. So CLAUDE.md's "SB 53 is to be 'liberally construed to effectuate its purposes'" is **correct for the whole bill**. My worry that it might apply only to part was unfounded. Good. That was a prediction worth having and worth retiring.
- **But it isn't in the codified text, and that has a real consequence.** SEC. 5 isn't added to any code. A reader of B&P §22757.10–16 or Labor Code §1107–1107.2, as compiled in the codes (on leginfo's "California Law" tab, or in annotated codes), **won't see the liberal-construction rule or the severability clause**, except in historical notes. Most practitioners read the codified sections. So the rule is easy to miss. For retrieval: the liberal-construction clause lives only in the bill text, and the corpus's canonical text is the bill (this leginfo page), so it's present here. A corpus holding only the codified sections would lack it.
- **What are "its purposes"?** The act's purposes aren't stated in any operative provision. They live in SEC. 1's findings, which are also uncodified. So the interpretive chain is: liberal construction (uncodified SEC. 5(b)) → purposes (uncodified SEC. 1) → most directly, (o) "potential catastrophic risk … which this act aims to address" and (p) "intent … to create more transparency" (u35–36). **For any ambiguity I've flagged** (materialization u51, the 24-hour reading A/B u110, fine-tuning u63, "meaningful oversight" u43), liberal construction toward transparency and catastrophic-risk mitigation would favour **the broader, more protective reading**:
  - materialization without the full threshold (u51) → broader reporting;
  - the 24-hour disclosure *in addition to* OES (u110) → reading B;
  - aggregation of public facts counting as not public (u46);
  - and so on.

  But for the fine-tuning question (u63), liberal construction points one way (more coverage) and the review section's stated target ("developers … themselves at the frontier", u131) points the other. That's a real conflict between two purpose signals.
- **"Liberally"** applied to a statute that also imposes civil penalties. Penalty provisions are traditionally construed strictly (the rule of lenity, in a civil-penalty form). So courts may apply liberal construction to the remedial and whistleblower parts and stricter construction to the penalty parts. It's a known tension in remedial statutes with penalties.
