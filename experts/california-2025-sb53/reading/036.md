# Unit 36 · L84 · p.3

> (p) It is the intent of the Legislature to create more transparency, but collective safety will depend in part on frontier developers taking due care in their development and deployment of frontier models proportional to the scale of the foreseeable risks.

## What differed

An intent clause, as I'd given at 20% in unit 34. Its content is the surprise: the intent is limited to *transparency*, and the rest of the sentence says outright that **safety depends on something the act does not require**.

## Wandering

- **This is the most important finding in SEC. 1, I think.** It splits two things:
  - what the Legislature *intends this act to do*: "create more transparency";
  - what *safety actually requires*: "frontier developers taking due care … proportional to the scale of the foreseeable risks".
  The "but" marks the gap between them. The Legislature is saying: we're doing transparency; safety needs due care too; we aren't legislating due care here. It answers my question from unit 30. **SB 53 does not impose a duty of care, and says so, in effect, in its own intent clause.** The "careful diligence and reasonable precaution" of (j) and the "due care" of (p) are both acknowledged and both left outside the operative regime.
- **The tort shadow, though.** "Due care … proportional to the scale of the foreseeable risks" is negligence language: due care, foreseeability, proportionality. California tort law already imposes a general duty of ordinary care (Civil Code §1714). A plaintiff suing a frontier developer in negligence after a catastrophe could cite (p) as *legislative recognition* that due care proportional to foreseeable risk is expected of frontier developers. So a finding that disclaims creating a duty might still feed the common-law duty analysis. That's speculative legal reasoning on my part, but it's the kind of thing a source expert should flag: **the findings may matter more in tort litigation than in TFAIA enforcement.** And to anyone asking "does SB 53 affect liability?": not directly, but (p) may be cited.
- "**Foreseeable risks**". Here is the knowledge standard's vocabulary arriving, as "material" arrived in (d). CLAUDE.md says the catastrophic-risk definition has a knowledge standard; I'd bet it's "foreseeable" ("a foreseeable and material risk"). The findings are seeding the definition's adjectives: material (d), foreseeable (p).
- "**Proportional to the scale**" is a proportionality principle again (after (d)'s "balance" and (k)'s burden on smaller companies). Proportionality runs through the whole bill.
- "**Collective safety**" is a new protected-good framing, maybe the most expansive: not consumers or the public of California, but *collective* safety. The protected party keeps broadening across the findings: Californians → consumers → the public → public safety → collective safety.
- "**In part**" is honest: transparency is part of safety, and the developers' own conduct is another part. The Legislature claims only partial contribution. That fits the hedging register of all the risk findings.
- "Development and deployment" are the two lifecycle stages. Internal use falls under development, presumably, or neither. The digest's "internal use" (u8) fits under "development" loosely.
