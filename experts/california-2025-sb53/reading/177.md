# Unit 177 · L255–257 · p.10

> 1107. For purposes of this chapter:
> (a) (1) "Catastrophic risk" means a foreseeable and material risk that a frontier developer's development, storage, use, or deployment of a **foundation** model will materially contribute to the death of, or serious injury to, more than 50 people or more than one billion dollars ($1,000,000,000) in damage to, or loss of, property arising from a single incident involving a **foundation** model doing any of the following:

## What differed

**The Labor Code redefines "catastrophic risk" itself, with no cross-reference to the TFAIA.** I predicted a cross-reference ("has the same meaning as defined in Section 22757.11"). Instead the definition is written out again, and with two substitutions: **"foundation model" replaces "frontier model"**, in both the conduct clause and the scenario clause.

(Joseph asked me, mid-unit, whether the material is boring. This unit is my answer.)

## Wandering

- **SB 53 has two definitions of catastrophic risk.** The question I raised at unit 4 now has its answer:
  - **TFAIA (B&P §22757.11(c)):** risk from a frontier developer's activities with a **frontier model** (>10^26 operations), in a single incident involving a **frontier model** doing the listed things.
  - **Labor Code (§1107(a)):** risk from a frontier developer's activities with a **foundation model** (any broad, general, adaptable model, at any compute), in a single incident involving a **foundation model** doing the listed things.

  The Labor Code definition is **broader**. A frontier developer's *sub-frontier* foundation models, say a small open-weight model a lab releases, generate "catastrophic risk" for whistleblower purposes but not for TFAIA purposes. So an employee at a frontier developer who reports a catastrophic danger from the company's *small* model is protected by the whistleblower chapter, even though that model's risks fall outside the TFAIA entirely. That's a sensible design: whistleblowers shouldn't need to know a model's compute count. And it's invisible to anyone who reads "catastrophic risk" once and assumes it means one thing.
  - **This is the most important cross-provision fact in the bill for this project.** The lexicon needs **two source-namespaced entries**, something like `ca-sb53-tfaia:catastrophic-risk` and `ca-sb53-labor:catastrophic-risk`, with the relation "same structure, broader artifact scope (foundation ⊃ frontier)". That fits the project's memory note on source-namespaced terms. One *source* holds two definitions, so the namespace has to be finer than the source.
  - CLAUDE.md said the catastrophic-risk definition "reaches into two other provisions". Now I'd say the full picture is: the TFAIA definition reaches into §22757.11(d) (incidents) and is narrowed by §22757.16 (equity). **A second, parallel definition exists in Labor Code §1107**, and whether §22757.16's equity exclusion reaches it is doubtful, because §22757.16 says "for the purposes of this chapter", the B&P chapter. Unless §1107 repeats the exclusion, **the Labor Code's catastrophic risk may count equity losses** (u145's speculation, now concrete). Watch the next units for an equity clause.
- **The actor is still "a frontier developer".** The Labor Code version keeps "a frontier developer's development …". So the *developer* must be frontier (trained a >10^26 model), and the *model* in question can be any foundation model of theirs. Developer-level gating, model-level breadth.
- **Is everything else identical?** Reading word by word against u41: "foreseeable and material risk", "development, storage, use, or deployment", "will materially contribute", "death of, or serious injury to, more than 50 people", "$1,000,000,000 in damage to, or loss of, property", "arising from a single incident", "doing any of the following". Identical apart from the two noun swaps. So it's a copy-and-edit, the same drafting move as the AG report copying the OES one (u140). The scenarios and exclusions that follow need the same word-by-word check. If any of them differs, that's a third definition-level divergence.
- **On recall:** I didn't remember this at all. My recall of the TFAIA definition was perfect, and I had no memory that the Labor Code repeats it with different nouns. That fits everything so far: the much-quoted text was memorized, the less-quoted parallel wasn't. **Anyone citing "SB 53's definition of catastrophic risk" from memory or from a summary will cite the TFAIA one and miss this.**
