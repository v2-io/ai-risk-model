# Unit 61 · L118 · p.4

> (h) "Frontier developer" means a person who has trained, or initiated the training of, a frontier model, with respect to which the person has used, or intends to use, at least as much computing power to train the frontier model as would meet the technical specifications found in subdivision (i).

## What differed

My recall was exact. That matches CLAUDE.md's description: "defined by a training act and a compute threshold".

## Wandering

- **The structure is odd, possibly circular.** A frontier developer is a person who trained a *frontier model* **and** used (or intends to use) enough compute to meet the specs in (i). If (i) defines a frontier model *by* the compute spec, the second clause adds nothing: anyone who trained a frontier model by definition used that compute. The clause does work only if (i) defines a frontier model in some way that compute alone doesn't capture. Or, more likely, it does work through the **"intends to use"** prong: someone who has *initiated* training of what *will be* a frontier model, intending to reach the threshold, is a frontier developer *before* the model exists as a frontier model. So the definition reaches **mid-training** developers. Their duties (incident reporting, the whistleblower rules) attach during the run, not after. That's sensible, since a training run can be months long, and catastrophic risk from *development* and *storage* (u41) arises during it.
  - Still, "trained, or initiated the training of, **a frontier model**" names a *frontier model* as the object even in the initiated case, while the model isn't one yet. Strictly, a model under training that hasn't reached 10^26 isn't yet a frontier model. The "intends to use" clause bridges this. The drafting is tangled but the intent is clear: **the frontier-developer status attaches at initiation if the intended compute crosses the threshold.**
- **"Intends to use"**: an intent element. A developer that plans a 10^26 run and starts it is a frontier developer from day one. One that stumbles over the threshold unintentionally becomes one when it crosses. Intent needs evidence (planning documents, compute contracts). That's an enforcement question.
- **"A person"**: undefined so far. If "person" includes natural persons, an individual who trains a frontier model is a frontier developer. That's possible in principle (with enough rented compute), and it matters for the "developer" collision (CLAUDE.md: NIST counts individuals as developers). Watch for "person".
- **"Trained … a frontier model"**: who counts as having trained it? The one who ran the training job, the one who paid for the compute, or the cloud provider operating the hardware? In practice, the commissioning entity. And **fine-tuning**: someone who fine-tunes a frontier model has "trained" it, in a sense. Do they become a frontier developer? Only if *their* compute meets the spec, since "the person has used … at least as much computing power to train the frontier model as would meet the specs". So a fine-tuner using small compute doesn't qualify, unless the spec in (i) counts cumulative compute including the original pretraining. **(i) is the hinge here: is compute cumulative across developers, or per-developer?** My memory says (i) includes "the original training run and any subsequent fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, or other material modifications the developer applies". "The developer applies" makes it per-developer. Check.
- **The object is the model, not the company.** You're a frontier developer by virtue of *having trained* one. Once a frontier developer, always? "Has trained" is perfect tense, so yes: a company that trained one frontier model in 2026 remains a frontier developer forever, even if it never trains another. Its duties (incident reporting) presumably attach to its frontier models.
