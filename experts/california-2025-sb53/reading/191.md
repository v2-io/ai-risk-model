# Unit 191 · L271 · p.10

> (d) "Foundation model" has the meaning defined in Section 22757.11 of the Business and Professions Code.

## What differed

This one is a **cross-reference**, not a restatement.

## Wandering

- **There's a drafting rule behind this.** The Labor Code *restates* catastrophic risk and critical safety incident, the two terms it *changes*. It *cross-references* foundation model, which it doesn't change. So the drafters restated a term exactly when they meant it to differ, and cross-referenced it when they meant it to match. That's a principled pattern, and evidence that the divergences in u177–u190 are intended. (The stylistic drift in (B) and (2)(C) is the exception: incidental changes inside restated text.)
  - If this rule holds, then "frontier developer" and "large frontier developer", if defined below, will be cross-references, because they're unchanged.
- **The cross-reference imports a chain.** The B&P foundation model is defined as an "artificial intelligence model" (B&P (b)) with three properties. Cross-referencing "the meaning defined in Section 22757.11" brings in (b) as well, presumably. So the system-like OECD definition comes along.
- **"Property" still isn't defined in the Labor Code chapter.** Item (1) added "damage to, or loss of, property", and catastrophic risk uses "property" too. Unless a later subdivision defines it, the Labor Code's "property" takes its ordinary meaning. The TFAIA's explicit "tangible or intangible" (§22757.11(l)) and its equity exclusion (§22757.16) are both scoped to *the TFAIA chapter*. So the Labor Code's property concept is undefined, and **its catastrophic risk probably includes loss of equity value**. That makes the Labor Code's $1B threshold easier to meet: a model-driven stock crash could qualify, for whistleblower purposes. This is still to be confirmed against the rest of §1107.
