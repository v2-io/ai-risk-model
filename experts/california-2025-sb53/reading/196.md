## Wandering

- **An unpenalized duty can still be violated.** In u143 I found that only *large* frontier developers face civil penalties. A small frontier developer that skips its transparency report, fails to report an incident, or makes a materially misleading statement about catastrophic risk faces no §22757.15 penalty. But it has still "**violated Chapter 25.1**", because the duty exists even without a penalty. So a covered employee at a small frontier developer who reports that violation is protected under prong (2). **The duties on small developers with no penalty attached aren't toothless after all.** Whistleblowing can expose them, the AG can pass reports on (§22757.13(e)), and reputational consequences follow. That changes my u143 conclusion: **small frontier developers have duties without civil penalties, but violating them is protected whistleblowing territory.**
- Prong (2) also takes in "comply with" for large developers. Any reasonable belief that the lab isn't following its own framework is protected. That's the broad prong noted in u67.
- The cross-reference is to "Chapter 25.1" as a whole, so it covers every duty in the TFAIA, including the five-year retention duty (u143 found it unpenalized). Failure to retain unredacted documents is a violation an employee can report.
- **The two prongs work as a pair.** Prong (1) is about danger (substantive, catastrophic) and prong (2) is about legality (any TFAIA duty). It mirrors 1102.5 (illegality) plus a danger extension.
