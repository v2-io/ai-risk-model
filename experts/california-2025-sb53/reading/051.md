## Wandering

- **This is one place the catastrophic-risk definition reaches into another provision** (CLAUDE.md's "reaches into two other provisions"). The incident definition imports all ten or so concepts of catastrophic risk through one noun phrase. Any change to (c) silently changes (d)(2).
- **What is it for a risk to "materialize"?** The catastrophic risk is "a foreseeable and material risk that [activity] will materially contribute to [51+ deaths/serious injuries or $1B property] arising from a single incident involving [an enumerated doing]". There are two readings of "materialization":
  1. **Full materialization:** the threshold outcome occurs (51+ harmed, or $1B). Then item (2) applies only once an actual catastrophe has happened.
  2. **Scenario materialization:** the enumerated doing occurs (the model gives expert-level CBRN assistance; it evades control), with *some* harm, below threshold. Then "harm resulting" covers any harm from the scenario.

  The definition builds the threshold into the risk, so (1) is the stricter reading: the risk *is* the risk of a threshold outcome, so its materialization is the threshold outcome. But then (2) reports only actual catastrophes, which is fairly useless as an early warning. Under (2) the model helps make a bioweapon, it's used, and 20 people die: a catastrophic risk partially materialized? The text doesn't settle it. **Interpretive hinge: does "materialization" require the threshold?** A source expert should flag it rather than pick a side.
- "**Harm** resulting from": any harm, unqualified. That's the event; the catastrophic risk is its cause.
- Under reading (1), the reporting regime has an odd shape: the bar for a *catastrophic-risk-materialization incident* is a realized mass-casualty event, while items (1) and (3) need only one bodily injury. The incident list mixes very high and very low severities. It's coherent, though: the items differ in *kind*, and each has its own severity condition.
