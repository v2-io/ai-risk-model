## Wandering

- **A capability-threshold architecture, codified.** "Thresholds … to identify and assess whether a frontier model has capabilities that could pose a catastrophic risk" is the RSP/PF/FSF architecture: capability thresholds (ASLs, PF "High/Critical", CCLs) trigger mitigations. "Which may include multiple-tiered thresholds" explicitly makes room for ASL-2/3/4-style tiers. **SB 53 writes the lab-framework genre's internal structure into law:** capability thresholds → assessment → mitigations. The thresholds are the developer's own. So the state now enforces compliance with thresholds the developer defines. A lab that lowers its own thresholds (some 2025 framework revisions did adjust thresholds, I recall, e.g. OpenAI's PF v2 dropping "persuasion" as a tracked category) changes what it must comply with. There's no ratchet in sight. Watch whether revisions must be justified (item (6), maybe).
- **Two thresholds talking past each other.** SB 53's catastrophic risk carries a *harm* threshold (51 people, $1B). The developer's thresholds here are *capability* thresholds ("capabilities that could pose a catastrophic risk"). So the developer has to bridge harm and capability: which capabilities could lead to a 51-casualty or $1B event? Each lab's framework is a theory of that bridge. That's the most interesting content an SB 53 framework can have, and the statute only requires that it be *described*.
- "**Could pose**" a catastrophic risk is a capability that could pose a risk: a possibility of a risk again (as in the findings). Lower than "poses".
- "**Defining and assessing**": two activities, making thresholds and evaluating models against them. Evaluations live under "assessing".
