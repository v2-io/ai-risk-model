- Three codes, three audiences:
  - The **Business and Professions Code** is where California regulates businesses, so this is the developer obligations.
  - The **Government Code** section is probably CalCompute, or the Office of Emergency Services' role. A single section, 11546.8, sits in the Government Code's state technology area: 11546 is around the Department of Technology. So it's probably CalCompute.
  - The **Labor Code** chapter is the whistleblower protections.
  That split reflects a legislative decision about who enforces what. The Labor Code part is enforced through employment law (the Labor Commissioner, civil actions by employees); the B&P part through the Attorney General's civil penalties. So "SB 53's definitions" may not be one set: each code chapter can have its own definitions section. The Labor Code chapter might define "covered employee", "catastrophic risk" or "frontier developer" by cross-reference to the B&P chapter, or on its own. **Watch whether the Labor Code chapter re-defines or cross-references.** If it re-defines, there could be two "catastrophic risk"s in one bill. That would be the most interesting kind of finding for this project.
  - CLAUDE.md said the catastrophic-risk definition "reaches into two other provisions". That might be exactly this cross-code reach.
- "Relating to artificial intelligence." The title clause is a legal requirement in California (the single-subject rule; the title must express the subject). "Artificial intelligence" is broad enough to cover all three codes.
- No urgency clause in the title. So, as I guessed, it's probably operative January 1, 2026, by default.
