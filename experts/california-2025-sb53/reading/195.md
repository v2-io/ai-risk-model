## Wandering

- **"Specific and substantial danger … resulting from a catastrophic risk".** Every "catastrophic risk" in this chapter is the Labor Code's broader one (foundation models, and equity loss not excluded). So prong (1) protects reports about danger from any of a frontier developer's foundation models.
- **The standards stack, but not in a straight line.** "Specific and substantial danger" is a long-standing whistleblower phrase (the federal Whistleblower Protection Act uses "substantial and specific danger to public health or safety"). Note the order: SB 53 says "specific and substantial", the WPA says "substantial and specific". So the danger standard is borrowed and adapted from federal law, and **"specific and substantial danger" brings with it a body of federal case law on what counts as specific and substantial.** A source expert could point to that lineage. To check: federal WPA 5 U.S.C. §2302(b)(8). That's from memory, and the order of the words is something to verify.
  - The stack: reasonable cause to believe → information discloses → activities pose → a specific and substantial danger → resulting from → a catastrophic risk (foreseeable, material) → material contribution → 51+ / $1B. That's many layers. Danger and risk are related here, but it isn't a clean chain.
- "**The frontier developer's activities**": the danger must come from the developer's *activities*, not from a third party's misuse alone. That matches the TFAIA's conduct element (development, storage, use, deployment).
