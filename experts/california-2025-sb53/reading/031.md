# Unit 31 · L79 · p.3

> (k) With the frontier of artificial intelligence rapidly evolving, there is a need for legislation to track the frontier of artificial intelligence research and alert policymakers and the public to serious risks and harms from the very most advanced artificial intelligence systems, while avoiding burdening smaller companies behind the frontier.

## What differed

My first prediction was "need for state action now", in the rapid-evolution theme. The sentence leads there, but its content is the **design principle**: *track the frontier* while *avoiding burdening smaller companies behind it*. It's the rationale for thresholds.

## Wandering

- **"Track the frontier"** is a principle with a hidden mechanism. If the frontier moves, a fixed threshold (10^26 FLOPs) stops tracking it: in time, many small companies cross it. So "track the frontier" implies the definitions must *move*. I recall a provision directing the Department of Technology to review the definitions annually and recommend updates [recall]. This finding is the purpose that provision serves. If that provision exists, it's the bill's answer to "how does a compute threshold track a moving frontier?": a recommendation to the Legislature, not automatic updating. Then whether the threshold tracks depends on the Legislature acting on it.
- **"The very most advanced"** is superlative and emphatic, almost colloquial for a statute. It signals that the regulated class is meant to be tiny: a handful of labs. The tiers (frontier developer, large frontier developer) are the implementation.
- **"Serious risks and harms"**. The finding uses "serious", not "catastrophic". So the purpose is to alert people to *serious* risks, a lower bar than the operative *catastrophic*. Does the statute use "serious" anywhere operatively? "Serious injury" sits in the casualty threshold (">50 people … death or serious injury" [primed]); "serious physical injury" in the 24-hour rule [recall]. Here "serious" qualifies risks and harms generally. **Lexicon note: "serious" is used at least in a findings sense here, and probably in an injury sense later.** Don't merge them.
- "Alert policymakers and the public" means the alerting function goes to two audiences. But the operative channels I know of are *confidential* to the government (incident reports, internal-use summaries, whistleblowers) or *self-authored* for the public (frameworks, transparency reports). Who alerts the public to *serious risks and harms*? Maybe an OES public report, aggregated and anonymized [recall: starting 2027]. If the only public alert is an annual anonymized summary, the gap between the purpose ("alert … the public") and the mechanism is wide. Someone should ask: **how does SB 53 actually alert the public to a specific serious harm?** Possibly it doesn't, except through what developers choose to publish.
- "Smaller companies behind the frontier" is the obverse of "companies large and small" (a). Unit 21's boosterism resolves here into a carve-out principle.
