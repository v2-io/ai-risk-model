# Unit 126 · L193 · p.7

> (A) The frontier developer shall be deemed in compliance with this section to the extent that the frontier developer meets the standards of, or complies with the requirements imposed or stated by, the designated federal law, regulation, or guidance document until the frontier developer declares the revocation of that intent to the Office of Emergency Services or the Office of Emergency Services revokes a relevant regulation pursuant to subdivision (j).

## What differed

My recall was exact. It's a little striking how complete my memory of this section is, given how mechanical it is.

## Wandering

- "**Deemed in compliance … to the extent that**" makes it a *partial* safe harbour. If the federal standard covers only part of SB 53's incident duties, the developer is compliant only for that part. That lets equivalence work piece by piece.
- **Two exits:** the developer revokes its declaration, or OES revokes the designating regulation under (j). So OES can revoke a designation, presumably if the federal standard weakens. **That's the anti-race-to-the-bottom guard:** if federal guidance is watered down, California can pull the designation and its own reporting resumes. Good drafting, and (j) will say when.
- I find myself admiring this mechanism. It's a careful piece of federalism engineering: a state law that steps back for an equivalent federal regime, asks the developer to declare it, keeps a revocation lever and, I expect, makes federal noncompliance a state violation. California keeps enforcement power even when deferring on substance.
