# Unit 214 · L301 · p.12

> (j) (1) This section does not impair or limit the applicability of Section 1102.5, including with respect to the rights of employees who are not covered employees to report violations of this chapter or Chapter 25.1 (commencing with Section 22757.10) of Division 8 of the Business and Professions Code.

## What differed

It's a savings clause, as I'd half expected. What I didn't expect is that it names **non-covered employees** explicitly.

## Wandering

- **This refines my unit-185 conclusion.** Non-covered employees aren't protected by §1107.1. But §1102.5 protects *any* employee's disclosure of a *violation of law*, and a TFAIA violation is a violation of law. So **any employee** at a frontier developer is protected (under 1102.5) when reporting a TFAIA violation or a violation of this Labor Code chapter. What non-covered employees *don't* get is:
  - the **danger prong** (reporting catastrophic danger without a legal violation);
  - the internal anonymous channel's good-faith standard;
  - §1107.1's contract bar for 1102.5 disclosures (u197: that applies only to covered employees);
  - possibly the specific remedies here (no-stay injunction, fee-shifting), though 1102.5 has its own similar remedies.

  So the answer to "Are all AI lab employees protected?" is now: **all employees are protected under existing law when reporting TFAIA violations; only covered employees are protected when reporting catastrophic dangers that aren't violations.** That's a crisper answer, and it differs from what I'd have said at u185.
- "**Violations of this chapter**" in the 1102.5 context means violations of the Labor Code chapter, such as retaliation against a covered employee. So a non-covered colleague who reports that a covered colleague was retaliated against is protected.
- "(j)(1)" means a (2) follows.
