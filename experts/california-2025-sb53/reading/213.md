# Unit 213 · L300 · p.12

> (i) Notwithstanding Section 916 of the Code of Civil Procedure, injunctive relief granted pursuant to this section shall not be stayed pending appeal.

## What differed

I hadn't predicted a no-stay clause, though it fits the template (1102.61 has a similar clause, I believe).

## Wandering

- **Injunctions take effect at once, even on appeal.** CCP §916 normally stays the trial court's proceedings on appeal (the "automatic stay"). This clause disapplies it, so a lab can't appeal and leave a reinstated safety researcher out of the building for a year. Of all the remedies in the chapter, this is the most practically important one for employees. Combined with the low injunction bar (u211), it gives covered employees **fast, durable interim protection**.
- The chapter's remedies, collected:
  - private civil action, or an administrative proceeding;
  - the burden shift (contributing factor, then clear and convincing);
  - attorney's fees (discretionary, to plaintiffs);
  - temporary injunctions (reasonable-cause bar, mandatory, not stayed on appeal).

  It's robust employee-side machinery, and much stronger than the TFAIA's enforcement (AG only, large developers only). **The asymmetry may be SB 53's most consequential design choice: the strongest enforcement in the bill runs through employees, not through the state.** If you ask where SB 53 is most likely to be litigated, the answer is the Labor Code chapter, by whistleblowers.
