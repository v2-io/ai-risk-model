# The California Report on Frontier AI Policy (`bommasani-2025-california`): before

*Read by the SB 53 expert after SB 53 and RAISE, read whole in large sequential chunks, with notes between chunks. Joseph's question for me: **whether and where SB 53 departs from it.***

**What I carry in** (from training; soft):
- It's the Joint California Policy Working Group on AI Frontier Models: Fei-Fei Li, Mariano-Florentino Cuéllar, Jennifer Tour Chayes, with Rishi Bommasani (the relata key's first author) as lead writer, I think. A draft came out in March 2025 and the final in June 2025.
- Themes as I remember them:
  - "trust but verify";
  - evidence-based policy, while acting under uncertainty (the "evidence dilemma");
  - transparency into training data, safety practices, security, pre-deployment testing and downstream impacts;
  - third-party risk assessment, with safe harbours for independent researchers;
  - whistleblower protections;
  - adverse-event reporting;
  - "scoping": thresholds based on compute alone are a blunt instrument, and should be combined with risk evaluations or downstream impact;
  - lessons from history (tobacco, energy, internet), where industry knew things the public didn't.

**What SB 53 claims about it:** that it "recommended sound principles" (SEC. 1(c)), and that its release, with the hearings, "reflect[s] the advances in AI model capabilities that could pose potential catastrophic risk" (SEC. 1(o)).

**Where I expect SB 53 to depart:**
1. **Third-party assessment**: the report recommends it, and possibly safe harbours for researchers. SB 53 only requires *describing* one's approach (u72, u92).
2. **Thresholds**: the report cautions against compute alone. SB 53 uses compute plus revenue.
3. **Adverse events**: the report probably wants downstream-impact reporting, broader than SB 53's closed four-item incident list.
4. **Transparency content**: the report wants training-data and downstream-use transparency. SB 53's base transparency report has no risk content (u87).
5. **Catastrophic focus**: the report covers broad "material risks". SB 53 narrows to catastrophe (51 / $1B).
