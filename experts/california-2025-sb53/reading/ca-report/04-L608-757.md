# CA Report, L608–757: §3.4 transparency principles, §4 adverse event reporting, §5 scoping (start)

## A correction to note 01

There I called SB 53's change from "adverse event" (Principle 7) to "incident" (finding (i)) a vocabulary swap that carried a departure. **Footnote 7 (L642) undercuts the vocabulary half of that.** The report says the terms are interchangeable across domains: "adverse event reporting" in medicine, "incident reporting" in transportation and cybersecurity. "Irrespective of the name, these systems monitor for unexpected risks." So **the word change isn't the departure. The scope is.** The report's reportable events are realized harms broadly. SB 53's critical safety incidents are a closed list of four. I'm leaving note 01 as it was written and correcting it here.

## §4 adverse events: where SB 53 follows and where it departs

| Report (§4) | SB 53 |
|---|---|
| Events "defined in terms of unwanted, dangerous, or damaging output … such as events involving material harms to life, physical property, or financial interests"; "without having to enumerate every possible harm" (L673) | **A closed enumeration of four**, three of them tied to catastrophe or to weight security (u49–53). That's the opposite of the report's "without enumerating" ideal. |
| But L710: "An **initially narrow** adverse event reporting criteria built around a **tightly defined set of harms** promotes consistency … Thereafter, periodic review … can inform updates to reporting criteria", and "Policymakers could also consider **granting agencies sufficient authority to expand the scope** of reporting criteria" | **It follows the first half and not the second.** SB 53 starts narrow, but no agency can expand the incident list. The Department of Technology's review covers three applicability definitions, not "critical safety incident" (u132). RAISE's office, by contrast, can add reporting requirements by rule (raise/05, §1429). |
| Hybrid: mandatory developer reporting, voluntary user reporting (L712) | **Follows**: frontier developers must report; members of the public may (u102). |
| Share with agencies holding domain-specific authority (L708) | **Partly follows**: OES and the AG *may* transmit to state agencies, the federal government, the Legislature and the Governor (u114). There's no domain-agency mapping. |
| Confidential reports, with authority to share anonymized findings with industry when others could benefit (L708) | **Partly**: SB 53 has confidentiality and an annual anonymized aggregate to the Legislature (u117–119). It gives **no authority to share findings with other developers**. |
| Use **existing, non-AI regulatory authorities** to act on reported risks (L722) | **Not implemented.** There's no response pathway (u113). |
| Incentives against under-reporting: safe harbours, "limited enforcement immunity" as at the NTSB (L700, fn 8) | **Absent.** SB 53 penalizes failure to report (for large developers) and offers no immunity for reporting. |
| "Incentivize … verifiable pre-market testing … to satisfy certain reporting requirements" (L718) | **Absent.** |

## §3.4 principles (transparency trade-offs), against SB 53

- **The First Amendment** (L630): mandates are more likely to survive if the information is "purely factual and uncontroversial" and not "highly subjective". SB 53's framework, which "describes how [it] approaches" ten topics, and its false-statement rule about catastrophic *risk* (u97) both concern information that is partly subjective. **The report anticipated the constitutional exposure I flagged at u97 and u217** **[reading]**.
- **Security** (L622–626): "'security through obscurity' will not work for AI". SB 53 lets developers redact for cybersecurity and public safety (u100). That fits the report's "disclose carefully, with advance notice" more than a total bar. It doesn't conflict, but the report's leaning is toward disclosure.
- **Trade secrets** (L628): sensitive information "may also be disclosed to the government at little risk, assuming the government can provide assurance of good information security". That's SB 53's need-to-know duty on OES (u108).
- **"Transparency washing"** (L616, L634). The cure the report offers is public-facing disclosure **plus "a concrete plan of action for how to analyze and base future decisions on disclosed information"**. SB 53 has no plan for analysing the published frameworks and transparency reports. No agency is told to read them. OES reviews incidents, not frameworks. **SB 53 is exposed to the report's own transparency-washing critique** **[reading]**.

## §5 scoping (first part), against SB 53

- **Aligning thresholds with intent** (L754): "if the intent is to encourage up-and-coming startups, then shielding them via exemptions … based on business-level head count or **revenue**." SB 53's revenue-based "large" tier implements this mapping exactly (u64). **SB 53 follows the report here.**
- But L754 goes on: dual-use misuse intent → **model-level evaluation thresholds**; large-scale harm intent → **impact-level** thresholds (users). SB 53 uses neither. Its catastrophic-misuse aim is scoped by *compute*, which is a cost proxy. L756 acknowledges that compute has "already" served "as a proxy for some combination of greater capabilities, greater misuse risks, and/or greater downstream harm". So the report allows SB 53's choice as a practical proxy, while recommending intent-aligned metrics. **That's a mild departure, and one the report's own pragmatism allows** **[reading]**.
- **Derivative models** (footnote 14, L747): "policymakers may want to differentiate the obligations for the initial in-scope developer vs. an entity downstream that modifies the model. Addressing derivative models … is essential". **This is exactly the hinge I left open at SB 53 u63**: does fine-tuning someone else's frontier model make you a frontier developer? The report says the question needs explicit treatment. SB 53 didn't give it. A clear departure, by omission **[text: no derivative-model provision in SB 53]**.
- **"Thresholds … readily updated to avoid ossification"** (L736). SB 53's thresholds can change only by statute (u129). The review only recommends.

## Wandering

- §4's design questions, (i) to (iv) (L648), map one to one onto SB 53 §22757.13: events (i) to (d); information (ii) to (a)(1)–(4); reporters (iii) to "frontier developer or a member of the public"; recipients (iv) to OES, the AG, the Legislature and the Governor. **SB 53's OES section reads as an answer to the report's four-part design template.** That's structural lineage, not just shared vocabulary **[reading]**.
- The report says "Thresholds are imperfect" (L736) and SB 53 says "additional legislation may be needed" (finding (n)). Both are honest about the limits of their scope.
