# CA Report, L1–250: front matter, Executive Summary, Key Principles, Introduction start

## What differed from my expectations

The surprise is the scale of the copying. **SB 53's SEC. 1 findings are lifted, nearly verbatim, from the report's Key Principles** (L134–166). The edits SB 53 made are where the bill departs from the report. Prediction 1 from 00-before (third-party assessment) is confirmed by a **single deleted phrase**.

## Wandering

- **The deletion in (h) is the bill's departure on third-party evaluation, made at the level of principle.** The report puts third-party evaluations among the "key instruments", alongside whistleblowers and public information. SB 53 copied the sentence and cut exactly that item. Then, in the operative text, it requires only that developers *describe their approach* to third parties (u72) and *disclose the extent* of their involvement (u92). The report's L158 is sharper still: "Clear whistleblower protections and **safe harbors for third-party evaluators** can enable increased transparency above and beyond information disclosed by foundation model developers." **SB 53 has no safe harbour for third-party evaluators** **[text: none found in my reading]**. That's departure #1, confirmed at both the principle level and the mechanism level.
- **"Adverse event" became "incident", and the closed list followed.** The report's concept is adverse events (post-deployment harms in practice, L162: "understanding harms that arise in practice"). SB 53 swapped in "incident" and then defined "critical safety incident" as a closed list of four, three of them about catastrophe or security. I'll check the report's §4 for its adverse-event scope, but this looks like departure #3, made by a vocabulary swap.
- **"Commensurate modernization of existing regulatory or enforcement authorities" was cut.** The report imagines incident reports feeding existing regulators who act on them (L162: "Existing regulatory authorities could offer clear pathways to address risks uncovered"). SB 53 routes reports to OES, gives OES no response powers, and adds an aggregate report. **The report's report-then-act loop is in SB 53 only as report-then-tell-the-Legislature.** That fits u33 and u113, where I found OES "reviews" and the purpose of "respond" had no mechanism behind it.
- **"Trust but verify" was deleted.** The report's motto doesn't appear in SB 53, though I'd have bet it did (u23 note, from memory). The verify half is the part SB 53 weakens (no third-party mandate), so dropping the motto is honest, in a way **[reading]**.
- **Oddities in SB 53 that turn out to be inherited.** I wondered at "protect consumers" (u25) and "competition" (u27) as purposes of a catastrophic-risk statute. Both come from the report's principles 4 and 5. So they're **copy artifacts**: SB 53 inherited the report's broader consumer and market framing along with its sentences. My u25/u27 notes should be read with that in mind.
- **The executive summary's "Without proper safeguards, however, powerful AI could induce severe and, in some cases, potentially irreversible harms"** (L136, L245) is the sentence I half-predicted for SB 53 at u24 and u25 and never found. So my "recall" there was really recall of the *report*, which I'd blended with the bill. That shows **source blending in my own memory**, which is exactly the hazard a primary read guards against.
- **The report disclaims advocacy.** It says it "does not argue for or against any particular piece of legislation" (L128). SB 53 cites it as authority anyway (SEC. 1(c), (o)). That's normal: a report built to be cited. It's also a lineage edge to tag precisely: SB 53 adopts the report's *principles*, not any recommendation (the report says it makes none).
- **Scope exclusions that are the report's own** (L130): labour, the environmental effects of data centres, and "the full range of specific ways in which AI capabilities can be misused". So the report itself leaves systemic labour and environment questions out. SB 53's omission of systemic risk (u30) partly follows the report's own scope.
- **Its "foundation model" vocabulary** (L229) is Bommasani's CRFM lineage. The report's lead writer is the 2021 coiner (the relata key `bommasani-2021-opportunities` is in the corpus). Lineage: SB 53 "foundation model" ← JCPWG report ← Bommasani et al. 2021. **The term in the statute traces to a coauthor of the report the statute cites.** Footnote 1 maps it to the EU's GPAI, which the lexicon can use.
- The reviewers on the draft include Bengio, Brundage, Toner, Narayanan, Lessig and others. A wide range, including AI-safety figures.
