## Wandering

- **The framework is defined as protocols for *catastrophic risks*, in the defined sense.** So an SB 53 frontier AI framework need only address SB 53 catastrophic risk: CBRN expert assistance, autonomous cyber or crime-equivalent conduct, evading control, at 51+ / $1B, single-incident. The labs' existing frameworks are organized around *their own* risk concepts:
  - Anthropic's RSP: ASL capability thresholds;
  - OpenAI's Preparedness Framework: "severe harm", "thousands of people / hundreds of billions" (CLAUDE.md);
  - Google DeepMind's Frontier Safety Framework: critical capability levels.

  Those concepts don't line up with SB 53's. So when a lab publishes its framework "for SB 53", it either maps its own thresholds onto SB 53's catastrophic risk, or publishes one document satisfying both and leaves the reader to reconcile them. **This is a live crosswalk problem in the world, not only in this project.** A lab's SB 53 framework is a document in which one bounded context (the lab's) is forced to address another (California's). Those documents, if the corpus holds them, would be rich specimens of translation between contexts.
- **Severity mismatch, concretely:** OpenAI's "severe harm" bar is *thousands* of deaths or *hundreds of billions* of dollars. SB 53's is *51* people or *$1B* property. SB 53's bar is roughly two orders of magnitude *lower*. So an OpenAI framework that addresses only "severe harm" would under-address SB 53 catastrophic risk. The California definition reaches risks OpenAI's PF calls sub-severe. This is a concrete, checkable consequence of the cross-source difference (and the reason CLAUDE.md flags OpenAI's two frameworks against SB 53's bar). If OpenAI's Frontier Governance Framework uses ">50 fatalities" (CLAUDE.md: "a risk of 'greater than 50 fatalities'"), that looks like OpenAI **adopting SB 53's bar** into a second framework, to comply. Lineage: SB 53 → OpenAI FGF. That fits CLAUDE.md's note that SB 53's bar recurs in four documents.
- "**Manage, assess, and mitigate**" is three verbs. And "management" is the word the preemption clause (digest, u16) uses: "management of catastrophic risk". So the preemption is about local laws regulating exactly what frameworks do.
- "**Documented**" and "**protocols**": the framework is documentation of protocols. "Technical and organizational": both engineering controls and governance. This definition says nothing about content quality. Any documented protocols count as a framework. Content requirements, if any, are in the obligations section ("describes how [it] approaches …" from the digest).
