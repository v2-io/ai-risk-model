# Unit 110 · L177 · p.7

> (2) If a frontier developer discovers that a critical safety incident poses an imminent risk of death or serious physical injury, the frontier developer shall disclose that incident within 24 hours to an authority, including any law enforcement agency or public safety agency with jurisdiction, that is appropriate based on the nature of that incident and as required by law.

## What differed

My recall was exact. Page break L173 → L177 (p.6 → p.7).

## Wandering

- **The third injury concept: "serious physical injury".** SB 53 now has:
  - *serious injury* (catastrophic-risk threshold, (c)(1));
  - *bodily injury* (incident items (d)(1), (d)(3));
  - *serious physical injury* (the 24-hour rule).

  All three are undefined. "Serious physical injury" probably excludes psychological injury; "serious injury" in (c)(1) doesn't say "physical". So does severe psychological harm count toward the 51 people? Arguably yes, under "serious injury" read liberally. Arguably no, given the bill's evident focus. **Lexicon: three injury concepts, possibly with different scopes.** A source expert asked "what injury standard does SB 53 use?" should say it depends on the provision.
- **The most urgent incidents may not go to OES.** The 24-hour disclosure goes to "**an authority** … that is appropriate based on the nature of that incident". That could be local police, the FBI, CISA, a public health department. OES isn't named. So for the most time-critical cases, the statute routes to whoever can act, not to the information hub. Sensible, but it means OES's incident picture may lack the most urgent events, unless the 15-day report also goes to OES.
- **Does the 24-hour disclosure replace or add to the 15-day OES report?** Paragraph (1) says "**Subject to paragraph (2)**", so (2) modifies (1). Reading A: in imminent-danger cases, (2)'s 24-hour disclosure to an appropriate authority *replaces* the 15-day OES report. Reading B: (2) *adds* an earlier disclosure, and the OES report is still due within 15 days. "Subject to" usually signals an exception or override, which favours A. If A, then **OES might never receive reports of the most dangerous incidents.** That's a genuine ambiguity with real consequences, and the finding (m) purpose ("public authorities are promptly informed") would favour B. Flag it.
- **"And as required by law"**, read narrowly, could mean disclosure is required only where some *other* law already requires it, which would hollow the provision out. Read naturally, it means: disclose to the appropriate authority, in the manner the law requires. Another slightly loose drafting seam.
- **"Imminent risk"**: imminent, not catastrophic. One person's imminent death triggers it. The bar is low in severity and high in immediacy.
- "**Discovers that a critical safety incident poses**": the trigger is an incident that also poses imminent risk. So the 24-hour rule applies only to defined incidents (the closed list). An imminent danger from a frontier model that isn't on the list (say, a model generating a credible, specific threat to someone) carries no SB 53 disclosure duty. General law may still apply.
