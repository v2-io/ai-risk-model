# Unit 23 · L71 · p.3

> (c) The Joint California Policy Working Group on AI Frontier Models has recommended sound principles for policy in artificial intelligence.

## What differed

It names the JCPWG, as recalled. But it's brief: no date, no convener, no authors, no "trust but verify". The finding just says it "recommended sound principles". The turn to risk hasn't come; the working group comes first.

## Wandering

- **Lineage edge, in the enacted text: SB 53 → JCPWG ("California Report on Frontier AI Policy", final version June 2025, I believe).** The Legislature adopts the report's authority by endorsing its principles as "sound", without saying which ones. A source model of SB 53 should record that dependency, and it would help to know whether our corpus holds the JCPWG report. (I won't check now, to stay inside the source.)
- It's a vague endorsement: "sound principles" with nothing enumerated. If the next findings list them, the bill is effectively quoting the report. If they don't, the bill borrows the report's authority without binding itself to specific content. The second is a common legislative move, citing a respected study for legitimacy while keeping drafting freedom.
- A question for later: does SB 53 implement the JCPWG's recommendations closely or loosely? From memory, the report emphasized transparency, third-party evaluation, whistleblower protections, adverse-event reporting and thresholds beyond compute alone. SB 53 has transparency, whistleblowers and incident reporting. Does it have **third-party evaluation**? I don't think so. And it uses a **compute threshold**, which the report cautioned against relying on alone. So **SB 53 may depart from the JCPWG on exactly two points**: no mandatory third-party audit, and a compute-plus-revenue threshold. That's recall-based. A question someone should ask, and I'll keep it open.
