# Unit 116 · L183 · p.7

> (f) A report of a critical safety incident submitted to the Office of Emergency Services pursuant to this section, a report of assessments of catastrophic risk from internal use pursuant to Section 22757.12, and a covered employee report made pursuant to Chapter 5.1 … of the Labor Code are exempt from the California Public Records Act (Division 10 (commencing with Section 7920.000) of Title 1 of the Government Code).

## What differed

This is the PRA exemption, not the annual report I'd recalled. My ordering recall keeps slipping in §22757.13, while my recall of each provision's wording holds. That fits the idea that the provisions were quoted individually far more often than in sequence.

## Wandering

- **The digest's typo, made plain.** Here the operative text says "assessments of catastrophic risk from **internal** use". The digest's sentence (u9) was a near-copy of this one with "internet use". So the digest's error was a slip in copying this exact sentence. That's useful for showing that our reading of the digest ("internal" intended) is right, not just plausible.
- **The exemption covers reports *submitted to OES*.** The 24-hour imminent-danger disclosures go to "an authority … appropriate" (u110), which may be local police or a federal agency. **Those aren't "submitted to OES pursuant to this section" in the sense here**, so their PRA status falls under whatever rules govern the receiving agency (police investigative records are often exempt anyway, under other PRA provisions). A small gap: the most urgent disclosures have the least specific confidentiality protection. And if reading A from u110 is right (24-hour disclosure replaces the OES report), those incidents are outside both OES review and this exemption.
- Covered-employee reports are exempt too, which protects whistleblowers' identities and the content of their disclosures from public records requests.
- The citation "Division 10 (commencing with Section 7920.000)" is the recodified PRA (2021 recodification, effective 2023). Lawyers will notice the new numbering; it's a sign of recent drafting.
- This is the provision the constitutional findings (digest, u17) will justify.
