# Unit 56 · L113 · p.4

> (f) "Foundation model" means an artificial intelligence model that is all of the following:

## What differed

As predicted: a conjunctive list ("all of the following").

## Wandering

- Foundation model is built on artificial intelligence model ((b), the system-like OECD definition). So, by inheritance, a "foundation model" is an "engineered or machine-based *system* …" that also meets the conditions to come. So the system-vs-model slide from unit 40 carries into the whole chain. **Every SB 53 "model" is, by definition, a system.** That has a consequence for "frontier model": defined by training compute, presumably, so it's a *system* defined by the compute used to train *it*. For a deployed system with scaffolding around weights, the training compute is the weights' compute. Probably no practical problem, but in strict reading the definitions are about systems throughout.
- "All of the following" makes it conjunctive, so a model must meet every condition. Expected triplet: broad data, generality of output, adaptability to many tasks.

## Predictions for unit 57

- [recall/inference] ≈70%: "(1) Trained on a broad data set."
- Then (2) "Designed for generality of output." (3) "Adaptable to a wide range of distinctive tasks." These come from the Stanford CRFM's definition by way of EO 14110's "dual-use foundation model" ("trained on broad data; generally uses self-supervision; contains at least tens of billions of parameters; is applicable across a wide range of contexts").
