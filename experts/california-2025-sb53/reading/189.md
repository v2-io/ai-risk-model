> (3) Loss of control of a foundation model causing death or bodily injury.

## What differed

My second prediction: only the noun changed. Property was **not** added here. So within the Labor Code list, item (1) has property harm and item (3) doesn't.

## Wandering

- **Property harm was added only to weight theft.** It seems deliberate, aimed at the security case. Weight theft typically causes property loss (the IP), while loss of control typically doesn't, except in cyber cases. The drafter widened the item where the TFAIA's injury condition fitted worst (u50). That suggests someone noticed the TFAIA's injury-conditioned weight-theft item was close to unusable, and fixed it in the whistleblower chapter only.
  - Why not fix the TFAIA too? Perhaps industry resisted mandatory OES reporting of security incidents, which carries reputational cost, while accepting broader whistleblower protection. That's speculation, but it fits the pattern of the TFAIA being narrower wherever the duty falls on the company.
