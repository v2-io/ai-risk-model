> This bill would preempt any rule, regulation, code, ordinance, or other law adopted by a city, county, city and county, municipality, or local agency on or after January 1, 2025, specifically related to the regulation of frontier developers with respect to their management of catastrophic risk.

## Wandering

- **Preemption narrow on four axes:**
  1. **Who is preempted:** local government only (city, county, municipality, local agency). Not state agencies, and not other states.
  2. **When:** only local laws adopted on or after January 1, 2025. That reaches back about nine months before signing, so it catches any ordinance a city rushed through during 2025. The date suggests the drafters had a specific worry, maybe San Francisco or another city considering its own frontier-AI rule. Laws from before 2025 are grandfathered.
  3. **Topic:** "specifically related to the regulation of frontier developers". Laws of general application (zoning, data centers, labor) aren't touched.
  4. **Subject matter within that:** "with respect to their management of catastrophic risk". So a local law on frontier developers' bias, privacy or energy use isn't preempted.
- This is the mirror image of the **federal preemption** fight that ran through 2025: the proposed moratorium on state AI laws in the federal reconciliation bill, stripped by the Senate 99–1 in July 2025 if I recall. California preempts its own localities, narrowly, while resisting federal preemption of itself. The bill wants California to be the sole regulator of frontier catastrophic risk *within* California, and it says nothing about federal law here, as far as the digest goes.
- "Management of catastrophic risk" is a phrase that asks for a definition it may not get. Is "management" defined? A frontier AI framework is presumably the developer's catastrophic-risk management document. So the preemption may reach any local law touching what goes into frameworks or incident reporting. Interpretive question: does a city ordinance requiring AI incident disclosure to the city fall under it? Probably, if it's specific to frontier developers.
- In the digest, the preemption sits right after the whistleblower item. Is it in the Labor Code chapter or the B&P chapter? I'd expect it in B&P, as part of the TFAIA. The digest's placement here suggests the paragraph order follows something other than strict code order. Or the preemption is a separate section of the bill (SEC. 5 or similar), uncodified. Watch where it lands.
