# Unit 185 · L265 · p.10

> (b) "Covered employee" means an employee responsible for assessing, managing, or addressing risk of critical safety incidents.

## What differed

My recall was exact (unit 9): the protection is role-gated.

## Wandering

- **Not every employee is covered.** Only employees "responsible for assessing, managing, or addressing risk of critical safety incidents". That's the safety, security and risk staff. An engineer who stumbles on a danger outside their role, or a researcher on a capabilities team, isn't a "covered employee", so the new chapter doesn't protect them. They fall back on Labor Code 1102.5, which covers violations of law only (u13). A question with a surprising answer: **"Are all AI lab employees protected by SB 53's whistleblower provisions?"** No. Only those whose role is assessing, managing or addressing critical-safety-incident risk.
  - "Responsible for" is unclear at the edges. Formally assigned? A job description? A senior engineer who reviews safety cases occasionally? Labs could narrow coverage by narrowing role definitions.
  - "**Employee**": contractors are probably excluded, so red-teamers on contract and external evaluators aren't covered.
- **The covered-employee definition and the catastrophic-risk definition don't line up.** The covered-employee definition rests on "**critical safety incidents**". If that term in this chapter is the TFAIA's (frontier models only; I'll see whether §1107 defines or cross-references it), coverage is tied to frontier-model incident risk, while §1107(a)'s catastrophic risk is foundation-model-wide (u177). Who reports a danger from a lab's *sub-frontier* foundation model, then? Only an employee responsible for (frontier-model) critical-safety-incident risk. In practice those staff work across all models, so the mismatch may not bite. Textually, the two definitions point at different model classes.
