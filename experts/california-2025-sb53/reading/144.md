# Unit 144 · L214 · p.8

> (b) A civil penalty described in this section shall be recovered in a civil action brought only by the Attorney General.

## What differed

My recall was exact.

## Wandering

- **"Brought only by the Attorney General"**: no private right of action, no district attorneys, no city attorneys. In California, B&P §17200 actions can be brought by DAs and city attorneys, so "only by the AG" closes off local prosecutors as well as private suits for *this* penalty. (Whether a §17200 "unlawful practice" action based on a TFAIA violation is also barred is a separate question. This clause speaks of "a civil penalty described in this section", so it may not bar other remedies.)
- The scope of "only" is precise: it limits who can recover *this section's* penalty. It doesn't say the TFAIA creates no other remedies. So my unit-143 speculation about §17200 survives as an open question rather than a closed one. Raise it, don't answer it.
- One enforcer for the whole TFAIA: the AG's resources and priorities decide everything. A future AG who deprioritizes AI enforcement would leave the TFAIA effectively unenforced. That's the political risk of single-enforcer designs.
