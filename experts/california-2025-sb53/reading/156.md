## Wandering

- **A labour clause, probably a sign of labour's support.** Telling a state body to follow the law is legally redundant, since it already must. Clauses like this usually record a bargain, likely with organized labour (the California Labor Federation was active in 2025 AI legislation, I believe). "Workforce … standards" may point at prevailing wage or public-sector employment rules for building and staffing CalCompute.
- The bill's political coalition shows in its drafting: industry deference in the TFAIA findings, labour in CalCompute, safety researchers in the incident definitions, UC in the hosting preference. **The text records its negotiation.** For the source model, that's worth saying plainly: SB 53's provisions answer different constituencies, and the inconsistencies between its registers (aspirational vs threshold, booster vs precautionary) are partly the signs of that negotiation.
