## Wandering

- **The digest's grammatical slip is also in the statute.** "A frontier developer shall not make … a rule … that prevents … or **retaliates against** a covered employee". The digest (u14) had the same structure, and I noted that a *rule* can't retaliate. The statute has the same grammar. So it was the statute's construction, which the digest copied. Read naturally: the developer shall not make a preventing rule, and shall not retaliate. Courts will read it that way. It's still an awkward attachment.
- **"Reasonable cause to believe" is confirmed in the statute** for the anti-retaliation rule. That's one of the four epistemic standards from u99.
- **"Enter into … contract"**: NDAs and non-disparagement agreements that prevent protected disclosures are barred. In 2024, OpenAI's departure agreements (equity clawback tied to non-disparagement) became public and caused a scandal. This clause would bar that kind of contract, to the extent it prevents disclosure *to these recipients*. It doesn't bar non-disparagement agreements that restrict *public* speech, since the press isn't a protected recipient. **A precise answer to "Does SB 53 ban AI lab NDAs?"**: it bars rules and contracts that prevent covered employees from disclosing to the AG, federal authorities, supervisors or authorized co-employees. Nothing more.
- "**A federal authority**" is still undefined. Any federal agency? Congress? A member of Congress? Probably any federal agency with relevant authority. Undefined, it reads broadly.
