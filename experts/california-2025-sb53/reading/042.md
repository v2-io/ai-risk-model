## Wandering

- **"Expert-level assistance"** has no definition here. It's the uplift concept, and the bar is *expert-level*. Read literally, that's assistance at the level an expert would provide. Assistance that is helpful but non-expert (a novice-level summary) doesn't qualify, even if it contributed. That's a capability bar inside the harm definition. Frontier frameworks use the same idea ("uplift to novices" vs "uplift to experts"). Anthropic's ASL-3 CBRN threshold is about "significantly helping individuals … with basic STEM backgrounds", i.e. uplift *to* novices. OpenAI's "High" bio threshold is about meaningful assistance to novices too. SB 53's "expert-level assistance" measures *the level of the assistance*, not *the recipient's level*. Those are different axes:
  - **SB 53:** the quality of help is expert-level, whoever receives it;
  - **lab frameworks:** the help lifts a novice (or an expert) to some capability.

  A crosswalk equating "SB 53 (A)" with "ASL-3 CBRN" would merge two different capability concepts. **Candidate lexicon distinction: *expert-level assistance* (SB 53) ≠ *novice uplift* (Anthropic RSP / OpenAI PF).** Worth confirming against those sources' exact text later. I'm working from recall here.
- **"Creation or release"** covers making the weapon and deploying it. Release assistance would cover dispersal, targeting and delivery. Not acquisition? "Creation" arguably includes acquiring precursors. And not *use* of a conventionally acquired weapon? "Release" covers that.
- **"Chemical, biological, radiological, or nuclear weapon"** is CBRN in full, with no definition of "weapon". There's an existing body of federal law (18 U.S.C. 175, 229, 831, 2332a's "weapon of mass destruction"). SB 53 doesn't cross-reference it. "Weapon" may be read in ordinary meaning, which leaves open: is a pathogen with pandemic potential a "biological weapon" if never weaponized for delivery? Probably yes, in common use.
- Combined with the stem: the model provides expert-level CBRN assistance → a single incident → 51+ dead or seriously injured, or $1B property → material contribution → a foreseeable and material risk thereof. Catastrophic risk is the *risk* of that chain, not the chain itself. So a model *capable* of expert-level bio assistance presents a catastrophic risk *if* it's foreseeable and material that its deployment will materially contribute to such an incident. Capability alone isn't enough. In practice, though, an expert-level bio capability plus deployment arguably makes the risk foreseeable. It's a judgment, and SB 53 leaves that judgment to the developer's framework.
