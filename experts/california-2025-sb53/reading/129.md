## Wandering

- **This is the "track the frontier" mechanism from finding (k), and it only recommends.** The Department of Technology assesses and *recommends* annually. It doesn't update. Updating takes legislation, as (n) anticipated ("additional legislation may be needed"). So the thresholds are fixed in statute until the Legislature acts. **A precise answer to "Can SB 53's thresholds change?": only by new legislation; an annual Department of Technology review produces recommendations from 2027.**
- **"Relevant to the purposes of this chapter"**: the purposes, again. They're in SEC. 1's findings, which aren't codified in the chapter. So the Department must read uncodified findings to know the chapter's purposes. The liberal-construction clause (if it says "effectuate its purposes") points to the same place.
- **Three benchmarks for the definitions:** "technological developments, scientific literature, and widely accepted national and international standards". Standards again: international ones (EU 10^25?) could prompt a recommendation to align. That would be a lineage edge running *forward* from other sources into future SB 53 versions.
- **Which definitions?** A list follows. I expect frontier model, frontier developer and large frontier developer. If catastrophic risk isn't listed, **the catastrophe thresholds (51 people, $1B) and the incident list aren't subject to review.** Only applicability is. That would mean the Legislature thought scope might drift, but not the definition of what's catastrophic. Let's see.
- The Department of Technology is the same body as in the digest's item (2) baseline (the ADS inventory). Its AI role grows.
- January 1, 2027 is now the date for three things: the OES annual report, the Department of Technology review, and the CalCompute report (if funded).
