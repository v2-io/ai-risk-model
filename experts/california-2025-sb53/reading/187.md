# Unit 187 · L267 · p.10

> (1) Unauthorized access to, modification of, or exfiltration of the model weights of a foundation model that results in death, bodily injury, or damage to, or loss of, property.

## What differed

The first item differs from the TFAIA's in **two substantive ways**, plus one small change:

- **Object:** "a **foundation** model", where the TFAIA's (d)(1) says "a frontier model".
- **Harm condition:** "results in death, bodily injury, **or damage to, or loss of, property**", where the TFAIA's says "results in death or bodily injury".
- **Punctuation:** "exfiltration of the model weights", where the TFAIA's has "exfiltration of, the model weights". The comma is gone.

## Wandering

- **This partly closes the unit-50 gap, but only for whistleblowers.** In the TFAIA, weight theft is a critical safety incident only if someone is killed or injured. In the Labor Code, weight theft that causes **any property damage or loss**, with no dollar threshold, is a critical safety incident. Stolen weights very likely involve "loss of property" in themselves. Weights are intangible property, and exfiltration arguably "loses" them, or at least the exclusive possession of them. If so, **weight exfiltration on its own is a Labor Code critical safety incident**, while under the TFAIA it isn't reportable unless someone is injured. That's an important divergence:
  - the employee responsible for weight security is a "covered employee" (responsible for critical-safety-incident risk), and is protected when reporting;
  - the *developer* has no TFAIA duty to report the same event to OES unless it caused injury.

  A rough paraphrase of the effect: **the law protects the whistleblower who reports a weight theft the company isn't required to report.** It may be the design. Whistleblowers become the reporting channel for the security events the TFAIA leaves out.
  - Caveat: the Labor Code's "property" isn't defined in §1107 so far. The TFAIA defined it as "tangible or intangible property" (§22757.11(l)) for *its* chapter only. If the Labor Code chapter defines it later, fine. If not, ordinary meaning applies, which probably includes intangible property anyway.
- **Why do the definitions differ?** It may be political: labour or whistleblower advocates got a broader version in their chapter, while industry got the narrower version in the enforcement chapter. Or one was amended and the other wasn't. Version history could tell. Either way, **the two definitions are different in substance**, not only in noun.
- **Tally update** for the Labor Code against the TFAIA:
  - catastrophic risk: same structure, foundation model throughout, two stylistic drifts;
  - critical safety incident (1): foundation model, and **property harm added**.

  So the divergence goes beyond the noun.
- The lexicon will need `ca-sb53-tfaia:critical-safety-incident` and `ca-sb53-labor:critical-safety-incident` as distinct entries, with the differences listed item by item.
