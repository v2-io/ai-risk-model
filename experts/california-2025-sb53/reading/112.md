# Unit 112 · L179 · p.7

> (4) A frontier developer is encouraged, but not required, to report critical safety incidents pertaining to foundation models that are not frontier models.

My recall was exact.

## Wandering

- **The second hortatory norm.** It's the same form as §22757.12(c)(4) ("encouraged, but not required"). Two in the TFAIA so far. The deontic inventory now:
  - "shall": obligations;
  - "may": permissions (amend, redact);
  - "shall not": prohibitions (false statements);
  - "encouraged, but not required": exhortations;
  - "is deemed": legal fictions (system-card compliance).

  Five speech-act types in one chapter. A good test case for the project's norm vocabulary.
- **A definitional oddity.** "Critical safety incidents pertaining to foundation models that are not frontier models". But "critical safety incident" is *defined* by reference to *frontier* models: (d)(1) "model weights of a frontier model", (d)(3) "loss of control of a frontier model", (d)(4) "a frontier model that uses deceptive techniques". So, strictly, a critical safety incident involving a non-frontier foundation model *can't exist* under the definition, except (d)(2) (harm from materialization of a catastrophic risk), which itself requires a frontier model in the catastrophic-risk definition. **The encouragement points at an empty set if read strictly.** The intended meaning is plainly "incidents *like* critical safety incidents, but involving non-frontier models". The drafting reuses a defined term outside its definition's domain. A source expert should note it: (c)(4) uses "critical safety incident" analogically.
- The purpose ties to finding (n): smaller or behind-frontier models may pose catastrophic risk in the future. So voluntary reporting on them is encouraged now, as a soft bridge across the declared gap.
- "**A frontier developer**" is encouraged, so only someone who is already a frontier developer (has at least one frontier model) is encouraged to report on its *other*, sub-frontier foundation models. A developer with *no* frontier model isn't addressed at all. Members of the public can still report to OES using the mechanism, but only "critical safety incidents", hence frontier models.
