# Unit 136 · L206 · p.8

> (3) The extent to which a person will be able to determine, before beginning to train or deploy a foundation model, whether that person will be subject to the definition as a frontier developer or as a large frontier developer with an aim toward allowing earlier determinations if possible.

## What differed

My recall had the core. The tail "with an aim toward allowing earlier determinations if possible" is new to me.

## Wandering

- **Ex-ante predictability is a design value.** A person should be able to know *before* training whether they'll be a frontier developer. That's the regulated party's interest in legal certainty, and it favours **input-based thresholds** (planned compute, revenue) over **output-based** ones (measured capability). A capability-based "frontier model" test (aiming at "the frontier", u130) would be less predictable, since you don't know a model's capabilities until it's trained. So criteria (b)(1)–(3) pull in different directions:
  - (a)(1)'s target is *being at the frontier*: capability-relative, unpredictable ex ante;
  - (b)(3)'s value is *knowing before training*: input-based, predictable.

  The Department has to balance these. It explains why compute thresholds persist in law despite their known flaws: they're what you can know in advance. This is exactly the tension the field argues about (compute vs capability thresholds), and **SB 53 names both sides of it in one section.**
- "**Train or deploy**": predictability before deployment too. That's relevant for "substantially modified".
- "**With an aim toward allowing earlier determinations if possible**": the hedge "if possible" shows the drafters know it may not be.
- "**A person**" again, the undefined term, now in a context (deciding whether to train) where an individual researcher could plausibly be that person.
