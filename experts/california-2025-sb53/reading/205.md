# Unit 205 · L288 · p.11

> (f) The court is authorized to award reasonable attorney's fees to a plaintiff who brings a successful action for a violation of this section.

## What differed

This is attorney's fees, my unit-14 option. It isn't the burden-shift.

## Wandering

- "**Is authorized to award**" makes fees **discretionary**. It's weaker than "shall award". The digest's "would authorize attorney's fees" (u15) was accurate on this point.
- "**A plaintiff who brings a successful action for a violation of this section**" implies a **private right of action**, though it's never stated in so many words. The fee clause presupposes that employees can sue. Contrast the TFAIA, where only the AG can (u144). So **SB 53 gives employees a private right of action in the Labor Code chapter, and gives no one a private right in the B&P chapter.** That's a real asymmetry. Unit 10 noted the digest's hint of it, and it's now confirmed. It's also oddly implicit: the right to sue is inferred from the fee clause, or from general Labor Code remedies, rather than created explicitly. Maybe the next units are explicit.
- One-way fee-shifting applies to plaintiffs only. Defendants who win don't recover fees.
