## Wandering

- **This settles the *intent* behind unit 63's hinge.** I asked whether fine-tuning someone else's frontier model makes you a frontier developer. This target says frontier developers should be "developers of frontier models **who are themselves at the frontier** of artificial intelligence development". A hobbyist fine-tuning an open-weight frontier model isn't "themselves at the frontier". So the intended reading is my reading 1 (per-developer compute), and reading 2's consequence (everyone who fine-tunes is a frontier developer) would be **contrary to the stated target**. A court would likely use this to resolve the ambiguity, with the review section serving as interpretive context. The text of (h)/(i) is still ambiguous, but now I can say what the statute says it *aims* for.
- "Who are themselves at the frontier": the *developer* must be at the frontier, not just its model. A double test: frontier model *and* frontier developer. The operational definition (compute-based) captures only the first. A well-funded newcomer that trains one 10^26 model is a frontier developer operationally even if it isn't "at the frontier" in reputation. The intent is fuzzy; the number is crisp.
