> (1) Existing law generally regulates artificial intelligence, including by requiring, on or before January 1, 2026, and before each time thereafter, that a generative artificial intelligence system or service … released on or after January 1, 2022, is made publicly available to Californians for use, the developer … to post on the developer's internet website documentation regarding the data used … to train …

## What differed

- The digest is **numbered**: "(1)". That tells me it has parts, probably one per code: (1) B&P, (2) Government Code, (3) Labor Code, or similar.
- The "existing law" it starts from isn't one I named. It's the generative-AI training-data transparency statute, AB 2013 (2024), if I recall rightly, Civil Code 3110 and following. My guesses were the Department of Technology and automated decision systems.

## Wandering

- **The sentence is broken**, and broken in a way that looks original, not caused by conversion: "requiring … that a generative artificial intelligence system or service … is made publicly available …, the developer … to post". It reads as if a phrase like "before each time … *a generative AI system … is made publicly available*" was meant, with "that" inserted wrongly, or as if "requiring … the developer … to post" is the frame with a "before each time that" clause embedded. The intended structure is probably "requiring, on or before January 1, 2026, and before each time thereafter that a [system] … is made publicly available to Californians for use, the developer … to post …". So the comma after "thereafter" is misplaced. It's minor, but it's the first evidence that even Legislative Counsel's prose has slips, and that conversion isn't the only source of noise. I'm not certain the original lacks this; it could be how leginfo renders it. Without the PDF I can't tell. (I'll leave that alone and keep reading.)
- Why open with AB 2013? It places SB 53 in a line of California AI law: training-data transparency already exists, and SB 53 adds framework and incident transparency. It's the "transparency" lineage, and the eventual short title is the *Transparency* in Frontier AI Act. The digest is telling the reader that California has already chosen disclosure as its regulatory tool. SB 1047 tried duties of care and liability (a "reasonable care" standard, a kill-switch requirement); SB 53 retreats to transparency. That's a **regulatory-theory choice** a source model of SB 53 should record: its theory of risk reduction is disclosure plus incident reporting plus whistleblowing, all information channels, not conduct mandates. Except, possibly, the requirement to *comply with your own framework*, which turns self-chosen commitments into enforceable obligations. I recall something like that. If it's there, that is the subtle point: SB 53 regulates conduct indirectly, by enforcing the developer's own published promises.
- "Generative artificial intelligence system or service" is the AB 2013 vocabulary. SB 53 will speak of "foundation models" and "frontier models". So three neighbouring statutes use three different nouns for the regulated thing. The lexicon will need near-neighbours here: AB 2013's *generative AI system or service*, SB 53's *foundation model / frontier model*, the EU's *general-purpose AI model*.
- Dates: "released on or after January 1, 2022" is AB 2013's retroactivity window. It isn't SB 53's, but a careless reader could take it as SB 53's.
