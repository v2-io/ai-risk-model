## Wandering

- **Partnerships have to stay consistent with public ownership.** Any partnerships with tech companies must "demonstrate compliance" with (c), the UC preference, and (d), "fully owned and hosted" and so on. So a deal where a big tech company hosts CalCompute on its own cloud would conflict with (d)(1)'s "fully owned and hosted". The statute anticipates corporate involvement and constrains it to fit public ownership. In effect, CalCompute can't be outsourced to Microsoft, Google or Amazon. It can still buy hardware and services from them.
- The pairing worth noticing: companies regulated by the TFAIA (large frontier developers, often also cloud providers) could be CalCompute's vendors under (F). Same bill, same companies, two roles: regulated party and potential state contractor.
