## Bill Summary:

SB 53 establishes a consortium within the Government Operations Agency (GovOps) and tasks the consortium with developing a framework for the creation of a public cloud computing cluster that benefits the public, to be known as “CalCompute,” as specified. SB 53 also establishes whistleblower protections for employees working with specified artificial intelligence (AI) models when reporting on critical risks, as specified.

## Fiscal Impact:

GovOps estimates costs ranging from \$2.5 million to \$7.5 million, depending on the scale of the CalCompute system framework the consortium is tasked with developing (General Fund). Costs include staffing a team to develop and manage the project, which would include contracting with experts and other staff time for research, strategic planning, and development.

## Background:

Cluster computing is a type of computing where multiple computers are connected to work together as a single system. The term “cluster” refers to the network of linked computer systems performing the same task. Computing clusters typically consist of servers, workstations, and personal computers that communicate over a local area network or a wide area network. Cluster computing is a kind of distributed computing, a type of computing that links computers together on a network to perform a computational task, increase computational power and function as a single computer. Each computer, or “node,” in a cluster network has an operating system and a central processing unit core that handles the tasks required for the software to run properly. Because of its high performance and high availability, cluster computing has many applications, including cloud computing, AI, scientific research, and big data analytics.

## Proposed Law: 

- Establishes a consortium within GovOps. Tasks the consortium with developing a framework for the creation of a public cloud computing cluster, to be known as “CalCompute,” that advances the development and deployment of AI that is safe, ethical, equitable, and sustainable by fostering research and innovation that benefits the public and enabling equitable innovation by expanding access to computational resources.

  - Provides the consortium consist of 14 members selected from: representatives of the University of California (UC) and other public and private academic research institutions; representatives of impacted workforce labor organizations; representatives of stakeholder groups; experts in technology and AI; and personnel from other relevant departments and agencies. Specifies eight consortium members be selected by GovOps. Specifies the President pro Tempore of the Senate and the Speaker of the Assembly each select three members.

- Requires the consortium to make reasonable efforts to ensure that CalCompute is established within the UC to the extent possible.

- Requires CalCompute to include, but not be limited to, a fully owned and hosted cloud platform and necessary human expertise to operate and maintain the platform through training and other support.

- Requires the consortium to operate in accordance with all relevant labor and workforce laws and standards.

- By January 1, 2027, requires GovOps to submit a report from the consortium to the Legislature with the framework developed for the creation and operation of CalCompute. Requires the report to include a landscape analysis of California’s current public, private, and nonprofit cloud computing platform infrastructure; an analysis of the cost to the state to build and main CalCompute; recommendations for CalCompute governance and structure; recommendations for CalCompute parameters; an analysis of the state’s technology workforce; a description of proposed partnerships for CalCompute; and recommendations regarding prioritizing the use of the current public sector workforce to create and manage CalCompute.

  - Requires the consortium to be dissolved upon submission of the report.

- Authorizes the UC to receive private donations to implement CalCompute if CalCompute is established within the UC.

- Provides that establishment of the consortium become operative only upon an appropriation in a budget act or other measure.

- Establishes whistleblower protections for employees working with specified AI models when reporting critical risks, as specified.

- Establishes definitions.

## Related Legislation:

SB 579 (Padilla, 2025) requires the Secretary of GovOps to appoint a mental health and AI working group to evaluate the role and risks of AI in mental health settings and issue a report to the Legislature on its findings and recommendations, as specified. SB 579 is pending in this committee.
