## Bill Summary:

SB 53 establishes a consortium within the Government Operations Agency (GovOps) and tasks the consortium with developing a framework for the creation of a public cloud computing cluster that benefits the public, to be known as “CalCompute,” as specified. SB 53 also establishes whistleblower protections for employees working with specified artificial intelligence (AI) models when reporting on critical risks, as specified.

## Fiscal Impact:

- GovOps estimates costs ranging from \$2.5 million to \$7.5 million, depending on the scale of the CalCompute system framework the consortium is tasked with developing (General Fund). Costs include staffing a team to develop and manage the project, which would include contracting with experts and other staff time for research, strategic planning, and development.

- Unknown, potentially significant workload cost pressures to the courts to adjudicate alleged violations of this measure (Trial Court Trust Fund, General Fund).

## Author Amendments:

- Modify the definitions of “employee” and “foundation model.”

- Define “labor contractor” as an individual or entity that supplies, either with or without a contract, a client employer with workers to perform labor within the client employer’s usual course of business.

- Expand whistleblower protections for an employee disclosing information to a person with authority over the employee.

- Additionally prohibit a developer from entering into a contract that prevents an employee from making a disclosure protected under Labor Code (LC) § 1102.5.

- Additionally prohibit a developer from making, adopting, enforcing, or entering into a rule, regulation, policy, or contract that would prevent an organization or entity that provides goods or services to the developer related to the assessment, management, or addressing of critical risk, or an employee of that organization or entity, from disclosing information to the Attorney General, a federal authority, or the developer if there is reasonable cause to believe that the information discloses either of the following:

  - The developer’s activities pose a critical risk.

  - The developer has made a false or misleading statement about its management of critical risk.

- Provide that the bill does not impair or limit the applicability of LC § 1102.5.

- Provide the remedies provided by the bill are cumulative to each other and the remedies or penalties available under all other laws of this state.

- Make other technical, clarifying, and conforming changes.
