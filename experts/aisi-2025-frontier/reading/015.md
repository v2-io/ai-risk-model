# Unit 15 — L139–143, p.7 ("5") — Why we're releasing this report

**Predicted:** a list of methods and caveats. Wrong. This is a *why*, and it contains the boldest sentence so far.

**Got:**
- "Our testing shows an **extraordinary pace** of development."
- "AI systems are becoming **competitive with – or even surpassing – human experts** in an increasing number of domains."
- "**It is plausible that in the coming years, this trend may lead to capabilities widely acknowledged as Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) or otherwise transformative AI.**"
- "This technological trajectory is **deeply consequential**: governments, industry, civil society, and the public need clear, evidence-based insights to navigate it."
- "just a **snapshot** of AISI's wide-ranging efforts", "accessible, data-driven insights" (repeated from the summary), and the goal: "highlight the **trajectory** of AI advancements and **evaluate the state of accompanying safeguards**". Then "promote a **shared understanding** of where AI capabilities are today and where they might be heading."

**What differed.**
- **AGI.** A UK government institute says in a public report that AGI "in the coming years" is *plausible*. The summary never went near this; its register was "early signs", "some of the capabilities". The introduction's register is much stronger ("extraordinary", "deeply consequential"). The summary is the cautious layer; the "why" is where the institution's actual view shows. I didn't predict that.
- The hedges are stacked: "It is **plausible** that … this trend **may** lead to capabilities **widely acknowledged as** AGI **or otherwise** transformative AI." Each one loosens a different joint:
  - *plausible*: a credence word, short of "likely";
  - *may*: a second modal on top;
  - *widely acknowledged as*: AGI defined by social consensus, not by a criterion. AISI doesn't commit to a definition of AGI; it defers to whatever will be "widely acknowledged";
  - *or otherwise transformative AI*: a disjunction that makes the claim robust to AGI being ill-defined.
- The safeguards aim appears explicitly: "evaluate the state of accompanying safeguards". So "trajectory" plus "safeguards" are the report's two stated objects. Risk itself isn't named as an object here; capabilities and safeguards are.

**Wandering.**
- For the lexicon: `aisi:agi` isn't defined; it's a social-acknowledgement term. The project could record that AISI uses "AGI" as a deferred-reference term ("widely acknowledged as") alongside "transformative AI". It's the "deliberate ambiguity where it is honest" that CLAUDE.md allows for. If the glossary defines AGI, that would be a different story; I'll check.
- "Trend may lead to": an extrapolation argument from trend to AGI. This is the bridge from "data-driven" to forecast. The report rests its *why* on extrapolating envelopes, the same envelopes that (unit 11–12) carry a winner's-curse bias and saturating evals. The extrapolation is as good as the curves. I can't fault AISI for saying "plausible", which is honest. But a reader quoting "AISI says AGI is plausible in the coming years" should know it rests on these envelopes.
- "Competitive with – or even surpassing – human experts in an increasing number of domains": the summary said "far surpassed PhD-level experts on some", and cyber shows expert tasks at about 25%. "Competitive with" is the accurate general description; "surpassing" applies to chem-bio protocol and troubleshooting tasks. The sentence keeps both, honestly.
- "Snapshot" vs "trends": a snapshot of trends. A dated document, December 2025; the curves end around mid/late 2025. If someone asks the expert "what's the state of AI capability now?", the answer is: as of AISI's testing through about late 2025, with the last data points around mid-to-late 2025 release dates.
- Whose understanding is "shared"? Government, industry, civil society, the public. Industry already knows its own models. The shared understanding is mostly a *public* understanding of what labs and governments see privately. The report narrows the information asymmetry a little. That's its political function, beyond the science.
