# Unit 21 — L184–188, p.10 ("8") — 02 Agents: definitions

**Predicted:** agents defined (autonomous, multi-step, tools), with the task-length metric early. The definition yes; the metric not yet. More definitions than I expected, each set in bold, which suggests the glossary's entries.

**Got:**
- Drivers of progress: "**algorithmic improvements, more and higher quality data, and increases in the computational power**" (footnote 2). The standard three.
- "recent progress has been **further accelerated** by the development of **agents** – AI systems that can not only answer users' queries, but **complete multi-step tasks on their behalf**."
- "**scaffolds**. These are **external structures built around models** that let them (for example) use external tools or decompose tasks."
- "**reasoning models** carry out step-by-step problem solving in their **chains-of-thought** – meaning they can keep track of context and break down complex problems."
- "It is **likely** that improvements in reasoning and more sophisticated scaffolds are **interacting** to enhance model performance."

**What differed.**
- **Agents as an accelerant of progress**, not just a product category. The causal claim is: agents → further acceleration. Agents "complete multi-step tasks **on their behalf**", meaning *users'* behalf. The definition builds in a principal: the user. For the repo's "Aligned to whom?" map, AISI's agent concept is user-delegated, the principal being the user. Not the developer, not the deployer.
- **Scaffolds and models are separated explicitly**: scaffolds are "external structures built around models". So AISI's *system* = model + scaffold (+ safeguards?). That addresses my "models vs systems" note (units 14, 16): a model plus scaffolding is the system that gets evaluated in agent tasks.
- **Reasoning models** and **chains-of-thought** are defined functionally (step-by-step problem solving; tracking context, breaking down problems). Nothing about monitorability of CoT, yet. CoT matters for loss of control (monitoring a model's reasoning); I'd expect a mention in Section 5.
- **"Likely… interacting"**: a hedged causal claim of interaction, not additivity. A gentle claim; no evidence offered here.

**Wandering.**
- Footnote 2 is the first numbered reference. References come at the end (pp. 48–49). The report cites external work for background claims, which matches "draw occasionally on external research".
- "Not only answer users' queries, but complete multi-step tasks": the shift from oracle to actor. In risk terms, it moves the human out of the loop between steps. Every risk in the report gets worse as the human is removed from the middle: misuse needs less skill, control is harder, societal integration deepens. The Agents section placed second, before the domains, now makes sense: agency is the cross-cutting amplifier.
- Scaffolds are a lever *outside* the model's weights: anyone can build a better scaffold for an open model. That bears on the open-closed gap and on the "underestimate the ceiling" caveat. AISI's scaffolds may be weaker than what a determined actor (or the developer) builds, so measured capability is a lower bound in part because of scaffolding.
- For the lexicon, candidate source terms: `aisi:agent` ("AI systems that can… complete multi-step tasks on their behalf"), `aisi:scaffold` ("external structures built around models"), `aisi:reasoning-model`, `aisi:chain-of-thought`. These are probably repeated in the glossary. I'll compare when I get there.
