# Unit 14 — L133–137, p.7 ("5") — Introduction, opening

**Predicted:** who AISI is and what it does, then methods and limits. The first half arrived; methods not yet.

**Got:**
- Framing: AI creates "**both opportunities and challenges** for society". Readers named: "**policymakers, industry leaders, and the public**", who need to understand "the pace of their development, impact on society, and transformative potential."
- "Established in 2023, the AI Security Institute (AISI) is a **government organisation dedicated to AI safety and security research**." Both words: safety *and* security.
- **Mission:** "to equip **governments** with a scientific understanding of the **risks** posed by **advanced AI**."
- "**more than 30 frontier systems**", over two years, "using a range of methods". Domains include "cyber, chemistry and biology capabilities".
- "This report **synthesises** key trends we've observed."

**What differed.**
- The N I wanted in unit 4: **more than 30 frontier systems**. Small, considering that every figure is an envelope over release dates. Thirty systems spread across, say, 4–6 developers and two-plus years leaves only a handful of models per year per domain, and not every system was run on every eval.
- "Safety and security" both appear, so the rename didn't drop "safety" from the self-description. My unit-1 question about the rename is answered here, softly: AISI describes itself as doing both.
- The mission names "governments" as the recipient; the intro's readers include industry and the public. The report is a public good aimed beyond the mission's stated audience.
- "Advanced AI" in the mission, "frontier systems" in the work, "frontier AI" in the title. Three nouns, possibly synonyms. I'll see if the glossary separates them.

**Wandering.**
- "Synthesises" is the right word for what this is: a secondary report over AISI's primary evaluations, most of them unpublished (pre-deployment tests are confidential to the developers). So the evidence base is mostly private, and the report is the public projection of it. For this project's evidence tiers: the claims are first-hand for AISI, but verification by others is impossible for most of them. Expert answer: "AISI's own testing, mostly non-public; the report presents aggregate trends."
- "Opportunities and challenges", "transformative potential": the generic opening of a UK government document, keeping the pro-innovation framing (the UK's AI Opportunities Action Plan, January 2025) beside a risk mission. A government institute must speak to both. It may also explain the summary's restraint: no alarm words, "early signs", "some of the capabilities".
- "Frontier systems", not "models": a system may include scaffolding, tools and safeguards. Evaluating systems vs models matters for safeguards (a system has classifiers; a model doesn't). The summary said "models" and "systems" interchangeably. Watch whether the glossary distinguishes them.
- "Using a range of methods": capability evals, red-teaming, human-uplift studies?, surveys (units 8 and 10). I expect the next paragraph lists them.
