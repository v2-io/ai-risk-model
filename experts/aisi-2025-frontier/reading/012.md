**Predicted:** the autonomy panel (software tasks over an hour), with Figure 1.3. Yes. What I didn't predict is that the panel's own definitions of the risk areas carry the content.

**Got (text):**

- FIGURE 1.3 caption: "Performance on autonomy tasks across **three risk areas**:
  - **precursors** (skills upstream of hazardous capabilities),

**Got (figure, read off the image):** "Autonomy skills — by task type". Average success rate against model release date.
- Basic precursor skills: about 10% (late 2022) → about 95% (around 2025.3).
- Simplified self-replication: about 2% → about 66% (around 2025.4).
- Simplified AI R&D: about 0–4% until late 2024 → about 42% (around 2025.4). A model released later (around 2025.6) sits far *below* the envelope, at about 19%, with an error bar from about 0 to about 40%.
- Error bars on the AI R&D points are wide: the 42% envelope point looks to span roughly 25–57%.

**What differed.**
1. **A relabelling between text and figure.** The panel says "hourlong software tasks", and the figure's matching line (above 40%, under 5% in late 2023) is "**Simplified AI R&D**". Software tasks *are* the AI R&D proxy. Unit 4 called them "software tasks that would take a human expert over an hour" under "autonomy skills". So the same data appears under three descriptions: software tasks, autonomy, AI R&D. A reader who quotes the summary ("models complete hour-long software tasks") won't know the source treats these as a **risk area: "AI dramatically increasing the rate of AI development."**
2. **"Simplified" is in the names of two risk areas.** AISI is telling us that its self-replication and AI R&D tasks are simplified versions of the real thing. That's an honest hedge built into the vocabulary, and it will disappear the moment someone cites "AISI's self-replication evals show 60%".
3. **"Risk areas"**: a term of the source. Autonomy is measured across three risk areas: precursors, AI R&D, self-replication. "Precursors" is defined as "skills upstream of hazardous capabilities", a causal-position concept ("upstream"), which this project's causal chain would recognise as a role relative to a focal event.

**Wandering.**
- **The winner's curse, visible.** The AI R&D envelope steps up to about 42% on a point with a wide error bar, and a later model scores about 19%. The envelope holds 42% from then on. Did capability jump, or did one model get a lucky draw on few tasks? The headline ">40% success on hourlong software tasks" may rest on one model's noisy estimate. A careful expert answer: "AISI's figure shows one model at about 42% with wide uncertainty; a later model scored lower." That's a question someone should ask and probably won't.
- "AI dramatically increasing the rate of AI development" is a definition of the *risk*, not the task. The task is "simplified" hour-long software work. Between them sits the whole argument for recursive self-improvement: that competence at software tasks predicts acceleration of AI R&D. The report states the risk and measures a proxy, and the link is assumed. Is it argued in Section 5? Note to check.
- Self-replication here is about 66%, and the summary says "5% to 60%" (FIGURE 16). Different figure, close numbers. Perhaps FIGURE 16 is a different aggregation (RepliBench's component tasks?), or a different task subset. "Representative subsets of our full suite" (unit 9) may explain the gap. Watch FIGURE 16.
- "Section 5" is the first section number, so the body has numbered sections: perhaps 1 overview, 2 cyber, 3 chem-bio, 4 safeguards?, 5 autonomy, 6 societal... I'll guess cyber and chem-bio come first, since the summary led with them.
- "Basic precursor skills" at 95%: saturated, like cyber's lowest tier. Every domain has a saturated bottom rung, which says the evals were built for weaker models and are now outrun. The report is, among other things, the history of its own evals being outgrown.
- "Autonomously replicating across compute": the definition doesn't say "without permission" or "evading shutdown". Replication across compute is neutral as stated; the hazard is unauthorised replication. Elsewhere (unit 7) self-replication is a pre-requisite for "evasion of control". So the definition is capability-neutral, and the risk framing lives in the surrounding text.
