**Predicted:** persuasion and/or emotional reliance, under an "AI is increasingly used for…" heading. Right on topics, and the heading is the hedged pattern again: "Early signs of AI's broader societal impacts are emerging."

**Got:**
- "increasing **use** of AI to research political issues, alongside an increase in persuasive **capabilities**" (FIGURE 18). It puts a use trend and a capability trend side by side and lets the reader connect them.
- "early signs of **emotional impact** on users; **over a third of UK citizens** have used AI for emotional support or social interaction" (FIGURE 21). This is a population survey, so a different kind of evidence from evals.
- **Not predicted:** "AI agents are being increasingly **entrusted with high-stakes activities such as asset transfers**" (FIGURE 23). Agents moving money, presumably crypto or financial actions. "Entrusted" is a word about *human delegation*, not model capability: humans handing agents authority.
- A separate finding: "The performance gap between open and closed source models has narrowed over the past two years." My priming had this. The gap is "**now between four and eight months** according to **external data**" (FIGURE 24, 25).
- **A definition:** open-source models are those "**whose code, parameters and training data are made freely available**."

**Wandering.**
- That definition matters for the lexicon, and it's arguably inaccurate for the models it must be talking about. Most leading "open" models (Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen, gpt-oss) release weights and some code, but **not training data**. By AISI's own definition, almost none of them would count as open-source, and the comparison in Figures 24–25 almost certainly uses open-*weight* models. So the summary's definition is stricter than the population it measures, an internal inconsistency (or a loose gloss). For the project: `aisi:open-source-model` as defined ≠ what's measured, and neighbours include OSI's open-source AI definition (which does require data information) and "open-weight". A fork asked "how does AISI define open-source?" should quote this, then say the measured set is probably open-weight. I'll check whether the body says "open-weight".
- "According to external data": the first time the report leans on someone else's measurements (Epoch AI? Artificial Analysis?). A different evidential tier from AISI's own evals, and AISI flags it honestly. For corroboration accounting, the open-closed claim is not independent AISI evidence; it's AISI relaying a third party.
- The societal section uses three kinds of evidence: use trends (people researching politics with AI), capability evals (persuasiveness), surveys ("over a third of UK citizens"), and something about agents and asset transfers, maybe observational (on-chain data?). The report is broader than "frontier evaluation"; it's a multi-method observatory. That fits the name "Trends", wider than "Evaluations".
- "Over a third of UK citizens have used AI for emotional support **or** social interaction." The "or" does work: "social interaction" could include casual chat with a chatbot. So it's not a third of citizens seeking emotional support. Most likely misquote: "a third of Brits use AI for emotional support."
