# Unit 20 — L174–180, p.9 ("7") — Reading this report

**Predicted:** a reading guide with caveats (release dates, frontier envelope, elicitation). Yes, nearly all of it, and the source answers several questions I'd been carrying.

**Got:**
- Purpose: "illustrate **high-level trends** … **not benchmark or compare specific models or developers**."
- "**This report should not be read as a forecast.**"
- "Our evaluations, **while robust**, do not capture all factors that will contribute to the **real-world impact** of capabilities we measure."
- "primarily based on **aggregated results from our internal evaluations**"; "draw **occasionally** on external research"; "**not … a comprehensive review of the literature**", and it "**may not include all recent models**."
- Figures: "**step lines that track best-so-far model performance**". "Unless otherwise stated… each task for each evaluation was **repeated 10 times for each model**." "**Standard mean error bars**" where applicable.
- "**To prevent misuse, the details of high-risk evaluation tasks are not disclosed.**"
- "we acknowledge we **may generally underestimate the ceiling of capabilities**: see the **Appendix** for more specifics."

**Answered from my earlier units:**
- Unit 11's "frontier envelope": confirmed, "best-so-far".
- Unit 11's and 12's error bars: standard error of the mean, over 10 repeats per task per model.
- Unit 9's "can we verify?": no, by design: high-risk task details withheld "to prevent misuse".
- Units 18–19's elicitation worry: acknowledged as possible underestimation of "the ceiling", with specifics deferred to the Appendix.
- Why no model names: "not benchmark or compare specific models or developers". It's a policy choice, not an oversight.

**What differed, and a tension.**
- **"This report should not be read as a forecast"** sits two pages after "It is **plausible** that in the coming years, this trend may lead to capabilities widely acknowledged as AGI" (unit 15). Strictly, a plausibility claim isn't a forecast, and the hedges in unit 15 were stacked precisely to keep it from being one. But a reader will feel the pull. An expert should hold both: AISI disclaims forecasting, yet presents its "why" in terms of where trends "may lead". The data part is non-forecast; the motivation contains a plausibility judgment.
- **"While robust"**: a self-assessment without evidence attached. It's the one unhedged self-claim in a paragraph of honest caveats. Mild.
- **Two error directions, named in one paragraph**: evals "do not capture all factors" of real-world impact (which could cut either way: real-world risk may be lower or higher than measured capability suggests), and "may generally underestimate the ceiling" (a one-directional caveat: capability is a lower bound). AISI commits to a direction only for capability, not for impact.

**Wandering.**
- **The winner's curse, revisited with numbers.** With 10 repeats per task, the error bars capture run-to-run variance *given the task set*, not variance from which tasks were chosen. A best-so-far line over models each estimated with noise is biased upward, a little, and the bias grows with the number of models. Against that, "underestimate the ceiling" (weak elicitation) biases downward. The two biases go opposite ways, and neither is quantified. I don't think this changes any qualitative conclusion, but it is exactly the methodological question a statistician would put to this report, and probably won't be answered in it.
- "Not benchmark or compare specific models or developers": a diplomatic necessity. AISI's access depends on voluntary cooperation from developers; naming laggards would cost it access. That's a structural fact about the source's constraints. Its independence is real but bounded by its access relationships. For this project's "declared interests" field: AISI depends on developer cooperation, which shapes what it can publish.
- "Withheld to prevent misuse": the source sets its own reading rule, in CLAUDE.md's sense. It's an epistemic boundary set for security reasons. The project can record a "non-disclosed method" marker on these claims: assertion warranted by AISI's internal evidence, not externally checkable.
- "May not include all recent models": a scope note with a consequence. The October 2025 cutoff and the selection mean the envelope might sit below the real frontier, another downward pull.
- "Aggregated results": aggregation over tasks, repeats, and possibly sub-domains. The percentages in the summary are aggregates of aggregates. "On average" (unit 4) is now clear: mean success rate across tasks and repeats, at a difficulty level, for the best-so-far model.
