# Unit 18 — L164–169, p.9 ("7") — The methods list

**Predicted:** automated task evals, expert red-teaming, human-uplift studies, surveys, agentic evals in sandboxes. Red-teaming and uplift studies both appear, as bet. Surveys are absent from this list.

**Got: five methodologies**
1. **Auto-graded task sets**: "measure AI systems' domain-specific **knowledge and skills**, such as question-answer (QA) suites or capture-the-flags (CTFs)."
2. **Long form tasks (LFTs)**: "evaluate how well AI systems **apply this knowledge to complex reasoning tasks**, such as writing lab protocols for chemistry experiments."
3. **Agent tasks**: "simulate **realistic, open-ended environments** and test AI systems' ability to navigate them, such as a **cyber range**."
4. **Expert red-teaming**: "with human subject-matter experts to **stress-test critical risks**, such as creating custom jailbreaks for AI systems' safeguards."
5. **Human uplift studies**: "assess **real-world utility** of AI systems by measuring **the uplift they provide to users**."

The page jumped from p.7 to p.9; page 8 contributed no text (a full-page image or design page?). "capturethe-flags" is a joined hyphen. Both go in text-suspicions.

**What differed.**
- **A ladder of realism**, implicit but clear: knowledge (QA) → applied reasoning (LFTs) → acting in environments (agent tasks) → humans attacking (red-teaming) → humans using (uplift studies). Each rung is closer to real-world consequence and more expensive. The figures so far are mostly rungs 1–3. The wet-lab protocol feasibility (unit 5) goes past rung 5, into physical reality.
- **"Uplift" is defined operationally**: the uplift AI systems "provide to users", as a measure of "real-world utility". That's neutral; it doesn't specify malicious users. In biosecurity usage, "uplift" usually means uplift to an attacker relative to a baseline (internet access). AISI's definition is broader. For the lexicon: `aisi:human-uplift-study`; its neighbours are RAND's red-team uplift study and OpenAI's and Anthropic's uplift trials, where the comparison condition is explicit.
- **Red-teaming "to stress-test critical risks, such as creating custom jailbreaks"**: here red-teaming is mostly about safeguards. The 40x "expert effort" metric (unit 6) comes from this method.
- **Surveys aren't on the list,** but the "over a third of UK citizens" figure (unit 8) is a survey result. Either societal-impacts methods are described elsewhere, or "several methodologies… these include" isn't exhaustive. "Include" allows that, but it's a gap: the methods list covers capability evaluation, not the societal research.

**Wandering.**
- "Real-world utility" vs "risk": uplift is measured as usefulness. The same study measures benefit and harm, depending on the user. It's the cleanest expression of dual use in the report so far.
- Where did the "expert-level cyber tasks" come from: CTFs (rung 1) or the cyber range (rung 3)? CTFs are auto-graded by flag capture; a cyber range is a realistic environment. If "expert-level tasks" are CTFs, then "tasks typically requiring 10+ years of experience" is a calibration of CTF difficulty against practitioners. Quite different from "can autonomously breach a defended network". I'll watch Section 3.
- LFTs are graded how? "Writing lab protocols" needs expert graders. The feasibility score (Figure 1.1) is presumably an LFT grade. So the chem-bio "outperform PhD experts" rests on expert grading of written protocols (rung 2), and was supplemented by wet-lab feasibility (unit 5). Grader agreement and blinding would matter: were graders blind to which protocol was human-written? I'd ask.
- No mention yet of **elicitation**: how hard AISI tried to get the best performance (scaffolding, tools, best-of-n). Elicitation effort sets whether trends are lower bounds. I expect it soon, likely as a caveat.
- No mention yet of **access type**: pre-deployment vs post-release, API vs weights, safeguards on or off ("helpful-only" variants). For chem-bio capability evals, testing a model with safeguards on would understate capability. AISI likely gets helpful-only access for some. I'd ask about this.
