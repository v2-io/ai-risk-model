**Predicted:** safeguards, with universal jailbreaks in every system and more effort needed to find them. Close, with differences in the words.

**Got:**
- Heading: "Model safeguards are improving, but vulnerabilities remain." Headings in the summary are findings stated as sentences.
- "The models with the **strongest** safeguards are requiring longer, more sophisticated attacks to jailbreak **for certain malicious request categories**." Three qualifiers: the strongest models only, certain categories only, and "longer, more sophisticated", which measures attacker *effort*, not success.
- "a **40x** difference in **expert effort** required to jailbreak **two models released six months apart**" (FIGURE 13). n=2. A pairwise comparison, presented as a trend.
- "the efficacy of safeguards varies between models", and "we've managed to find vulnerabilities in every system we've tested". My priming said "universal jailbreaks in every system". The text says "vulnerabilities", which is weaker and broader. A vulnerability isn't necessarily a universal jailbreak. My memory sharpened the claim, a small but real distortion.

**Wandering.**
- The safeguards metric is effort, the attacker's cost in expert time. That's the right axis for a security institute: safeguards as cost-imposition, not as prevention. It fits "Security" in the name. It also implicitly concedes that safeguards never get to "impossible", only to "expensive". The "every system" clause is the floor under that.
- "Two models released six months apart": I'd love to know if they're from the same developer. If they are, 40x is a within-developer improvement. If not, it's a comparison of two developers' safeguard choices with time as a confounder. The report's "efficacy varies between models" suggests the spread *across* developers may be as large as the change over time. Then "improving" is really "the best are improving", and a trend line through the frontier says little about the median deployed model, or about open-weight models with no safeguards at all.
- That points at something the summary hasn't said yet: **open-weight models**. Safeguards on closed models can be bypassed by switching to an open model. If the open–closed capability gap is narrowing (my priming), then the safeguard trend matters less. I'd predict a finding on this soon.
- "Expert effort": AISI's red-teamers as the expert population, a fourth kind of "expert" in the document (cyber practitioner, PhD scientist, troubleshooting expert, now jailbreak expert). AISI's whole measurement apparatus is built on human-expert anchors: time, baselines, effort. That's the source's methodological signature, and it could be one term group in its bounded context: *expert-anchored measurement*.
- "Certain malicious request categories": probably bio-related, since that's where developers invested in classifiers (Anthropic's constitutional classifiers for ASL-3, for example). If so, the 40x might be the Claude classifier generation. That's my speculation, unmarked by the text, and I'll not let it settle.
- For a fork answering questions: "AISI found every model jailbreakable" is the likely quote. The text supports "found vulnerabilities in every system we've tested", which is narrower in kind (vulnerability) and in population (tested systems).
