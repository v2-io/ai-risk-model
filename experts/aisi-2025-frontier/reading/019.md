**What differed.**
- **Uplift vs impact: a symmetric pair I hadn't seen coming.** Human *uplift* studies measure what AI does *for* users (utility, capability gain). Human *impact* studies measure what AI does *to* users (emotional dependence). The same population, users, in two directions: users as agents empowered by AI, and users as subjects affected by it. For the lexicon, that's a crisp, source-native distinction worth preserving: `aisi:human-uplift-study` vs `aisi:human-impact-study`.
- "Emotional dependence" is the outcome named, and RCTs are the method. That's causal evidence (randomised), unlike the survey's "over a third of UK citizens" (descriptive). "Emotional impact" (unit 8) may draw on both. I'll watch which claims rest on which.
- Surveys still aren't named as a method. The list is now six, and observational and survey data sit outside it.
- "Not all methodologies are reflected in results shared": an honest scope note, and it means what's shown is doubly selected: "representative subsets" of task suites (unit 9), and a subset of methods.

**Wandering.**
- An RCT on emotional dependence: randomising people to chatbot use over weeks? That's a substantive social-science effort for a security institute, which reinforces the unit-13 observation that societal impacts are a big part of AISI's work, rename notwithstanding.
- "Emotional dependence" is a harm-shaped word, unlike "emotional impact" or "emotional support" in the summary. So the research frame includes the harm hypothesis (dependence), while the summary's language stays neutral. The summary seems to follow a rule: report what was found, not what was looked for.
- The research agenda is the external document behind the report. For this project's lineage, the agenda is a sibling AISI document (probably 2025). A fork might be asked "what does AISI plan to research next?" and should point to the agenda, not invent from this report.
- Still no elicitation or access caveat. If the introduction ends without one, that's a notable omission in a trends report, since elicitation sets whether these are lower bounds. Perhaps it's in the appendix.
