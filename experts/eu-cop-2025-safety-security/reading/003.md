**What it held.** Only the heading `# **Recitals**`, at the top of page 3. The tool served it as its own unit, presumably because a subheading or a structurally separate block follows it.

**What differed.** I predicted an Objective C. There is none: the specific objectives are **only A and B**. So my "living document" guess was wrong, at least here. And Objectives fill page 2 alone and stop. Two objectives, both about compliance. Nothing about the state of the art or innovation among the *specific* objectives (innovation appears only in the overarching one, from Art. 1(1)).

**Wandering.**
- A Code whose specific objectives are only about compliance is a compliance instrument first. Any reading of it as a "safety framework" in the lab sense (like Anthropic's RSP) should keep that in view: its stated purpose is to make Arts. 53/55 demonstrable and assessable, not to make models safe as such. Safety is the content of the obligations it serves. The difference matters when comparing it with company frameworks, whose stated purposes are about the company's own risk.
- "Recitals" is EU legislative vocabulary: the "Whereas" clauses before a regulation's articles, which aid interpretation but don't bind. Choosing that word signals that the Code wants to be read like EU law: recitals to interpret, Commitments to bind. I expect the recitals to be lettered or numbered and to give interpretive principles, which forks will need when someone asks "how should Measure X be read?"
- One odd thing: the tool says unit 3 is a single line (L43), so the heading stands alone. A unit "never crosses a heading", so the next line must be another heading, or something the tool doesn't join (a table, or a restored block). Maybe the recitals open with an intro sentence styled as a heading, or with a subheading like "Whereas:".
