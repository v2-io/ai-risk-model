# Unit 80 · L637–639 · p.28: Measure 10.2, Public transparency

**What it held.** "**If and insofar as necessary to assess and/or mitigate systemic risks**, Signatories will publish (e.g. via their websites) a **summarised version** of their Framework and Model Report(s), and updates thereof…, **with removals to not undermine the effectiveness of safety and/or security mitigations and to protect sensitive commercial information**. For Model Reports, such publication will include **high-level descriptions of the systemic risk assessment results and the safety and security mitigations implemented**. For Frameworks, such publication is **not necessary if all of the Signatory's models are similarly safe or safer** models pursuant to Appendix 2.2. For Model Reports, such publication is not necessary if the model is a similarly safe or safer model pursuant to Appendix 2.2."

**What differed.** My prediction was close to verbatim (memory). Not predicted: the minimum content of a published Model Report summary (high-level assessment results and mitigations), and the two Appendix 2.2 exemptions.

**Wandering.**

- **The condition is the crux: "if and insofar as necessary to assess and/or mitigate systemic risks."** Who judges necessity? Textually, the signatory, under purposive interpretation and AI Office guidance. Two readings:
  - *Narrow*: publish only where publication itself mitigates risk (e.g. warning downstream deployers, enabling external scrutiny of a specific risk). Most of the time, not necessary.
  - *Broad*: public scrutiny is a general means of assessing systemic risk (external researchers, civil society, independent evaluation all depend on information), so for frontier models publication is generally necessary.
  The structure of the Measure favours the broad reading in one way: it exempts "similarly safe or safer" models explicitly. If publication were rarely necessary, there'd be little need to exempt anyone. The exemptions imply that, for models *not* similarly safe or safer (i.e. frontier-pushing models), publication is the expected case. That's an argument, not the text. A fork should give both readings, the structural argument, and say the text doesn't settle it.
- **Removals for mitigation effectiveness and commercial sensitivity**: the published summary may omit jailbreak-relevant details and trade secrets. That's broadly what lab system cards already do. So C10 probably codifies existing system-card practice.
- **Minimum content for Model Report summaries**: high-level assessment results and mitigations. Not the justification (M7.2), not the defeaters, not the six-month plans, not the evaluation transcripts. The public sees outcomes, not reasoning.
- **Framework publication exemption** needs *all* the signatory's models to be similarly safe or safer. A single frontier model brings the Framework publication duty (if "necessary").
- **Public vs regulator**: everything rich in the Chapter goes to the AI Office; the public gets summaries "as necessary". For this project's corpus, it means the public record of how a signatory applies the Code will be the summaries (system cards, published frameworks), not the Model Reports. A fork asked "can we see what labs told the AI Office?" should say: no; at most summarised versions under M10.2.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Must signatories publish anything?" M10.2: summarised Framework and Model Reports "if and insofar as necessary to assess and/or mitigate systemic risks", with removals for mitigation effectiveness and commercial sensitivity; Model Report summaries include high-level assessment results and mitigations; not necessary for similarly safe or safer models.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Who decides whether publication is 'necessary'?" Not stated; the exemptions suggest publication is expected for frontier models (structural argument).
