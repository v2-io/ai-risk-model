# Unit 6 · L50 · p.3: recital (d), Principle of Integration with Existing Laws

**What it held.** Five recognitions under one principle:
1. The Chapter "forms part of, and is complemented by, other Union laws."
2. Confidentiality, "including commercial confidentiality", obligations "are preserved to the extent required by Union law". Information sent to the "European AI Office ("AI Office")" will be treated under Art. 78 AI Act.
3. "Information about future developments and future business activities that they submit to the AI Office will be understood as subject to change."
4. "The Measure under this Chapter to promote a healthy risk culture (Measure 8.3) is without prejudice to any obligations arising from Directive (EU) 2019/1937 on the protection of whistleblowers and implementing laws of Member States in conjunction with Article 87 AI Act."
5. Signatories "may be able to rely on international standards to the extent they cover the provisions of this Chapter."

**What differed.** The name was in my list again (labels primed). The content is more concrete and more industry-protective than I guessed: three of the five recognitions protect the signatory (confidentiality, forward-looking statements as non-binding, reliance on international standards). And a forward reference: **Measure 8.3** is about "a healthy risk culture", so Commitment 8 is organisational/governance (that fits WG4's remit) and has at least three Measures. It's the first hard fact about the operative structure I've been given.

**Wandering.**

- **"Subject to change" for future developments.** The Code will apparently ask signatories to tell the AI Office about *future* models and plans: forecasts of capabilities, planned releases, maybe timelines. (d) says such information is "understood as subject to change", i.e. not a promise. That reads like a direct answer to provider fears that a forecast would become an enforceable commitment. It also tells me the Code asks for forward-looking information at all, which I didn't remember. Watch for which Measures ask for it: maybe the Framework must include forecasts of when models will cross risk tiers.
- **Art. 78 AI Act** (confidentiality of information obtained by authorities). So information flows into the AI Office under a confidentiality regime. Consequence for this project: the Safety and Security Model Reports are *not public*. The Code's transparency is to the regulator, not to the public. If a later Measure asks for public summaries, that will be a separate and probably weaker thing. A fork asked "will we be able to read the providers' Model Reports?" should answer from this: the reports go to the AI Office, treated under Art. 78; public disclosure, if any, is elsewhere.
- **Whistleblowers.** Measure 8.3's "healthy risk culture" is linked to the EU Whistleblower Directive and Art. 87 AI Act (which, as I recall, applies the Directive to reporting AI Act infringements). "Without prejudice to" means the Code's risk-culture Measure doesn't replace or narrow the legal protections. So the Code has a whistleblower-adjacent Measure framed as *risk culture* rather than as whistleblower protection. Interesting framing: protection for people who raise concerns is treated as part of the culture of risk management, not only as a legal right. Compare SB 53, which (I think) has explicit whistleblower provisions; this is a cross-source point a fork should note, but I haven't read SB 53 here, so the comparison is a hypothesis.
- **"May be able to rely on international standards to the extent they cover the provisions."** A route to equivalence: if an ISO/IEC standard (42001? 23894?) covers a Measure, the signatory may rely on it. "May be able to" is weak; it doesn't say the AI Office will accept it. And the Act's own harmonised-standards route (Art. 40-ish, presumption of conformity) is different from "international standards". This is the Code admitting it's one layer in a stack.
- **What I expected and didn't get:** the "definitions shall prevail" clause and the "AI Office guidance" clause from my priming. Not here. Integration with existing law would have been a natural home. So they're probably in a later recital or in the glossary's preamble. Note: my priming tells me the *phrase* exists but not where; I'll know it when I see it and read around it.
- **The AI Office is now a defined term** ("European AI Office ("AI Office")"), defined at its first full mention, in a recital. So defined terms are introduced inline as they arise, not only in a glossary. A fork extracting definitions has to scan recitals too.
- Thinking about who this unit is for: almost entirely for providers' lawyers. It answers the questions a general counsel would ask before signing: does this override other law? Is what we send confidential? Are our forecasts binding? Does this create new whistleblower exposure? Can we use our existing ISO certification? Each is answered in the signatory's favour, or neutrally. It's a recital written to get signatures.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Are the Model Reports public?" (d): information sent to the AI Office is treated under Art. 78 AI Act; confidentiality, including commercial confidentiality, preserved. Check later for any public summary.
- "Are a provider's statements to the AI Office about future models binding?" (d): "understood as subject to change."
- "How does the Code relate to whistleblower law?" (d): Measure 8.3 is "without prejudice to" Directive (EU) 2019/1937 and Art. 87 AI Act.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Can compliance with an ISO standard substitute for a Measure?" (d): signatories "may be able to rely on international standards to the extent they cover the provisions", which is weak and unconfirmed by the regulator in this text.

**Prediction for unit 7.** More principles. Perhaps (e) "Principle of Cooperation" (with the AI Office and with other providers), (f) "Principle of Innovation in AI Safety and Security" (the Code mustn't stifle safety research; maybe sharing of tools), and an SME/startup recital ("simplified ways of compliance for SMEs", which the Act asks for in Art. 56(5)?). And the interpretation recital ("AI Act definitions prevail", "AI Office guidance") could come at the end of the recitals.
