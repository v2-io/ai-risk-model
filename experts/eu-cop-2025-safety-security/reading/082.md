# Unit 82 · L662–669 · p.30 (table): 'insider threats', 'internal validity', 'management body', 'model' (first part)

**What it held.**
- **'insider threats'**: "hostile operations by **humans, AI models, and/or AI systems** (e.g. **senior management**, a senior member of the organisation's research team, other disgruntled employees, perpetrators of industrial espionage operations that have infiltrated their target, and/or **model self-exfiltration**) with access to sensitive organisational resources, and/or **accidental model leakage**."
- **'internal validity'**: "…ensures model evaluation results are as accurate as scientifically possible in the evaluation setting and are free from methodological shortcomings…." May be shown by e.g. sample sizes; significance and power; disclosure of environmental parameters; controlling confounders and spurious correlation; **preventing use of test data in training (e.g. train-test splits and respecting canary strings)**; re-running under different conditions, **varying individual parts (e.g. the strength of prompts and safety and security mitigations)**; detailed inspection of trajectories; avoiding labelling bias (blinding, inter-annotator agreement); **transparency-increasing techniques (e.g. reasoning traces… representative of the model's "inner workings" and legible by evaluators)**; **techniques to measure and/or reduce the model's capability to evade oversight**; and/or disclosing methods for creating and managing new evaluations "to ensure their integrity".
- **'management body'**: "a corporate organ **appointed pursuant to national law**" with (1) an executive function (strategy; day-to-day management) and (2) a supervisory function (overseeing executive decision-making); depending on national law, performed by different personnel in one body or distinct parts.
- **'model'**: "**a general-purpose AI model with systemic risk**." Versions ("fine tuned for different purposes, versions with access to different tools, and/or versions with different safety and/or security mitigations"): "All references to 'model'… refer to the relevant model version(s), as the context requires." "Generally, in the context of systemic risk assessment and mitigation, all references to 'model' refer to **all model versions that, in aggregate, constitute the systemic risk(s)** stemming from the model, including all model versions that: (1) are the most advanced; (2) correspond to point (1) and have limited or no safety…" (continues)
- Conversion note: the canonical text breaks 'internal validity' into one word per line in places ("measuring / statistical / significance…", "blinding / or / reporting…"). The words are complete; the line breaks are artefacts.

**What differed.** I expected 'insider threats' to mean staff. It includes **AI models and AI systems** as insiders, with **model self-exfiltration** as an example, and **senior management**. That's a major correction to my reading of M6.1 (unit 45).

**Wandering.**

- **The Security Goal must cover the model itself.** M6.1 requires the Security Goal to include "insider threats"; the Glossary defines insider threats to include hostile operations by AI models, e.g. model self-exfiltration. So every signatory's security mitigations must be intended to protect against its own model trying to exfiltrate itself (when the model has access to sensitive resources). That's a loss-of-control requirement hidden in a security definition. I read M6.1 as a conventional security clause; the Glossary makes it a misalignment clause. A fork asked "does the Code require labs to defend against their own models?" should say yes: via M6.1's insider threats, per the Glossary's definition. This is the clearest example in the Chapter of the definitions carrying operative weight unseen from the body.
- **Senior management as insider threat.** The Code names the people at the top as possible hostile actors. A remarkable inclusion. It means security controls must not rely on trusting executives (e.g. a CEO taking weights). Two-person rules, access controls even for leaders.
- **Accidental leakage** is in "insider threats" too, though not hostile. A definitional stretch, deliberately broad.
- **Internal validity's list is a checklist of evaluation science**, and several items are pointed at AI-specific threats to validity:
  - canary strings and test-data hygiene (contamination);
  - varying "the strength of prompts and safety and security mitigations" (elicitation sensitivity; testing with mitigations weakened);
  - reasoning traces representative of "inner workings" (CoT faithfulness again: five or six places now);
  - measuring and reducing "the model's capability to evade oversight": evaluation-aware models (cf. 'deception') threaten internal validity. The Code knows a model that detects testing invalidates the test.
  For a fork: these are "may be shown by, e.g." items, not requirements; internal validity itself is required as part of "high scientific and technical rigour" (Appendix 3.2).
- **'model' is redefined as the GPAI model with systemic risk**, and then made plural across versions: "all model versions that, in aggregate, constitute the systemic risk(s)". So every "the model" in the Chapter means a family of versions, aggregated. This answers several earlier questions:
  - "Most capable version", "fewest safety mitigations version" (M3.5) are the versions the 'model' definition highlights.
  - The risk of "the model" is the aggregate risk of its versions (including fine-tunes with tools).
  - A lab can't assess only the safest deployed version and call it "the model".
- **But note the precedence clause** (unit 81): "general-purpose AI model" and "general-purpose AI model with systemic risk" are Art. 3 terms (3(63), 3(65)-ish). The Glossary's 'model' is a short form for an Art. 3 term plus an aggregation rule. If any conflict arose between the aggregation rule and the Act's "model" concept, the Act would prevail. A subtle point for a fork: the version-aggregation rule is the Code's, layered on the Act's term.
- **'management body' "appointed pursuant to national law"**: national law of *which* state? A US company's board is appointed under Delaware law. Fine: "national law" isn't limited to EU Member States. The definition accommodates one-tier and two-tier boards. Banking lineage again (CRD's definition is similar).

**Questions to keep.**
- "Does the Code treat the model itself as a security threat?" Yes: 'insider threats' include "hostile operations by humans, AI models, and/or AI systems", e.g. "model self-exfiltration"; M6.1's Security Goal must cover insider threats.
- "Does the Code treat executives as possible threats?" 'insider threats' examples include "senior management".
- "What makes an evaluation internally valid?" Glossary 'internal validity', with its e.g. list (contamination controls, varied mitigation strength, legible reasoning traces, measuring capability to evade oversight, …).
- "What does 'the model' mean in the Code?" A GPAI model with systemic risk, understood as all versions that in aggregate constitute its systemic risk, including the most advanced and the least-mitigated.
