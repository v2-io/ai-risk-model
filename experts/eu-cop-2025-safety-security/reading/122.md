# Unit 122 · L968–975 · p.42: Appendix 4.4, Insider threats

**What it held.** "Signatories will protect against insider threats, **including in the form of (self-)exfiltration or sabotage carried out by models**, by implementing security mitigations that achieve the following mitigation objectives:
1. protection of model parameters from insider threats attempting to gain work-related access…, through (a) **background checks on employees and contractors** that have or might reasonably obtain read or write access to unreleased model parameters or systems that manage the access to such parameters;
2. awareness of the risk of insider threats, through (a) the provision of training on recognising and reporting insider threats;
3. reduction of the risk of **model self-exfiltration**, through (a) **sandboxes around models, such as virtual machines and code execution isolation**; and
4. reduction of the risk of **sabotage to model training and use**, through (a) **checking training data for indications of tampering**."

**What differed.** Predicted: background checks, training, sandboxing against self-exfiltration (the shape at least). Not predicted:
- **Sabotage carried out by models** as an insider threat, in the opening sentence. Not only escape, but models *sabotaging* (training, use).
- (4) **training-data tampering** as sabotage: this links back to M3.1(4) (training data review for poisoning or tampering), unit 27.
- And the answer to unit 115's puzzle: Appendix 3.5's "without prejudice to Appendix 4.4, point (1)" means **external evaluators with weight-level access may be subject to background checks** (as "contractors that have or might reasonably obtain read or write access"). The security limit on evaluator access is vetting, not denial.

**Wandering.**
- **The model as insider, made operative.** The Glossary's 'insider threats' (unit 82) included model self-exfiltration; here the appendix makes it a mitigation objective with a named measure: sandboxes (VMs, code execution isolation). Plus "sabotage carried out by models". So the Code requires signatories to contain their own models (sandboxing, especially in agentic and AI-R&D settings), as a *security* measure. This is the AI-control agenda written into security controls. A fork asked "does the Code require containment of the model itself?" should cite Appendix 4.4(3), the opening sentence's "sabotage carried out by models", and the Glossary's 'insider threats'.
- **Sabotage of training**: a model used in AI R&D (M7.1(2), M7.3(4)(a)) could sabotage the training of its successors (e.g. inserting subtle flaws, data poisoning). The Code's measure is checking training data for tampering, which addresses human and model saboteurs alike. Thin, but present.
- **Background checks for those who "might reasonably obtain" access**: forward-looking, includes people who could escalate to access.
- **Connection with M8.3 culture**: insider-threat training includes "reporting", so the internal reporting channels (M8.3(5)) carry insider-threat reports too.
- I'm noticing how much of the Code's loss-of-control content sits in security and definitions rather than in a single "loss of control" section: Appendix 1.4(2) defines it, 1.3 lists sources, the Glossary defines deception and insider threats, M5.1(8) names mitigations against subversion, M9.3 makes self-exfiltration reportable, Appendix 4.4 requires sandboxes. A fork answering "what does the Code require about loss of control?" must assemble at least these seven places.
