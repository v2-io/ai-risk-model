# Unit 10 · L62 · p.5: recital (j), Serious Incident Reporting

**What it held.**
- "The reporting of a serious incident is not an admission of wrongdoing."
- "Relevant information about serious incidents cannot be kept track of, documented, and reported at the model level only in retrospect after a serious incident has occurred."
- "The information that could directly or indirectly lead up to such an event is often dispersed and may be lost, overwritten, or fragmented by the time Signatories become aware of a serious incident."
- "This justifies the establishment of processes and measures to keep track of and document relevant information **before** serious incidents occur."

**What differed.** I was wrong that the recitals ended at (i). There's a (j), placed *after* the Interpretation recital, which is an odd place: interpretation clauses usually close a preamble. So (j) looks like a late addition in drafting, appended after the list was settled. (A drafting-history hypothesis, for the Chairs' statement to confirm or not.) Its content I didn't expect at all from the recitals: not a principle but a justification for one Commitment.

**Wandering.**

- **"Not an admission of wrongdoing."** The same provider-protective move as (d): it lowers the cost of reporting. In aviation and medicine, incident reporting works only when it's decoupled from blame ("just culture"). The Code imports that logic. But note what it doesn't say: it doesn't say reports can't be used in enforcement. "Not an admission" is about the act of reporting, not about the use of what's reported. A careful fork shouldn't overstate (j) as a safe harbour.
- **The real content is an argument for pre-incident logging.** You can't reconstruct what led to an incident after the fact, because the precursors are "dispersed", "lost, overwritten, or fragmented". So the Code justifies *ongoing* tracking and documentation of "relevant information" before anything goes wrong. What information? Probably: model inputs/outputs where possible, deployment changes, evaluations, mitigations' status, user reports. This is the most surveillance-adjacent idea in the Chapter so far: logs kept just in case. Privacy law (GDPR) will press against it, and (d)'s "integration with existing laws" is the relief valve.
- **"At the model level."** Incidents are tracked at the *model* level, consistent with (b): the Chapter governs models, not systems. But many serious incidents happen in deployments the provider doesn't run (an open-weight model fine-tuned by a downstream modifier). How does a model provider keep pre-incident information for deployments it can't see? It can't, for those. So pre-incident tracking will fall mostly on the provider's own API and products. A fork should note this structural limit for open-weight releases.
- **Why argue for it in a recital?** Because a Measure requiring logs "before incidents occur" would draw objections (cost, privacy, scope). The recital pre-justifies it. Recitals as advocacy for the operative text: I've now seen this pattern in (a), (g) and (j).
- "Serious incident" isn't defined yet. The AI Act defines it in Art. 3(49) (death or serious harm to health, serious and irreversible disruption of critical infrastructure, infringement of fundamental-rights obligations, serious harm to property or the environment, as I recall). The Glossary may refer to it. When I meet the definition, check whether the Code widens it for GPAI models (Art. 3(49) was written for systems).
- A connection: (g)'s precaution is about looking forward to identify risks; (j) is about recording forward so you can look back. Both are temporal-asymmetry arguments: what you didn't anticipate, or didn't record, is lost to you later.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Is reporting a serious incident an admission of fault?" (j): "not an admission of wrongdoing". But that isn't a bar on the report's use in enforcement; don't overstate it.
- "Why must providers document things before incidents happen?" (j): precursors "may be lost, overwritten, or fragmented" by the time a provider becomes aware.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Can a provider of an open-weight model meet the pre-incident tracking expectation?" (j) says model level; the structural limit is obvious once you imagine it.
