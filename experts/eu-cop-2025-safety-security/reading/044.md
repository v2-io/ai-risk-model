# Unit 44 · L368–376 · p.18: Commitment 6, Security mitigations; the open-weight exemption; until when

**What it held.**
- "**LEGAL TEXT:** Article 55(1), and recitals 114 and 115 AI Act"
- "Signatories commit to implementing an **adequate level of cybersecurity protection** for their models and their **physical infrastructure** along the entire model lifecycle, as specified in the Measures for this Commitment, to ensure the systemic risks stemming from their models that could arise from **unauthorised releases, unauthorised access, and/or model theft** are acceptable (pursuant to Commitment 4)."
- "**A model is exempt from this Commitment if the model's capabilities are inferior to the capabilities of at least one model for which the parameters are publicly available for download.**"
- "Signatories will implement these security mitigations for a model **until its parameters are made publicly available for download or securely deleted**."

**What differed.** Predicted: cybersecurity for models and physical infrastructure; theft and unauthorised access; an exemption tied to wide availability. Not predicted:
- The exemption's exact form: the comparator is **any one open-weight model with superior capabilities**. A clean, operational marginal-risk rule.
- **The security duty ends** when the weights are published or securely deleted. Security is about keeping weights secret; once they're public, there's nothing to protect.
- "**Unauthorised releases**" listed separately from theft, i.e. leaks, including insider leaks and accidental release.

**Wandering.**

- **Here at last is an explicit marginal-risk rule**, the thing I've been looking for since unit 27. But it's scoped to *security*: if an open-weight model already exceeds yours in capability, stealing your weights adds nothing that isn't already freely available, so protecting them gives no systemic-risk benefit. That logic is clean and hard to argue with. For the rest of the Chapter (safety mitigations, acceptance), I haven't seen an equivalent rule; Appendix 2.2's "similarly safe or safer" may be the general version.
- **"Inferior to the capabilities"** is a comparison that's harder than it looks. Capabilities are multidimensional: a model might be weaker overall and stronger in bio. Read with purposive interpretation (recital (i)), "inferior" should mean inferior in the capabilities relevant to systemic risk, presumably on every relevant dimension. But the text says just "the model's capabilities are inferior". A fork should flag the dimensionality problem: inferior on all relevant capabilities, or on average? The text doesn't say; the purposive reading favours "all relevant".
- **The exemption makes the frontier of open weights the security line.** As open-weight models improve, more closed models fall below them and lose their security obligations. So the security duty applies only to models above the open-weight frontier. That's elegant and also means the Code's security regime is dynamic: a lab's obligations change when a competitor releases weights. An open-weight release by one firm lightens others' duties.
- **"Until… securely deleted"**: deletion ends the duty too. So a retired model whose weights are kept still needs protection. Weight archives are a real attack surface; the Code covers them.
- **The scope is "systemic risks… that could arise from unauthorised releases, unauthorised access, and/or model theft".** So C6 isn't general information security; it's security against the model getting into the wrong hands. That supports my unit-41 guess: safety = harm from (authorised) use and behaviour; security = harm from unauthorised possession or access. A fork asked to distinguish should cite C6's scope sentence.
- **Physical infrastructure** included: data centres, hardware. The Code reaches into physical security.
- And "adequate level": C6 uses "adequate", C5 used "appropriate". Different adjectives; possibly no difference intended, possibly "adequate level of cybersecurity" echoes Art. 55(1)(d) of the Act ("ensure an adequate level of cybersecurity protection", as I recall). Likely the Act's wording.

**Questions to keep.**
- "When is a model exempt from the Code's security requirements?" C6: "if the model's capabilities are inferior to the capabilities of at least one model for which the parameters are publicly available for download."
- "How long must a model's weights be protected?" "Until its parameters are made publicly available for download or securely deleted."
- "What does C6 protect against?" Systemic risks from "unauthorised releases, unauthorised access, and/or model theft"; covers models and physical infrastructure.
- Should be asked but might not be: "What does 'inferior capabilities' mean for a model stronger in one domain?" Undefined; flag it.
- Should be asked: "Does the Code have a general marginal-risk rule?" Explicitly only here (C6). Check Appendix 2.2.

**Prediction for unit 45.** Measure 6.1, Definition of the security goal: Signatories will define a goal that specifies the threat actors their security mitigations are intended to protect against, "including non-state external threats, insider threats, and other expected threat actors", taking into account at least the current and expected capabilities of their models. Possibly an "at least RAND SL3"-like level (but I suspect the Code avoids naming RAND). Then Measure 6.2 pointing to Appendix 4.
