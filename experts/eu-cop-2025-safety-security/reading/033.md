# Unit 33 · L280–286 · p.14: M3.5, what external evaluators get; the "similarly safe or safer" exception; published selection criteria

**What it held.** Independent external evaluators get adequate free access to:
- "(1) the model's **most capable model version(s)** with regard to the systemic risk **that is made available on the market**;"
- "(2) the **chains-of-thought** of the model version(s) in point (1), **if available**; and"
- "(3) the model version(s) corresponding to… point (1) **with the fewest safety mitigations implemented** with regard to the systemic risk (**such as the helpful-only model version, if it exists**) and, as available, its chains-of-thought;"
- "**unless the model is considered a similarly safe or safer model** with regard to the same systemic risk (pursuant to **Appendix 2.2**)."
- Access means: "through an API, on-premise access (including transport), access via Signatory-provided hardware, or by making the model parameters publicly available for download, as appropriate."
- "For the purpose of selecting independent external evaluators…, Signatories will **publish suitable criteria for assessing applications**. The number of such evaluators, the selection criteria, and security measures may differ for points (1), (2), and (3)."

**What differed.** I predicted the most capable version, CoT and a "helpful-only" variant. All there (I suspect memory again for "helpful-only"). Not predicted:
- The **"similarly safe or safer model"** exception, pointing to **Appendix 2.2**. A whole concept I didn't anticipate: a model that's no riskier than some reference is exempt from this access duty. Appendix 2 must define safety relative to other models.
- **Published selection criteria.** The first thing in the Chapter that must be made *public*. Everything so far went to the AI Office under Art. 78.
- **Open weights as a form of access** ("making the model parameters publicly available for download"). Releasing the weights satisfies the access duty. So for open-weight models this duty is met by default (for the released version; not for a helpful-only version that wasn't released).
- **No non-retaliation / safe harbour** for evaluators here. I predicted one. Not in this unit.

**Wandering.**

- **Helpful-only access is a big deal.** The version "with the fewest safety mitigations implemented" is what an attacker would get if safeguards were removed, i.e. what a weight theft or a fine-tuning attack would yield. Giving external evaluators access to it lets them measure the *underlying* capability, not only the safeguarded product. That's the right object for assessing worst-case misuse risk and for assessing how much work the safeguards are doing. "If it exists" spares providers from creating one. A question: if a provider never makes a helpful-only version (safety training fully integrated), point (3) gives the least-mitigated version that does exist, which might be the deployed one.
- **"Most capable… that is made available on the market."** Internal-only models are excluded again: evaluators get the most capable *marketed* version. A lab's more capable internal model isn't covered by this access duty. The internal-deployment pattern continues, drawn by the phrase "made available on the market".
- **CoT access "if available"**, and for (3) "as available". Hidden CoTs (unit 32's (11)) become accessible to evaluators. Providers have argued that raw CoTs are commercially sensitive (distillation risk) and that showing them could pressure them to be "cleaned". The Code's security-measures allowance ("may differ for points (1), (2), and (3)") lets providers protect CoTs more tightly.
- **The "similarly safe or safer" exception** (Appendix 2.2) suggests a *reference model* idea: if there's already a model on the market at least as risky (say, a well-studied model), a new model that's "similarly safe or safer" doesn't need fresh external post-market evaluation. This is the marginal-risk logic I asked about at unit 27, appearing as an exemption mechanism. I suspect Appendix 2 defines "safely derived models" or "similarly safe or safer models" and is used across several Measures as a lightening device. If so, it's one of the most consequential definitions in the Chapter. A fork should treat Appendix 2.2 as load-bearing.
- **Published criteria, unpublished selection.** Criteria for assessing applications are public; the provider still selects. So "independent external evaluators" are chosen by the evaluated party, using criteria it publishes. Independence is bounded by the selection power. Whether "independent" is defined (Glossary?) and whether the AI Office can nominate evaluators: open. Comparing with the AI Act: Art. 92 lets the AI Office conduct evaluations itself or through independent experts. So the AI Office has its own route; this one is the provider's.
- **"Adequate number", "adequate free access", "suitable criteria"**: the openness again carried by adjectives. The purposive rule (i) and guidance fill them.
- Imagining the effect: academics apply under published criteria, get free API access to the frontier model, its CoTs, and a helpful-only variant under security conditions. That's much more access than external researchers routinely had in 2024. If implemented as written, M3.5 is a significant opening for third-party research.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What must providers give external evaluators after release?" M3.5: adequate free access to the most capable marketed version(s); their CoTs if available; the least-mitigated corresponding version (e.g. helpful-only, if it exists) and its CoTs as available; unless the model is "similarly safe or safer" (Appendix 2.2).
- "How are external evaluators chosen?" By the signatory, against published "suitable criteria for assessing applications".
- "Does releasing weights satisfy the access duty?" Yes for the released version: "making the model parameters publicly available for download" is a listed form of access.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Are a provider's most capable *internal* models subject to external access?" No: "made available on the market".
- "What's a 'similarly safe or safer model'?" Appendix 2.2: read it with care.

**Prediction for unit 34.** More on external evaluators: a non-retaliation / safe-harbour commitment (Signatories won't take legal or technical action, such as account bans, against evaluators acting in good faith within the rules), confidentiality allowed, publication of evaluators' findings permitted (maybe with a delay for security review). Then possibly an SME note. Then Commitment 4.
