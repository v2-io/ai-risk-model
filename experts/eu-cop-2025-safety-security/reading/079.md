# Unit 79 · L632–635 · p.28: M10.1 adherence evidence; compile on request

**What it held.** "(1) their processes, measures, and **key decisions** that form part of their systemic risk assessment and mitigation; and (2) **justifications for choices of a particular best practice, state-of-the-art, or other more innovative process or measure** if a Signatory relies upon it for adherence to this Chapter." "Signatories need not collect the information of the third paragraph in one medium or place but may compile it upon the AI Office's request."

**What differed.** Much shorter and more general than my prediction. Two items: key decisions, and justifications for choices of method. No itemised records (no surveys, no trigger-point results). And the record needn't exist as a single file: it can be compiled on request.

**Wandering.**
- **(2) is where recital (a) and (f) become evidential.** Recital (a): at least the state of the art unless risk "conclusively ruled out" with less; recital (f): alternative means with equal or superior outcomes. M10.1(2): keep the justification for whichever you chose. So the burden-of-proof logic of the recitals has a documentary home. A fork asked "how does a signatory show it used the state of the art?" should cite M10.1(2): it keeps justifications for its choices, available to the AI Office on request.
- **"Key decisions"**: undefined, but it plausibly includes acceptance determinations, decisions to proceed, choice of trigger points, deviations from Appendix 4, non-adherence decisions. A records-of-decisions requirement, like board minutes for risk.
- **"Third paragraph"**: the Code counts M10.1's paragraphs again; the adherence-evidence list is "the third paragraph" (paragraph 1: the four detailed descriptions; paragraph 2: retention; paragraph 3: the "Further, Signatories will keep track" list). Hmm: retention and the "Further" sentence are in one paragraph in the canonical text (L630). The count may reflect the PDF layout. Small matter; a fork resolving "third paragraph" should read it as the adherence-evidence list.
- **Compile on request** relieves documentation burden, at the cost that evidence may be reconstructed after the fact. Reconstruction invites hindsight. The Code accepts that.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What evidence of adherence must be kept?" M10.1: processes, measures and key decisions; justifications for choices of best practice, state-of-the-art, or more innovative processes or measures relied on. Need not be in one place; may be compiled on AI Office request.
