# Unit 85 · L709–713 · p.33 (table): the Glossary's last entries

**What it held.**
- **'systemic risk scenario'**: "a scenario in which a systemic risk stemming from a model might materialise. See further Measure 2.2."
- **'systemic risk source'**: "**a factor which alone or in combination with other factors might give rise to systemic risk.** See further Appendix 1.3."
- **'systemic risk tiers'**: "tiers defined in the Framework that **corresponds to a certain level of systemic risk** stemming from a model. Systemic risk tiers are a type of systemic risk acceptance criteria."
- **'use' (of a model)**: "**use of the model by the Signatory or other actors.**"

**What differed.** Scenario is defined almost circularly (unit 25's thinness confirmed). 'use' is the short surprise: it's explicitly **use by the signatory or others**. Every "development, making available on the market, and/or use" in the Chapter thus covers the signatory's own internal use by definition. My internal-deployment notes need that sentence.

**Wandering.**
- **Internal use is in the Glossary.** The triad in M1.1, M1.3, C4, M4.2, M7.1 is defined to include use "by the Signatory". So: Framework coverage, the go/no-go rule ("only proceed with… use… if acceptable"), and Model Report descriptions all reach internal use. What doesn't: the full assessment's timing (before market), the Model Report's existence (before market), periodic clocks (from market), post-market monitoring, external access ("made available on the market"). My final picture of internal deployment, for forks: *the substantive rules reach internal use; the procedural clocks and documents are anchored to market placement.*
- **Tiers correspond to "a certain level of systemic risk"**, but M4.1 requires them to be "defined in terms of model capabilities". So a tier is a risk level, operationalised by capabilities. This is how the Code joins its two risk models (unit 12 and 37): tiers are risk levels by definition, capability thresholds by construction. The bridge is an assumption that capability levels correspond to risk levels, which a signatory's M4.1(3) justification must make good.
- **'systemic risk source'**: a factor, alone or in combination, that "might give rise to systemic risk". A clean definition of a risk factor, with combination built in (risk from conjunctions of factors, e.g. capability + affordance + propensity). For our causal chain's "sources & causes", this is the Code's term: "systemic risk source". Appendix 1.3 will list them.
- **The Glossary is done.** ~30 terms. It changed my reading of: 'appropriate' (demanding), 'insider threats' (includes models and executives), 'non-state external threats' (quantified), 'use' (includes internal), 'model' (aggregated versions), 'including' (minimum), 'systemic risk mitigations' (includes governance), 'state of the art' vs 'best practice'. Eight material corrections from a Glossary placed after the operative text. The Code's design makes body-only reading actively misleading. A fork's first move on any question should be: check the Glossary.
- **Not defined, notably**: 'acceptable' (the pivot word!), 'systemic risk' (Art. 3), 'serious incident' (Art. 3), 'similarly safe or safer' and 'safe reference model' (pointed to Appendix 2), 'trigger points', 'propensity', 'capability', 'affordance'. The absence of 'acceptable' is significant: the Chapter's central predicate is undefined, delegated entirely to signatories' criteria (M4.1) plus the Act's systemic-risk concept.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Does 'use' include the provider's own internal use?" Yes: "use of the model by the Signatory or other actors".
- "What's a systemic risk source?" "A factor which alone or in combination with other factors might give rise to systemic risk" (Appendix 1.3).
- "What are systemic risk tiers?" Levels of systemic risk, defined in the Framework, a type of acceptance criteria; operationalised in terms of capabilities (M4.1).
- Should be asked but might not be: "Does the Code define 'acceptable'?" No.
- Meta-question for forks: "Has the Glossary been checked?" Several body terms are much stronger or broader than they read.

**Prediction for unit 86.** "Appendix 1 Systemic Risks and Other Considerations" (or similar), with 1.1 "Types of risks": a list such as (1) risks to public health, safety, public security; (2) risks to fundamental rights; (3) risks to society as a whole (democratic processes, information ecosystem, environment, critical infrastructure); (4) loss of control; etc. Perhaps the "types" align with Art. 3(65)'s "negative effects on public health, safety, public security, fundamental rights, or the society as a whole".
