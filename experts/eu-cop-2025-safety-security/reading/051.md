# Unit 51 · L423–431 · p.20: Measure 7.3, Documentation of identification, analysis and mitigation (first part)

**What it held.** "Signatories will provide in the Model Report: (1) a description of the results of their systemic risk identification and analysis and any information relevant to understanding them including:
- (a) a description of their systemic risk identification process for risks belonging to the types of risks in Appendix 1.1 (pursuant to Measure 2.1, point (1));
- (b) explanations of **uncertainties and assumptions about how the model would be used and integrated into AI systems**;
- (c) a description of the results of their systemic risk modelling… (Measure 3.3);
- (d) a description of the systemic risks stemming from the model and a justification therefor, including: (i) the systemic risk estimates (Measure 3.4); and (ii) **a comparison between systemic risks with safety and security mitigations implemented and with the model fully elicited** (pursuant to **Appendix 3.2**);" (continues)

**What differed.** I predicted most of the content items. Not predicted:
- **(d)(ii), mitigated vs fully elicited**: the Report must compare the risk *with* mitigations against the risk of the model **fully elicited**. That's the inherent-vs-residual comparison, from risk management. It answers my unit-16 question ("is the assessed risk with or without baseline mitigations?"): both, compared.
- **Appendix 3.2 = elicitation** (presumably "Model elicitation" requirements).
- (a) restricts to "types of risks in Appendix 1.1" for the *process* description, since the specified risks (1.4) don't go through the process.
- (b) puts **integration into AI systems** in the Report as an uncertainty to explain. The model/system boundary yet again.

**Wandering.**
- **Inherent vs residual risk, in the Code's own terms.** "With safety and security mitigations implemented" = residual; "with the model fully elicited" = inherent (or at least, capability-maximal). The comparison shows how much work the mitigations are doing. If residual ≪ inherent, the acceptance determination rests heavily on mitigations, and the safety margin's "mitigations circumvented, deactivated, or subverted" (M4.1) becomes decisive. A careful AI Office reader would focus there. For our lexicon: "fully elicited" is the Code's operational stand-in for inherent capability.
- **"Fully elicited"** is a strong phrase. Full elicitation is an asymptote: you can never be sure you've elicited everything. Appendix 3.2 presumably defines what counts (e.g. state-of-the-art prompting, fine-tuning, scaffolding, tools, best-of-n, inference compute). Read Appendix 3.2 hard.
- **(b) uncertainty about integration.** The signatory must explain what it assumed about how others will build systems around the model. Since many risks depend on scaffolds (agents), this is where the Report admits what it doesn't know. Paired with M7.2(2) (defeaters), the Report has two places for honesty about limits.
- **"Any information relevant to understanding them"**: an open-ended duty to include whatever is needed for comprehension. The AI Office is a reader to be made to understand, not just a box to tick. Purposive.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Does the Model Report compare mitigated and unmitigated risk?" Yes: M7.3(1)(d)(ii), "a comparison between systemic risks with safety and security mitigations implemented and with the model fully elicited (pursuant to Appendix 3.2)".
- "Must providers disclose their assumptions about how the model will be integrated into systems?" M7.3(1)(b): "explanations of uncertainties and assumptions about how the model would be used and integrated into AI systems".

**Prediction for unit 52.** M7.3(1) continued: (e) results of model evaluations, with examples of inputs/outputs; (f) the tiers reached; (g) description of the methods, including elicitation and their limitations; possibly (h) the external evaluators' reports; then (2) safety mitigations and their effectiveness and limitations; (3) security mitigations, the Security Goal and how it's met (and any deviations from Appendix 4).
