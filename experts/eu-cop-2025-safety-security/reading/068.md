# Unit 68 · L551–553 · p.25: M8.3 indicators (5)–(7): reporting channels, whistleblower policy, non-retaliation

**What it held.**
- "(5) internal reporting channels are actively used and reports are acted upon appropriately;"
- "(6) **annually** informing workers of the Signatory's **whistleblower protection policy** and making such policy readily available to workers such as by **publishing it on their website**; and/or"
- "(7) **not retaliating in any form**, including any direct or indirect detrimental action such as termination, demotion, legal action, negative evaluations, or creation of hostile work environments, against any person **publishing or providing information** acquired in the context of work-related activities performed for the Signatory **to competent authorities** about systemic risks stemming from their models for which the person has **reasonable grounds to believe its veracity**."

(Joseph, mid-unit: anything under 800k is fine. Noted; no change to the reading.)

**What differed.** Predicted: reporting channels, informing staff of protections, non-retaliation. What I didn't expect, and what matters most:
- **These are all "examples of indicators… and/or".** Non-retaliation against whistleblowers is an **example indicator** of a healthy risk culture, not a standalone obligation of the Code. The operative duty is to "promote a healthy risk culture and take appropriate measures…". The legal protection comes from the Whistleblower Directive and Art. 87 AI Act (recital (d)), not from the Code itself.
- (7) is drafted *broadly* inside that weak frame: "not retaliating in any form", with an explicit list (termination, demotion, legal action, negative evaluations, hostile environment), and "any person" (not only employees: contractors too, presumably).
- (6) suggests **publishing** the whistleblower policy on the website.

**Wandering.**
- **The strongest wording sits in the weakest position.** (7) is the most protective whistleblower sentence I could imagine in a code, and it's an example indicator. Why? Probably because the Code can't create whistleblower rights that conflict with or duplicate EU law (recital (d): "without prejudice to" the Directive), and because whistleblower protection is a matter of Member States' implementing laws. So the drafters put the content in as an indicator of culture. A fork asked "does the Code protect whistleblowers?" must be precise: the Code lists non-retaliation as an example indicator of a healthy risk culture (M8.3(7)); the legal protection is in Directive (EU) 2019/1937 and Art. 87 AI Act, which the Code doesn't displace.
- **"Publishing or providing information… to competent authorities."** Grammar: does "to competent authorities" attach to both "publishing" and "providing"? If yes, it's odd ("publishing… to competent authorities"). If "publishing" stands alone, then publishing to the world is covered too, a much broader protection, beyond the Directive's tiered approach (internal → authorities → public, with public disclosure protected only in limited cases). The ambiguity is real and consequential. The narrow reading (both attach to authorities) is more consistent with the Directive; the broad reading is the literal parse of "publishing". A fork should flag the ambiguity and not resolve it.
- **"Reasonable grounds to believe its veracity"**: the Directive's standard for protection, imported. Good-faith whistleblowers who turn out wrong are protected.
- **"Information acquired in the context of work-related activities"**: again the Directive's wording. So (7) is largely the Directive's protection restated as a culture indicator.
- **Annually informing workers**: a concrete cadence.
- Thinking of the 2024 controversies (non-disparagement agreements at a lab; the "right to warn" letter), (7)'s "legal action" and "any form" read as aimed at exactly that kind of practice. Lineage plausible, not stated.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Does the Code protect whistleblowers?" M8.3 lists, as example indicators of a healthy risk culture: annual information on the whistleblower policy, published (6); non-retaliation "in any form" against persons providing information to competent authorities about systemic risks (7). The legal protection is the Directive's and Art. 87's (recital (d)).
- Should be asked but might not be: "Does M8.3(7) cover public disclosure?" The phrase "publishing or providing information… to competent authorities" is ambiguous.
- "Is non-retaliation a firm obligation in the Code?" No, an example indicator ("and/or").
