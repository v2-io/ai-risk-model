# Unit 27 · L212–225 · p.12: Measure 3.1, Model-independent information

**What it held.**
- "Signatories will gather model-independent information relevant to the systemic risk."
- "Signatories will search for and gather such information with varying degrees of breadth and depth appropriate for the systemic risk, using methods **such as**:"
  1. "web searches (e.g. making use of open-source intelligence methods…)";
  2. "literature reviews";
  3. "market analyses (e.g. focused on **capabilities of other models available on the market**)";
  4. "reviews of **training data** (e.g. for indications of **data poisoning or tampering**)";
  5. "reviewing and analysing historical incident data and incident databases";
  6. "forecasting of general trends (e.g. forecasts concerning the development of algorithmic efficiency, compute use, data availability, and **energy use**)";
  7. "expert interviews and/or panels; and/or"
  8. "**lay interviews, surveys, community consultations, or other participatory research methods** investigating, e.g. the effects of models on natural persons, including **vulnerable groups**."

**What differed.** I predicted the general kinds (literature, market, incidents, experts, forecasting, stakeholders) and got them. Not predicted:
- **Training data review** listed as *model-independent* information, with **data poisoning or tampering** as the example. A security concern placed in the analysis of systemic risk.
- **OSINT** named.
- **Energy use** among forecast topics.
- **Participatory methods** and **vulnerable groups**: the lay voice from recital (e) gets a concrete method here.
- The list is "such as" and ends "and/or": **no method is mandatory**. The obligation is to gather information "with varying degrees of breadth and depth appropriate for the systemic risk".

**Wandering.**

- **Market analysis of other models' capabilities is the Code's counterfactual baseline, perhaps.** Why look at other models on the market? To know what capability is already available: if every open model can already do X, this model's marginal contribution to risk X is smaller. That's the "marginal risk" or uplift-over-baseline idea (my priming says SB 53 has a counterfactual baseline in its catastrophic-risk definition). The Code doesn't state a marginal-risk rule here; it only lists the market analysis as information. Whether marginal risk is part of *estimation* (M3.4) or *acceptance* (C4) is the question to carry. It's one of the most consequential interpretive questions for open-weight releases.
- **Training data is "model-independent"?** Odd at first: training data is very much about *this* model. Read again: "model-independent" seems to mean "not obtained by querying/evaluating the model", i.e. information that doesn't come from running the model. Training data reviews fit that sense. So "model-independent information" = information not derived from the model's behaviour. Worth a precise note for the lexicon, because the natural reading ("not about the model") is wrong. A fork defining the term should give the operative contrast: M3.1 (not from the model's behaviour) vs M3.2 (model evaluations).
- **Data poisoning** as an analysis concern: if someone has poisoned training data (backdoors, sleeper behaviours), the model's risk changes in ways evaluations may not catch. Its presence here shows the Code has absorbed the 2024 "sleeper agents"/backdoor concern. It also links to the security Commitment (C6): tampering is an attack.
- **Energy use** reappears from the overarching objective's "environmental protection" (unit 2). But here it's an input to *forecasting general trends* (energy as a constraint on scaling), not a risk being assessed. So my unit-2 prediction holds so far: environmental harm isn't operationalised as a systemic risk; energy appears only as a forecasting variable.
- **"Effects of models on natural persons, including vulnerable groups"**: the first appearance of fundamental-rights-ish harm to individuals. The Code's risks so far sounded catastrophic (bio, cyber); participatory research about effects on vulnerable groups hints at a broader risk list (manipulation, discrimination, mental-health effects?). Appendix 1.1's types will tell.
- **Breadth and depth "appropriate for the systemic risk"**: proportionality to the risk again. A high-severity risk gets wider search. An honest Model Report would say what methods were used for each risk.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What is 'model-independent information'?" M3.1's list; operatively, information not derived from evaluating the model's behaviour (it includes training data reviews).
- "Must providers consult affected people?" M3.1(8) lists lay interviews, surveys, community consultations and participatory methods, but only as methods "such as… and/or". Not mandatory.
- "Does the Code use a counterfactual or marginal-risk baseline?" M3.1(3) asks for market analysis of other models' capabilities as information. Whether marginal risk enters estimation or acceptance: open; watch M3.4 and C4.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Does the Code treat data poisoning as a systemic-risk issue?" M3.1(4).

**Prediction for unit 28.** Measure 3.2, Model evaluations. Signatories will conduct at least state-of-the-art model evaluations in the modalities relevant to the systemic risk, to assess capabilities, propensities, affordances and/or effects; using appropriate methods (Q&A sets, benchmarks, red-teaming, human uplift studies, model organisms, simulations, proxy evaluations for classified materials). Evaluations must meet Appendix 3 (rigour: elicitation, appropriate time, compute, access). The four objects of evaluation may be named "capabilities, propensities, affordances, and effects".
