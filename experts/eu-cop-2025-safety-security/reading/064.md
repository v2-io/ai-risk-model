# Unit 64 · L526–527 · p.24: M8.1 presumption (2) ownership and (3) support and monitoring

**What it held.**
- "(2) **Systemic risk ownership:** The responsibility for managing systemic risks from models has been assigned to suitable members of the management body in its executive function **who are also responsible for relevant Signatory core business activities that may give rise to systemic risk**, such as research and product development (e.g. **Head of Research or Head of Product**). The members… have assigned lower-level responsibilities to operational managers who oversee parts of the systemic-risk-producing business activities (e.g. specific research domains or specific products). Depending on the organisational complexity, there may be a cascading responsibility structure."
- "(3) **Systemic risk support and monitoring:** The responsibility for supporting and monitoring…, **including conducting risk assessments**, has been assigned to at least one member of the management body in its executive function (e.g. a **Chief Risk Officer** or a **Vice President, Safety & Security Framework**). This member(s) **must not also be responsible for the Signatory's core business activities that may produce systemic risk** (e.g. research and product development). For Signatories that are SMEs or SMCs, there is at least one individual… tasked with supporting and monitoring…."
- Conversion note: "systemic-riskproducing" in the canonical text has lost a hyphen at a line break ("systemic-risk-producing"). Quote with care.

**What differed.** Predicted: CRO-like role with independence; ownership at executive level. Not predicted:
- **Risk ownership sits with the people who produce the risk**: Head of Research, Head of Product. They own the risk because they run the activities that create it.
- **"Must not"** for separation of duties, the first "must" I've met in operative text. Used for the one thing the drafters wouldn't flex: the risk monitor can't also run research or product.
- **"Conducting risk assessments"** is the CRO-side function, not the owner's. So the people who build the model don't assess its risk; the independent function does.
- An unusual title: "**Vice President, Safety & Security Framework**". It reads like a real role at a real company.

**Wandering.**
- **The design separates producing risk from judging it.** Owners (research/product heads) manage risk and are accountable for it; the support-and-monitoring function (CRO) conducts the risk assessments and must be independent of risk-producing work. That's the classic separation that failed at many banks before 2008, and here it's applied to frontier labs. If taken seriously, it means the person who decides the model is "acceptable" under C4 shouldn't be the person whose product launch depends on it. (The Code doesn't say who makes the C4 determination; M7.2(3) requires describing how the decision was made. But conducting risk assessments sits with the independent function.)
- **"Must" vs "will".** In a document where "will" is the obligation verb, "must not" stands out. It's in the presumption conditions, so strictly it's a condition of the presumption, not an obligation: a signatory that doesn't separate duties loses the presumption and must show its arrangement meets M8.1 otherwise. Still, the word choice shows where the drafters' red line is.
- **"Vice President, Safety & Security Framework"** is a curious example. It names the Code's own artefact (the Framework) in a job title. Perhaps an existing title (I'm not certain any lab uses it), or the drafters inventing a role around their instrument. Either way, a fork shouldn't treat it as a reference to a known person.
- **Cascading responsibility**: operational managers for specific research domains or products. So risk ownership reaches down to team level. For our actor model (G2), the Code's actors inside the provider are: management body (supervisory, executive), committee/independent bodies, risk owners at executive level, operational managers, risk function (CRO), internal and external assurance providers. A detailed intra-organisational actor model, which most sources lack.
- **SMEs**: at least one executive person for monitoring, without the separation-of-duties condition (the SME sentence doesn't repeat "must not"). Small labs may have the CTO also do risk monitoring. Proportionality, at a real cost in independence.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Who owns systemic risk inside a provider, under the Code?" Presumption (2): executive members who run risk-producing activities (e.g. Head of Research, Head of Product), with cascading operational responsibility.
- "Must the risk function be independent?" Presumption (3): the support-and-monitoring member (e.g. CRO) "must not also be responsible for the Signatory's core business activities that may produce systemic risk". SMEs/SMCs: no separation stated.
- "Who conducts risk assessments?" The support-and-monitoring function ("including conducting risk assessments").
- Should be asked but might not be: "Who makes the acceptance determination?" Not said in C8 so far; M7.2(3) requires describing how the decision was made.

**Prediction for unit 65.** (4) Systemic risk assurance: an internal audit function, independent, reporting to the oversight committee; "as appropriate" external assurance (third-party audit). Then perhaps "Signatories will ensure that the management body in its supervisory function oversees…" and the end of M8.1. Then M8.2, Allocation of appropriate resources (human, financial, computational, access to information).
