# Unit 62 · L504–513 · p.23: Measure 8.1, four responsibilities

**What it held.** "Signatories will clearly define responsibilities for managing the systemic risks… across all levels of the organisation. This includes:
1. **Systemic risk oversight:** Overseeing the Signatories' systemic risk assessment and mitigation processes and measures.
2. **Systemic risk ownership:** Managing systemic risks stemming from Signatories' models, including the… processes and measures, and **managing the response to serious incidents**.
3. **Systemic risk support and monitoring:** Supporting and monitoring the… processes and measures.
4. **Systemic risk assurance:** Providing internal and, as appropriate, external assurance about the adequacy of the… processes and measures **to the management body in its supervisory function or another suitable independent body (such as a council or board)**."
Then: "Signatories will allocate these responsibilities, as suitable for the Signatories' governance structure and organisational complexity, across the following levels of their organisation:" (continues)

**What differed.** The four roles match my prediction's names (memory). Notable:
- Ownership includes **managing the response to serious incidents**: incident response is the risk owner's job.
- Assurance reports to "the management body in its supervisory function **or another suitable independent body (such as a council or board)**". An alternative to a corporate board: e.g. a long-term benefit trust, a nonprofit board, a safety council. This accommodates unusual governance (OpenAI's nonprofit board; Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust). I didn't predict that flexibility.
- The level-allocation comes next: roles × levels.

**Wandering.**
- **The four-roles model is the "three lines" model plus oversight**: ownership (first line), support & monitoring (second line: risk function), assurance (third line: internal audit), oversight (board). It's financial-sector governance (EBA/ECB style) imported. The phrase "management body in its supervisory function" is literally EU banking-regulation vocabulary (CRD IV), where the management body has a "management function" and a "supervisory function". A fork should know the lineage: this is bank-governance language. That's a cross-domain borrowing our lexicon should note: "management body", "supervisory function", "risk ownership", "assurance" carry banking-regulation meanings.
- **"As appropriate, external assurance"**: external audit of the risk management system is optional. Compare M1.3's internal Framework assessment. So far, the Code never mandates an external audit of governance, only external *evaluation* of models (Appendix 3.5) and external *security* review (Appendix 4.5).
- **"Council or board"** as independent body: the Code anticipates mission-governance structures. Whether a body controlled by the CEO counts as "independent" is the obvious question; "suitable independent body" sets the test.
- **"Across all levels"** and "as suitable for… governance structure and organisational complexity": flexibility for small firms; but each role must exist somewhere.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What governance roles does the Code require?" M8.1: systemic risk oversight, ownership (including serious-incident response), support and monitoring, and assurance (internal, and as appropriate external, to the management body in its supervisory function or another suitable independent body).
- Should be asked but might not be: "Where does this governance model come from?" EU banking governance vocabulary ("management body in its supervisory function"); three-lines model. Lineage inference, but the phrase is distinctive.
- "Is external audit of the risk process required?" Only "as appropriate" (M8.1(4)).

**Prediction for unit 63.** The levels: (1) the management body in its supervisory function (oversight, e.g. via a risk committee); (2) the management body in its executive function / senior management (ownership: a member responsible for systemic risk); (3) operational teams (ownership at operational level); (4) a risk function / CRO (support and monitoring), with independence; (5) internal audit (assurance), independent. Possibly examples like "Chief Risk Officer".
