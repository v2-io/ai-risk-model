# Unit 9 · L56–61 · p.4: recitals (g) Precautionary Principle, (h) SMEs and SMCs, (i) Interpretation

**What it held.**
- (g) "**Precautionary Principle.**" "The important role of the Precautionary Principle, particularly for systemic risks for which the lack or quality of scientific data does not yet permit a complete assessment. Accordingly,… the extrapolation of current adoption rates and research and development trajectories of models should be taken into account for the **identification** of systemic risks."
- (h) "**Small and medium enterprises ("SMEs") and small mid-cap enterprises ("SMCs").**" "Simplified ways of compliance for SMEs and SMCs, including startups, should be possible as proportionate. For example,… may be exempted from some reporting commitments (Article 56(5) AI Act)." Those exempted "may nonetheless voluntarily adhere to them."
- (i) "**Interpretation.**"
  1. "All Commitments and Measures shall be interpreted in light of the objective to assess and mitigate systemic risks."
  2. "Given the rapid pace of AI development, purposive interpretation focused on systemic risk assessment and mitigation is particularly important to ensure this Chapter remains effective, relevant, and future-proof."
  3. "Any term appearing in this Chapter that is defined in the Glossary for this Chapter has the meaning set forth in that Glossary."
  4. "Appendix 1 should be interpreted, in instances of doubt, in good faith in light of: (1) the probability and severity of harm pursuant to the definition of 'risk' in Article 3(2) AI Act; and (2) the definition of 'systemic risk' in Article 3(65) AI Act."
  5. "This Chapter is to be interpreted in conjunction and in accordance with any AI Office guidance on the AI Act."

**What differed, and the priming check.**
- **The priming phrase is here, but its context differs from the gloss in `CLAUDE.md`.** "In accordance with any AI Office guidance": yes, verbatim, and it's preceded by "in conjunction and". So the Chapter is read *with* the guidance and *in accordance with* it. But the other primed phrase, that the AI Act's definitions "**shall prevail**", is **not** here. Instead (i) says Glossary terms have the Glossary's meaning. That's the opposite direction from what I'd have assumed if I'd skimmed: in this recital, the *Code's own* Glossary governs its defined terms. So "shall prevail" must be elsewhere, probably in the Glossary itself, as a conflict rule (AI Act definitions prevail where the Glossary conflicts with them?). I must not merge the two clauses in memory. A fork asked "which definitions govern the Code?" has to give both: (i)'s Glossary rule, and wherever "shall prevail" turns out to be.
- **The precautionary principle is narrower than I expected.** I predicted the classic formulation (lack of certainty mustn't delay *mitigation*). Instead it's operationalised for **identification**: extrapolate "current adoption rates and research and development trajectories" when identifying systemic risks. So precaution here means *looking ahead* at what models will be able to do and how widely they'll be used, not acting before proof of harm. Mitigation isn't mentioned in (g) at all. That's a significant and easily missed scoping.
- **SMCs.** I predicted SMEs; I didn't predict "small mid-cap enterprises", a newer EU category (I think from the 2025 simplification/omnibus agenda). Relief extends to larger firms than SMEs. And the example of relief is **exemption from some reporting commitments**, not from assessment or mitigation. So the size-relief touches the paperwork, consistent with (c), which made proportionality about documentation detail.

**Wandering.**

- **Purposive interpretation is a strong choice.** "All Commitments and Measures shall be interpreted in light of the objective to assess and mitigate systemic risks." In EU law, teleological interpretation is normal (the CJEU does it constantly), but stating it in the Code, with "shall", means a signatory can't rely on a literal reading that defeats the risk-reduction purpose. This is the counterweight to all the "as appropriate" and "reasonably foreseeable" hedges: when a hedge is ambiguous, read it toward assessing and mitigating risk. Compare SB 53, which my priming says is to be "liberally construed to effectuate its purposes"; that's a parallel purposive rule. Both sources carry anti-loophole interpretive rules. (Cross-source; noted, not verified here.)
- **Note the "shall".** Recitals so far "recognise" things, softly. (i) slips in "shall be interpreted" (twice: Commitments and Measures "shall be interpreted"; terms "ha[ve] the meaning"). The interpretive rules are stated with more force than the principles. That's a drafting choice worth noticing: the Code binds its reader's *reading* more firmly than it binds the principles.
- **Appendix 1 is about risk, and it's interpreted via probability and severity.** So Appendix 1 is probably the list/taxonomy of systemic risks (types, the specified ones, and the sources/characteristics of risk). "In instances of doubt", interpret it "in good faith" in light of (1) risk = combination of probability and severity of harm (Art. 3(2)), and (2) systemic risk's definition (Art. 3(65)). So the Code's risk model is explicitly the AI Act's: risk is "the combination of the probability of an occurrence of harm and the severity of that harm" (Art. 3(2), as I recall the text). That answers the question I raised at (c): yes, severity and probability are the components. A fork mapping "risk" into our lexicon: the Code adopts Art. 3(2)'s definition by reference, at least for Appendix 1.
- **"Good faith."** Appendix 1's interpretation has a good-faith requirement. That's a signal that the authors expected Appendix 1 to be argued over: whether a given risk falls in a category, e.g. whether some manipulation risk is "harmful manipulation" or whether some cyber capability is "cyber offence". Good faith rules out the signatory reading a category narrowly to escape it.
- **The guidance clause gives the AI Office interpretive authority over a voluntary instrument it didn't draft (the Chairs did).** If AI Office guidance on the AI Act changes, the Chapter's meaning moves with it. That's a big dependency: a fork answering "what does the Code require?" should note that the answer is conditional on AI Office guidance, which may post-date the Code. (I recall the Commission published guidelines on GPAI obligations around July 2025. Hold as memory.)
- **Precaution as extrapolation**, imagined at work: a provider identifying systemic risks shouldn't ask only "can today's model do X?" but "given the R&D trajectory, will the next one, and given adoption rates, how many people will be exposed?" That links identification to forecasting, and links to (d)'s "future developments… subject to change": the Code wants forward-looking information and protects it from becoming a promise. These two recitals fit together: forecast freely, without the forecast binding you.
- **SMEs "may nonetheless voluntarily adhere"** to reporting they're exempted from. Voluntary within voluntary. Probably there for reputational signalling.
- **The recitals end here, I think.** Nine principles: (a) lifecycle, (b) contextual, (c) proportionality, (d) integration with law, (e) cooperation, (f) innovation, (g) precaution, (h) SMEs/SMCs, (i) interpretation. That's the interpretive frame for everything after. Strikingly absent: any recital on *transparency to the public*, on *fundamental rights* specifically, or on *independence* of evaluators. If external evaluation appears later, it's not grounded in a principle.

**Questions to keep.**
- "How should an ambiguous Measure be read?" (i): "in light of the objective to assess and mitigate systemic risks"; "purposive interpretation".
- "Whose definitions apply?" (i): the Chapter's Glossary for its defined terms. **And** look for the "shall prevail" clause (not in (i)).
- "Does the Code depend on AI Office guidance?" (i): "to be interpreted in conjunction and in accordance with any AI Office guidance on the AI Act."
- "What does the Code mean by 'risk'?" (i) points Appendix 1 at Art. 3(2): probability and severity of harm.
- "What is the precautionary principle used for in the Code?" (g): the *identification* of systemic risks, via extrapolating adoption rates and R&D trajectories. Should be asked, because readers will assume the broader meaning.
- "Is there relief for small providers?" (h): SMEs and SMCs, "simplified ways of compliance… as proportionate", e.g. exemption from some reporting commitments.
