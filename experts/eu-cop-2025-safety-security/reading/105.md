# Unit 105 · L845–851 · p.38: Appendix 2.2, Similarly safe or safer models

**What it held.** "A model may be considered a similarly safe or safer model with regard to a systemic risk if:
1. Signatories **do not reasonably foresee any materially different systemic risk scenario**… regarding the systemic risk for the model compared to the safe reference model **after conducting systemic risk identification** (pursuant to Commitment 2);
2. the scores of the model on **relevant at least state-of-the-art, light-weight benchmarks** are **all lower than or equal to (within a negligible margin of error)** the scores of the safe reference model. **Minor increases** in capabilities… that result in no material increase in the systemic risk **may be disregarded**. Such benchmarks **must** have been run pursuant to Measure 3.2; and
3. there are **no known differences** in the model's characteristics such as relevant architectural details, capabilities, propensities, affordances, and safety mitigations compared to the safe reference model that could be reasonably foreseen to result in a material increase in the systemic risk, and there are no other reasonable grounds to believe that the systemic risks… are materially increased…."

**What differed.** Predicted: benchmarks no higher within a margin; no new characteristics. Not predicted:
- **Identification still runs.** (1) requires C2 identification and scenario comparison. So "similarly safe or safer" doesn't skip the process; it skips *later* burdens after an initial identification.
- **"Light-weight benchmarks"** suffice for the comparison, but they must be "at least state-of-the-art" and run under M3.2. Cheap tests are enough to establish non-superiority.
- **"All lower than or equal to"**: every relevant benchmark, not an average. That resolves my unit-44 dimensionality worry, for Appendix 2: comparison is on all relevant benchmarks.
- And then a softener: **"minor increases… that result in no material increase in the systemic risk may be disregarded."** "All" is qualified immediately.

**Wandering.**
- **The structure is three-pronged**: scenarios (no materially different ways the risk could materialise), benchmarks (no higher, within negligible margin, minor increases disregardable), characteristics (no known differences that would materially increase risk). Plus a residual "no other reasonable grounds". A well-designed equivalence test. It operationalises marginal risk without the word.
- **"All… lower than or equal to" vs "minor increases… may be disregarded"**: the second sentence partly undoes the first. A model could score higher on several benchmarks and still qualify if each increase is "minor" and doesn't materially increase risk. Who judges "minor" and "material"? The signatory, justified in the Model Report (M7.3(1)(h)). A fork should report both sentences together, never "all lower" alone.
- **"Light-weight benchmarks"** is pragmatic: comparing a new model with a reference on cheap automated benchmarks. Risk: light-weight benchmarks may miss exactly the capability jumps that matter (agentic, long-horizon) and that need heavier evaluation. Prong (3)'s "no known differences… capabilities… propensities, affordances" catches some of that, if known. Under-elicitation would make a model look "similarly safe" when it isn't; the safety margin (M4.1) applies to acceptance, but Appendix 2.2 doesn't mention a margin beyond "negligible margin of error". A question to ask.
- **"Must have been run pursuant to Measure 3.2"**: the second "must" in the Chapter (after M8.1's separation of duties, in a presumption). Again in a definitional condition rather than an obligation.
- **"With regard to a systemic risk"**: per-risk. A model can be similarly safe for CBRN and not for cyber. Then the lightening applies only where it holds: e.g. M7.6(3) exempts six-monthly updates only if similarly safe or safer "for each identified systemic risk"; M10.2 exempts publication only if "a similarly safe or safer model" (per risk? the text there said "if the model is a similarly safe or safer model", ambiguous).
- **Distillation and fine-tunes**: a smaller model distilled from a frontier model will often be "similarly safe or safer" than its parent (lower benchmarks, same characteristics). This is how the Code avoids imposing full burdens on every derivative model.

**Questions to keep.**
- "When is a model 'similarly safe or safer'?" Appendix 2.2, per systemic risk: (1) no materially different risk scenario after C2 identification; (2) scores on relevant, at least state-of-the-art, light-weight benchmarks (run per M3.2) all ≤ the safe reference model's within a negligible margin, minor non-material increases disregardable; (3) no known characteristic differences reasonably foreseen to materially increase the risk, and no other reasonable grounds to believe risk is materially increased.
- "Does a 'similarly safe' model skip risk identification?" No: (1) requires C2 identification.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Does under-elicitation on light-weight benchmarks threaten the comparison?" Yes; Appendix 2.2 has no safety margin beyond "negligible margin of error".
- "What does the 'similarly safe or safer' status lighten?" Seen so far: post-market external access (M3.5); six-monthly Model Report updates (M7.6); public summaries (M10.2); Framework publication (if all models qualify). Possibly external evaluation (Appendix 3.5). Use must be justified in the Model Report (M7.3(1)(h)).

**Prediction for unit 106.** Appendix 3, Model evaluations: "Appendix 3.1 Rigorous model evaluations": Signatories will ensure model evaluations have high scientific and technical rigour (internal/external validity, reproducibility); "Appendix 3.2 Model elicitation": evaluations elicit the model's capabilities, propensities, affordances and effects with at least state-of-the-art techniques, e.g. adequate compute, fine-tuning, scaffolding, best-of-n, prompt engineering, tool access, matching the capacity of plausible actors…; "3.3 Assessing mitigation effectiveness"; "3.4 Qualified model evaluation teams and adequate evaluation access and resources"; "3.5 Independent external model evaluations".
