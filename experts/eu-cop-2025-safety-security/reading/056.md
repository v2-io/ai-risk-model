# Unit 56 · L458–467 · p.21: Measure 7.5, Material changes to the systemic risk landscape

**What it held.** "Signatories will ensure that the Model Report contains information relevant for the AI Office to understand **whether and how the development, making available on the market, and/or use of the model result in material changes in the systemic risk landscape** that are relevant for the implementation of systemic risk assessment and mitigation measures and processes under this Chapter." Examples:
1. "a description of **scaling laws** that suggest novel ways of improving model capabilities;"
2. "a summary of the characteristics of **novel architectures** that materially improve the state of the art in computational efficiency or model capabilities;"
3. "a description of information relevant to assessing the effectiveness of mitigations, e.g. **if the model's chain-of-thought is less legible by humans**; and/or"
4. "a description of training techniques that materially improve the efficiency or feasibility of **distributed training**."

**What differed.** I predicted "changes in the state of the art". The direction is different: it's not what the *world* changed for the signatory, but what *this model's development* changes for the world. The signatory reports its own contributions to the risk landscape: scaling laws, architectures, CoT legibility, distributed training.

**Wandering.**

- **The AI Office as a frontier-research observatory.** These examples ask labs to tell the regulator about research advances that change the game for everyone: new scaling laws (capabilities can grow faster or by new routes), efficient architectures (lower compute for the same capability, which undermines compute thresholds), illegible CoT (monitoring stops working), distributed training (training at scale without a single data centre, which undermines compute governance and the visibility of large training runs). Each is a "landscape" change affecting how *everyone's* risk should be assessed. The AI Office can then update guidance or alert other signatories (M2.1(1)(a)(iii) channel). It's an elegant feedback loop: labs report landscape changes, the AI Office redistributes risk-relevant information.
- **Each example is a threat to an existing governance lever:**
  - scaling laws / architectures → compute thresholds (the AI Act's 10^25 FLOP presumption) lose meaning if capability decouples from compute;
  - illegible CoT → CoT monitoring (M3.5(11), M5.1(8)) loses value;
  - distributed training → monitoring of compute concentration loses value.
  So M7.5 is the Code's early-warning system for the obsolescence of its own instruments. That's a striking self-awareness in the drafting.
- **CoT legibility as a mitigation-effectiveness signal** is the third time CoT appears as a load-bearing safety resource (M3.5(11), M5.1(1)/(8), M7.3(2)(c)'s example, now here). The Code treats legible CoT as a precious and fragile asset. A fork asked "what's the Code's position on chain-of-thought?" should assemble all four.
- **Commercial sensitivity is extreme here.** Novel scaling laws and architectures are trade secrets of the highest value. The Code asks for "a description" / "a summary", protected by Art. 78. Labs will be very careful what they say. The "examples… and/or" framing gives them room.
- **"Material changes in the systemic risk landscape"**: "landscape" is a word our project would want in the lexicon: the population-level state of risk, beyond one model.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Must providers tell the AI Office about research breakthroughs?" M7.5: information on how the model's development, market availability or use "result in material changes in the systemic risk landscape", with examples (scaling laws, novel architectures, CoT legibility, distributed training).
- Should be asked but might not be: "Why does the Code care about distributed training?" It undermines compute-based oversight. That's my inference, but the example makes it plain.
- "What's the Code's view of chain-of-thought?" Assemble M3.5(11), M5.1(1),(8), M7.3(2)(c), M7.5(3).

**Prediction for unit 57.** Measure 7.6, Model Report updates: Signatories will update the Model Report (with a full systemic risk assessment first, per Fig. 2) when they have reasonable grounds to believe the justification for acceptability (M7.2(1)) no longer holds, e.g. the conditions in M7.2(2) occur; on material changes to capabilities, mitigations, or use; and at least every N months (perhaps "every 6 months for the most capable models"?). Third paragraph: perhaps the periodic update rule.
