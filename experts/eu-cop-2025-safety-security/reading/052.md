# Unit 52 · L432–433 · p.20: M7.3(1)(e)–(f), evaluation results and random samples

**What it held.**
- "(e) **all results** of model evaluations relevant to understanding the systemic risks… and descriptions of: (i) how the evaluations were conducted; (ii) the tests and tasks involved…; (iii) how the model evaluations were scored; (iv) how the model was elicited (pursuant to Appendix 3.2); (v) how the scores compare to **human baselines** (where applicable), across the model versions, and across the evaluation settings;"
- "(f) **at least five, random samples** of inputs and outputs from **each relevant model evaluation**, such as completions, generations, and/or **trajectories**, to facilitate **independent interpretation** of the model evaluation results…. If particular trajectories materially inform the understanding of a systemic risk, such trajectories will also be provided. Further, Signatories will provide a sufficiently large number of random samples… **if subsequently asked by the AI Office**;"

**What differed.** I predicted evaluation results and "examples of inputs and outputs". Not predicted:
- **A number**: at least five random samples per relevant evaluation. The Code's numbers so far were deadlines (4 weeks, 2 weeks, 5 business days, 12 months, 30 business days); this is the first number about *evidence*.
- **Random**, not curated: samples chosen at random, so the provider can't pick flattering examples. And separately, the materially informative trajectories (cherry-picked by relevance, for the right reason).
- **"All results"** relevant: not a selection.
- **Human baselines** and comparisons across versions and settings.

**Wandering.**
- **Random sampling is an anti-cherry-picking device**, and an elegant one. Five random samples won't establish a statistic, but they let the AI Office see what the evaluation actually looked like: whether the tasks are meaningful, whether the scoring is sensible, whether the model's failures are real or artefacts (e.g. refusals counted as incapability). "To facilitate independent interpretation": the AI Office (or its experts) can read transcripts and disagree with the provider's reading. This is a direct remedy for the known problem of evaluations reported only as scores.
- **"Trajectories"** is agentic vocabulary: multi-step runs with tool calls. The Code expects agentic evaluations and wants their transcripts.
- **"If particular trajectories materially inform the understanding"**: e.g. the one run where the model attempted to disable monitoring, or where it found a novel exploit. Those must be provided even if not randomly sampled. That's the "notable incident in evaluation" reporting duty. For loss of control, this is where alarming evaluation transcripts would land with the regulator.
- **"All results… relevant"**: combined with M3.3(3) and M4.1's "at least the information gathered", the Code consistently blocks selective reporting.
- **Human baselines "where applicable"**: uplift and capability claims need comparison to humans (an expert virologist's score vs the model's). The Code knows that a score without a baseline is uninterpretable.
- **The AI Office can ask for more samples.** A follow-up power within the Code. So the five is a floor for the default submission.
- The comma in "at least five, random samples" is odd ("at least five random samples" would be standard). Maybe drafting, maybe meant "at least five, [and they must be] random". No effect on meaning that I can see.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What evaluation evidence goes in the Model Report?" M7.3(1)(e)–(f): "all results" relevant, with methods, tasks, scoring, elicitation, human baselines; "at least five, random samples of inputs and outputs from each relevant model evaluation", plus materially informative trajectories; more on AI Office request.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Can providers choose which evaluation transcripts to show?" No: samples must be random (plus materially informative trajectories).
