# Unit 92 · L755–762 · p.35: Appendix 1.2.2, Contributing characteristics (1)–(6)

**What it held.**
1. "**Capability-dependent:** The risk increases with model capabilities or may emerge at the frontier of model capabilities."
2. "**Reach-dependent:** The risk increases with model reach."
3. "**High velocity:** The risk can materialise rapidly, potentially outpacing mitigations."
4. "**Compounding or cascading:** The risk can trigger other systemic risks or chain reactions."
5. "**Difficult or impossible to reverse:** Once materialised, the risk creates persistent changes that require extraordinary effort, resources, or time to remediate, or are permanently irreversible."
6. "**Asymmetric impact:** A small number of actors or events can trigger the materialisation of the risk, causing disproportionate impact relative to the number of actors or events."

**What differed.** Predicted: velocity, cascading, irreversibility, scale/reach, asymmetry. Not predicted as such: **capability-dependent** as a *contributing* characteristic (I'd thought capability-specificity was all in the essential set), and **reach** reappearing here, answering my unit-91 note: "due to their reach" from Art. 3(65) lands in the contributing characteristics.

**Wandering.**
- **A clean risk-property vocabulary.** Capability-dependent, reach-dependent, high velocity, compounding/cascading, irreversible, asymmetric. These are classic systemic-risk properties from finance and catastrophe studies (contagion, irreversibility, fat tails) adapted to AI. For our lexicon (G3), each is a named property with a one-line definition from the source. The project could adopt them nearly as-is, attributed.
- **"Potentially outpacing mitigations"** defines velocity relative to the response, not in absolute time. That's the right definition: a risk is fast if it beats the defence.
- **Asymmetric impact** is the misuse problem in one sentence: a few bad actors, huge harm. It's what makes CBRN and cyber systemic despite low base rates.
- **Capability-dependent vs "specific to high-impact capabilities"**: essential (1.2.1) requires specificity to high-impact capabilities; contributing (1.2.2(1)) adds that the risk *increases* with capabilities or *emerges* at the frontier. So risks that scale with capability are more systemic than risks that merely exist at the frontier. Graded.
- **Irreversibility "permanently irreversible"**: loss of control and extinction-type outcomes sit here, without naming them.
- The list may continue (my predicted lack of mitigations, covertness, vulnerable groups…).
