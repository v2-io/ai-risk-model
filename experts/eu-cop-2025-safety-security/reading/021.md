# Unit 21 · L158–160 · p.10: Measure 1.4, Framework notifications

**What it held.** "Signatories will provide the AI Office with (unredacted) access to their Framework, and updates thereof, within **five business days** of either being confirmed."

**What differed.**
- I predicted "(unredacted) access" (memory, probably) and "two weeks". It's **five business days**.
- It's **access**, not submission: the AI Office gets access to the document. In practice the same, but "access" admits a reading room / portal rather than a copy.
- It covers the **Framework and its updates**, **not the Framework assessment**. So the adequacy/adherence review, with its log of non-adherence and remediation plans (unit 20), isn't among the things notified here. My unit-20 question gets a provisional answer: the non-adherence record isn't sent to the AI Office by M1.4. It may surface elsewhere (C7's Model Report? an AI Office request?), or it may stay internal.

**Wandering.**
- The **parenthetical "(unredacted)"** is telling. Someone must have proposed that providers could redact commercially sensitive parts before giving the Framework to the regulator. The parentheses look like a compromise in typography: the word is there but bracketed. Its effect is still full: the AI Office sees the whole Framework. Confidentiality is protected by Art. 78 (recital (d)), not by redaction.
- **Notification, not approval** (confirmed now, as I guessed at unit 11). Nothing in C1 lets the AI Office reject a Framework. Its influence comes through the general enforcement of Art. 55 and through "AI Office guidance" (recital (i)). A fork asked "does the AI Office approve Frameworks?" should say no, per C1: it receives unredacted access within five business days of confirmation.
- **The changelog travels with the updates**, so the AI Office sees *how and why* the Framework changed. A Framework weakened over time would show it in the changelog. That's the one channel by which the substance of a Framework assessment reaches the regulator, indirectly: if the assessment leads to an update, the changelog explains it. If an assessment finds non-adherence and leads to remediation but no Framework change, the regulator may never hear of it. Worth stating precisely.
- **Commitment 1 ends here**: four Measures. In summary: create (M1.1; contents, deadline 4 weeks after Art. 52(1) notification / 2 weeks before market), implement (M1.2; continuous light layer + full four-step process), update (M1.3; assessments every 12 months or on grounds; adequacy + adherence; changelog), notify (M1.4; unredacted access within five business days). It's a closed, auditable loop at firm level.
- **No public disclosure in C1.** No published Framework, no public summary. (Company frameworks are usually published voluntarily; SB 53, I believe, requires publication of a frontier AI framework. That would be a sharp contrast if true. Not asserted from here.)

**Questions to keep.**
- "Does the AI Office see the Framework?" M1.4: "(unredacted) access… within five business days" of confirmation, for the Framework and every update.
- "Does the AI Office see the Framework assessment?" Not under M1.4. Watch.
- "Must the Framework be published?" Not in C1.
