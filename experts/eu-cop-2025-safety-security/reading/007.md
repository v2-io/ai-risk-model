# Unit 7 · L54 · p.4: recital (e), Principle of Cooperation

**What it held.**
- Assessment and mitigation "merit significant investment of time and resources", and there are "advantages of collaborative efficiency, e.g. by sharing model evaluations methods and/or infrastructure."
- "Cooperation with **licensees, downstream modifiers, and downstream providers**" in assessment and mitigation, and "engaging expert or lay representatives of civil society, academia, and other relevant stakeholders in understanding the model effects."
- Cooperation "may involve entering into agreements to share information…, while ensuring proportionate protection of sensitive information and compliance with applicable Union law."
- "Cooperating with the AI Office (Article 53(3) AI Act) to foster collaboration between providers…, researchers, and regulatory bodies".

**What differed.** I expected cooperation mainly with the AI Office. Instead its first move is **between providers** (shared evaluation methods and infrastructure), justified by *cost* ("significant investment"). The AI Office comes last. And three downstream actor kinds are named that I hadn't anticipated as distinct: *licensees*, *downstream modifiers*, *downstream providers*.

**Wandering.**

- **Three downstream actors.** Licensees (who take the model under licence, e.g. weights or API terms), downstream modifiers (who change the model: fine-tune, merge, quantise), and downstream providers (who integrate the model into their own AI system or model and place it on the market; the AI Act's "downstream provider" is defined in Art. 3(68), as I recall). For our actor vocabulary these are three roles, possibly held by one party. "Downstream modifier" matters most for open-weight models and for the AI Act's question of when a modifier *becomes* a provider of a new model. A fork asked about the Code's actor model should list these three plus "stakeholders likely to be affected" from (a), "expert or lay representatives of civil society, academia" here, and the AI Office. I expect the glossary or a Measure to define at least "downstream modifier".
- **"Expert or lay representatives."** Lay people are named as having a role "in understanding the model effects". That's an unusual inclusion in a technical risk text. It suggests a model of risk where affected publics are a source of knowledge about effects, not only an object of protection. But it's "recognise the importance of", in a recital. Watch whether any Measure gives lay representatives an actual channel. My bet: weakly, if at all.
- **Collaboration between competitors** has an antitrust shadow. "Compliance with applicable Union law" in the information-sharing clause probably points at competition law as well as data protection. Shared evaluation infrastructure: e.g. common dangerous-capability benchmarks or shared CBRN red-team resources, as industry bodies (Frontier Model Forum) do. A cross-corpus tie: this is the Code endorsing exactly what the FMF exists for. Correlation alert again: if providers share evaluation *methods*, their results become correlated, and agreement between their Model Reports is less independent evidence.
- **Art. 53(3) AI Act**: if I recall, it's the duty of providers to cooperate with the Commission and national authorities. So the Code anchors cooperation with the AI Office to a legal duty, while the other kinds of cooperation are recognised as good practice. The recital mixes a legal obligation with an aspiration in one sentence. A fork should keep them apart.
- Imagining it: a mid-sized provider without a bio red-team can't afford one. (e) says sharing evaluation infrastructure is efficient; combined with (a)'s "at least the state of the art", shared infrastructure may be how smaller signatories reach the state of the art. That's (e) doing quiet SME work without naming SMEs.
- The pace of the recitals: each one is a balance of carrot and burden, and each ends with something for the AI Office. The Code keeps placing the regulator in the frame.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Which downstream actors does the Code name?" (e): licensees, downstream modifiers, downstream providers; plus stakeholders likely to be affected (a), civil society, academia, lay representatives (e).
- "Does the Code encourage providers to share evaluations?" (e): "sharing model evaluations methods and/or infrastructure", with information-sharing agreements and "proportionate protection of sensitive information". (Ask whether a Measure operationalises this.)
- Should be asked but might not be: "If providers share evaluation methods, are their safety results independent?" (e) makes correlation plausible.

**Prediction for unit 8.** (f): "Principle of Innovation in AI Safety and Security" is my strong guess: the Code shouldn't freeze practice; signatories may use alternative methods if they achieve equal or better outcomes; advance the science of evaluation. Then perhaps (g) about SMEs, (h) about precaution, and the interpretation recital.
