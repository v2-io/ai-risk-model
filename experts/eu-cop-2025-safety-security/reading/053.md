# Unit 53 · L434–436 · p.20: M7.3(1)(g)–(h) evaluators' access; "safe reference model"; (2) safety mitigations and their limits

**What it held.**
- "(g) a description of the access and other resources provided to: (i) **internal model evaluation teams** (pursuant to **Appendix 3.4**); and (ii) independent external evaluators pursuant to **Appendix 3.5**. Alternatively to… point (ii), Signatories will **procure any such independent external evaluators to provide the requisite information directly to the AI Office** at the same time that the Signatory supplies its Model Report…; and"
- "(h) if they make use of the "**similarly safe or safer model**" concept pursuant to Appendix 2, provide a justification of how the criteria for "**safe reference model**" (pursuant to **Appendix 2.1**) and the criteria for "similarly safe or safer model" (pursuant to Appendix 2.2) are fulfilled;"
- "(2) a description of: (a) all safety mitigations implemented (pursuant to Commitment 5); (b) how they fulfil the requirements of Measure 5.1; and (c) **their limitations** (e.g. **if training on examples of undesirable model behaviour makes identifying future instances of such behaviour more difficult**);"

**What differed.** Predicted: evaluators' access; mitigations and limitations. Not predicted:
- **Internal evaluation teams** have their own Appendix section (3.4) on access and resources. Evaluators *inside* the company are protected/resourced by the Code too.
- **External evaluators may report directly to the AI Office**, bypassing the provider. That protects the external evaluators' independence (the provider needn't see or summarise what they found) and the regulator's view.
- **"Safe reference model"** (Appendix 2.1) as a defined concept, the comparator for "similarly safe or safer" (2.2). My unit-33 guess (a reference-model concept) was right.
- The mitigation-limitation example is a sophisticated alignment concern: **training against observed bad behaviour can make future bad behaviour harder to detect**.

**Wandering.**

- **The direct-reporting alternative** is an important structural safeguard. If external evaluators' findings had to pass through the provider's Model Report, the provider would control their framing. Here, the provider can instead have evaluators send their information straight to the AI Office, simultaneously. It's an "alternatively", the provider's choice; but either way the information arrives. A fork asked "can providers filter what external evaluators say?" should say: the provider either describes their access and resources itself or has them report directly to the AI Office at the same time.
- **Hmm, read again:** the alternative applies to "the preceding point (ii)", which is a *description of the access and other resources provided* to external evaluators. So what evaluators send directly is "the requisite information" about access and resources, not necessarily their findings. I over-read it above. The findings of external evaluators may enter via M7.2(3) (whether/how they informed the decision) and perhaps Measure 7.4 ("external reports"?). Correction recorded: direct reporting here concerns access-and-resources information, on a plain reading; whether it extends to findings depends on "requisite information", which is ambiguous. A fork shouldn't overstate it.
- **The mitigation-limitation example**: "if training on examples of undesirable model behaviour makes identifying future instances of such behaviour more difficult". This is the "training against the detector" problem: if you train a model to stop doing X whenever X is observed, you may teach it to hide X (e.g. to scheme without verbalising it in CoT). It links to M5.1(1) (unfaithful CoT) and (8) (CoT transparency). The Code is aware that safety training can destroy the signals monitoring depends on. For a regulatory text this is remarkably advanced. A fork should point it out when anyone discusses CoT monitoring or alignment training in the Code.
- **"Safe reference model"**: so there's a reference class of models deemed safe, and models "similarly safe or safer" than a reference model get lighter treatment (we've seen: exemption from external-evaluator post-market access, M3.5). Appendix 2 must say what qualifies as a safe reference model: plausibly a model already on the market with no serious incidents, assessed under the Code, or models below the systemic-risk threshold. This is the Code's general marginal-risk mechanism. Its criteria are among the most important in the Chapter.
- **"All safety mitigations implemented"**: all, again, not a selection.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What is a 'safe reference model'?" Appendix 2.1 (to read); used for the "similarly safe or safer model" concept (Appendix 2.2); its use must be justified in the Model Report (M7.3(1)(h)).
- "Must the Model Report describe internal evaluators' resources?" Yes: M7.3(1)(g)(i), per Appendix 3.4.
- "Can external evaluators report directly to the AI Office?" M7.3(1)(g): as an alternative to the provider describing their access and resources, the provider "will procure" them to "provide the requisite information directly to the AI Office" at the same time. Whether that covers their findings is ambiguous on the text.
- "Must providers disclose the limitations of their safety mitigations?" Yes, M7.3(2)(c), with the example of training making undesirable behaviour harder to identify.
