# Unit 112 · L906–910 · p.40: Appendix 3.4, what evaluation teams get (1)–(3)

**What it held.** "Model evaluation teams will be provided with:
1. adequate access to the model…, including, as appropriate, access to **model activations, gradients, logits (or other forms of raw model outputs), chains-of-thought**, and/or other technical details, and access to the model version(s) **with the fewest safety mitigations** implemented (such as a helpful-only model version, if it exists). Regarding the adequacy of heightened model access…, Signatories will take into account the potential risks to model security… and implement appropriate security measures…;
2. information, including model specifications (including the system prompt), relevant training data, test sets, and past model evaluation results, as appropriate for: (a) the systemic risk; and (b) the model evaluation method;
3. time to competently design and/or adapt, debug, execute, and analyse the model evaluations…, as appropriate for: (a) the systemic risk; and (b) the model evaluation method and its novelty. **For example, a period of at least 20 business days is appropriate for most systemic risks and model evaluation methods**; and" (continues)

**What differed.** My prediction had "at least 20 business days" verbatim. That's training memory, not inference. **Priming note**: I held this number before reading it, so my confidence about the remaining Appendix 3 numbers is suspect in the same way. Not predicted: **white-box access** (activations, gradients) as part of "adequate access".

**Wandering.**
- **White-box access for internal evaluators**: activations and gradients enable interpretability-based evaluation (probing for deception, representation engineering). That's the frontier of evaluation science named as part of what evaluation teams get "as appropriate". For *internal* teams, this is mostly organisational (do evaluation teams get research-level access?). "Model evaluation teams" here may include external evaluators too: Appendix 3.4's title says "teams responsible for conducting the model evaluations"; 3.5 will probably refer back. If external evaluators get gradients and activations, that's extraordinary access. A fork should check 3.5's wording.
- **Security tension acknowledged**: heightened access creates exfiltration risk (more people with weight-level access). The Code asks for appropriate security measures, not for withholding access.
- **"At least 20 business days" is an example**, "appropriate for most systemic risks and model evaluation methods". About four weeks of evaluation time. It answers the 2024 complaint that external testers got days, not weeks, before launches. As an *example*, it's not mandatory; but as the Code's stated benchmark of appropriateness, deviations would need justification.
- **Information**: model spec with system prompt, relevant training data, test sets, past results. Evaluators need context to evaluate; the Code says so.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What access must evaluation teams get?" Appendix 3.4(1): adequate access incl., as appropriate, activations, gradients, logits/raw outputs, chains-of-thought, and the least-mitigated version (e.g. helpful-only); with appropriate security measures.
- "How long should evaluations take?" Appendix 3.4(3): time appropriate to risk and method; "for example, a period of at least 20 business days is appropriate for most systemic risks and model evaluation methods".
- Should be asked but might not be: "Does Appendix 3.4 apply to external evaluators too?" Check 3.5.

**Prediction for unit 113.** (4) compute budget and engineering support / "staffing"; then Appendix 3.5 Independent external model evaluations: "In addition to internal evaluations, Signatories will ensure that adequately qualified independent external evaluators conduct model evaluations… unless: (1) the model is similarly safe or safer…; or (2) despite reasonable efforts (e.g. public calls), the Signatory fails to identify an adequately qualified evaluator…". Plus external evaluators' access/resources as per 3.4, and security/confidentiality terms, and allowing publication.

# Unit 113 · L911 · p.40: Appendix 3.4 (4), compute, staffing, engineering

**What it held.** "(4) (a) adequate compute budgets, including to allow for sufficiently long model evaluation runs, parallel execution, and re-runs; (b) adequate staffing; and (c) adequate engineering budgets and support, including to inspect model evaluation results to identify and fix **software bugs or model refusals which might lead to artificially lowered capability estimates**. With respect to point (b), **if Signatories engage independent external evaluators**, they may rely on the latter's assurances as to whether their staffing is adequate."

**What differed.** Compute, staffing, engineering predicted. New: the explicit concern that **bugs and refusals artificially lower capability estimates**, i.e. that a refusal in an eval is misread as incapability. And confirmation that Appendix 3.4 covers **external evaluators** too (the staffing sentence refers to them), answering unit 112's question.

**Wandering.**
- **Refusals as measurement error.** A safety-trained model that refuses a dangerous-capability eval scores low, and a careless report says "not capable". The Code names this failure mode and requires engineering support to find and fix it. It's the same concern as under-elicitation, at the level of evaluation hygiene. A fork asked "does the Code worry about evaluations underestimating capabilities?" can cite this, Appendix 3.2 (under-elicitation, sandbagging), and the Glossary's internal validity list.
- **Appendix 3.4 applies to external evaluators** too (since (b) mentions them). So external evaluators engaged by the signatory should get the 3.4 access (including, as appropriate, activations, gradients, logits, CoT, helpful-only version), information, at least ~20 business days, and compute. That's a strong external-evaluation regime, if implemented.

**Questions to keep.** "Must providers resource evaluations?" Appendix 3.4(4): adequate compute (long runs, parallel, re-runs), staffing, engineering support incl. fixing bugs/refusals that would artificially lower capability estimates. Applies to external evaluators too (staffing assurance clause).

**Prediction for unit 114.** Appendix 3.5, Independent external model evaluations: required unless the model is similarly safe or safer, or no qualified evaluator found despite effort; qualification criteria for external evaluators (expertise per 3.4, technical capacity, security protocols, independence per Glossary); provider must not unduly interfere; evaluators may publish (cf. M7.4).
