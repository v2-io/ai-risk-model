# Unit 34 · L288–296 · p.14: M3.5, use of evaluators' results; non-retaliation and its conditions (1)–(4)

**What it held.**
- "Signatories will only access, store, and/or analyse evaluation results from independent external evaluators to assess and mitigate systemic risk from the model. In particular, Signatories **refrain from training their models on the inputs and/or outputs from such test runs without express permission** from the evaluators."
- "Additionally, Signatories will **not take any legal or technical retaliation** against the independent external evaluators as a consequence of their **testing and/or publication of findings** as long as the evaluators:
  - (1) do not intentionally disrupt model availability through the testing, unless expressly permitted;
  - (2) do not intentionally access, modify, and/or use sensitive or confidential user data in violation of Union law, and if evaluators do access such data, collect only what is necessary, refrain from disseminating it, and delete it as soon as legally feasible;
  - (3) do not intentionally use their access for activities that pose a significant risk to public safety and security;
  - (4) do not use findings to threaten Signatories, users, or other actors in the value chain, provided that disclosure under pre-agreed policies and timelines will not be counted as such coercion; and" (continues)

**What differed.** The non-retaliation commitment is here (predicted). Not predicted:
- It explicitly protects **publication of findings**, not only testing. Evaluators may publish, and the provider won't retaliate legally or technically.
- **A use restriction on the provider**: evaluation results only for risk assessment and mitigation, and **no training on evaluators' test inputs/outputs** without permission. That protects the evaluations from being "trained against", which would contaminate future evaluations (benchmark leakage), and protects evaluators' IP.
- The conditions read like a responsible-disclosure code from cybersecurity: don't disrupt availability, minimise user data, don't endanger public safety, don't extort.

**Wandering.**

- **Not training on the test runs** is a quietly important evaluation-validity provision. If a provider trained on red-teamers' prompts, the next evaluation would measure memorised refusals, not robustness. So this protects the *validity* of future external evaluations, not only the evaluators' rights. A fork asked "why can't providers train on evaluators' data?" should give both reasons, while marking the validity reason as my inference (the text says only "refrain").
- **Legal *or technical* retaliation.** Technical retaliation means account bans, rate limiting, API key revocation. That's what actually happens to independent researchers who probe ToS limits. Naming it closes the usual route of quiet exclusion. This matches the 2024 open-letter campaign for a "safe harbor for independent AI evaluation" (as I recall, signed by many researchers). Lineage: likely, not stated.
- **"Intentionally"** in (1)–(3) protects the honest mistake: an evaluator who accidentally disrupts availability or touches user data unintentionally keeps protection (subject to (2)'s minimisation and deletion duties). But (4) has no "intentionally": using findings to threaten is excluded regardless.
- **(4)'s proviso** is precise: disclosure "under pre-agreed policies and timelines" isn't coercion. So coordinated disclosure ("we'll publish in 90 days") is protected; "pay us or we publish" isn't. Standard vulnerability-disclosure ethics imported.
- **(3) "significant risk to public safety and security"**: an evaluator who, say, synthesises a real pathogen protocol and posts it loses protection. The line is drawn at public harm, not at embarrassment to the provider.
- **Who judges whether a condition was breached?** The provider, presumably, at first. So a provider could claim a condition was breached and retaliate. The protection is only as strong as the AI Office's willingness to adjudicate. Still: written into a compliance instrument, the commitment gives evaluators something to point to.
- The protections are attached to "independent external evaluators" given access under M3.5. Does it extend to researchers who probe the public product without being selected? The text says "the independent external evaluators", referring back to those given access. Unselected researchers aren't covered, on this reading. A fork should be careful on this point.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Can providers retaliate against external evaluators who publish findings?" M3.5: no "legal or technical retaliation… as a consequence of their testing and/or publication of findings", as long as conditions (1)–(5?) hold.
- "Can providers train on evaluators' test data?" Not "without express permission from the evaluators".
- Should be asked but might not be: "Does the non-retaliation protection cover researchers who weren't selected under M3.5?" On the text, it attaches to "the independent external evaluators" given access.
- "Is coordinated disclosure protected?" Yes, (4)'s proviso.

**Prediction for unit 35.** Condition (5): perhaps "adhere to the Signatory's publicly available process for responsible disclosure" or "do not violate security measures". Then possibly an exemption for SMEs or for open-weight models; then Commitment 4, Systemic risk acceptance determination, with LEGAL TEXT Art. 55(1)(b).
