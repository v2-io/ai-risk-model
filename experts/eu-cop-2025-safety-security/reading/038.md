# Unit 38 · L321–323 · p.16: M4.1(3) justification; applying the criteria with a safety margin

**What it held.**
- "(3) **justify how** the use of these tiers and/or other criteria pursuant to point (2) **ensures** that each identified systemic risk… and the overall systemic risk are acceptable."
- "Signatories will apply the systemic risk acceptance criteria to each identified systemic risk…, **incorporating a safety margin** (as specified in the following paragraph), to determine whether each identified systemic risk… and the overall systemic risk are acceptable. This acceptance determination will take into account at least the information gathered via systemic risk identification and analysis (pursuant to Commitments 2 and 3)."

**What differed.** The safety margin arrives (predicted), and its specification is deferred to the next paragraph. Not predicted: (3) requires a justification that the criteria *ensure* acceptability. That's a stronger demand than describing them. It asks the signatory to argue that its thresholds are set right.

**Wandering.**
- **The justification is the crux of the whole acceptability architecture.** The Code doesn't say what's acceptable. It requires the signatory to justify that its criteria "ensure" acceptability. So the substance of "acceptable" lives in each signatory's argument, and the AI Office's ability to scrutinise it. What would such a justification look like? "Our Tier 2 CBRN threshold ensures acceptability because, below it, the model's uplift is smaller than freely available resources (market analysis), so the marginal risk is negligible" — a marginal-risk argument. Or "because below it, the expected harm is below X deaths/year". The Code invites either. This is where risk-acceptance philosophy (ALARP, societal risk curves, etc.) would enter, and the Code is silent on which philosophy.
- **Circularity worry.** "Acceptable" is never defined independently. Criteria must ensure risks are acceptable; acceptability is determined by applying the criteria. Without an external standard, it's circular. The external anchors are: AI Act's notion of systemic risk (Art. 3(65)), purposive interpretation, state of the art, and the AI Office's view. A fork asked "what does 'acceptable' mean in the Code?" should say: it's not defined in substance; it's determined by signatory-defined, justified criteria applied with a safety margin, and anchored by those external constraints. (I'll check the Glossary for "acceptable".)
- **Safety margin incorporated into the application**, not into the criteria. That's a design choice: the criteria (thresholds) stay as stated, and when applying them, the signatory leaves room for error. Equivalent to treating a model as having reached a tier if it's *close* to it. I expect the next paragraph to say the margin must account for (a) uncertainty in evaluations / elicitation gaps, (b) post-deployment capability gains (fine-tuning, scaffolding, tools), (c) mitigation failure.
- **"At least the information gathered via… Commitments 2 and 3."** The determination can't cherry-pick. Same anti-cherry-picking pattern as M3.3(3).

**Questions to keep.**
- "Must signatories justify their acceptance thresholds?" Yes: M4.1(3), "justify how the use of these tiers and/or other criteria… ensures that each… and the overall systemic risk are acceptable".
- "Does the Code define 'acceptable'?" Not in substance so far. Check Glossary.
- "Is a safety margin required?" Yes, "incorporating a safety margin" when applying the criteria (specified next).

**Prediction for unit 39.** The safety margin paragraph: the margin "will be appropriate to the systemic risk" and "take into account potential limitations, changes, and uncertainties of systemic risk sources (e.g. capability improvements after the time of assessment), systemic risk assessment (e.g. under-elicitation of evaluations or historical accuracy of similar assessments), and the effectiveness of mitigations (e.g. mitigations being circumvented, deactivated, or subverted)". Then Measure 4.2.
