# Unit 12 · L84–98 · p.6: Measure 1.1, Creating the Framework (first part)

**What it held.**
- "Signatories **will** create a state-of-the-art Framework, taking into account the models they are **developing, making available on the market, and/or using**."
- "The Framework will contain a high-level description of implemented **and planned** processes and measures for systemic risk assessment and mitigation to adhere to this Chapter."
- "In addition", it will contain:
  - (1) "a description and justification of the **trigger points** and their usage, at which the Signatories will conduct additional **lighter-touch model evaluations** along the entire model lifecycle, as specified in Measure 1.2, second paragraph, point (1)(a)";
  - (2) "for the Signatories' determination of whether systemic risk is considered acceptable, as specified in Commitment 4":
    - (a) "a description and justification of the **systemic risk acceptance criteria, including the systemic risk tiers**, and their usage as specified in Measure 4.1";
    - (b) "a high-level description of what safety and security mitigations Signatories would need to implement once each systemic risk tier is reached;"
- The unit stops mid-list (the tool's 150-word join hit the end of what it could join; the list continues).

**What differed.**
- **The verb is "will"**, not "commit to" or "shall". Commitments say "commit to"; Measures say "will". Descriptive-future register for obligations: "Signatories will create". A fork should know that in this Code "will" is the obligation verb in Measures, not a prediction.
- **"Using"**: the Framework covers models a signatory is *using*, not only developing or putting on the market. So **internal deployment** is in scope. I didn't expect that at the very start. It's important for loss-of-control scenarios (a model used internally to do AI R&D is the classic worry).
- **Trigger points for *lighter-touch* evaluations.** I predicted "trigger points for assessments". The text distinguishes **lighter-touch model evaluations** at trigger points along the lifecycle from (presumably) fuller assessments. So there are at least two grades of evaluation: light checks during development at trigger points, and a full systemic risk assessment (probably before placing on the market). My memory had "trigger points" but not the two grades.
- **Commitment 4 is acceptance determination**, Measure 4.1 holds the acceptance criteria and tiers. Numbered landmarks: C1 Framework, C4 acceptance, C8 includes risk culture (8.3).
- I predicted acceptance criteria and tiers (memory). I didn't predict that the Framework must state, in advance, **what mitigations would be needed once each tier is reached**. That's the if-then structure of company frameworks (Anthropic's ASL-style "if capability X, then safeguards Y"). The Code requires every signatory's Framework to have that if-then shape.

**Wandering.**

- **"Description and justification."** Twice. The Framework must *justify* its trigger points and its acceptance criteria, not just state them. A justification is something the AI Office can assess and disagree with. That partly answers my unit-11 question ("who sets acceptability?"): the signatory sets the criteria, but must justify them in a document the regulator reads. Self-set, but answerable.
- **Tiers, reached.** "Once each systemic risk tier is reached." Tiers are reached, which means they're thresholds a model *crosses*, probably on capability or on risk. Are tiers defined per risk (e.g. CBRN tier 1, tier 2)? Likely. And "reached" is what the evaluations measure. So the chain is: evaluations at trigger points → determine tier reached → mitigations for that tier → acceptance determination. That's a capability-threshold risk model, like the labs' frameworks, inside an Art. 3(2) probability × severity frame. Whether those two models (thresholds vs probability × severity) fit together cleanly is a real question. A tier is a discrete marker; risk is a continuous product. The Code joins them by having acceptance criteria "including" tiers. I want to see whether the criteria can be anything other than tiers.
- **"Implemented and planned"** processes. The Framework can describe processes not yet in place. Combined with (d)'s "future developments… subject to change", there's room for a Framework that's partly aspirational. Measure 1.2 (implementing) and the AI Office's assessment would be the check.
- **Internal use and the Framework.** If a lab uses an unreleased model internally for months, the Framework covers it. But will the *Model Report* and the "placing on the market" triggers? The Chapter is anchored in the AI Act, which regulates models "placed on the market". Internal use is a stretch of the Act's scope. Watch: does the Code's operative text keep "using" as it goes, or does it fall back to "placing on the market"? A fork should know where internal deployment is and isn't covered.
- **"Taking into account the models"**: one Framework per signatory covering all its models, not one per model. A firm-level policy "taking into account" its portfolio.
- Thinking about trigger points concretely: e.g. every N× increase in effective training compute, every major checkpoint, before any internal deployment, before external release. The Code asks the signatory to choose and justify them. The labs' frameworks differ here a lot (4× compute increments in Anthropic's early RSP, as I remember). So the Code doesn't harmonise *when* labs check; it requires them to say and justify.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Does the Code cover internal deployment?" M1.1: the Framework takes into account models signatories are "developing, making available on the market, and/or using". Follow it through the Measures.
- "What must a Framework contain?" M1.1 list (continues next unit): trigger points for lighter-touch evaluations, acceptance criteria including tiers, mitigations per tier, …
- "Who sets the acceptance criteria?" The signatory, with "description and justification" in the Framework (M1.1(2)(a)), as specified in M4.1.
- Should be asked but might not be: "What does 'will' mean in a Measure?" It's the obligation verb.
