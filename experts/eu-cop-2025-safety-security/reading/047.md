# Unit 47 · L388–398 · p.18: Commitment 7, Safety and Security Model Reports

**What it held.**
- "**LEGAL TEXT:** Articles 55(1) and 56(5) AI Act"
- "Signatories commit to reporting to the AI Office information about their model and their systemic risk assessment and mitigation processes and measures by creating a Safety and Security Model Report ("**Model Report**") **before placing a model on the market** (as specified in Measures 7.1 to 7.5). Further, Signatories commit to keeping the Model Report up-to-date (Measure 7.6) and notifying the AI Office of their Model Report (Measure 7.7)."
- "If Signatories have already provided relevant information to the AI Office in other reports and/or notifications, they may reference those…. Signatories may create a **single Model Report for several models** if the systemic risk assessment and mitigation processes and measures for one model **cannot be understood without reference to the other model(s)**."
- "Signatories that are SMEs or SMCs may reduce the level of detail in their Model Report to the extent necessary to reflect size and capacity constraints."

**What differed.** Predicted: Model Report before market, Measures 7.1–7.7 with update (7.6) and notification (7.7). Not predicted:
- **The joint-report condition** is narrow: several models in one Report only if one can't be understood without the others (e.g. a model family, or a model and its distilled variant). Not "for convenience".
- The SME relief here is **reduced detail**, matching recital (h) (simplified reporting) and (c) (proportionate documentation).
- Cross-referencing earlier reports is allowed.

**Wandering.**
- **"Before placing a model on the market."** The Model Report is market-anchored. No Model Report for internal-only models. Consistent with Figure 2. So the AI Office's per-model visibility starts at market placement. For a lab running a far more capable model internally, the AI Office sees only the Framework (which "takes into account" models being used) and any notification under Art. 52. That's the clearest single statement of the internal-deployment gap: **no Model Report without market placement**.
- **Framework vs Model Report, now confirmed:** Framework is firm-level, notified within five business days of confirmation; Model Report is model-level, before market placement. Two documents, two levels, both confidential to the AI Office.
- **Art. 56(5)** cited: I believe that's about the AI Office's ability to request information / the code's reporting provisions (Art. 56(5): "the AI Office shall aim to ensure that participants… report regularly… on the implementation of the commitments"?). Not sure. Mark uncertain.
- **Joint reports for models that "cannot be understood without reference to the other"**: e.g. a large model and smaller models distilled from it, whose risk assessments lean on the large model's evaluations. Here Appendix 2's "similarly safe or safer" / derived-model logic will probably meet the Model Report.
- The Model Report is where everything from C1–C6 lands per model: identification, analysis, acceptance, mitigations, security. I expect Measures 7.1–7.5 to list contents in detail (thick, like M1.1).

**Questions to keep.**
- "When is a Model Report due?" "Before placing a model on the market" (C7); then kept up to date (M7.6) and notified (M7.7).
- "Is there a Model Report for internally used models?" Not by C7's terms: it's created "before placing a model on the market".
- "Can one Model Report cover several models?" Only if one model's assessment "cannot be understood without reference to the other model(s)".
- "Do SMEs get relief?" They "may reduce the level of detail… to the extent necessary".

**Prediction for unit 48.** Measure 7.1, Model description and behaviour: architecture, capabilities, propensities, affordances; how the model was developed (training methods, data); how it's intended to be used and made available (release strategy, access); its specification (intended behaviour, e.g. a "model spec" or system prompt); versions covered.
