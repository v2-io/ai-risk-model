# Unit 83 · L673–687 · p.31 (table): 'model' (end) … 'reproducibility' (first part)

**What it held.**
- 'model' (end): versions that "(2) correspond to point (1) and have limited or no safety and/or security mitigations… implemented; and/or **(3) are used widely**." "In the context of comparisons between different 'models' (such as in Measure 3.5 and Appendix 3.5, in conjunction with Appendix 2, and Commitment 6), all references to 'model' refer to **a single model version**." "If the term 'AI' precedes the term 'model', this term exceptionally… also includes all models other than general-purpose AI models with systemic risk."
- **'model elicitation'**: "technical work to **systematically enhance** a model's capabilities, propensities, affordances, and/or effects, thereby facilitating an accurate measurement of the full range… that **can likely be attained**."
- **'model evaluation'**: "a systemic risk assessment technique that can be used in all stages of systemic risk assessment".
- **'model independent information'**: "information, including data and research, that is **not tied to a specific model**, but can inform systemic risk assessment and mitigation across several models."
- **'near miss'**: "a situation in which a serious incident could have, but ultimately did not, materialise."
- **'non-state external threats'**: "hostile operations conducted by non-state actors that: (1) are **roughly comparable to ten experienced, professional individuals in cybersecurity**; (2) spend **several months** with a total budget of **up to EUR 1 million** on the specific operation; and (3) have **major pre-existing cyberattack infrastructure but no pre-existing access** to the target organisation."
- **'post-market monitoring'**: "the monitoring of a model in the time span from when it is placed on the market **until the retirement** of the model from being made available on the market."
- **'process'**: "a structured set of actions that comprise or result in measures stipulated by this Chapter."
- **'reproducibility'**: "the ability to obtain consistent model evaluation results using the same input data, computational techniques, code, and model evaluation conditions, allowing for other researchers and engineers to validate, reproduce, or improve…." May be shown by e.g. peer review, reproduction by independent third parties, "**securely releasing to the AI Office** adequate amounts of model evaluation data, model evaluation code, documentation…" (continues)

**What differed.** Three corrections to my earlier reading, and one large surprise.
- **The surprise: 'non-state external threats' is quantified.** Ten experienced professionals, several months, up to EUR 1 million, major infrastructure, no prior access. At unit 45 I said the Code defines security "by whom you defend against, not how much", stripped of RAND's levels. Wrong: the *minimum* attacker is specified concretely. It reads like RAND's operational capacity category for a well-resourced non-state group (OC3?), which would correspond to a security level around SL3. That's my inference about the mapping; the Code doesn't name RAND here.
- **Correction (unit 27): 'model independent information'** means "not tied to a specific model", information across models. My inference ("information not derived from the model's behaviour") was wrong in emphasis. Under the definition, M3.1's "reviews of training data" fits if training data is considered shared resource (datasets used across models) or if the definition is loose. The tension stands: training-data review is listed under M3.1 but training data is model-specific. A fork should cite the definition and flag M3.1(4) as an awkward fit.
- **Correction-to-extension (units 33, 40): versions "used widely"** are part of "the model". So aggregation covers the most advanced, the least-mitigated, *and* the most widely used versions.
- **'near miss'** is now defined (unit 19 asked). Simple counterfactual: a serious incident could have materialised and didn't. So an attempted self-exfiltration that failed is a near miss to a serious cybersecurity breach. That answers my unit-73 question about attempts: a near miss, tracked in patterns (M9.2(9)), and grounds for Framework assessment (M1.3(2)) and Model Report update (M7.6(4)). Not a serious incident report on its own.

**Wandering.**

- **The security floor is now concrete.** A Security Goal must cover non-state actors of at least this calibre: ten professionals, months, €1M, existing infrastructure, no prior access. Plus insiders (including models, senior management). Not nation-states by name. That's demanding (many companies can't withstand a €1M, ten-person, months-long targeted operation) but well below state-actor capacity. A fork asked "how strong must security be?" should give this definition verbatim. It's one of the most concrete standards in the Chapter, and it's in the Glossary, where a body-only reader would miss it.
- **'model elicitation' = systematically enhancing the model** to measure what "can likely be attained". Elicitation isn't measurement of what the model does by default; it's deliberate enhancement (fine-tuning, scaffolding, prompting) to find its realistic ceiling. "Fully elicited" (M7.3(1)(d)(ii)) is thus "as enhanced as can likely be attained". "Likely" bounds it: not every conceivable enhancement, but those likely attainable (by whom? presumably by plausible actors, including the provider and downstream modifiers).
- **Comparisons are between single versions.** For C6's exemption ("capabilities inferior to… at least one model for which the parameters are publicly available"), the comparison is version-to-version. That partly answers my unit-44 dimensionality worry: compare a specific version against a specific open-weight version. Still multidimensional within the comparison.
- **'post-market monitoring' until retirement from the market.** Monitoring ends when the model stops being offered. For open weights, "retirement… from being made available" is a strange notion (the weights stay out there). Can an open-weight provider "retire" a model by taking down its download page and stop monitoring? Literally, yes. A fork should flag it.
- **Reproducibility via releasing evaluation materials "securely… to the AI Office"**: the AI Office as a reproduction venue. An agency that can rerun evaluation code would be a strong check. Capacity question again.
- **'AI model' vs 'model'**: a careful drafting device. "AI model" includes all models; "model" alone means the systemic-risk model (versions aggregated). Every earlier phrase "other AI models" (M7.3(4)(a)) thus includes non-systemic-risk models. Forks extracting text must keep the distinction.

**Questions to keep.**
- "How capable must the attackers be that security protects against?" 'non-state external threats': ~ten experienced professionals, several months, up to EUR 1 million, major pre-existing cyberattack infrastructure, no pre-existing access. Plus insider threats (including models). Not nation-states by name.
- "What's a near miss?" "A situation in which a serious incident could have, but ultimately did not, materialise." (An attempted but failed self-exfiltration would be one.)
- "What does elicitation mean?" "Technical work to systematically enhance a model's capabilities, propensities, affordances, and/or effects" to measure the full range "that can likely be attained".
- "Which versions does 'the model' include?" The most advanced, the corresponding least-mitigated, and widely used versions; single versions when comparing models.
- "What does 'model-independent information' mean?" "Not tied to a specific model". (My unit-27 gloss was off.)
- Should be asked but might not be: "When does post-market monitoring end for an open-weight model?" At "retirement… from being made available on the market": odd for open weights.
