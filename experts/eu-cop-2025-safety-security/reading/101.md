# Unit 101 · L830–831 · p.37: Appendix 1.4 (3) Cyber offence; (4) Harmful manipulation

**What it held.**
- "(3) **Cyber offence:** Risks from enabling **large-scale sophisticated** cyber-attacks, including on critical systems (e.g. critical infrastructure). This includes significantly lowering the barriers to entry for malicious actors, or significantly increasing the potential impact achieved in offensive cyber operations, e.g. through **automated vulnerability discovery, exploit generation, operational use, and attack scaling**."
- "(4) **Harmful manipulation:** Risks from enabling the **strategic distortion** of human behaviour or beliefs by **targeting large populations or high-stakes decision-makers** through persuasion, deception, or personalised targeting. This includes significantly enhancing capabilities for persuasion, deception, and personalised targeting, particularly through **multi-turn interactions** and where individuals are **unaware of or cannot reasonably detect** such influence. Such capabilities could undermine democratic processes and fundamental rights, **including exploitation based on protected characteristics**."

**What differed.** Close to my prediction (memory again: "strategic distortion", "large populations or high-stakes decision-makers"). Not predicted: cyber's **four-stage kill chain** (discovery, exploit generation, operational use, attack scaling); manipulation's **multi-turn** emphasis and **undetectability**; **protected characteristics**. And: **four** specified risks, as remembered, but I don't see a sentence closing the list or adding more. Maybe the next unit.

**Wandering.**

- **All three misuse risks share a template**: "Risks from enabling [X]. This includes significantly lowering the barriers to entry for malicious actors, or significantly increasing the potential impact…" (CBRN and cyber explicitly; manipulation as "significantly enhancing capabilities"). Loss of control is the odd one out: no malicious actor, no "enabling"; the risk is *from humans losing ability*. The specified list thus has three misuse risks with an uplift template and one non-misuse risk with a control-capacity definition. For G3: the Code's four specified risks split cleanly into misuse (3) and loss of control (1). Accidents appear only in CBRN.
- **Cyber: "large-scale sophisticated"** limits scope: a model helping script kiddies with simple phishing isn't the specified risk; it's about sophisticated, large-scale operations. The four stages are a cyber kill chain compressed (discovery → weaponisation → operation → scale). Useful for evaluation design.
- **Manipulation: "strategic distortion"** is the key phrase: not all persuasion, but distortion, strategic (goal-directed), and targeted at *large populations* or *high-stakes decision-makers*. So manipulation of one ordinary person isn't the specified risk; manipulation of a minister or an electorate is. "Multi-turn interactions" points to chatbots' sustained relationships (companion apps, long conversations), a 2025 concern. "Unaware of or cannot reasonably detect" is the covertness I said was missing from Appendix 1.2's characteristics (unit 93): it appears here, specific to manipulation.
- **Whose manipulation?** "Risks from enabling" suggests a human manipulator using the model. But "deception" as a propensity (Glossary; 1.3.2(3)) means the model itself might manipulate. The definition is agnostic: it's the distortion that matters, whoever directs it. A fork asked "is the model manipulating users on its own covered?" should say: the definition covers "enabling" distortion via persuasion, deception or targeting; it doesn't require a human manipulator, though "enabling" reads most naturally that way.
- **"Exploitation based on protected characteristics"**: discriminatory targeting (e.g. manipulating people via their religion or ethnicity). Ties the manipulation risk to EU non-discrimination law.
- **The specified list**, all four, in summary for forks: CBRN (attacks or accidents; two uplift forms; full weapons lifecycle); loss of control (humans losing the ability to reliably direct, modify, or shut down a model); cyber offence (large-scale sophisticated attacks; four stages); harmful manipulation (strategic distortion targeting large populations or high-stakes decision-makers, especially multi-turn and undetectable).

**Questions to keep.**
- "What are the Code's specified systemic risks?" Appendix 1.4: CBRN; loss of control; cyber offence; harmful manipulation.
- "Is all manipulation a specified systemic risk?" No: "strategic distortion of human behaviour or beliefs by targeting large populations or high-stakes decision-makers".
- "Does cyber offence cover low-skill attacks?" The specified risk is "large-scale sophisticated cyber-attacks".
- Should be asked but might not be: "Does the manipulation risk cover a model manipulating on its own initiative?" The text is agnostic; "enabling" reads as human-directed.
- Should be asked: "Which specified risk includes accidents?" Only CBRN.
