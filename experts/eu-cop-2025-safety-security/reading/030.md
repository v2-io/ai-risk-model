# Unit 30 · L245–249 · p.13: Measure 3.4, Systemic risk estimation

**What it held.**
- "Signatories will estimate the **probability and severity of harm** for the systemic risk."
- "Signatories will use at least state-of-the-art risk estimation methods and take into account at least the information gathered pursuant to Commitment 2, this Commitment, and Commitment 9."
- "Estimates of systemic risk will be expressed as a risk score, risk matrix, probability distribution, or in other adequate formats, and may be quantitative, semi-quantitative, and/or qualitative."
- Examples: "(1) a qualitative systemic risk score (e.g. "moderate" or "critical"); (2) a qualitative systemic risk matrix (e.g. "probability: unlikely" x "impact: high"); and/or (3) a quantitative systemic risk matrix (e.g. "X-Y%" x "X-Y EUR damage")."

**What differed.** Predicted: probability and severity; quantitative/qualitative; risk matrices. Not predicted:
- **Commitment 9 (serious incidents)** is explicitly an input to estimation.
- The examples' vocabulary shifts: the Measure says "**severity** of harm", and example (2) says "**impact**: high". Severity and impact are used as if interchangeable.
- Example (3) quantifies severity in **EUR damage**. Harm measured in money.
- Example (1) is a **single score** ("moderate" or "critical") that doesn't separate probability from severity at all. So a one-word rating satisfies "estimate the probability and severity of harm"?

**Wandering.**

- **This is where Art. 3(2) becomes operational.** Recital (i) told us Appendix 1 is read in light of "the probability and severity of harm pursuant to the definition of 'risk' in Article 3(2)". M3.4 makes every systemic risk estimate a probability-and-severity estimate. The Code's risk model is formally the classic one: risk = f(probability, severity). This is a hard fact for G3 ("what risk and hazard are made of"): for this source, *risk* is the combination of probability and severity of harm, by reference to Art. 3(2), and estimation outputs are scores, matrices or distributions.
- **But the examples dilute it.** A qualitative "risk score" like "critical" collapses probability and severity into one ordinal label. The Measure says signatories "will estimate the probability and severity", and then offers as an example a format in which neither is separately visible. Either the score is understood as summarising both (with the components in the analysis behind it), or the example is a loophole. A fork should report this tension, not resolve it.
- **Severity ≠ impact?** In risk-management vocabularies (ISO 31000), "consequence" or "impact" and "severity" are near-synonyms; but for our lexicon they may need separating (impact as the event's effects; severity as the magnitude ordering of harm). The Code uses "impact" only in an example. The operative term is "severity of harm". A fork mapping terms should map the operative word and note the example's synonym.
- **EUR damage.** Monetised severity puts deaths and economic damage on one scale only if one converts lives to euros. The example doesn't say how. SB 53 and OpenAI (per my priming) use separate casualty and dollar thresholds; the Code's example uses only euros. Just an example, but it suggests the drafters imagined economic quantification as natural. A question to ask: does the Code anywhere set severity thresholds in casualties, or only via the signatory's own tiers? So far, only the signatory's tiers.
- **Incidents (C9) feed estimation**, so real-world events update the probability estimates. That makes estimation Bayesian in spirit: priors from modelling, updated by monitoring and incidents.
- **"At least state-of-the-art risk estimation methods"**: same remark as M3.3. In 2025, quantitative AI risk estimation was nascent. The Code allows qualitative formats while demanding the state of the art. As the field matures, qualitative-only estimates may stop being state of the art. That's a ratchet built into the wording: today's acceptable format may become inadequate.
- **The output feeds C4.** Acceptance criteria (M4.1) must be stated in terms that can be compared with these estimates. If estimates can be qualitative scores, acceptance criteria can be qualitative too. The coupling between estimate format and acceptance criteria is the place to watch.

**Questions to keep.**
- "How does the Code define risk?" Operatively in M3.4: "estimate the probability and severity of harm", by reference to Art. 3(2) (recital (i)).
- "Must risk estimates be quantitative?" No: "may be quantitative, semi-quantitative, and/or qualitative"; formats include risk score, risk matrix, probability distribution.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Does a single qualitative score ('critical') satisfy 'estimate the probability and severity'?" The example suggests yes; the operative sentence suggests the components must be estimated. A tension.
- "What inputs must estimation consider?" At least the information from C2, C3 and C9.

**Prediction for unit 31.** Measure 3.5, Post-market monitoring: Signatories will conduct appropriate post-market monitoring to gather information relevant to assessing whether the systemic risk might be unacceptable, e.g. collecting end-user feedback, providing (anonymous) reporting channels, bug bounties, monitoring of social media / forums, research access for external researchers (API access, maybe free), and, for open-weight models, monitoring of downstream modifications. Possibly something about "serious incident tracking".
