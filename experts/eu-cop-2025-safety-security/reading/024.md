# Unit 24 · L189–190 · p.11: M2.1 end: (1)(c) identifying; (2) the specified systemic risks

**What it held.**
- "(c) identifying, based on point (b), the systemic risks stemming from the model; and"
- "(2) the specified systemic risks in Appendix 1.4."

**What differed.** Exactly as predicted, including Appendix 1.4. A short unit, a clean hit. My predictions are now landing at the structural level. That makes the clause-level surprises the information.

**Wandering.**
- **The grammar matters.** "Signatories will identify: (1) the systemic risks obtained through the following process… and (2) the specified systemic risks in Appendix 1.4." So the specified risks are identified **by stipulation**, not through the process. A signatory doesn't get to conclude, from its process, that CBNR (or whatever 1.4 lists) doesn't stem from its model. They're identified, full stop. Then C3 and C4 analyse and determine acceptability for them. This is the Code's floor: every signatory's identified risks include the specified ones.
- **The process can only add.** Process-identified risks (1) plus specified risks (2). The process can identify more risks than Appendix 1.4, never fewer. Coverage claims about any signatory's risk list: at least Appendix 1.4.
- **Two-tier risk universe**, then: specified systemic risks (stipulated) and other systemic risks (found by process from Appendix 1.1 types). I want to see in Appendix 1 whether 1.4 is a subset of 1.1's types (likely) and how the "nature" and "sources" in 1.2/1.3 are used to decide that a 1.1 type is systemic for a model.
- A question about the Code's logic: what if a specified risk genuinely can't stem from a model (a text-only model with no bio knowledge)? It's still "identified", but C3's analysis could then estimate it as negligible, and C4 would find it acceptable. So the stipulation costs analysis, not a conclusion. Fine design: it forces the signatory to *show* the specified risks are acceptable, rather than *assume* they're absent. The burden-of-proof theme from recital (a) again.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Can a signatory decide a specified systemic risk doesn't apply to its model?" No: M2.1(2) identifies the Appendix 1.4 risks by stipulation; the signatory can only show, in analysis and acceptance, that they're acceptable.
- "What risks must every signatory assess?" At least the specified systemic risks in Appendix 1.4, plus any others its process finds.

**Prediction for unit 25.** Measure 2.2, Systemic risk scenarios: Signatories will develop appropriate systemic risk scenarios for each identified systemic risk, considering the number and level of detail appropriate; scenarios describe pathways to harm, including the relevant risk sources, the actors involved (e.g. malicious actors, the model itself), and the resulting harms. Possibly they must cover worst-case or "most severe plausible" outcomes.
