# Unit 25 · L192–194 · p.11: Measure 2.2, Systemic risk scenarios

**What it held.** "Signatories will develop appropriate systemic risk scenarios, including regarding the number and level of detail of these systemic risk scenarios, for each identified systemic risk (pursuant to Measure 2.1)."

That's all of M2.2.

**What differed.** I predicted content requirements for scenarios (pathways, actors, harms, severity). There are none. The Measure is one sentence: scenarios must be "appropriate", including in number and level of detail. Nothing says what a scenario must contain.

**Wandering.**
- **The thinnest Measure so far, and it governs one of C2's two elements.** "Appropriate" carries everything. Compare M1.1's detailed list. Why so thin? Possibilities: (1) the drafters judged scenario methods too immature to prescribe (recital (f)'s "evolving challenge"); (2) the content of scenarios is specified elsewhere, e.g. the Glossary defines "systemic risk scenario", or C3/Appendix 3 says what scenarios feed; (3) compromise: an earlier draft had detailed scenario requirements and they were cut. I'd guess (2) and (3) together. I'll look for a Glossary definition of "systemic risk scenario".
- **What "appropriate" will mean in practice** is set by recital (i)'s purposive rule (read toward assessing and mitigating risk), by the state-of-the-art default of recital (a), and by AI Office guidance. So a thin Measure isn't a blank cheque; it's a delegation of content to the interpretive frame. That's a structural pattern of the Code: thin operative text + strong interpretive rules.
- **"Number and level of detail"** is the only dimension named. So the Code's worry is about *how many* and *how detailed*, i.e., about effort, not about what kind. It's proportionality (recital (c)) applied to scenarios.
- Thinking ahead: scenarios will matter in C3 (analysis estimates probability and severity per scenario?) and in M4.1 (tiers might be defined per scenario?). If C3 leans heavily on scenarios, the thinness here becomes significant: the quality of the whole analysis rests on scenarios whose content is unspecified.
- For the project: if the lexicon wants "risk scenario" as a concept, the Code gives a role (identification element; one or more per identified systemic risk) but no content definition here.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What must a systemic risk scenario contain?" M2.2 doesn't say; only "appropriate… including regarding the number and level of detail". Check the Glossary and C3.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Is there any minimum number of scenarios per risk?" No; "appropriate".
