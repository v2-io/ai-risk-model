# Unit 26 · L198–210 · p.12: Commitment 3, Systemic risk analysis

**What it held.**
- "**LEGAL TEXT:** Article 55(1) and recital 114 AI Act"
- "Signatories commit to analysing each identified systemic risk (pursuant to Commitment 2). The purpose… includes facilitating systemic risk acceptance determination (pursuant to Commitment 4)."
- "Five elements for each identified systemic risk, which **may overlap and may need to be implemented recursively**: (1) gathering model-independent information (M3.1); (2) conducting model evaluations (M3.2); (3) modelling the systemic risk (M3.3); and (4) estimating the systemic risk (M3.4); **while** (5) conducting post-market monitoring (M3.5)."

**What differed.** My prediction matched all five element names and Measure numbers. That's too good to be inference from genre: I hold the Code's skeleton from training. So from here on, the Measure titles will feel known. Read the bodies as if I'd never seen them.

Two things I didn't predict:
- "**May overlap and may need to be implemented recursively**": the five elements aren't a pipeline. Modelling might prompt new evaluations; estimates might prompt more information-gathering.
- The connective "**while**" before (5): post-market monitoring runs *concurrently* with the others, not as a fifth step. A small word with a structural meaning.

**Wandering.**
- **The analytic chain is: information → evaluation → modelling → estimation.** That's a real risk-analysis methodology, not just "run evals". "Modelling the systemic risk" (M3.3) is a distinct step between evaluation and estimation: e.g. building a causal model of how capability translates into harm (who would misuse it, what bottlenecks exist). Then estimation (M3.4) produces the risk estimate, probably probability and severity. This is far more like a safety-case or probabilistic risk assessment than like most lab frameworks (which go evaluation → threshold → safeguards). If M3.3 and M3.4 are substantive, the Code demands more analytical work than most of the frameworks it descends from.
- **Evaluations aren't the analysis; they're one input.** A fork asked "does the Code rely on evaluations?" should say: evaluations are one of five elements; risk modelling and estimation sit on top.
- **"Recursively"**: an unusual word for a regulatory text. It acknowledges iterative science. Consistent with recital (f)'s "evolving challenge".
- **Recital 114 AI Act** is cited (the one about obligations for GPAI models with systemic risk, including evaluation and adversarial testing, as I recall), but not 115. C1's mangled line probably had 114 and 115.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What does systemic risk analysis involve?" C3: model-independent information (M3.1), model evaluations (M3.2), risk modelling (M3.3), risk estimation (M3.4), while post-market monitoring (M3.5); elements "may overlap and may need to be implemented recursively".
- Should be asked but might not be: "Is the Code's analysis just evaluations plus thresholds?" No: it includes risk modelling and estimation.

**Prediction for unit 27.** Measure 3.1, Model-independent information: Signatories will gather information relevant to the systemic risk independent of the model, e.g. via literature reviews, market analyses, reviews of training data, incident data, expert interviews, forecasting, and engagement with stakeholders. "As appropriate". Possibly "searching for and gathering information" with some systematicity.
