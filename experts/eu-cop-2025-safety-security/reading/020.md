# Unit 20 · L153–156 · p.10: what a Framework assessment includes

**What it held.** "A Framework assessment will include the following:"
- "(1) **Framework adequacy**: An assessment of whether the processes and measures in the Framework are appropriate for the systemic risks stemming from the Signatories' models. This assessment will take into account how the models are currently being developed, made available on the market, and/or used, and how they are expected to be developed, made available on the market, and/or used **over the next 12 months**."
- "(2) **Framework adherence**: An assessment focused on the Signatories' adherence to the Framework, including: (a) any **instances of, and reasons for, non-adherence** to the Framework since the last Framework assessment; and (b) any measures, including safety and security mitigations, that need to be implemented to ensure continued adherence to the Framework. If point(s) (a) and/or (b) give rise to risks of future non-adherence, Signatories will make **remediation plans** as part of their Framework assessment."

**What differed.**
- Two named components, adequacy and adherence. I predicted adequacy and adherence (from unit 18), and the update, but not:
  - a **12-month look-ahead** in the adequacy test (paired with the 12-month cycle; each assessment covers the coming year);
  - the requirement to record **instances of, and reasons for, non-adherence**: a confession log, essentially;
  - **remediation plans**.
- No independent experts here. I guessed "where appropriate, independent experts"; nothing of the kind. The Framework assessment is internal.

**Wandering.**

- **Non-adherence as a documented category.** The Code expects that signatories won't always follow their own Frameworks, and requires them to record when and why. That's realistic and important. Where does this record go? If the Framework assessment is reported to the AI Office (M1.4 or C7?), the regulator sees the non-adherence log. That would be a powerful oversight tool. If it stays internal, it's a governance tool only. I'll watch M1.4.
- **"Reasons for" non-adherence** matter: e.g. "we skipped the evaluation at checkpoint X because of a deadline" vs "because a better evaluation replaced it". The second might be fine under recital (f) (alternative means). The record would distinguish deviation from innovation.
- **Remediation plans "if… give rise to risks of future non-adherence"**: only forward-risky non-adherence requires a plan. One-off past deviations don't, if they're not likely to recur. Pragmatic.
- **The 12-month horizon** fits M1.1(2)(c)'s timeline forecasts: the Framework must be adequate for how models are *expected* to be developed and used over the next year. So if a lab expects to start large-scale agentic deployment in the next 12 months, the Framework must already be adequate for that. Forward-looking adequacy is a high bar and very good design.
- **Develop / market / use again.** The triad is applied consistently in C1. The Framework, at least, really does cover internal use. My internal-deployment map so far: Framework (yes), continuous light layer (yes), full process (market-anchored), periodic Framework assessment clock (market-anchored), adequacy test (covers use). Mixed, and worth giving as a mixed picture, not "the Code covers / doesn't cover internal deployment".
- The internal character of the Framework assessment (no outside reviewer) contrasts with the Code's emphasis on external evaluation of *models* (which I expect later). The Code externalises model evaluation more than it externalises governance review. A fork comparing to SB 53 (which I believe has third-party audits from 2030) should be careful: I haven't seen any audit of the Framework here.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What does a Framework assessment cover?" M1.3: adequacy (current and expected development/market/use over the next 12 months) and adherence (instances and reasons of non-adherence; measures for continued adherence; remediation plans).
- "Is the Framework assessment independent?" Nothing here says so; it's the signatory's own.
- Should be asked: "Does the AI Office see the non-adherence record?" Check M1.4 and C7.

**Prediction for unit 21.** Maybe a (3) to the assessment (e.g. "best practices": comparison with the state of the art), then Measure 1.4: notify the AI Office, providing (unredacted) access to the Framework and updates within two weeks of confirmation. Perhaps something about the AI Office being able to request the Framework assessment.
