# Unit 29 · L237–243 · p.13: Measure 3.3, Systemic risk modelling

**What it held.** "Signatories will conduct systemic risk modelling for the systemic risk. To this end, Signatories will: (1) use at least state-of-the-art risk modelling methods; (2) build on the systemic risk scenarios developed pursuant to Measure 2.2; and (3) take into account at least the information gathered pursuant to Measure 2.1 and this Commitment."

**What differed.** Close to my prediction: thin, three conditions, scenarios as input. Not predicted: no examples of modelling methods at all (I guessed causal models, threat models, decomposition). And "risk modelling" is never defined here.

**Wandering.**
- **The pattern of thin Measures is now clear:** M2.2 (scenarios) and M3.3 (modelling) are both one-liners resting on "appropriate" / "at least state-of-the-art". The thick Measures are about *process and documentation* (M1.1, M1.3). The Code is prescriptive about governance and the shape of the loop, and deferential about analytical method. A plausible reason: risk modelling for AI was (in 2025) immature and contested; prescribing a method would have frozen a bad one. Recital (f) said exactly this. The cost: "state-of-the-art risk modelling methods" for AI systemic risk barely existed in 2025, so the floor is set by a thin field. Who decides what the state of the art in AI risk modelling is? Probably whoever publishes first (labs, AISIs, academics such as those working on quantitative risk models). The Code points to a field and lets it fill in.
- **Scenarios are the backbone.** M3.3 "build[s] on" M2.2's scenarios. So the chain is: identify risk → scenarios (M2.2) → model each risk using the scenarios (M3.3) → estimate (M3.4). The quality of scenarios propagates. Since M2.2 specifies nothing about scenario content, the modelling inherits that openness.
- **"At least the information gathered pursuant to Measure 2.1 and this Commitment"**: the modelling must use all of identification's information and all of C3's (model-independent info, evaluations, post-market monitoring). "At least" means the modelling can't ignore evaluation results that cut against the provider's preferred story. A fork could use this: if a Model Report's risk model ignores an evaluation result, that's a departure from M3.3(3).
- **What a risk model would be**, imagined for CBRN: a pathway from a novice actor to a mass-casualty attack, broken into steps (ideation, acquisition, production, delivery), with the model's uplift estimated at each step from human uplift studies (M3.2) and the baseline from literature and other models (M3.1). That's what "state-of-the-art risk modelling" would plausibly mean. It's demanding if taken seriously.
- Our project's interest: the Code is one of very few sources that separates *risk modelling* from *risk estimation* as named steps. For a lexicon, these are distinct activities: modelling = constructing the causal account; estimation = putting values on it.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What is 'systemic risk modelling' in the Code?" M3.3 defines it only by conditions: state-of-the-art methods, built on the M2.2 scenarios, taking into account at least the information from M2.1 and C3. No method is named.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Can a provider's risk model ignore inconvenient evaluation results?" No: "take into account at least the information gathered" (M3.3(3)).

**Prediction for unit 30.** Measure 3.4, Systemic risk estimation: Signatories will estimate the probability and severity of harm for the systemic risk, using the modelling; estimates may be quantitative, semi-quantitative, or qualitative ("e.g. risk matrices"), with uncertainty stated. Possibly: "taking into account at least the systemic risk tiers" or expressing the estimate in tier terms. This is where I'd expect the Art. 3(2) probability × severity frame to become operational.
