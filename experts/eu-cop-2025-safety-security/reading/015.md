# Unit 15 · L106–120 · p.7: Measure 1.2, Implementing the Framework (first part)

**What it held.**
- "Signatories will implement the processes and measures outlined in their Framework as specified in the following paragraphs."
- "Along the entire model lifecycle, Signatories will **continuously**:"
  - (1) assess the systemic risks by:
    - (a) "conducting lighter-touch model evaluations that **need not adhere to Appendix 3** (e.g. automated evaluations) at appropriate trigger points defined in terms of, e.g. **time, training compute, development stages, user access, inference compute, and/or affordances**";
    - (b) "conducting post-market monitoring after placing the model on the market, as specified in **Measure 3.5**";
    - (c) "taking into account relevant information about serious incidents (pursuant to **Commitment 9**)"; and
    - (d) "increasing the breadth and/or depth of assessment or conducting a full systemic risk assessment and mitigation process that is specified in the following paragraph, based on the results of points (a), (b), and (c)"; and
  - (2) "implement systemic risk mitigations taking into account the results of point (1), including addressing serious incidents as appropriate."

**What differed.**
- I predicted lighter-touch at triggers, post-market monitoring, and a full assessment. All there. What I didn't predict: (1)(d)'s **escalation logic**. The light evaluations, monitoring and incidents are *inputs* that decide whether to deepen or widen assessment, or to run the full process. So the continuous loop is a **screening layer**: cheap signals → escalation to a full assessment when warranted.
- **Appendix 3** is now visible as a standard for evaluations (rigour requirements, I'd guess: something like "high-quality evaluations", scientific validity, possibly external evaluator conditions). Lighter-touch evaluations are exempted from it; full assessments presumably aren't.
- The **trigger-point dimensions** are listed: time, training compute, development stages, user access, inference compute, affordances. I didn't expect "user access" or "affordances" (tools, internet, code execution) as trigger dimensions.
- Commitment 9 = serious incidents. Measure 3.5 = post-market monitoring, so Commitment 3 is probably systemic risk analysis. The skeleton so far: C1 Framework, C2 (identification?), C3 analysis (incl. 3.5 monitoring), C4 acceptance (4.1 criteria, 4.2 proceed), …, C8 responsibility (8.3 culture), C9 incidents.

**Wandering.**

- **The trigger dimensions are a small risk-factor taxonomy.** Time (periodic), training compute (scale), development stages (checkpoints, fine-tuning phases), user access (who can use it: internal staff, trusted testers, the public), inference compute (echo of recital (b)), affordances (what the model can act with). Each of these is a way a model's risk can change *without the weights changing*: user access and affordances especially. That's a clear statement that risk is a property of model-in-context. For our lexicon: "affordance" here likely means tools/permissions available to the model; check the Glossary. And "user access" as a risk factor is a deployment concept, linking to staged release.
- **"Need not adhere to Appendix 3 (e.g. automated evaluations)."** Automated evals are the paradigm of lighter-touch. So Appendix 3 probably sets requirements like human expert involvement, elicitation, adequate time, or independence. The cheaper screen is allowed to be less rigorous; the decision to escalate rests on it. Failure mode to imagine: an automated eval misses a capability because elicitation was weak (the classic "sandbagging" or under-elicitation problem), so no escalation happens. The Code's protection is that trigger points and their usage must be "justified" (M1.1(1)). A fork asked about under-elicitation should note the lighter-touch layer is where it would bite.
- **"Continuously"** is now operationalised. The recitals' "continuously assess and mitigate" becomes: a screening loop of light evals + monitoring + incident information, with escalation. That's a concrete and sensible reading of "continuous". It answers the worry that "continuous assessment" is either meaningless or impossibly costly.
- **(2) mitigations "taking into account the results"**, "including addressing serious incidents as appropriate". Mitigation is coupled to assessment results continuously, not only at release. Post-market mitigations (e.g. tightening filters after an incident) are in scope.
- **Post-market monitoring "after placing the model on the market"**: the monitoring applies after placement. For internally used models, (1)(a) and (1)(c) still apply, but (b) doesn't by its terms. Internal use has lighter-touch evaluations and incidents, but no post-market monitoring. One more brick in the internal-deployment picture.
- I keep reading this as an engineer would: it's a well-designed control loop. Sensors (light evals, monitoring, incidents), a threshold for escalation, a heavy process when escalated, actuators (mitigations). In bow-tie terms, the loop sits across prevention and mitigation.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What does 'continuous' risk assessment mean in the Code?" M1.2: lighter-touch evaluations at trigger points; post-market monitoring (M3.5); serious-incident information (C9); escalation to broader/deeper or full assessment.
- "What are trigger points?" M1.2(1)(a): defined "in terms of, e.g. time, training compute, development stages, user access, inference compute, and/or affordances".
- "Do all evaluations have to meet Appendix 3?" No: lighter-touch evaluations "need not adhere to Appendix 3". Full assessments presumably must.
- Should be asked but might not be: "What if the lighter-touch screen under-elicits a capability?" Then nothing escalates; the protection is the justification of trigger points.

**Prediction for unit 16.** M1.2's next paragraph: the full systemic risk assessment and mitigation process: Signatories will conduct it (a) at least before placing a model on the market, (b) whenever (1)(d) says so; and it comprises identification (C2), analysis (C3), acceptance determination (C4), mitigations (C5 safety, C6 security), and a Model Report (C7). Possibly "if the systemic risk is not acceptable, Signatories will not proceed…" pointing at M4.2.
