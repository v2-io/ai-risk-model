# Units 102–103 · L833–840 · p.37: Appendix 2, Similarly safe or safer models; 2.1 Safe reference models (1)–(2)

*(Unit 102 was the heading "Appendix 2 Similarly safe or safer models"; one file for both. Also received: Joseph's invitation, via ai-risk-search-tool, to explore and delegate web searches after the reading, and then to talk with the SB 53 expert. Noted for after the source is done.)*

**What it held.** "A model **may be considered** a safe reference model **with regard to a systemic risk** if:
- (1) the model has: **(a) been placed on the market before the publication of this Chapter**; or (b) completed the full systemic risk assessment and mitigation process…, including that the systemic risks… have been determined to be acceptable…, and the AI Office has received its Model Report…;
- (2) the Signatory has **sufficient visibility** into the model's characteristics such as relevant architectural details, capabilities, propensities, affordances, and safety mitigations. Such visibility is **assumed for all models developed by the Signatory itself** and for all models for which the Signatory has access to all information that would be necessary… **including the model parameters**; and" (continues)

**What differed.** I predicted grandfathering ("placed on the market before the Code's publication") and visibility (memory, likely). The exact effect is still striking now I read it:
- **Every model marketed before the Chapter's publication (July 2025) can be a safe reference model** for a given systemic risk, if the visibility condition (and whatever (3) says) holds. No assessment required for them.
- Safe-reference status is **per systemic risk**: a model may be a safe reference for cyber and not for CBRN.

**Wandering.**

- **Grandfathering sets the baseline at the 2025 frontier.** The models already out in mid-2025 (GPT-4o, Claude 3.x/4, Gemini 2.x, Llama 3/4, DeepSeek-R1, …) become the reference class against which new models are "similarly safe or safer". A new model no riskier than one of these gets: lighter external-evaluator access duties (M3.5), no six-monthly Model Report updates (M7.6), no public summaries (M10.2), and possibly more (Appendix 3.5's exemption from external evaluation?). That's the Code's answer to "what's the baseline?": the status quo at publication. It's a political settlement in technical form: the existing frontier is accepted as safe enough, and the Code's heavy machinery applies to whatever exceeds it.
- **Is that defensible?** The Code's own logic (marginal risk) supports it: if a risk is already present in the world via models on the market, a new model that's no worse doesn't add much *marginal* risk. But "placed on the market before the publication" isn't an assessment; it's a date. Those models weren't found safe; they were already there. The label "safe reference model" overstates, perhaps. A fork should be precise: grandfathered models qualify by date, not by having been assessed.
- **Visibility is the real gate.** (2) requires enough knowledge of the reference model's characteristics to compare. For one's own models: assumed. For other models: only if the signatory has all the information it would need to run a full assessment, **including the parameters**. So a closed competitor model is generally *not* usable as a reference (no parameters, no full information). Open-weight models are usable (parameters available, though "all information that would be necessary" also includes architecture, safety mitigations… the parameters suffice for much). Effect: each signatory's reference class is its own previous models plus open-weight models. That's a fair design: you can't claim to be "as safe as" a model you can't inspect.
- **Interaction with C6's open-weight exemption.** C6 exempts models whose capabilities are inferior to any open-weight model. Appendix 2 lets open-weight models be safe references. Both make the open-weight frontier a baseline. As open weights advance, both lighten closed providers' duties. The open-weight frontier is thus a regulatory variable for everyone.
- **(1)(b) path**: a model assessed under the Code, found acceptable, with a Model Report at the AI Office, becomes a reference. So each assessed generation becomes the reference for the next, a ratchet that only lightens for models no riskier than the last accepted one.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What is a safe reference model?" Appendix 2.1: with regard to a systemic risk, a model (1) placed on the market before the Chapter's publication, or assessed under the Code with risks found acceptable and a Model Report with the AI Office; (2) into which the signatory has sufficient visibility (assumed for its own models and for models with all necessary information incl. parameters); and (3) [continues].
- "Are pre-Code models treated as safe?" They can be safe reference models by date, "placed on the market before the publication of this Chapter". Not by assessment.
- "Can a competitor's closed model be a safe reference?" Only with sufficient visibility (all necessary information incl. parameters), which a closed model normally doesn't offer.

**Prediction for unit 104.** (3) "there is no other scientific evidence that suggests the model poses risks…" or "the model's systemic risks are not known to be unacceptable (e.g. no serious incidents)". Then Appendix 2.2: a model is similarly safe or safer than a safe reference model with regard to a systemic risk if (1) the Signatory doesn't reasonably foresee any material increase in risk, e.g. its capability benchmarks are not materially higher (within a margin of error), no new capabilities, propensities or affordances; (2) mitigations at least as effective; (3) acceptance determination, etc.

# Unit 104 · L841 · p.37: Appendix 2.1 (3)

**What it held.** "(3) there are no other reasonable grounds to believe that the systemic risks stemming from the model are not acceptable."

**What differed.** As predicted, in substance. It's the grandfathering's limit: a pre-Code model with known unacceptable risks (e.g. serious incidents showing it) can't be a safe reference.

**Wandering.** "No other reasonable grounds to believe… not acceptable": a negative condition. Grandfathered models are presumed acceptable unless there are grounds otherwise. It's a weak filter for pre-2025 models (few were assessed rigorously; absence of grounds is easy to claim) but it does exclude models with known problems. A fork should say: grandfathered models qualify unless there are "reasonable grounds to believe" their risks aren't acceptable.

**Questions to keep.** "Can any pre-Code model be a safe reference?" Only if, also, there are "no other reasonable grounds to believe that the systemic risks stemming from the model are not acceptable" (2.1(3)) and visibility (2.1(2)) holds.

**Prediction for unit 105.** Appendix 2.2: similarly safe or safer if, per systemic risk: (1) no material increase in capabilities/propensities/affordances relative to the safe reference (e.g. benchmark scores not materially higher, within a margin), (2) no new risks, (3) the comparison made using state-of-the-art evaluations, with a margin; "for each identified systemic risk".
