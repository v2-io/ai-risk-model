# Unit 88 · L721–727 · p.34: Appendix 1, LEGAL TEXT — AI Act Art. 3(64) and 3(65), quoted

**What it held.** "**LEGAL TEXT**":
- "Article 3(64) AI Act: '**high-impact capabilities**' means capabilities that match or exceed the capabilities recorded in **the most advanced general-purpose AI models**;"
- "Article 3(65) AI Act: '**systemic risk**' means a risk that is **specific to the high-impact capabilities** of general-purpose AI models, having a significant impact on the Union market **due to their reach**, **or** due to actual or reasonably foreseeable negative effects on **public health, safety, public security, fundamental rights, or the society as a whole**, that can be **propagated at scale across the value chain**;"
- "**ADDITIONAL LEGAL TEXT: Recital 110 AI Act**" (the recital's text follows, presumably).
- Conversion note: "generalpurpose" in Art. 3(65) has lost its hyphen at a line break.

**What differed.** I'd guessed Appendix 1 would *paraphrase* Art. 3(65). Instead it quotes Art. 3(64) and 3(65) verbatim as "LEGAL TEXT". So the Glossary's silence on 'systemic risk' (unit 85) is answered here: the Code places the Act's definition at the head of the risk appendix.

**Wandering.**

- **Now I can read the definition the whole Chapter hangs on.** "Systemic risk" is a risk:
  1. "specific to the high-impact capabilities of general-purpose AI models", where high-impact capabilities "match or exceed the capabilities recorded in the most advanced general-purpose AI models";
  2. "having a significant impact on the Union market";
  3. "due to their reach, or due to actual or reasonably foreseeable negative effects on public health, safety, public security, fundamental rights, or the society as a whole";
  4. "that can be propagated at scale across the value chain".
- **"Specific to the high-impact capabilities"** is a strong limiting condition: the risk must be *specific to* frontier-level capabilities. Risks any model poses (e.g. generic misinformation) wouldn't be systemic unless tied to high-impact capabilities. That's why the M2.1 compilation step asked for risks that "could stem from the model *and be systemic*". It also explains why the Chapter's specified risks are capability-heavy (CBRN, cyber, …).
- **"Match or exceed the capabilities recorded in the most advanced"**: a relative, moving standard. As the frontier moves, what counts as high-impact moves. A model that was high-impact in 2025 may not be in 2027. That's consistent with the "similarly safe or safer" logic: models below the frontier carry less.
- **"Due to their reach, or due to…"**: two routes to significance: *reach* (scale of the model's use, perhaps irrespective of harm type), or *negative effects* on the listed objects. The "or" matters: a model with enormous reach can have systemic risk through reach alone. That links to the 'model' definition's inclusion of versions "used widely" (unit 83).
- **"Propagated at scale across the value chain"**: harm that travels through downstream integrations. This is why the Code cares about downstream modifiers and providers (recital (e), M9.1).
- **The listed objects** ("public health, safety, public security, fundamental rights, or the society as a whole") will likely be Appendix 1.1's "types of risks". "Society as a whole" is the catch-all where democracy, information ecosystems, and perhaps environment live.
- **For our lexicon:** this is the anchor definition for the Code's bounded context. Note its ambiguity: "a risk that is specific to the high-impact capabilities… having a significant impact…": is the impact required (actual) or potential? "Actual or reasonably foreseeable negative effects" makes effects potential. But "having a significant impact on the Union market" is grammatically actual. A fork asked "must a systemic risk have already caused impact?" should point to "actual or reasonably foreseeable", which governs the effects, and note that the "having a significant impact" clause reads awkwardly.
- **Recital 110 AI Act** will follow. I believe it lists examples of systemic risks: major accidents, disruptions of critical sectors, serious consequences to public health and safety, negative effects on democratic processes, public and economic security, dissemination of illegal, false or discriminatory content; CBRN; offensive cyber; interference with critical infrastructure; models making copies of themselves or "self-replicating" or training other models; discrimination; disinformation; privacy. If so, it's where loss-of-control vocabulary first entered EU law.

**Questions to keep.**
- "How does the Code define 'systemic risk'?" It quotes Art. 3(65) AI Act (Appendix 1 LEGAL TEXT): "a risk that is specific to the high-impact capabilities of general-purpose AI models, having a significant impact on the Union market due to their reach, or due to actual or reasonably foreseeable negative effects on public health, safety, public security, fundamental rights, or the society as a whole, that can be propagated at scale across the value chain".
- "What are high-impact capabilities?" Art. 3(64): "capabilities that match or exceed the capabilities recorded in the most advanced general-purpose AI models".
- Should be asked but might not be: "Can a risk be systemic because of reach alone?" Art. 3(65)'s "due to their reach, or due to…" suggests yes.
