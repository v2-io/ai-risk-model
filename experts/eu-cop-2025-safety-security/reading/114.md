# Unit 114 · L913–922 · p.40: Appendix 3.5, Independent external model evaluations — duty, exceptions, qualification (1)

**What it held.**
- "In addition to internal model evaluations, Signatories **will ensure** that adequately qualified independent external evaluators conduct model evaluations pursuant to this Appendix 3, **with regards to the systemic risk**, **unless**:
  - (1) the model is a similarly safe or safer model pursuant to Appendix 2.2; or
  - (2) Signatories **fail to appoint** adequately qualified independent external evaluators, **despite using early search efforts (such as through a public call open for 20 business days)** and promptly notifying identified evaluators, **in which case** Signatories will take into account the **potential additional uncertainty arising from the absence of independent external evaluations**… when determining whether the systemic risks… are acceptable…."
- "Adequate qualification of independent external evaluators requires: (1) having significant domain expertise for the systemic risk and being technically skilled and experienced in conducting model evaluations;" (continues)

**What differed.** Exactly the two exceptions I predicted (memory, again). Not predicted: the **public call open for 20 business days** as the example of search effort, and the **uncertainty penalty**: if no external evaluator is found, the acceptance determination must account for the extra uncertainty.

**Wandering.**
- **Pre-market external evaluation is the default** ("will ensure… unless"). These are the "conditions in Appendix 3.5, first paragraph, points (1) or (2)" that M7.4(2) referred to. Now the M7.4 chain is complete: absence of external evaluators must be justified by one of these two conditions.
- **The uncertainty penalty is clever.** Not finding an evaluator isn't free: the missing evaluation becomes added uncertainty in C4, presumably widening the safety margin. So a signatory has an incentive to find one. And a lazy search can't be cost-free.
- **"With regards to the systemic risk"**: external evaluation per risk. A model may need external CBRN evaluators and not cyber evaluators, or vice versa (per-risk similarly-safe status, or per-risk failure to find evaluators).
- **"Early search efforts"** and "promptly notifying": the provider must look early, not at the last minute when nobody's available. 20 business days for the call.
- **Who are qualified external evaluators?** The list starts: significant domain expertise and technical evaluation skill. I expect independence (Glossary), security capacity, and willingness to keep confidentiality.
- **Is there an AI Office role in choosing evaluators?** Not so far. The signatory appoints; M7.4(3) requires explaining the choice. AISIs could be appointed as external evaluators, presumably, if "independent external".

**Questions to keep.**
- "Is independent external evaluation required before release?" Appendix 3.5: yes, "Signatories will ensure that adequately qualified independent external evaluators conduct model evaluations… with regards to the systemic risk, unless" (1) the model is similarly safe or safer, or (2) none could be appointed despite early search efforts (e.g. a public call open 20 business days) and prompt notification — in which case the added uncertainty is taken into account in the acceptance determination.
- Should be asked but might not be: "What's the cost of not finding an external evaluator?" "The potential additional uncertainty arising from the absence of independent external evaluations" must be taken into account in C4.

**Prediction for unit 115.** Qualification continued: (2) appropriate security protocols / capacity to protect model and confidential info; (3) independence (Glossary 'independent external'); (4) commitment to confidentiality and not misusing access; then: external evaluators get access/resources per 3.4; signatories won't unduly influence them; evaluators may publish findings (subject to M3.5-like disclosure terms); maybe "the AI Office may…".

# Unit 115 · L923–930 · p.40: Appendix 3.5 qualification (2)–(3); access; integrity; SMEs

**What it held.**
- "(2) having appropriate internal and external information security protocols in place; and (3) having agreed to protect commercially confidential information, if they need access to such information."
- "Signatories will provide independent external evaluators with adequate access, information, time, and other resources (pursuant to Appendix 3.4), **without prejudice to Appendix 4.4, point (1)**. Signatories will **not undermine the integrity of external model evaluations by storing and/or analysing inputs and/or outputs from test runs without express permission** from the evaluators."
- "Signatories that are SMEs or SMCs may contact the AI Office, which may provide support or resources to facilitate adherence to this Appendix 3.5."

**What differed.** Qualification (security protocols, confidentiality) predicted in substance. Independence isn't in the qualification list: it's carried by the term itself ('independent external', Glossary). The access link to 3.4 confirmed. Not predicted:
- The pre-market integrity rule is **stricter than M3.5's post-market one**: here signatories may not even **store or analyse** the test runs' inputs/outputs without permission, whereas M3.5 barred *training* on them (and limited use to risk assessment). Pre-market: don't look at evaluators' prompts at all, without permission.
- "**Without prejudice to Appendix 4.4, point (1)**": some security measure in Appendix 4.4 limits evaluator access. Probably something like "limiting access to unreleased model weights" (security over full weight access). I'll read Appendix 4.4 with that in mind.
- No explicit publication right here; M7.4 handles it ("maintain control over the publication of their findings").

**Wandering.**
- **Why forbid storing and analysing?** If the provider sees the evaluators' test prompts, it can patch exactly those (teaching to the test), making later evaluations unrepresentative; it can also learn the evaluators' methods (their IP). The integrity of evaluation depends on test secrecy. The Code makes the pre-market regime stricter because these are the evaluations that decide release.
- **The external-evaluation regime, assembled**: default duty (3.5); exceptions (similarly safe; failure to appoint after early public search, with an uncertainty penalty); qualifications (domain + technical skill; security protocols; confidentiality; independence by definition); resources (3.4: white-box-ish access, information, ~20 business days, compute, staffing assurances, engineering); integrity (no storing/analysing test runs without permission); reporting (M7.4: their reports, under their control of publication, no implied endorsement; M7.2(3): whether and how their input informed the decision; M7.4(3): why that evaluator). A complete regime, anchored in an appendix.
- **SME support** again: AI Office "may provide support or resources". Perhaps the AI Office itself, or AISIs, could act as external evaluators for SMEs.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What qualifies an external evaluator?" Appendix 3.5: significant domain expertise and technical evaluation skill/experience; appropriate internal and external information-security protocols; agreement to protect commercially confidential information; and independence (Glossary 'independent external').
- "Can a provider look at the external evaluators' test prompts?" Not without "express permission": it "will not undermine the integrity of external model evaluations by storing and/or analysing inputs and/or outputs from test runs".
- Should be asked but might not be: "What limits external evaluators' access?" "Without prejudice to Appendix 4.4, point (1)" (security). Read 4.4.

**Prediction for unit 116.** Appendix 4, Security mitigations: 4.1 General security mitigations (e.g. (a) network security, access controls, zero trust…; (b) objectives); 4.2 Protection of unreleased model parameters (secure storage, encryption, limited access, secure transport); 4.3 Hardening interface-access to unreleased models (API security, rate limits); 4.4 Insider threats (background checks, two-person rules, limiting weight access, monitoring); 4.5 Security assurance (independent external security reviews, red-teaming / penetration tests, bug bounties, incident response).
