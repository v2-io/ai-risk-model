# Unit 37 · L307–317 · p.15: Measure 4.1, acceptance criteria (tiers) — first part

**What it held.**
- "Signatories will describe and justify (in the Framework…) how they will determine whether the systemic risks stemming from the model are acceptable. To do so, Signatories will:"
- "(1) for each identified systemic risk…, at least:
  - (a) define appropriate systemic risk tiers that:
    - (i) **are defined in terms of model capabilities**, and **may additionally incorporate** model propensities, risk estimates, and/or other suitable metrics;
    - (ii) **are measurable**; and
    - (iii) **comprise at least one systemic risk tier that has not been reached by the model**; or
  - (b) define other appropriate systemic risk acceptance criteria, **if systemic risk tiers are not suitable for the systemic risk and the systemic risk is not a specified systemic risk** (pursuant to Appendix 1.4);"
- "(2) describe how they will use these tiers and/or other criteria to determine whether **each identified systemic risk… and the overall systemic risk** are acceptable; and" (continues)

**What differed.** I predicted capability-defined, measurable tiers. Not predicted:
- **(iii) at least one tier not yet reached.** That closes the gap I flagged at unit 13 ("what happens to the timelines forecast when a firm reaches its top tier?"). The tier ladder must always have a rung above the model. The forecast in M1.1(2)(c) never goes silent: there's always a next tier to forecast. I'm pleased; I'd thought it a gap and the Code had already closed it.
- **Specified risks must use tiers.** Option (b) (other acceptance criteria) is available only for non-specified risks where tiers don't suit. So for every specified systemic risk (CBRN, etc., whatever Appendix 1.4 holds), there must be capability-defined, measurable tiers. Strong harmonisation of form across signatories.
- **"Overall systemic risk"** must also be determined acceptable, not only each risk. Aggregation appears.
- No safety margin yet. Maybe in point (3).

**Wandering.**

- **Capabilities are mandatory; propensities optional.** Tiers "are defined in terms of model capabilities" and "may additionally incorporate model propensities, risk estimates". So the Code's acceptance architecture is capability-first. Propensity (e.g. a misaligned disposition) can enter tiers but can't stand alone as a tier definition. That's a real modelling commitment: the Code thinks risk tiers are anchored in what models can do. For loss of control, where the worry is about propensity (e.g. scheming), a capability-only tier (e.g. "ability to evade monitoring", "ability to self-exfiltrate") is the anchor; propensity is an add-on. A fork should say this precisely, because loss-of-control researchers might expect propensity-based thresholds.
- **"Risk estimates" may be incorporated into tiers.** So a tier can combine "capability X" with "estimated probability of harm Y". That's the bridge between the threshold model (tiers) and the probability × severity model (M3.4) that I worried about at unit 12. The Code permits but doesn't require the bridge. So most signatories can use pure capability thresholds and treat M3.4's estimates as context. The two risk models coexist, loosely coupled.
- **"Measurable"**: tiers must be measurable. A tier like "the model is dangerous" isn't. A tier like "the model provides significant uplift to novices in synthesising agent X, as measured by uplift study Y" is. Measurability makes tiers falsifiable by evaluation, which links back to M3.2.
- **"Overall systemic risk."** Each risk can be acceptable while the sum isn't. E.g. moderate cyber + moderate manipulation + moderate CBRN might together be unacceptable. The Code requires a description of how overall acceptability is determined but gives no method. That's an under-specified but important requirement: aggregation across risk types is notoriously hard (incommensurable harms). A question someone should ask: "how do signatories aggregate risks?" The Code requires them to describe it.
- **"At least"** after "(1) for each identified systemic risk…": a signatory may add more criteria than tiers.
- Imagining a signatory's CBRN tiers: Tier 1 — model provides meaningful uplift to novices on known agents; Tier 2 — uplift to experts / novel agents; Tier 3 — autonomous end-to-end capability. Defined in terms of capability, measured by evaluations, with at least one tier unreached. This is nearly the shape of Anthropic's ASL thresholds or OpenAI's High/Critical. The Code harmonises the *form* (capability-defined, measurable, with headroom) and leaves the *content* (where the thresholds sit) to signatories.

**Questions to keep.**
- "How must systemic risk tiers be defined?" M4.1(1)(a): "defined in terms of model capabilities" (may also incorporate propensities, risk estimates, other metrics); "measurable"; "at least one systemic risk tier that has not been reached by the model".
- "Can a signatory use criteria other than tiers?" Only for non-specified risks where tiers "are not suitable" (M4.1(1)(b)). Specified risks always get tiers.
- "Does the Code use propensity-based thresholds?" Propensities may be incorporated into tiers; tiers must be defined in terms of capabilities.
- Should be asked but might not be: "How is overall (aggregate) systemic risk judged acceptable?" M4.1(2) requires a description; no method given.

**Prediction for unit 38.** M4.1(3): probably "take into account a safety margin" — determination of acceptability must include margins for uncertainty in evaluations, for capability improvements post-market (e.g. scaffolding, fine-tuning), and for limitations of mitigations. Then the determination itself: "Signatories will determine whether the systemic risk is acceptable by comparing… with the criteria", and "risk is acceptable if… the model hasn't reached a tier for which the mitigations aren't in place".
