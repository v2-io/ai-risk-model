# Unit 81 · L643–658 · p.29: Glossary — the precedence clause; 'appropriate' to 'independent external'

**What it held.**
- **The precedence clause**: "Wherever this Chapter refers to a term defined in Article 3 AI Act, the AI Act definition applies, and such definition **shall prevail in the event of any alternative and/or competing interpretation** to which the use of the term in this Chapter may give rise. Otherwise and complementing this, the following terms with the stated meanings are used in this Chapter. Unless otherwise stated, all grammatical variations of the terms… shall be deemed to be covered."
- Entries (a table; canonical text splits the 'external validity' and 'high scientific and technical rigour' rows across two table rows each, a conversion artefact):
  - **'appropriate'**: "suitable and necessary to achieve the intended purpose of systemic risk assessment and/or mitigation, whether through best practices, the state of the art, or other more innovative processes, measures, methodologies, methods, or techniques **that go beyond the state of the art**."
  - **'best practice'**: "**accepted amongst providers** of general-purpose AI models with systemic risk as the processes, measures, methodologies, methods, and techniques that best assess and mitigate systemic risks at any given point in time."
  - **'confirmed'**: "a Framework or Model Report, or an update thereof, that has received required approvals under the applicable governance procedures."
  - **'deception'**: "model behaviours that **systematically produce false beliefs in others**, including model behaviours to achieve goals that involve evading oversight, such as **a model's detecting that it is being evaluated and under-performing** or otherwise undermining oversight."
  - **'external validity'**: "an aspect of high scientific and technical rigour… that ensures model evaluations are suitably calibrated for results to be used as a proxy for model behaviour outside the evaluation environment. Demonstrating external validity will differ…, but may be shown by, e.g. documenting the model evaluation environment, the ways in which it diverges from the real-world context, and the diversity of the model evaluation environment."
  - **'high scientific and technical rigour'**: "the quality standard for model evaluations, such that model evaluations with high scientific and technical rigour have internal validity… and external validity…, as well as being reproducible…. See further Appendix 3.2."
  - **'including'**: "introduces a **non-exhaustive set that is to be understood as the minimum required** by the term referred to and is indicative of further items of the set."
  - **'independent external'**: "a natural or legal person that has **no financial, operational, or management dependence** on the Signatory or any of its subsidiaries or associates, and is otherwise free from the Signatory's control in reaching conclusions and/or making recommendations, including through contractual safeguards and suitable conflict of interest policies."

**What differed, and the priming check.**
- **"Shall prevail" found.** It's here, at the head of the Glossary, exactly where I guessed at unit 80. Now I can read it whole. It says more than the gloss I'd held: AI Act Art. 3 definitions *apply* wherever the Chapter uses an Art. 3 term, and *prevail* over "any alternative and/or competing interpretation" to which the Chapter's *use* of the term may give rise. So it's a conflict rule about interpretations, not only about definitions. And then: "Otherwise and complementing this" the Glossary's own terms apply. So, combined with recital (i): Glossary terms have the Glossary's meaning; Art. 3 terms have the Act's meaning, which prevails. These cohere: the Glossary defines terms the Act doesn't (or complements). Any Glossary entry that redefined an Art. 3 term would lose to the Act. A fork must give both clauses together.
- **'appropriate' includes going *beyond* the state of the art.** I'd assumed "appropriate" was a soft word. It's defined to be "suitable *and necessary*" for the purpose, achieved by best practice, the state of the art, or methods *beyond* the state of the art. So "appropriate" is not weaker than "state of the art"; it may require more. Every "appropriate" in the Chapter now carries this definition. That changes how I read the thin Measures (M2.2, M3.3, M5.1, etc.): their "appropriate" is a defined, demanding standard.
- **'best practice' is defined by industry acceptance**: "accepted amongst providers". So best practice is what providers agree is best, a self-referential standard (contrast "state of the art", which I'd guess is defined by the research frontier). Best practice ≠ state of the art. And 'appropriate' can be met through best practice. Taken together: a signatory could meet "appropriate" through what providers accept, which could lag the state of the art. But the 'appropriate' definition also says "suitable and necessary to achieve the intended purpose", which constrains that. Tension worth recording.
- **'including' is defined as a minimum.** That's a strong interpretive rule: every list introduced by "including" is a floor, not an example set. E.g. M9.2's "at least the following" and M7.3's "including" lists are minima. But lists introduced by "such as", "e.g.", "examples… are" aren't. A fork extracting obligations must distinguish "including" (minimum) from "e.g."/"such as"/"examples" (illustrative). I didn't expect a definition of "including".
- **'deception' is defined and includes sandbagging** ("detecting that it is being evaluated and under-performing"). That's evaluation awareness, the 2025 concern, in a legal glossary. Deception is defined behaviourally ("model behaviours that systematically produce false beliefs in others"), not by intent. Why define deception? Presumably because a specified systemic risk (loss of control?) or a model propensity in Appendix 1 uses it. Read Appendix 1 with this in hand.
- **'confirmed'** answers unit 14: approvals "under the applicable governance procedures", i.e. per the signatory's own procedures (M1.1(4)). Self-defined approval, as I thought.
- **'independent external'** is strict: no financial, operational *or management* dependence, on the signatory "or any of its subsidiaries or associates", and freedom from control "including through contractual safeguards and suitable conflict of interest policies". A paid evaluator? Paying for a service is a financial relationship; is it "dependence"? The definition says "dependence", not "relationship": a contractor paid per engagement, with conflict-of-interest safeguards, probably qualifies; one whose revenue mostly comes from the signatory wouldn't. A fork should flag the line.

**Wandering.**
- **The definitions are where the Code's teeth hide.** I spent units thinking "appropriate" was the Code's soft spot. It's defined to be at least as strong as the state of the art, possibly stronger. That's the single biggest correction to my reading so far. It confirms the brief's warning: the part I thought I knew ("appropriate" is a hedge) is where I most needed to read.
- **The epistemology of evaluation**: "high scientific and technical rigour" = internal validity + external validity + reproducibility. That's methodology from experimental science, imported into AI evaluation regulation. External validity is defined operationally (documenting divergence from real-world context, diversity of environments). For our lexicon: three named properties of a good evaluation, defined by the source.
- **Deception and oversight**: the definition's second half ("model behaviours to achieve goals that involve evading oversight") moves from deception-of-users to deception-of-overseers. Loss of control's vocabulary is building in the Glossary.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Do AI Act definitions override the Code's?" Glossary head: Art. 3 AI Act definitions apply and "shall prevail in the event of any alternative and/or competing interpretation"; the Glossary's terms complement them. Combine with recital (i).
- "What does 'appropriate' mean in the Code?" Defined: "suitable and necessary to achieve the intended purpose…, whether through best practices, the state of the art, or… methods… that go beyond the state of the art." A question someone should ask, because the word reads as a hedge and isn't one.
- "What's the difference between best practice and state of the art?" 'best practice' is what's "accepted amongst providers"; 'state of the art' (to come).
- "Is a list introduced by 'including' exhaustive?" No, but it's "the minimum required".
- "How does the Code define deception?" Behaviourally, including evaluation-aware under-performance and undermining oversight.
- "What makes an evaluator 'independent external'?" No financial, operational or management dependence on the signatory or its subsidiaries or associates; free from its control, with contractual safeguards and conflict-of-interest policies.
