# Unit 22 · L162–177 · p.10: Commitment 2, Systemic risk identification

**What it held.**
- "**LEGAL TEXT:** Article 55(1) and recital 110 AI Act". (Intact here, unlike C1's, which supports my unit-11 suspicion that C1's line lost numbers in conversion. C1 cited more provisions and lost some of them; this one is short and clean.)
- "Signatories commit to identifying the systemic risks stemming from the model. The purpose of systemic risk identification **includes** facilitating systemic risk analysis (pursuant to Commitment 3) and systemic risk acceptance determination (pursuant to Commitment 4)."
- Two elements: "(1) following a structured process to identify the systemic risks stemming from the model (as specified in Measure 2.1); and (2) developing **systemic risk scenarios** for each identified systemic risk (as specified in Measure 2.2)."
- Figure 4 caption, with the precedence disclaimer.

**What differed.**
- I predicted C2's two parts as "a process" and "specified risks treated as identified". The second element is **scenarios**, not the specified risks. Scenarios are part of *identification*, not analysis. I'd have put scenarios in analysis. The Code treats "what could happen, how" as part of naming the risk.
- The purpose "**includes**" facilitating analysis and acceptance: identification has other purposes too, unnamed. The Code avoids closing the list.
- No specified-risk language yet. Probably in M2.1, pointing to Appendix 1.

**Wandering.**
- **"The systemic risks stemming from the model."** The phrase "stemming from" has appeared since C1 and is consistent. Risks stem *from the model*. That's a causal-direction choice: the model is the source, not the deployer or the user. Combined with recital (b) (assessment includes the reasonably foreseeable system context), it means: risks are attributed to the model, assessed in context. For our causal chain (sources & causes → events → impacts), the Code places the *model* as the focal source. Misuse by humans is a risk "stemming from the model" too, presumably via the model's capabilities. A fork mapping "stemming from" into our lexicon should note it's a source-attribution relation, not a strict causal one.
- **Scenarios as part of identification.** A systemic risk, in the Code's sense, seems to be a *category* (e.g. CBRN), and a scenario is a concrete path by which that category could be realised by this model. So identification produces: a list of risk categories that apply, and for each, scenarios. Analysis (C3) then estimates them. That's close to the bow-tie: the risk is the focal event-type, scenarios are threat paths. It's also close to SB 53's model of catastrophic risk, if that is scenario-based (not known from here).
- **"For each identified systemic risk."** Every identified risk gets scenarios. So scenarios are mandatory, not optional illustrations. That's a strong analytical demand: you can't just say "cyber risk: tier 1"; you must say how the cyber risk would play out.
- **Figure 4 lost again.** Four figures in five pages, all in canonical text as captions only. I'm starting to suspect the PDF has a heavily diagrammatic presentation of the process that the text alone conveys more drily.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What does systemic risk identification consist of?" C2: a structured process (M2.1) and systemic risk scenarios for each identified risk (M2.2).
- "Are scenarios part of identification or analysis?" Identification (C2(2)). A question someone should ask, because most frameworks put scenarios in analysis.
- "What's the legal basis of C2?" Art. 55(1) and recital 110 AI Act.

**Prediction for unit 23.** Measure 2.1, systemic risk identification process: (1) compile a list of risks that could stem from the model, drawing on the types of risks in Appendix 1.1, taking into account model-independent information (literature, incidents, stakeholder input) and model-specific information (evaluations, characteristics in Appendix 1.3); (2) the specified systemic risks in Appendix 1.4 are always treated as identified; (3) other risks are identified if they meet the systemic-risk definition, considering the nature of the risk (Appendix 1.2).
