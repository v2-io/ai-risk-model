# Unit 58 · L481–483 · p.22: M7.6 timing; deliberate changes; the six-month periodic update and its exceptions

**What it held.**
- "Model Report updates **should** be completed within **a reasonable amount of time** after the Signatory becomes aware of the grounds…, e.g. after discovering them as part of their continuous systemic risk assessment and mitigation (pursuant to Measure 1.2, second paragraph). **If a Model Report update is triggered by a deliberate change to a model and that change is made available on the market, the Model Report update and the underlying full systemic risk assessment and mitigation process… need to be completed before the change is made available on the market.**"
- "Further, **if the model is amongst their respective most capable models available on the market**, Signatories will provide the AI Office with an updated Model Report **at least every six months**. Signatories do not need to do so if: (1) the model's capabilities, propensities, and/or affordances have not changed since… the Model Report, or update thereof; (2) they will place a more capable model on the market in less than a month; and/or (3) the model is considered similarly safe or safer (pursuant to Appendix 2.2) for each identified systemic risk."

**What differed.** I predicted a six-month periodic rule for the most capable models (memory). Not predicted:
- **"Should"**, not "will", for the reasonable-time standard. One of the few "should"s in operative text. The timing of event-driven updates is soft.
- **The deliberate-change rule**: if *you* change the model and release the change, the update and full assessment come *before* release. Hard sequencing for self-caused changes; soft timing for externally discovered grounds.
- **Three exceptions** to the six-month rule, including "a more capable model in less than a month" (no point updating a soon-superseded model's report) and Appendix 2.2's "similarly safe or safer".

**Wandering.**

- **The two-speed update regime makes sense.** For deliberate changes the provider controls the timing, so it can do the assessment first; the Code requires it. For discovered problems (incidents, new research, evaluation-validity doubts), the provider can't have anticipated them, so it gets "a reasonable amount of time". "Should" softens even that. Whether "reasonable" is days or months: unspecified. Purposive interpretation pushes towards quickly when risk is high.
- **"Deliberate change… made available on the market"**: and a deliberate change *not* made available (used internally)? No sequencing rule. Again the market anchor. A lab can deploy a materially changed model internally without first updating the Model Report.
- **"Amongst their respective most capable models available on the market"**: "respective" = each signatory's own most capable models. "Amongst" makes it fuzzy: the top one? top few? A fork should flag the vagueness: the six-month rule applies to models "amongst their respective most capable models available on the market", undefined beyond that.
- **Exception (1) "not changed"**: if nothing about the model changed in six months, no update. But the *world* may have changed (new evaluations, incidents with similar models). Those are event-driven grounds under the first paragraph, so they're covered there. Coherent.
- **Exception (3), Appendix 2.2, once more.** "Similarly safe or safer" now lightens: post-market external access (M3.5), periodic Model Report updates (M7.6), and must be justified in the Report (M7.3(1)(h)). It's becoming clear that Appendix 2 is a general lightening device for models that aren't pushing the frontier of risk.
- **"Third paragraph"** of M7.6 (cross-referenced at unit 16 as a trigger for the full process): that's the six-month periodic one, if I count paragraphs: (1) grounds, (2) timing/deliberate change, (3) six-month rule. So a full systemic risk assessment precedes each six-monthly update for frontier models. That makes the full process at least semi-annual for the most capable marketed models. A substantial burden, and a strong oversight cadence.

**Questions to keep.**
- "How often must Model Reports be updated?" Event-driven (M7.6 grounds), "within a reasonable amount of time" (should); before market release of a deliberate change; and at least every six months for models "amongst their respective most capable models available on the market", unless unchanged, superseded within a month, or similarly safe or safer.
- "Must a full risk assessment precede releasing a model update?" Yes, for a deliberate change made available on the market (M7.6, second paragraph).
- Should be asked but might not be: "Does a model change deployed only internally trigger a pre-deployment Model Report update?" Not by M7.6's terms.

**Prediction for unit 59.** Measure 7.7, Model Report notifications: Signatories will provide the AI Office with (unredacted) access to the Model Report (and updates) by the time they place the model on the market, or within N business days of an update. Possibly a provision for the AI Office to ask for more info, or for staggered submission when evaluations are ongoing. Then Commitment 8.
