# Unit 35 · L297–299 · p.15: M3.5 condition (5), responsible disclosure and the 30-business-day limit; SME support

**What it held.**
- "(5) adhere to the Signatory's **publicly available procedure for responsible vulnerability disclosure**, which will specify at least that **the Signatory cannot delay or block publication for more than 30 business days** from the date that the Signatory is made aware of the findings, **unless a longer timeline is exceptionally necessary such as if disclosure of the findings would materially increase the systemic risk**."
- "Signatories that are SMEs or SMCs may contact the AI Office, which may provide support or resources to facilitate adherence to this Measure."

**What differed.** I predicted a responsible-disclosure condition (right). Not predicted:
- A **hard ceiling** on the provider's power to delay: **30 business days**. That's a constraint on the provider, embedded in a condition on the evaluator. Clever drafting: the evaluator must follow the provider's disclosure procedure, *and* that procedure must cap delay at 30 business days.
- The provider must have a **publicly available** disclosure procedure. Second public element (after the selection criteria).
- **SME relief here is support, not exemption**: SMEs/SMCs "may contact the AI Office, which may provide support or resources". Not an exemption from external-evaluator access.

**Wandering.**

- **The structure is elegant.** Non-retaliation is conditioned on evaluators following the provider's procedure; the procedure must contain the 30-day cap; so the provider can't write a procedure that blocks publication indefinitely and then retaliate against those who publish. The condition protects the provider from ambush and the evaluator from suppression.
- **"Delay or block."** "Block" is included, so a provider can't permanently suppress findings either, beyond 30 business days (~6 weeks), save the exception.
- **The exception is the soft spot:** "exceptionally necessary such as if disclosure… would materially increase the systemic risk". Info-hazard logic: publishing a working jailbreak for a bio-capable model before it's patched would increase risk. Legitimate. But who decides "exceptionally necessary"? The provider, in the first instance. The word "exceptionally" and the purposive rule push against overuse. A fork should name the exception as the place where suppression could re-enter.
- **Business days, not calendar days**: the Code uses "business days" for short deadlines (five business days in M1.4; 30 here) and weeks for longer ones (M1.1). Worth recording consistently in any extraction: units matter.
- **"Made aware of the findings"**: the clock starts on provider awareness, not on evaluator discovery. An evaluator who sits on findings doesn't burn the provider's window.
- **The SME line** is the second SME mention (after recital (h)). It's a small sign that the AI Office is cast as a resource provider, not only a regulator. And "may… may": doubly permissive.
- **M3.5 ends here**, I think. In total it's the richest Measure so far: monitoring for disconfirmation; eleven example methods; free external-evaluator access to the most capable marketed version, its CoT, and the least-mitigated version; published selection criteria; data-use limits; non-retaliation with five conditions; a public responsible-disclosure procedure with a 30-business-day cap. This is where the Code most directly creates public-interest checks on providers. Interesting that it's housed under *post-market monitoring*, not under a Commitment on external scrutiny.

**Questions to keep.**
- "How long can a provider hold back an evaluator's findings?" M3.5(5): no more than "30 business days from the date that the Signatory is made aware of the findings", unless "exceptionally necessary such as if disclosure… would materially increase the systemic risk".
- "Must providers publish a vulnerability disclosure procedure?" Yes, it must be "publicly available" (M3.5(5)).
- "Are SMEs exempt from external-evaluator access?" Not here: they "may contact the AI Office, which may provide support or resources".
