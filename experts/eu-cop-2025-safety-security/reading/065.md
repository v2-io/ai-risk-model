# Unit 65 · L528 · p.24: M8.1 presumption (4), assurance

**What it held.** "(4) **Systemic risk assurance:** The responsibility for providing assurance… to the management body in its supervisory function or another suitable independent body… has been assigned to a relevant party (e.g. a **Chief Audit Executive, a Head of Internal Audit, or a relevant sub-committee**). This individual is **supported by an internal audit function, or equivalent**, and external assurance as appropriate. The Signatories' internal assurance activities are appropriate. For Signatories that are SMEs or SMCs, the management body in its supervisory function periodically assesses the Signatory's systemic risk assessment and mitigation processes… (e.g. **by approving the Signatory's Framework assessment**)."

(Checkpoint aside: Joseph's reading was 379k at unit 60, and the /context view shows 381k after unit 64.)

**What differed.** Predicted, and it answers my unit-63 question: the presumption *does* require an internal audit function "or equivalent". So the earlier "if available" in the levels list means: without internal audit, you don't get the presumption and must show fulfilment otherwise. The incentive structure I guessed holds.

**Wandering.**
- **"The Signatories' internal assurance activities are appropriate."** A condition of the presumption that's itself a judgement ("appropriate"), placed among structural conditions. It makes the presumption partly self-referential: the presumption holds if your structures are in place *and* your assurance is appropriate. A drafting softness: the presumption is supposed to make compliance easy to establish, and this clause reintroduces a judgement.
- **SME route**: the board "periodically assesses" the processes, e.g. by **approving the Framework assessment**. That's the first mention of who might approve a Framework assessment (M1.3). It suggests that for larger firms too, the board (or its committee) could be the natural approver. So "confirmed" (for Frameworks) might well mean board approval in practice, though the Code doesn't say so.
- **The governance model is complete**: board committee oversight; executive risk owners who run research/product; an independent CRO-type function that conducts assessments; internal audit reporting to the board. It's a bank. Whether frontier labs have these functions in 2025 is another question: some had a Chief Risk Officer or a safety head; few had internal audit for AI risk. The presumption sets a target organisational form.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Does the Code require an internal audit function?" Not strictly; it's a condition of M8.1's presumption ("supported by an internal audit function, or equivalent"). Without it, the signatory must show M8.1 is met otherwise.
- "Who approves a Framework assessment?" Not specified; for SMEs/SMCs the board's periodic assessment can be done "by approving the Signatory's Framework assessment".

**Prediction for unit 66.** End of M8.1 (maybe a line on reviewing the allocation). Measure 8.2, Allocation of appropriate resources: Signatories will ensure that those with systemic-risk responsibilities have appropriate resources: human, financial, access to information and knowledge, computational resources; and the management body oversees this. Possibly examples: "sufficient compute for evaluations", "access to models", "time".
