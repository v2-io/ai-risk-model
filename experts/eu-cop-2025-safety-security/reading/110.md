# Unit 110 · L888–894 · p.39: Appendix 3.3, Assessing the effectiveness of mitigations

**What it held.** "Signatories will ensure that the model evaluations assess the effectiveness of their **safety** mitigations at a breadth and depth **appropriate for the extent to which systemic risk acceptance determination depends on the effectiveness of specific mitigations**, including under adversarial pressure (e.g. finetuning attacks or jailbreaking). To this end…, taking into account: (1) the extent to which their mitigations work as planned; (2) the extent to which their mitigations **are or have been circumvented, deactivated, or subverted**; and (3) **the probability that the effectiveness of their mitigations will change in the future**."

**What differed.** Predicted the theme. Not predicted: the **proportionality rule**, testing depth scales with *how much the acceptance determination depends on* specific mitigations. And it's about **safety** mitigations only (security mitigations are assessed by Appendix 4's external review, presumably).

**Wandering.**
- **The dependency-proportional rule is excellent design.** If acceptability rests on a refusal classifier, test that classifier hard; if acceptability rests on the model's inherent lack of capability, the mitigations matter less. It follows from M7.3(1)(d)(ii)'s mitigated-vs-fully-elicited comparison: the bigger the gap, the more the conclusion depends on mitigations, so the deeper their testing must be. A coherent chain: comparison → dependency → testing depth.
- **The triad again**: circumvented, deactivated, subverted (M4.1 safety margin). The Code uses the same three verbs at margin-setting and at evaluation. Consistent vocabulary.
- **(3) future effectiveness probability**: mitigations decay (new jailbreak techniques; fine-tuning attacks get cheaper). The signatory must estimate how likely its mitigations are to weaken. This links to M7.6's grounds (mitigations becoming less effective) and the safety margin's "changes".
- **"Finetuning"** (one word here, "fine-tuning" elsewhere): drafting inconsistency, no meaning.
