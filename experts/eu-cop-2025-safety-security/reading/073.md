# Unit 73 · L591–599 · p.26: Measure 9.3, Reporting timelines (1)–(2)

**What it held.** "Signatories will provide the information in **points (1) to (7) of Measure 9.2 in an initial report**…, at the following points in time, **save in exceptional circumstances**, if the involvement of their model (directly or indirectly) led to:
1. a **serious and irreversible disruption of the management or operation of critical infrastructure**, or if the Signatories establish or **suspect with reasonable likelihood** such a causal relationship…, **not later than two days** after the Signatories become aware of the involvement of their model in the incident;
2. a **serious cybersecurity breach, including the (self-)exfiltration of model weights and cyberattacks**, or if… [same suspicion clause]…, **not later than five days** after…;" (continues)

**What differed.** The two-day and five-day tiers match my memory (from unit 0 and unit 72: 2/5/10/15). Not predicted:
- **"(Self-)exfiltration of model weights"**: a model exfiltrating *itself* named as a reportable cybersecurity breach. Loss of control appears in incident reporting.
- **Initial report = points (1)–(7)** only. Root-cause analysis (8) and patterns (9) come later.
- **"Save in exceptional circumstances"**: an escape valve on the deadlines.
- **"Suspect with reasonable likelihood"**: reporting is triggered by reasonable suspicion of a causal link, not only by established causation.

**Wandering.**
- **Self-exfiltration in a legal deadline.** "(Self-)" in parentheses: the Code's typographic habit for an optional or extended reading. So weight exfiltration counts whether done by an attacker or by the model itself. This is the first explicit appearance, in operative text, of a canonical loss-of-control event. It's in the *cybersecurity* category, not a loss-of-control category: the Code classifies incidents by harm type (infrastructure, cyber, …), and a self-exfiltration is a breach. A fork asked "does the Code require reporting if a model tries to escape?" should say: (self-)exfiltration of model weights is a serious cybersecurity breach, initial report within five days of awareness. Attempted? The text says "breach… including… exfiltration"; an attempt that failed might be a near miss (reportable via patterns, (9)), not a serious incident. Ambiguous for attempts.
- **The harm categories follow the AI Act's Art. 3(49) "serious incident"** definition, as I remember it: (a) death or serious harm to health, (b) serious and irreversible disruption of critical infrastructure, (c) infringement of fundamental-rights obligations, (d) serious harm to property or the environment. The Code adds "serious cybersecurity breach" as its own category (Art. 3(49) doesn't list it, I believe). So the Code *widens* the serious-incident concept for GPAI models. I'll check the Glossary to confirm.
- **"Aware of the involvement of their model"**: the clock starts at awareness of *involvement*, not of the incident. A provider may know of a major outage and only later learn its model was involved; the clock starts then.
- **"Suspect with reasonable likelihood"**: probabilistic causation as a trigger. Good: providers can't wait for certainty.
- **Two days for critical infrastructure** is very short, reflecting the urgency (and matching NIS2-style early warnings, perhaps). Five days for cyber.

**Questions to keep.**
- "How fast must serious incidents be reported?" M9.3: initial report (M9.2 points 1–7) within two days for "serious and irreversible disruption of… critical infrastructure"; five days for "a serious cybersecurity breach, including the (self-)exfiltration of model weights and cyberattacks"; (continues: likely 10 and 15 days). "Save in exceptional circumstances."
- "Does the Code treat a model exfiltrating itself as a reportable incident?" Yes: "(self-)exfiltration of model weights" is a serious cybersecurity breach.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Is an attempted self-exfiltration reportable?" Not clearly a serious incident; possibly a near miss.
- "When does the reporting clock start?" When the signatory becomes aware of its model's involvement, including reasonable-likelihood suspicion.

**Prediction for unit 74.** (3) death of a person: 10 days; (4) serious harm to a person's health, infringement of fundamental-rights obligations, serious harm to property or the environment: 15 days. Then intermediate reports (every 4 weeks?) and final report (within 60 days of resolution); documentation retention (5 years).
