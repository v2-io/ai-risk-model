# Unit 70 · L561–573 · p.25: Measure 9.1, Methods for serious incident identification

**What it held.** "Signatories will consider the exemplary methods in Measure 3.5 to keep track of relevant information about serious incidents. Additionally, Signatories will: (1) review other sources of information, such as **police and media reports**, posts on social media, research papers, and incident databases; and (2) facilitate the reporting of relevant information about serious incidents by **downstream modifiers, downstream providers, users, and other third parties** to: (a) the Signatory; or (b) the AI Office and, as applicable, national competent authorities; by informing such third parties of direct reporting channels, if available, **without prejudice to any of their reporting obligations under Article 73 AI Act**."

**What differed.** Close to my prediction. Notable details: **police reports** as a source; third parties may report **directly to the AI Office**, bypassing the provider, and the provider must tell them how ("informing such third parties of direct reporting channels, if available"). And **Art. 73 AI Act**: the high-risk *system* incident-reporting duty of deployers/providers, preserved.

**Wandering.**
- **The provider must advertise channels that bypass itself.** It facilitates reporting to the AI Office directly. That's a structural guard against providers sitting on incident information: the reporter can go straight to the regulator, and the provider has to have told them they can. "If available": if the AI Office has a channel. (I believe the AI Office launched a whistleblower/reporting tool in 2025; not certain.)
- **Downstream actors as incident sensors.** Downstream modifiers (fine-tuners of open weights), downstream providers (system builders), users. This is how a model provider learns of incidents in deployments it can't see (unit 10's structural limit for open weights). The Code's answer to that limit: facilitate reporting by those who can see.
- **Art. 73** is the high-risk AI *system* incident regime. A downstream provider of a high-risk system built on a GPAI model has its own Art. 73 duty; the Code doesn't displace it. Two incident regimes touch at the model/system seam: model-level (C9, to the AI Office) and system-level (Art. 73, to market surveillance authorities). A fork asked about incident reporting across the stack should name both.
- **"Consider the exemplary methods in Measure 3.5"**: the post-market monitoring menu doubles as incident tracking. Coherent design: the same sensors feed both risk reassessment and incident reporting.

**Questions to keep.**
- "How must providers find out about serious incidents?" M9.1: consider M3.5's monitoring methods; review police and media reports, social media, research papers, incident databases; facilitate reporting by downstream modifiers, downstream providers, users and third parties, to the provider or directly to the AI Office.
- "Can third parties report incidents straight to the AI Office?" Yes, and providers must inform them of direct channels "if available".
- Should be asked but might not be: "How does model-level incident reporting relate to Art. 73's system-level regime?" "Without prejudice to" Art. 73.

**Prediction for unit 71.** Measure 9.2, Relevant information for serious incident tracking, documentation, and reporting: the report contains start and end dates, harm and affected persons, the chain of events, the model involved, information on involvement, root-cause analysis, corrective measures taken or planned, recommendations to the AI Office; "to the best of their knowledge"; reports may be incomplete initially and updated. Then 9.3 timelines.
