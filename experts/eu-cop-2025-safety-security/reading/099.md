# Unit 99 · L817–820 · p.36: Appendix 1.3.3 (10)–(13)

**What it held.** "(10) **vulnerability of the specific environment potentially affected** by the model (e.g. social environment, ecological environment); (11) lack of appropriate model explainability or transparency; (12) interactions with other AI models and/or AI systems; and (13) **inappropriate use** of the model (e.g. using the model for applications that do not match its capabilities or propensities)."

**What differed.** Explainability predicted; the others not: **vulnerability of the affected environment** (the receiving system's fragility), and **inappropriate use** (over-reliance on a model for tasks beyond it).

**Wandering.**
- **(10) brings exposure/vulnerability into the risk sources.** Classic disaster-risk thinking: risk = hazard × exposure × vulnerability. The Code's sources now include the vulnerability of what's affected: a fragile social environment (polarised, low-trust) amplifies manipulation risk; a fragile ecological environment amplifies, e.g., an AI-optimised industrial process gone wrong. This is the only place the Code clearly brings in the *receiving* side of risk. For our causal chain (impact radius: harmed groups, degree), it's the hook.
- **(13) inappropriate use**: deploying a model where its reliability doesn't justify it (medical, legal, infrastructure). The risk comes from humans' misjudgement of the model, not from the model's misbehaviour. Its counterpart propensity is (6) lack of performance reliability. Note this source is about *use*, typically by others; the provider can affect it via documentation, use restrictions, and the model spec.
- **(12) interactions with other AI** appear as an affordance/context too, besides the propensities (9)–(10). Multi-agent risk is thus covered both as tendency and as situation.
- **Appendix 1.3 complete**: 14 capabilities, 10 propensities, 13 affordances/context sources. A minimum set of 37 named risk sources. That's a rich, structured source taxonomy, more granular than most sources in the corpus, I'd guess.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Does the Code consider the vulnerability of those affected?" Yes: "vulnerability of the specific environment potentially affected by the model (e.g. social environment, ecological environment)" (Appendix 1.3.3(10)).
- "Does over-reliance on a model count as a risk source?" "Inappropriate use of the model (e.g. using the model for applications that do not match its capabilities or propensities)" (1.3.3(13)).
