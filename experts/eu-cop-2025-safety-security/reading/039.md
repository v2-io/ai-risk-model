# Unit 39 · L325–331 · p.16: the safety margin

**What it held.** "The safety margin will: (1) be appropriate for the systemic risk; and (2) take into account potential limitations, changes, and uncertainties of: (a) systemic risk sources (e.g. capability improvements after the time of assessment); (b) systemic risk assessments (e.g. under-elicitation of model evaluations or historical accuracy of similar assessments); and (c) the effectiveness of safety and security mitigations (e.g. mitigations being circumvented, deactivated, or subverted)."

**Wandering.**
- **What I can still notice, reading slowly:** the margin covers three distinct uncertainty sources: the *thing* (sources: capabilities can grow after assessment), the *measurement* (assessment: under-elicitation; historical accuracy of similar assessments), and the *intervention* (mitigations: circumvention, deactivation, subversion). That's a clean taxonomy of epistemic uncertainty in risk management: object, measurement, control. A useful lexicon structure.
- **"Historical accuracy of similar assessments"** is a calibration requirement in disguise: if past assessments of similar models underestimated capabilities (e.g. evaluations later surpassed by scaffolding), the margin must grow. The Code asks signatories to learn from their own (and others') track record.
- **"Subverted"** among mitigation failures: that's the loss-of-control case, where the model itself subverts the mitigation (e.g. evading monitoring). "Circumvented" is the misuse case (users jailbreaking); "deactivated" is the open-weight / theft case (safeguards removed). Three verbs, three threat actors: users, modifiers/thieves, the model. That reading is mine, but the verb choice fits it exactly.
- **"Capability improvements after the time of assessment"**: post-training enhancements (better prompting, tools, fine-tuning, more inference compute). Recital (b)'s inference compute returns as a margin consideration. So a model just below a tier at assessment, with known room for scaffolding gains, may have to be treated as at the tier.
- **No number.** The margin is "appropriate", never quantified. Same pattern: the Code specifies dimensions, not values.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What must the safety margin account for?" Uncertainties in risk sources (e.g. post-assessment capability gains), assessments (e.g. under-elicitation, historical accuracy), and mitigations (circumvention, deactivation, subversion).
- "How big must the safety margin be?" "Appropriate for the systemic risk"; no value.

**Prediction for unit 40.** End of M4.1 or start of M4.2 ("Proceeding or not proceeding based on systemic risk acceptance determination"): Signatories will only proceed if the risks are determined acceptable. If not acceptable, they will take appropriate steps: implement additional mitigations, or not make the model available on the market, restrict access, withdraw/recall the model, and re-run the process.
