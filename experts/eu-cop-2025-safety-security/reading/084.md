# Unit 84 · L691–705 · p.32 (table): 'reproducibility' (end) … 'systemic risk modelling'

**What it held.**
- 'reproducibility' examples end: "…model elicitation techniques; and/or use of publicly available APIs, technical model evaluation standards, and tools."
- **'resolved'** (serious incident): "a serious incident… for which the Signatory **adopted corrective measures to rectify the harm, if possible, and to assess and mitigate systemic risks related to it**."
- **'scaling law'**: "a systematic relationship between some variable relevant to the development or use of an AI model or AI system, such as size or the amount of time, data, or computational resources used in **training or inference**, and its performance."
- **'(self-)exfiltration of model weights'**: "access or transfer of weights or associated assets of a model from their secure storage **by the model itself and/or an unauthorised actor**."
- **'similar model'**: "a general-purpose AI model **with or without systemic risk**, **assumed** to have materially similar capabilities, propensities, and affordances based on public and/or private information available to the Signatory, including "safe reference models"… and "similarly safe or safer models"…."
- **'state of the art'**: "the forefront of relevant research, governance, and technology **that goes beyond best practice**."
- **'system prompt'**: "a set of instructions, guidelines, and contextual information provided to a model before a user interaction begins."
- **'systemic risk acceptance criteria'**: "criteria defined in the Framework…. Systemic risk tiers… are a type of systemic risk acceptance criteria."
- **'systemic risk assessment'**: "the overarching term referring to all of systemic risk identification…, analysis…, and… acceptance determination."
- **'systemic risk management'**: "coordinated processes and measures to direct an organisation with regard to systemic risk, including systemic risk assessment and mitigation."
- **'systemic risk mitigations'**: "comprise safety mitigations (C5), security mitigations (C6), and **governance mitigations (pursuant to Commitments 1 and 7 to 10)**."
- **'systemic risk modelling'**: "a structured process aimed at specifying **pathways** through which a systemic risk… might materialise; often used interchangeably with the term '**threat modelling**'. This Chapter uses the term 'risk modelling' because the term 'threat modelling' has a specific meaning in cybersecurity."

**What differed.**
- **'state of the art' sits above 'best practice'**: "the forefront… that goes beyond best practice". So the hierarchy is: best practice (accepted among providers) < state of the art (the forefront, beyond best practice) < beyond the state of the art. And 'appropriate' can be met "through best practices, the state of the art, or… beyond". So "appropriate" can sit *below* the state of the art (via best practice) when best practice is "suitable and necessary". But recital (a) says appropriate measures "will often require… at least the state of the art". The two fit: often appropriate = state of the art; sometimes best practice suffices; sometimes beyond is needed. The definitions resolve what looked like a tension at unit 81.
- **Governance mitigations exist**: C1, C7, C8, C9, C10 are themselves *mitigations*. I'd assumed mitigations meant C5 and C6. So the Framework, reporting, governance, incident reporting and transparency are risk-reducing measures in the Code's own taxonomy. For our causal chain, governance sits among mitigations as well as "policies & decision-making".
- **'systemic risk modelling'** finally has a definition: "specifying pathways", ≈ threat modelling, renamed to avoid the cybersecurity sense. That fills unit 29's gap.
- **'similar model'** includes models *without* systemic risk and is based on what's "assumed" from available information. Fills unit 19/23's question.
- **'resolved'** answers unit 75: resolved means corrective measures adopted (rectify harm if possible; assess and mitigate related risk). Not "harm ended", but "provider responded". So "resolved" can come quickly.
- **No definitions of 'serious incident' or 'systemic risk'** (so far): both are Art. 3 terms, so the AI Act's definitions apply and prevail. The Chapter's M9.3 categories (adding cyber breaches) must therefore be read as the Code's specification of *which* incidents to report on what timeline, not as a redefinition of "serious incident". Interesting: the Code reports cyber breaches under the heading of serious incidents even though Art. 3(49) (as I recall) doesn't list them. A fork should flag that cyber breaches may be a widening of the Act's category, which the precedence clause would not allow if it were a redefinition. Maybe the Code treats them as falling under "serious harm to property" or "critical infrastructure"? Unclear. I can't settle this without the Act's text.

**Wandering.**
- **"Training or inference"** in 'scaling law': inference scaling is recognised, consistent with recital (b) and the Code's attention to inference compute.
- **"By the model itself"** in the exfiltration definition: the Code formally contemplates a model accessing or moving its own weights. Paired with 'insider threats', the Chapter's loss-of-control content is substantially in the Glossary.
- **"Associated assets"**: not only weights but e.g. training code, optimizer states, model components that would let someone reconstruct the model. Security covers more than the weights file.
- **Risk modelling's terminological care** is a rare example of a source explaining its own word choice. Our project should love this: the source tells us its term choice was made to avoid collision with another domain. A fork mapping "risk modelling" ↔ "threat modelling" should cite it.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What's the difference between best practice and state of the art?" 'best practice': accepted amongst providers; 'state of the art': "the forefront of relevant research, governance, and technology that goes beyond best practice".
- "Are governance measures mitigations under the Code?" Yes: 'systemic risk mitigations' include "governance mitigations (pursuant to Commitments 1 and 7 to 10)".
- "What does 'systemic risk assessment' cover?" Identification, analysis, acceptance determination (not mitigation).
- "Does the Code define 'serious incident' or 'systemic risk'?" Not in the Glossary (so far); they're AI Act Art. 3 terms, which "shall prevail".
- "When is an incident 'resolved'?" When the signatory "adopted corrective measures to rectify the harm, if possible, and to assess and mitigate systemic risks related to it".
- Should be asked but might not be: "Do the M9.3 cyber-breach incidents fit the AI Act's definition of serious incident?" Possibly a widening; check the Act.
