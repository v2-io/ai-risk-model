# Unit 60 · L490–496 · p.22: Measure 7.7, Model Report notifications

**What it held.**
- "Signatories will provide the AI Office with access to the Model Report (**without redactions, unless they are required by national security laws to which Signatories are subject**) **by the time they place a model on the market**, e.g. through **a publicly accessible link** or through a sufficiently secure channel specified by the AI Office. If a Model Report is updated, Signatories will provide… access to the updated Model Report (without redactions, unless…) **within five business days of a confirmed update**."
- "To facilitate the placing on the market of a model, Signatories **may delay** providing the AI Office with a Model Report, or an update thereof, **by up to 15 business days**. This may be done **only if the AI Office considers the Signatory to be acting in good faith** and if the Signatory provides an **interim Model Report, containing the information specified in Measures 7.2 and 7.5**, to the AI Office without delay."

**What differed.** Predicted: unredacted access at market placement; a delay allowance. Not predicted:
- The **national-security exception** to "without redactions". (M1.4's Framework access had a bare "(unredacted)".)
- **"A publicly accessible link"** as a means of access. A Model Report *could* be public.
- The **interim report's contents**: M7.2 (reasons for proceeding) and M7.5 (landscape changes). The two the drafters consider most urgent.
- **"Confirmed update"**: updates are confirmed, like Frameworks.

**Wandering.**
- **National-security redactions.** Signatories "subject to" national security laws, i.e. US firms under US export control or classification rules (and possibly under any future US government arrangement about frontier-model information), may redact. This is the transatlantic seam of the Code: EU oversight stops where a provider's home-state national-security law starts. The AI Office can't see what US law forbids showing. A fork asked "can the AI Office see everything?" should say: Model Reports are unredacted "unless [redactions] are required by national security laws to which Signatories are subject". For Frameworks, M1.4 has no such exception written, which may be an inconsistency or a reflection that Frameworks are less sensitive.
- **"Publicly accessible link"**: the Code contemplates that a provider might publish its Model Report (as labs publish system cards). It doesn't require it. If a lab publishes a system card that meets M7.1–7.5 in full, unredacted, it could simply link it. In practice public system cards are redacted; so for most, the secure channel is the route. Still: the Code leaves the door open to full publication, and treats it as equivalent.
- **The 15-business-day delay** is a release-pressure valve: a model can go to market before the full Model Report is with the AI Office, but only (a) in the AI Office's view of good faith and (b) with an interim report giving reasons for proceeding and landscape changes. The **assessment** must still be complete before market (M1.2); only the *report* may lag. A fork should be precise on this: the assessment isn't delayed, the paperwork is.
- **"Only if the AI Office considers"**: one of the few places the AI Office has a discretionary gate in the operative text. Good faith is the AI Office's judgement, not the signatory's.
- **"By the time they place a model on the market"**: the deadline is market placement itself. No pre-market review period. Contrast M1.1's "two weeks before placing the model on the market" for the Framework. The AI Office doesn't get time to review a Model Report before release; it receives it at release. So the AI Office's role is ex post, not ex ante: no approval, no waiting period. Worth emphasising in comparisons with other regimes.

**Questions to keep.**
- "When does the AI Office get the Model Report?" By the time the model is placed on the market (M7.7); updates within five business days of a confirmed update; up to 15 business days' delay allowed if the AI Office considers the signatory in good faith and an interim report (M7.2 + M7.5 information) is provided without delay.
- "Can Model Reports be redacted?" Only where "required by national security laws to which Signatories are subject".
- "Does the AI Office approve or pre-review models before release?" No: access "by the time they place a model on the market".
- Should be asked but might not be: "Can a Model Report be public?" Access may be "through a publicly accessible link"; not required.
