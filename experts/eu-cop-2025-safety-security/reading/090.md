**What differed.** I predicted a single list of characteristics. It's split in two: **essential** (what makes a risk systemic at all, presumably the Art. 3(65) elements) and **contributing** (what makes it more systemic or more concerning: speed, reversibility, etc.).

**Wandering.**
- **Essential vs contributing** is a necessary-vs-aggravating distinction: like elements of an offence vs aggravating factors. Essential characteristics are presumably the definitional conditions from Art. 3(65) (specific to high-impact capabilities; significant Union impact; propagation at scale). Contributing ones would include my predicted list. For the funnel (Fig. 4): essential characteristics are the filter; contributing characteristics are weights for analysis and acceptance.
- **"Inform"** identification: soft. They're considerations, not tests. But the Glossary's "including"… doesn't apply here (no "including"). So the nature characteristics guide judgement rather than mechanically sort risks.
- For our lexicon: the Code gives an explicit two-tier characterisation of *systemicness*. That's useful for G3 ("what risk and hazard are made of"): systemic risk has essential and contributing characteristics, according to this source.
