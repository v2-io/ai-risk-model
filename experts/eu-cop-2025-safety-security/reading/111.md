# Unit 111 · L896–902 · p.39: Appendix 3.4, Qualified model evaluation teams (qualifications)

**What it held.** "Signatories will ensure that the teams responsible for conducting the model evaluations **combine technical expertise with relevant domain knowledge** of the systemic risk to enable a holistic and multidisciplinary understanding. **Indicative** qualifications… are: (1) a PhD, peer-reviewed and recognised publications, or equivalent research or engineering experience, relevant to the systemic risk; (2) having designed or developed a published, and peer-reviewed or widely used, model evaluation method for the systemic risk; or (3) **three years of work-experience** in a field directly relevant to the systemic risk or, if that field is nascent, equivalent experience from studying in the field or working in a field with directly transferable knowledge."

**What differed.** I predicted PhD-level expertise (yes); not the specific three-route structure or the **"three years"** figure; and the qualifications are **indicative** (not mandatory) and alternatives ("or").

**Wandering.**
- **Teams, not individuals**: the requirement is on the team composition: technical + domain. A CBRN evaluation team needs ML people and biologists/chemists. "Holistic and multidisciplinary".
- **"Indicative"**: a fork must not say the Code requires PhDs. It gives three indicative routes (degree/publications/equivalent; evaluation-method authorship; three years' experience).
- **"If that field is nascent"**: AI evaluation and AI safety are nascent fields; the Code allows equivalent experience from study or adjacent fields. Sensible for 2025 hiring realities.
- **Qualifications as a lever on evaluation quality**: who runs evaluations matters as much as which. Combined with M7.3(1)(g)(i) (describe internal teams' access and resources), the AI Office can see who did the work.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Who may run model evaluations?" Teams combining technical expertise and domain knowledge; indicative qualifications: PhD or equivalent publications/experience; authorship of a recognised evaluation method; three years' directly relevant work experience (or equivalent if the field is nascent). Indicative, not mandatory.
