# Unit 36 · L301–305 · p.15: Commitment 4, Systemic risk acceptance determination

**What it held.**
- "**LEGAL TEXT:** Article 55(1) AI Act"
- "Signatories commit to **specifying systemic risk acceptance criteria** and **determining whether the systemic risks stemming from the model are acceptable** (as specified in Measure 4.1). Signatories commit to **deciding whether or not to proceed** with the development, the making available on the market, and/or the use of the model **based on the systemic risk acceptance determination** (as specified in Measure 4.2)."

**What differed.** Nearly what I predicted. One thing differs: the Commitment says "deciding **whether or not** to proceed", so "not proceeding" is named at Commitment level, unlike in Figure 3, where only "Proceed" was drawn. And again the full triad: development, making available on the market, **use**. The go/no-go explicitly covers internal use and continued development, not only release.

**Wandering.**
- **The go/no-go covers development itself.** "Whether or not to proceed with the development" means the acceptance determination can stop *training or further development*, not only release. That's the strongest form of a frontier safety commitment: pause development if risk isn't acceptable. Whether M4.2 actually says "will not proceed" or something softer is the question that matters most in this whole Commitment.
- **Signatories specify their own criteria.** "Commit to specifying systemic risk acceptance criteria." Confirmed: the criteria are the signatory's, justified in the Framework (M1.1(2)(a)). The Code's acceptability standard is self-set, bounded by the state-of-the-art rule, the purposive interpretation rule, AI Office guidance, and perhaps something in M4.1 about how criteria must be built (e.g. tiers "defined in terms of model capabilities", "at least one tier per specified risk", "safety margin").
- **"Based on the systemic risk acceptance determination"**: the decision must rest on the determination. A decision to proceed despite an "unacceptable" determination would break C4.
- Only Art. 55(1) as LEGAL TEXT. The AI Act doesn't itself speak of acceptance criteria or go/no-go decisions; it speaks of assessing and mitigating systemic risks. C4 is where the Code goes furthest beyond the Act's words, which may be why it cites only the general Article.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Can the Code's acceptance determination stop development, not just release?" C4: "deciding whether or not to proceed with the development, the making available on the market, and/or the use of the model based on the… determination". Check M4.2 for the operative force.
- "Who sets the acceptance criteria?" The signatory (C4, M1.1(2)(a)).

**Prediction for unit 37.** Measure 4.1, Systemic risk acceptance criteria and acceptance determination: Signatories will describe and justify, in the Framework, criteria for each identified systemic risk, at least for the specified ones, including systemic risk tiers that are measurable and defined in terms of model capabilities (and possibly propensities, risk estimates). At least one tier at which the risk is unacceptable without mitigations? A "safety margin" to account for uncertainty in evaluations (e.g. evaluations under-eliciting). Then: Signatories will determine whether the risk is acceptable by comparing the risk estimates (M3.4) with the criteria, accounting for the margin.
