# Unit 13 · L99–101 · p.7: Measure 1.1 continued: (2)(c) timelines, (2)(d) external input, (3) responsibility

**What it held.**
- (2)(c): "for each systemic risk that Signatories defined systemic risk tiers for…, estimates of timelines when Signatories reasonably foresee that they will have a model that **exceeds the highest systemic risk tier already reached by any of their existing models**. Such estimates: (i) may consist of time ranges or probability distributions; and (ii) may take into account aggregate forecasts, surveys, and other estimates produced with other providers. Further, such estimates will be supported by justifications, including underlying assumptions and uncertainties"
- (2)(d): "a description of **whether and, if so, by what process** input from external actors, including governments, influences proceeding with the development, making available on the market, and/or use of the Signatories' models as specified in Measure 4.2, **other than as the result of independent external evaluations**"
- (3): "a description of how systemic risk responsibility is allocated for the processes by which systemic risk is assessed and mitigated as specified in Commitment 8"

**What differed.**
- Timelines: predicted. But the *object* of the forecast is specific and I didn't predict it: not "when will a model reach tier N" in general, but when **the signatory** will have a model that **exceeds its own current highest tier**. A forecast of the firm's own next step up. That's sharper and more useful than a generic capability forecast.
- External input (2)(d): I predicted "how external input is used". The text says "**whether** and, if so, by what process". So "no external input influences our go/no-go decisions" is a permissible answer, as long as it's described. That's a disclosure requirement, not a requirement to let anyone in. I didn't expect that weakness.
- And "including governments" is named. The Framework must say whether governments have any say in proceeding.
- Commitment 8 = responsibility allocation (with 8.3 risk culture). Measure 4.2 = proceeding/not proceeding. Landmarks growing.

**Wandering.**

- **The forecast is about crossing the provider's own frontier.** "Exceeds the highest systemic risk tier already reached by any of their existing models." So a firm whose models are at tier 1 for CBRN forecasts when it'll have a tier-2 model. This is exactly the forward-looking "future developments" that (d) protected as "subject to change". The recitals and this Measure are designed together: you must forecast your own escalation, and the forecast isn't a promise. For the AI Office, this is early warning: the regulator learns roughly when each firm expects to cross the next threshold. For a public-interest reader, this is the most valuable information in a Framework, and (d) of the recitals plus Art. 78 keep it confidential.
- **"Probability distributions."** The Code explicitly accepts probabilistic forecasts. It's sophisticated forecasting language for a regulatory text (it reads like Epoch/Metaculus thinking). Combined with "justifications, including underlying assumptions and uncertainties", the Code asks for calibrated forecasting with stated uncertainty. That's an unusually epistemically careful demand.
- **"Produced with other providers."** Forecasts "may take into account aggregate forecasts, surveys, and other estimates produced with other providers". Joint forecasting among competitors. Another correlation channel (alongside (e)'s shared evaluations): if firms forecast together, their timelines are not independent, and agreement among them isn't corroboration. A fork should flag this when someone says "all frontier labs forecast X".
- **"Exceeds the highest tier… already reached."** What if a firm has never reached tier 1? Then "highest tier already reached" is tier 0 (below all tiers), and it forecasts when it'll first reach tier 1. OK. But what if a firm reaches the *top* tier? Then "exceeds the highest tier" is impossible to forecast; there's nothing above. The forecast requirement goes silent exactly at the most dangerous point. Probably not intended, but a real gap: the Code's tiers would need to be open-ended at the top, or the requirement lapses. A question to ask.
- **"Other than as the result of independent external evaluations."** External evaluations are carved out because (I'd guess) they're handled elsewhere (an independent-external-evaluation Measure, perhaps under Commitment 3). So (2)(d) is about *non-evaluative* influence: governments, advisory boards, civil society, maybe the AI Office itself. Does a government get a veto? Is there a process for national-security input? The Framework must say. This is subtle: it's where government involvement in frontier development decisions becomes documentable without being required.
- **"Influences proceeding with the development, making available on the market, and/or use."** Same triad as (1): develop / market / use. The Code is consistent about covering internal use. And "proceeding" signals a go/no-go decision point (Measure 4.2), which I'd guess says: if risk is not acceptable, don't proceed (or proceed only with additional mitigations).
- Imagining the AI Office reading a Framework: trigger points (justified), acceptance criteria with tiers (justified), mitigations per tier, timelines to the next tier (with distributions and assumptions), whether externals influence go/no-go, who's responsible. That's a lot of structure. It's a template for an RSP, essentially, with forecasting and governance disclosures that most company frameworks (as of 2024–25) didn't have.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Must providers forecast their own capability growth?" M1.1(2)(c): yes, timelines for exceeding their highest tier yet reached, per risk with tiers; ranges or distributions; justified with assumptions and uncertainties; may use forecasts produced with other providers.
- "Do governments or outsiders have any say in whether a model proceeds?" M1.1(2)(d): the Framework describes "whether and, if so, by what process"; "no" is a permitted answer.
- Should be asked but might not be: "What happens to the forecasting requirement when a firm reaches its top tier?" A possible gap.
- Should be asked: "Are providers' forecasts independent?" (ii) permits joint forecasts.

**Prediction for unit 14.** (4) probably: a description of the process for updating the Framework (M1.3), and maybe of how serious incidents are handled. Then Measure 1.1's timing: the Framework is created within N weeks of notifying the Commission that a model meets the systemic-risk threshold (Art. 52), and in any case before placing on the market. Then the heading Measure 1.2.
