# Unit 63 · L515–525 · p.23: M8.1 levels; the presumption of fulfilment begins (oversight)

**What it held.**
- Levels: "(1) the management body in its supervisory function or another suitable independent body (such as a council or board); (2) the management body in its executive function; (3) relevant operational teams; (4) **if available**, internal assurance providers (e.g. an internal audit function); and (5) **if available**, external assurance providers (e.g. third-party auditors)."
- "**This Measure is presumed to be fulfilled**, if Signatories, as appropriate for the systemic risks stemming from their models, adhere to all of the following:"
- "(1) **Systemic risk oversight:** The responsibility… has been assigned to **a specific committee of the management body in its supervisory function (e.g. a risk committee or audit committee)** or one or multiple suitable independent bodies (such as councils or boards). For Signatories that are SMEs or SMCs, this responsibility may be primarily assigned to an individual member of the management body in its supervisory function." (continues)

**What differed.** Predicted: management body functions, operational teams, internal audit. Not predicted:
- **"If available"** for both internal and external assurance providers. A signatory without an internal audit function isn't required to create one by this list.
- A **presumption of fulfilment**: a safe-harbour structure *inside* a Measure. If the signatory adopts a specific governance configuration (to be listed), M8.1 is presumed met. Otherwise it must show its own arrangement meets the general standard. I didn't expect presumption logic here.
- No CRO named (so far).

**Wandering.**
- **"If available" is a big concession.** It means a lab without internal audit can still allocate responsibilities across levels (1)–(3) only, and assurance happens... where? The four-roles list says assurance is a responsibility that must be defined. The levels list says the providers are "if available". Combined: assurance must be someone's responsibility, but a dedicated assurance function isn't required. Maybe the presumption list (next) requires one. That would be the incentive structure: you can do without, but then you don't get the presumption.
- **Presumption of fulfilment** is an EU regulatory technique (harmonised standards give a "presumption of conformity"). Here it's internal to the Code: a model governance structure that, if adopted, presumptively satisfies M8.1. This lets the Code be flexible in principle and concrete in practice. For our project's methodology: the source has nested levels of normativity, from general Commitment to Measure to a presumption-triggering configuration. Assertions extracted from M8.1 should note which level they're at.
- **"A specific committee of the management body in its supervisory function"**: a board committee for AI systemic risk (or the risk or audit committee). Or "one or multiple suitable independent bodies". So OpenAI's Safety and Security Committee or Anthropic's LTBT-related bodies could qualify, if "suitable" and "independent". The Code's flexibility accommodates specific labs' structures, perhaps deliberately.
- **SMEs**: one board member can carry oversight. Proportional.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Does the Code require an internal audit function?" Not in M8.1's levels: assurance providers are "if available". Check the presumption list.
- "What governance structure satisfies the Code?" M8.1 has a presumption of fulfilment if all listed conditions are met; first, oversight by a specific board committee (e.g. risk or audit committee) or suitable independent bodies; SMEs/SMCs may use an individual board member.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Could a lab's special governance body (e.g. a trust or safety council) be the oversight body?" Yes, if a "suitable independent body".
