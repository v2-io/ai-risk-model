# Unit 41 · L345–349 · p.17: Commitment 5, Safety mitigations

**What it held.** "**LEGAL TEXT:** Article 55(1) and recital 114 AI Act". "Signatories commit to implementing appropriate safety mitigations along the entire model lifecycle, as specified **in the Measure** for this Commitment, to ensure the systemic risks stemming from the model are acceptable (pursuant to Commitment 4)."

**What differed.** As predicted, except: "the Measure" is **singular**. Commitment 5 has only one Measure (5.1). The whole of "safety mitigations", in a Chapter about safety and security, fits in one Measure. (Checkpoint aside: Joseph's reading was 318k at unit 39.)

**Wandering.**
- **Mitigations serve acceptability**, explicitly "to ensure the systemic risks… are acceptable (pursuant to Commitment 4)". Mitigations aren't valued for themselves; they're the means to reach acceptability. A mitigation that doesn't move a risk towards acceptability isn't required by C5. And a risk already acceptable needs no *additional* mitigation (consistent with M1.2's step 4).
- **"Safety" versus "security"** is about to be distinguished: C5 safety mitigations, C6 security mitigations. I'd guess safety = mitigations against harm from the model's use and behaviour (refusals, filtering, staged access, alignment), and security = protection of the model itself (weights, against theft and tampering; insider threats). The Glossary or the Measures will say. For our lexicon this is an important pair: the Code uses "safety and security" throughout, and its two mitigation Commitments give it operational meaning.
- **One Measure for safety mitigations** suggests a list of example mitigation types with a general "appropriate" duty, not detailed requirements. Consistent with the thin-analysis / thick-governance pattern: the Code doesn't prescribe *which* mitigations; it requires that whatever is chosen make risk acceptable.
- **"Along the entire model lifecycle"**: mitigations at training time (data filtering), at deployment (filters), post-market (monitoring-driven changes). The lifecycle principle of recital (a) again.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What safety mitigations does the Code require?" C5: "appropriate safety mitigations along the entire model lifecycle… to ensure the systemic risks… are acceptable"; detail in the single Measure 5.1.
- Should be asked but might not be: "What's the difference between safety and security mitigations in the Code?" C5 vs C6; look for definitions.

**Prediction for unit 42.** Measure 5.1, Appropriate safety mitigations: Signatories will implement safety mitigations that are appropriate, including sufficiently robust under adversarial pressure (e.g. fine-tuning attacks, jailbreaking), taking into account the model's release and distribution strategy (open weights). Examples: (1) filtering and cleaning training data; (2) monitoring and filtering inputs/outputs; (3) changing model behaviour (fine-tuning to refuse); (4) staging access (API, KYC, trusted users); (5) offering countermeasures/tools to other actors; (6) techniques for a safe ecosystem of AI agents (identification, communication protocols); (7) other emerging mitigations (e.g. transparency into CoT, alignment techniques) for loss of control.
