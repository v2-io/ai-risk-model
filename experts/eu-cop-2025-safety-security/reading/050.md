# Unit 50 · L413–419 · p.19: Measure 7.2, Reasons for proceeding

**What it held.** "Signatories will provide in the Model Report:
1. a **detailed justification** for why the systemic risks stemming from the model are acceptable, including details of the safety margins incorporated (pursuant to Measure 4.1);
2. **the reasonably foreseeable conditions under which the justification in point (1) would no longer hold**; and
3. a description of **how the decision to proceed**… was made, including whether input from external actors informed such a decision (pursuant to Measure 1.1, point (2)(d)), and **whether and how input from independent external evaluators pursuant to Appendix 3.5** informed such a decision."

**What differed.** Predicted the justification with margins. Not predicted:
- **(2), the defeaters**: the conditions under which the justification would fail. I'd have guessed "uncertainties and assumptions"; this is sharper.
- **(3), the decision process itself**: not only *why* acceptable but *how the decision was made*, with external actors' and external evaluators' roles.
- **Appendix 3.5** concerns **independent external evaluators** (pre-market, presumably, as part of evaluation rigour). So Appendix 3 contains the pre-market external evaluation requirements.

**Wandering.**

- **M7.2 is a safety case in all but name.** A claim (risks acceptable), an argument with evidence (detailed justification with margins), and **defeaters** (conditions under which the argument fails). Safety-case literature (and recent proposals for AI safety cases, e.g. from UK AISI and GovAI; Markus Anderljung, WG4 Vice-Chair, worked on this) has exactly this structure. The Code avoids the term "safety case" but requires its skeleton. A fork asked "does the Code require safety cases?" should say: not by name; M7.2 requires a detailed justification of acceptability plus the "reasonably foreseeable conditions under which the justification… would no longer hold", which is the core of one. (Lineage via WG4: inference.)
- **Defeaters make monitoring targeted.** If the Model Report says "this justification fails if the model is fine-tuned with tool access" or "if jailbreak success exceeds X%", then post-market monitoring (M3.5) and the Model Report update triggers (M7.6) have specific conditions to watch. I'd bet M7.6 refers back to M7.2(2): when a defeater condition occurs, update. That would make the system self-consistent.
- **"How the decision… was made"**: who decided, by what process. With M1.1(3) (responsibility allocation) and C8 (governance), this lets the AI Office check that the decision was taken by the right people. It's governance accountability at the moment of release.
- **"Whether and how input from independent external evaluators… informed such a decision."** Pre-market external evaluators exist (Appendix 3.5), and the Report must say whether their input mattered. It doesn't require that it matter, only that its role be stated. Same disclosure-not-mandate pattern as M1.1(2)(d) for government input.
- **"Detailed"** is an unusual adjective in this Code: most requirements are "high-level" (M1.1, M7.1(1)). The justification is the one place the Code asks for detail explicitly. That tells me where the drafters think the substance is.

**Questions to keep.**
- "Does the Code require safety cases?" Not by name; M7.2 requires the core: "a detailed justification for why the systemic risks… are acceptable, including… safety margins", plus "the reasonably foreseeable conditions under which the justification… would no longer hold".
- "Must providers say how the release decision was made?" Yes, M7.2(3), including the roles of external actors and of independent external evaluators (Appendix 3.5).
- Should be asked but might not be: "Must external evaluators' findings change the decision?" No; the Report states "whether and how" they informed it.

**Prediction for unit 51.** Measure 7.3, Documentation of systemic risk identification, analysis, and mitigation: the Model Report includes (1) the identified systemic risks and scenarios; (2) results of model evaluations and risk modelling/estimation, with uncertainties; (3) the tiers reached; (4) safety mitigations and their effectiveness, including against adversarial pressure; (5) security mitigations and the Security Goal; (6) examples of model inputs and outputs (e.g. evaluation transcripts); (7) the methods used and their limitations (e.g. elicitation). Perhaps also a description of the "similarly safe or safer" comparison.
