# Unit 106 · L853–858 · p.38: Appendix 2.2, safety margin for comparisons; losing a safe reference

**What it held.**
- "In making their assessment of points (2) and (3)… and Appendix 2.1, point (2), Signatories will appropriately take into account the uncertainty that may stem from, e.g. a lack of information about the reference model and measurement errors, **by incorporating a sufficiently wide safety margin**."
- "In the event that a model previously considered to be a safe reference model… **subsequently loses this status**…, the Signatory will **within six months**: (1) identify another safe reference model…; or (2) treat the other model as **subject to all Commitments and Measures** of this Chapter if previously adherence had relied on exemptions and/or reductions…, including completing all previously exempted and/or reduced parts of the full systemic risk assessment and mitigation process…."

(Also from Joseph, via ai-risk-search-tool: experiential reading is for this core document only; the Chairs' statement and anything after may be read however serves best. Noted.)

**What differed.** My unit-105 worry ("no safety margin beyond 'negligible margin of error'") is **answered**: the comparison requires "a sufficiently wide safety margin" against uncertainty from lack of information and measurement error. And a mechanism I didn't anticipate: what happens when a reference model **loses** safe-reference status: six months to find another or come into full compliance.

**Wandering.**
- **Margin on both sides of the comparison**: uncertainty about the reference model ("lack of information") and about measurements. And it applies to visibility (2.1(2)) too: if you know less about the reference, widen the margin. That's coherent and careful. Under-elicitation of the *new* model isn't named, but "measurement errors" arguably covers it.
- **How does a reference model lose status?** By 2.1(3): new reasonable grounds to believe its risks aren't acceptable (e.g. a serious incident, or new evaluations revealing a dangerous capability in a grandfathered model). When that happens, every model leaning on it loses its lightened treatment within six months. That's a cascade: one discovery about an old model can raise obligations across a family of new ones. It makes the reference class dynamic and self-correcting.
- **Six months is long.** A model whose reference has been found unsafe can keep its exemptions for half a year. A fork should note that the Code allows this transition window; whether the AI Office would accept it in a high-risk case is a matter for the purposive rule.
- **"Exemptions and/or reductions"**: the Code's own words for what similarly-safe status buys. A neat summary term for forks: Appendix 2 provides "exemptions and/or reductions".

**Questions to keep.**
- "Does the similarly-safe comparison include a safety margin?" Yes: "a sufficiently wide safety margin" against uncertainty from lack of information about the reference and measurement errors.
- "What if a safe reference model turns out not to be safe?" Within six months, find another safe reference or treat the dependent model as subject to all Commitments and Measures, completing previously exempted/reduced parts of the full process.
