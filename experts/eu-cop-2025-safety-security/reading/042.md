# Unit 42 · L351–353 · p.17: Measure 5.1, Appropriate safety mitigations (the duty)

**What it held.** "Signatories will implement safety mitigations that are appropriate, including **sufficiently robust under adversarial pressure (e.g. fine-tuning attacks or jailbreaking)**, taking into account the model's **release and distribution strategy**."

**What differed.** Word for word what I predicted. Training memory again. I'll read the example list next as if new.

**Wandering.**
- **Robustness is part of appropriateness.** A mitigation that holds only against cooperative users isn't appropriate. "Sufficiently robust under adversarial pressure" sets an adversarial standard, the standard of security engineering, for *safety* mitigations.
- **Fine-tuning attacks** named first. That's the open-weight problem in two words: if weights are released, refusal training can be fine-tuned away in minutes. So for open-weight release, "sufficiently robust under… fine-tuning attacks" is a very high bar, perhaps unattainable with 2025 methods (tamper-resistant training was a research topic). Read with "taking into account the model's release and distribution strategy": the strategy is something the mitigations must account for. Strictly, a signatory planning open release must have safety mitigations robust to fine-tuning, *or* must judge that the risk is acceptable without such robustness (because the capabilities aren't dangerous enough). In practice the second is what the Code allows open-weight providers: their acceptance determination has to assume mitigations can be stripped, which the safety margin's "deactivated" (unit 39) already covers.
- **"Release and distribution strategy"** treats release mode as a variable the signatory chooses, and that the mitigation analysis must account for. Release mode is thus itself a mitigation lever (choosing API-only over open weights). I expect it among the examples ("staging the access").
- A thin duty again; the substance is in the examples, which come next, and in the acceptability coupling.
