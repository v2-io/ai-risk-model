# Unit 71 · L575–585 · p.26: Measure 9.2, information in incident reports (1)–(7)

**What it held.** "Signatories will keep track of, document, and report…, **at least** the following information **to the best of their knowledge**, **redacted to the extent necessary to comply with other Union law** applicable to such information:
1. the start and end dates…, or best approximations…;
2. the resulting harm and **the victim or affected group**;
3. the chain of events that (**directly or indirectly**) led to the serious incident;
4. the model involved;
5. a description of material available setting out the model's involvement;
6. what, if anything, the Signatory intends to do or has done in response;
7. what, if anything, the Signatory **recommends the AI Office** and, as applicable, national competent authorities **to do** in response;" (continues)

**What differed.** Predicted most. Not predicted:
- **Redaction to comply with other Union law** (e.g. GDPR for victims' data). A third redaction rule (after M1.4's none and M7.7's national security).
- **(7) recommendations to the regulator**: the provider advises the AI Office on its response.
- No root-cause analysis yet (maybe next).

**Wandering.**
- **"Victim or affected group"** introduces a vocabulary of harm recipients: victim (individual) or affected group (collective). Useful for our impact-radius dimension ("harmed groups, degree/scale"). Systemic incidents may have no single victim.
- **"Directly or indirectly"** echoes recital (j). The chain of events includes indirect causal contributions. Paired with (5)'s "model's involvement" and C9's "degree of involvement", the incident model is a causal chain in which the model is one link of varying weight. For the lexicon: the Code's incident reporting asks for a chain-of-events causal account, not a single-cause attribution.
- **"What, if anything"** in (6) and (7): a provider may do nothing, and say so. The report must state it.
- **(7) is regulatory co-production**: the provider, closest to the facts, proposes what the authority should do (e.g. alert other providers about a technique, warn critical-infrastructure operators). It also lets the provider shape the response. Both.
- **"To the best of their knowledge"** and "best approximations": the report is due even if incomplete. Timeliness over completeness, presumably with later updates.
- The **"Union law" redaction** is narrower than it looks: it's for compliance (e.g. personal data minimisation), not commercial confidentiality.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What must an incident report contain?" M9.2 (at least): dates; harm and victim/affected group; chain of events (directly or indirectly); model involved; material on the model's involvement; the provider's response; the provider's recommendations to authorities; (continues).
- "Can incident reports be redacted?" Only "to the extent necessary to comply with other Union law".

**Prediction for unit 72.** (8) a root-cause analysis, including the outputs of the model that contributed, and whether the model was used as intended; (9) any patterns (similar incidents); (10) near-miss information? Then "Signatories will update reports as new information emerges", and Measure 9.3, Reporting timelines.
