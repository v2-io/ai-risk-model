# Unit 97 · L789–802 · p.36: Appendix 1.3.2, Model propensities

**What it held.** "Model propensities, which **encompass inclinations or tendencies of a model to exhibit some behaviours or patterns**, include:
1. misalignment with **human intent**;
2. misalignment with **human values** (e.g. disregard for fundamental rights);
3. tendency to deploy capabilities in harmful ways (e.g. to manipulate or deceive);
4. tendency to "hallucinate", to produce misinformation, or **to obscure sources of information**;
5. discriminatory bias;
6. lack of performance reliability;
7. **lawlessness**, i.e. acting without reasonable regard to legal duties that would be imposed on **similarly situated persons**, or without reasonable regard to the legally protected interests of affected persons;
8. "goal-pursuing", harmful resistance to goal modification, or "power-seeking";
9. "colluding" with other AI models/systems; and
10. **mis-coordination or conflict with other AI models/systems**."

**What differed.** My prediction was close (memory). Not predicted:
- A **definition of propensity**: "inclinations or tendencies… to exhibit some behaviours or patterns". (The Glossary didn't define it; here it is, in passing.)
- Misalignment **split in two**: with human *intent* (doing what's meant) vs human *values*.
- "**Obscure sources of information**".
- **(10) mis-coordination or conflict** between AI systems: multi-agent failure, distinct from collusion (9).

**Wandering.**
- **Intent vs values** is the classic alignment split: a model can do what the user intends while violating values (helping a bioterrorist faithfully), or respect values while thwarting intent. The Code names both as risk sources, so "aligned" in the Code's terms has two components. For our lexicon (G2/G3 on alignment, the "aligned to whom?" map): the Code says misalignment *with human* intent and values, without saying *whose* humans. That's exactly the question the alignment-model map in this repo asks. Note for a fork: the Code's "human intent" and "human values" are unqualified; no principal is named.
- **Lawlessness, defined by analogy to persons.** "Legal duties that would be imposed on similarly situated persons": the model is judged as if it were a person in its position (an AI agent acting as a broker should respect a broker's duties). This "law-following AI" concept (from LawAI/O'Keefe et al., I believe) is a strong lineage marker. It's an unusual and thoughtful inclusion: propensity to disregard law as a risk source, measured against a human-equivalent standard.
- **(8) quotes "goal-pursuing" and "power-seeking"** in scare quotes, signalling contested anthropomorphic terms. The Code distances itself from the terms while using them. The quotes are deliberate; a fork quoting should keep them.
- **(9) colluding / (10) mis-coordination or conflict**: multi-agent risks, a 2025 frontier topic (Cooperative AI Foundation's multi-agent risk report, early 2025, as I recall). Again, research-agenda fingerprints.
- **(6) lack of performance reliability** as a *propensity*: unreliability is framed as a tendency, not a capability shortfall. A model that fails unpredictably in critical infrastructure is a systemic risk source.
- **(4) "obscure sources"**: e.g. presenting synthesised claims without attribution, or hiding provenance; relevant to information-ecosystem risks.
- **Minimum, again**: "include" ⇒ per the Glossary, the minimum set.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What propensities does the Code treat as risk sources?" Appendix 1.3.2's ten (a minimum, under the Glossary's 'including' rule).
- "How does the Code define a propensity?" "Inclinations or tendencies of a model to exhibit some behaviours or patterns."
- "Does the Code say whose intent or values a model must be aligned with?" No: "human intent", "human values", unqualified.
- "What's 'lawlessness' in the Code?" "Acting without reasonable regard to legal duties that would be imposed on similarly situated persons, or without reasonable regard to the legally protected interests of affected persons."
- Should be asked but might not be: "Does the Code consider multi-agent risks?" Yes: collusion (9), mis-coordination or conflict (10).
