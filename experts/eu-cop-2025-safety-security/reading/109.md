# Unit 109 · L874–886 · p.39: Appendix 3.2, Model elicitation

**What it held.** "Signatories will ensure that the model evaluations are conducted with **at least a state-of-the-art level of model elicitation**…, by using at least state-of-the-art techniques that: (1) minimise the risk of **under-elicitation**; and (2) minimise the risk of **model deception during model evaluations (e.g. sandbagging)**; such as by adapting test-time compute, rate limits, scaffolding, and tools, and conducting fine-tuning and prompt engineering."
"For this, Signatories will **at least**: (1) **match the model elicitation capabilities of misuse actors relevant to the systemic risk scenario** (pursuant to Measure 2.2); and (2) **match the expected use context** (e.g. equivalent scaffolding and/or tool access) of the model, as informed by integrations into AI systems that are: (a) planned or considered for the model; and/or (b) **currently used for similar models**, if such integrations are known to the Signatory and the Signatory **cannot exclude a similar use** of their model."

**What differed.** Predicted: under-elicitation, sandbagging, test-time compute, scaffolding, tools, fine-tuning, prompting, and matching misuse actors (memory). Not predicted: the two **floor** conditions stated as "at least" — matching **misuse actors' elicitation capabilities** *per scenario*, and matching **expected use context**, including integrations *used for similar models* that the signatory can't exclude.

**Wandering.**

- **Elicitation is defined relative to adversaries.** The floor is: elicit as well as the relevant misuse actors could. For a scenario involving a state-sponsored group, that means elicitation at a state-sponsored level; for a novice, novice-level plus easily available tools. This ties elicitation (Appendix 3.2) to scenarios (M2.2) to threat actors. Neat: the thin M2.2 scenarios now carry weight, because the scenario fixes who the misuse actor is, which sets the elicitation floor. So the content of scenarios matters a lot, even if M2.2 didn't specify it.
- **Fine-tuning in elicitation.** Including "conducting fine-tuning" means the provider must test what a fine-tuning attacker could unlock, at least where the scenario's actors could fine-tune (open weights, or fine-tuning APIs). For open-weight models, the elicitation floor includes fine-tuning away safeguards. Combined with M7.3(1)(d)(ii) (compare mitigated vs fully elicited) and C6's open-weight logic, the Code does have a path to the "what can someone do with the weights" question.
- **Sandbagging minimisation** is named in the elicitation standard. So evaluation-aware models (Glossary 'deception'; capability 1.3.1(8)) aren't only a risk source; they're a threat to the measurement, which the elicitation must counter.
- **"Cannot exclude a similar use"** is a burden-of-proof rule: if others integrate similar models into agents with code execution and web access, the signatory must evaluate its model in such a context unless it can *exclude* that use. That's the recital (b) principle ("as reasonably foreseeable") made concrete and strict. A fork asked "must providers test agentic use of a chat model?" should say: yes, if such integrations are used for similar models and the signatory can't exclude similar use (Appendix 3.2(2)(b)).
- **"Rate limits"** as an elicitation variable: models given more queries or parallelism do more. Test-time compute, again.

**Questions to keep.**
- "How hard must providers try to elicit dangerous capabilities?" Appendix 3.2: at least state-of-the-art elicitation, minimising under-elicitation and model deception (e.g. sandbagging); at least matching "the model elicitation capabilities of misuse actors relevant to the systemic risk scenario" and "the expected use context".
- "Must evaluations include fine-tuning?" "Such as by… conducting fine-tuning and prompt engineering", and at least matching relevant misuse actors' elicitation capabilities.
- "Must a provider evaluate its model in agentic setups?" If integrations are planned/considered, or are "currently used for similar models" and the signatory "cannot exclude a similar use".
- Should be asked but might not be: "How do scenarios affect evaluation?" They fix the relevant misuse actors, whose elicitation capability sets the floor.

**Prediction for unit 110.** Appendix 3.3, Assessing the effectiveness of mitigations: evaluations of mitigations (red-teaming safeguards, measuring jailbreak robustness, testing against fine-tuning attacks, assessing whether mitigations can be circumvented), "commensurate with the systemic risk". Then 3.4 (qualified internal teams: expertise, access incl. to CoT, sufficient time, compute) and 3.5 (external evaluators: required unless the model is similarly safe or safer, or no adequately qualified evaluator could be found).
