# Unit 57 · L471–479 · p.22: Measure 7.6, Model Report updates (first paragraph and grounds)

**What it held.** "Signatories will update their Model Report if they have **reasonable grounds to believe that the justification** for why the systemic risks stemming from the model are acceptable (pursuant to Measure 7.2, point (1)) **has been materially undermined**." Example grounds:
1. "one of the conditions listed pursuant to Measure 7.2, point (2), has materialised;"
2. "the model's capabilities, propensities, and/or affordances have changed or will change materially, such as through **further post-training, access to additional tools, or increase in inference compute**;"
3. "the model's use and/or integrations into AI systems have changed or will change materially;"
4. "serious incidents and/or near misses involving the model or a similar model have occurred; and/or"
5. "developments have occurred that materially undermine the **external validity of model evaluations** conducted, materially improve the state of the art of model evaluation methods, and/or for other reasons suggest that the systemic risk assessment conducted is materially inaccurate."

**What differed.** As predicted, including the link to M7.2(2)'s defeaters: the design is self-consistent, and I'd anticipated it. What I didn't predict: (5) **external validity** of evaluations, and **improved evaluation state of the art**, as grounds. A better evaluation method appearing makes your old assessment potentially "materially inaccurate".

**Wandering.**
- **The safety case lives.** M7.2 states the justification and its defeaters; M3.5 monitors for disconfirmation; M7.6 updates when a defeater materialises or the justification is otherwise undermined; Figure 2 shows a full reassessment before each update. A closed loop: claim → defeaters → monitoring → update. This is the most coherent risk-governance design I've read in a regulatory text; worth saying plainly, while marking it as my judgement.
- **(2) is the trigger for post-release capability change**: post-training, tools, inference compute. These are exactly recital (b)'s context factors and M1.2's trigger-point dimensions. Consistency across the Chapter is striking: the same small set of risk factors (capabilities, propensities, affordances, use/integration, inference compute) recurs at every stage.
- **(5) is epistemically sophisticated.** "External validity of model evaluations": whether evaluation results generalise to real use. If a new paper shows that a benchmark doesn't predict real-world uplift, the assessment's foundation is undermined; update. And if evaluation methods improve, the old assessment may now be below the state of the art, which the Code treats as a reason to redo it. The state-of-the-art ratchet (recital (a)) applies retroactively to assessments.
- **"Will change"** again: anticipated changes count. The provider must update *before* releasing a tool integration that will materially change risk, not after.
- **"Similar model"** again in (4). Cross-provider learning.
- The first paragraph is what the earlier cross-reference ("Measure 7.6, first and third paragraph", unit 16) pointed to: this paragraph triggers a full assessment. The third paragraph is still to come.

**Questions to keep.**
- "When must a Model Report be updated?" M7.6: when there are reasonable grounds to believe the acceptability justification (M7.2(1)) "has been materially undermined"; examples: defeaters materialising; material changes in capabilities/propensities/affordances (post-training, tools, inference compute); changed use/integration; incidents or near misses (also with similar models); evaluation-validity or state-of-the-art developments.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Does a better evaluation method published by others oblige a provider to reassess?" Arguably yes: M7.6(5).

**Prediction for unit 58.** M7.6 second and third paragraphs: the update is completed within some time (e.g. "without undue delay" or N weeks) after the grounds arise; and a periodic duty: for models that are among the most capable (e.g. "if the model is one of the Signatory's most capable models available on the market"), update at least every 6 months, unless no material changes. Then Measure 7.7, Model Report notifications (within N days, unredacted access, before market placement).
