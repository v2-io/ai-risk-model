# Unit 98 · L804–816 · p.36: Appendix 1.3.3, Model affordances and other sources (1)–(9)

**What it held.** "Model affordances and other systemic risk sources, **encompassing model configurations, model properties, and the context in which the model is made available on the market**, include:
1. access to tools (**including other AI models/systems**), computational power (e.g. allowing a model to increase its speed of operations), or physical systems including critical infrastructure;
2. scalability (e.g. enabling high-volume data processing, rapid inference, or parallelisation);
3. release and distribution strategies;
4. level of human oversight (e.g. degree of model autonomy);
5. vulnerability to adversarial removal of guardrails;
6. vulnerability to model exfiltration (e.g. model leakage/theft);
7. lack of appropriate infrastructure security;
8. number of business users and number of end-users…, including the number of end-users using an AI system in which the model is integrated;
9. **offence-defence balance**, including the potential number, capacity, and motivation of malicious actors to misuse the model;" (continues)

**What differed.** My prediction listed nearly all of these (memory). New: the definition of the category ("model configurations, model properties, and the context in which the model is made available on the market"), and that **other AI models/systems count as tools**.

**Wandering.**
- **Affordances, defined broadly.** "Model configurations, model properties, and context" make this a catch-all for everything that isn't a capability or propensity: deployment choices (release strategy, oversight level), security posture (exfiltration vulnerability, infrastructure security), market facts (user numbers), threat environment (offence-defence balance). For our lexicon: the Code's "affordance" is broader than the HCI sense (what a system lets an agent do); it includes properties of the environment and the market. A fork mapping "affordance" should say so.
- **Release strategy is a risk source**, (3), and its mitigation counterpart was staged access (M5.1(4)). The Code is consistent: the same factor appears as risk source and as mitigation lever.
- **Offence-defence balance** brings in the attacker population ("number, capacity, and motivation of malicious actors"). That's a threat-environment variable, external to the model. The marginal-risk logic shows here too: if defenders can easily counter what the model enables, the risk is smaller.
- **"Context in which the model is made available on the market"**: market-anchored again; for internal use, the context factors would be the signatory's own deployment. The text says "made available on the market", so internal context isn't named. Small, but consistent with the pattern.
- **Inference compute** reappears as "computational power… allowing a model to increase its speed of operations" — and scalability via parallelisation. Recital (b)'s concern now has a place in the risk-source taxonomy.
- **Minimum list** again ("include").
