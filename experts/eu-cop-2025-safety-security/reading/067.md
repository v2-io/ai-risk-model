# Unit 67 · L541–550 · p.25: Measure 8.3, Promotion of a healthy risk culture (first part)

**What it held.**
- "Signatories will promote a healthy risk culture and take appropriate measures to ensure that actors who have been assigned responsibilities… take **a reasoned and balanced approach** to systemic risk."
- "Examples of **indicators** of a healthy risk culture… are:
  1. setting the tone… from the top, e.g. by the leadership clearly communicating the Signatory's Framework to staff;
  2. allowing clear communication and **challenge of decisions** concerning systemic risk;
  3. setting incentives and affording sufficient independence of staff involved in systemic risk assessment and mitigation to **discourage excessive systemic-risk-taking** and encourage an unbiased assessment…;
  4. **anonymous surveys find that staff are comfortable raising concerns** about systemic risks, are aware of channels for doing so, and understand the Signatory's Framework;" (continues)

**What differed.** Predicted most themes (tone from the top, challenge, independence, surveys, reporting channels). Not predicted:
- They're **indicators**, not measures: signs by which a healthy culture can be recognised. (4) is phrased as an *outcome* ("surveys find that…"), not an action.
- **"A reasoned and balanced approach"**: "balanced" cuts both ways.
- **"Excessive** systemic-risk-taking": some risk-taking is accepted; excess is discouraged.

**Wandering.**
- **"Balanced" is a quietly important word.** A culture that's healthy in the Code's sense isn't maximally cautious: it takes a "reasoned and balanced approach". This protects against the reading that the Code wants risk-aversion; it also gives a signatory a phrase to defend proceeding against internal objections ("our approach is balanced"). The innovation leg of Art. 1(1) shows up in the governance Measure. A fork should report both readings, and that (3)'s "discourage excessive systemic-risk-taking" and "unbiased assessment" set the direction: the bias to guard against is towards taking too much risk.
- **Indicators as evidence.** "Anonymous surveys find…" means the Code expects signatories to survey staff on risk culture, and the result to be evidence. That's measurable (unlike "tone from the top"). Will Model Reports or Frameworks contain survey results? Not required by C7; C8's evidence may surface only on AI Office request.
- **"Challenge of decisions"**: staff can contest risk decisions. Combined with M1.3's adherence review (non-adherence instances and reasons) and recital (d)'s whistleblower link, the Code builds an internal dissent channel.
- **Incentives**: e.g. risk staff's compensation shouldn't depend on launch success. That's the separation-of-duties logic of M8.1's presumption, applied to pay. Banking again (remuneration rules in CRD).
- This is the Measure recital (d) pre-referenced: "Measure 8.3 is without prejudice to… Directive (EU) 2019/1937". I expect whistleblower-adjacent content next: channels, non-retaliation, informing staff of legal protections.

**Questions to keep.**
- "What does a healthy risk culture mean in the Code?" M8.3: actors with risk responsibilities "take a reasoned and balanced approach to systemic risk"; indicators: tone from the top, challenge of decisions, incentives and independence "to discourage excessive systemic-risk-taking", staff surveys showing comfort in raising concerns.
- Should be asked but might not be: "Does 'balanced' mean the Code discourages too much caution?" Ambiguous; the indicators point against excessive risk-taking.

**Prediction for unit 68.** Indicators continued: (5) internal reporting channels used and concerns acted on; (6) no retaliation against staff who raise concerns, including to the AI Office; (7) informing workers of whistleblower protections under Directive 2019/1937 and the AI Office's whistleblower tool; (8) a process for staff to report to the AI Office? Then Commitment 9, serious incident reporting.
